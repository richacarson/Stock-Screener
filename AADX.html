Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Applied Aerospace & Defense, Inc. designs, engineers, and manufactures integrated aerospace and defense subsystems for space and defense applications. Products include propellant tanks, engine nozzles, satellite assemblies, wings and control surfaces, radar and communications antennas, missile bodies, directed energy systems, and Spinnaker de-orbit systems. Headquartered in Huntsville, Alabama, the company IPO'd in June 2026 at $20/share, raising $650M. Q2 2026 record revenue of $167.3M (+47.4% YoY), with FY2026 guidance of $670-690M and backlog exceeding $1.1B. Chris Rogers was elevated to President and CSO in August 2026.

Investment Thesis
Applied Aerospace & Defense is a pure-play defense subsystems manufacturer riding a generational demand cycle in space proliferation, hypersonics, and directed energy weapons. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $167.3M (+47.4% YoY), with adjusted EBITDA of $36.4M (+38.4%) and backlog exceeding $1.1B providing 6-7 quarters of visibility [1]. FY2026 guidance of $670-690M revenue and $150-155M adjusted EBITDA implies ~22% margins on an adjusted basis. CEO Ferguson's 'iPhone model' thesis — that defense technology is shifting from multi-decade programs to rapid refresh cycles — creates structural demand growth for AADX's mission-critical subsystems [2]. The IPO dramatically improved the balance sheet, with D/E falling from ~445% to 52%, and the elevation of Chris Rogers to President signals organizational maturation for the next growth phase. Physical manufacturing of flight-rated hardware with defense security clearances is maximally immune to AI disruption, while AI-driven satellite and autonomous combat demand creates a secular tailwind.
The bear case centers on GAAP profitability, valuation, and execution risk. The company reported a $154M GAAP net loss in Q2 2026, driven by $110M in IPO-related accelerated SBC vesting, producing a -30.24% profit margin that masks the underlying adjusted profitability [1][3]. Forward P/E of 42.67 is expensive for a company that has not yet delivered positive GAAP earnings, pricing in substantial margin expansion and revenue growth [3]. The stock has declined 11.5% from its $20 IPO price to $17.71, suggesting early investor skepticism. Free cash flow of -$129M reflects the capital-intensive nature of aerospace manufacturing and working capital absorption from rapid growth [3]. The PE-backed rollup strategy carries integration risk across multiple acquired businesses (including the recent CBI acquisition), and private equity sponsors will eventually seek exits that could create selling pressure. Defense spending is politically subject to budget debates and administration priorities, and any shift away from space/hypersonics could compress the backlog pipeline. AI Risk Protection (8/9): Applied Aerospace manufactures physical aerospace and defense hardware — propellant tanks, satellite assemblies, missile bodies, radar antennas, and directed energy systems [1]. These are tangible, precision-manufactured products requiring specialized facilities, tooling, material science expertise, and defense security clearances that AI cannot replicate.
