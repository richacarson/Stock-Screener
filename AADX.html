Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYQ ·United States
Applied Aerospace & Defense, Inc. designs, engineers, and manufactures integrated aerospace and defense subsystems for space and defense applications across three segments: Space and Launch Systems (propellant tanks, engine nozzles, solar arrays, Spinnaker de-orbit systems), Defense Aviation and Airborne Systems (wings, landing gear, fuel tanks, engine components), and C5ISR and Precision Strike Systems (radar antennas, missile bodies, directed energy systems). IPO'd in June 2026 at $20/share, AADX holds 87% sole- or single-source contract positions and reported Q2 2026 revenue of $167.3M (+47.4% YoY) with contract backlog exceeding $1.1B. Headquartered in Huntsville, Alabama.

Investment Thesis
Applied Aerospace & Defense at $11.45 with a $2.0B market cap offers exposure to a high-growth defense and space manufacturing platform trading 43% below its June 2026 IPO price of $20. Revenue surged 47.4% YoY to a record $167.3M in Q2 2026, with all three segments growing — Space and Launch up 58.5%, C5ISR up $13.7M, and Defense Aviation up 4.8% [1][2]. The $1.13B contract backlog provides 7+ quarters of revenue visibility, and 87% sole-source positions create durable competitive moats with multi-decade qualification cycles [1][3]. Adjusted EBITDA of $36.4M (approximately 22% margin) demonstrates underlying profitability masked by $110M in one-time IPO share-based compensation charges, and analysts project 47% annual earnings growth with a consensus Buy rating and $25.14 price target — representing over 100% upside from current levels [5]. The defense spending environment remains favorable with bipartisan support for military modernization and space infrastructure investment.
The bear case centers on early-stage public company risk and current unprofitability. AADX reported a $154M net loss in Q2 2026, and while the majority was IPO-related non-cash charges, the -30.2% GAAP profit margin and negative operating cash flow of -$94M raise questions about the company's path to reported profitability [1][5]. The stock's 43% decline from its $20 IPO price to $11.45 reflects market skepticism about the company's ability to convert backlog into earnings, and the Forward P/E of 28.8x on forward EPS of $0.40 demands meaningful execution improvement. The company carries $431M in debt against only $18M in cash, and with no dividend and negative free cash flow, shareholders rely entirely on capital appreciation. As a recent IPO with limited public operating history, AADX lacks the track record to validate management's execution capability across a full cycle. The defense sector faces budget uncertainty with potential sequestration, continuing resolution risks, and shifting administration priorities that could delay or cancel programs in AADX's backlog [3][5].
