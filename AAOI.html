Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Applied Optoelectronics designs, manufactures, and sells fiber-optic networking products including optical transceivers, lasers, and laser components for data center, CATV, and telecom markets. The company is vertically integrated, fabricating its own indium phosphide laser chips in Sugar Land, TX and Taipei, Taiwan. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $191.9M (+86% YoY) with 800G revenue of $12.8M (more than 10x YoY) and record CATV revenue of $80.6M. Q3 2026 guidance of $255-290M implies 130% YoY growth. Full-year 2026 revenue expected at approximately $1.1B. CEO Dr. Thompson Lin has led the company for nearly 30 years. Market cap ~$8.5B.
4,691 Employees ·www.ao-inc.com

Investment Thesis
AOI at $100.68 with an $8.5B market cap is a vertically integrated AI infrastructure play at the epicenter of the optical interconnect buildout — its 800G and 1.6T optical transceivers are essential components connecting GPUs in hyperscale AI data centers [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue surged 86% to $191.9M with 800G revenue exceeding $12.8M (10x+ YoY), and the company returned to non-GAAP profitability ($0.06 EPS). Q3 guidance of $255-290M implies 130% YoY growth, and full-year 2026 revenue is tracking toward $1.1B — more than doubling 2025's $456M [1]. After the 32.6% pullback from $149, valuation has become significantly more attractive: Forward P/E compressed to 21.88 with PEG of 0.78, suggesting the growth rate more than justifies the current price [4]. D/E of 18% with $500M cash provides balance sheet flexibility, and the vertically integrated InP laser manufacturing plus U.S. onshoring strategy create defensible competitive advantages as Western hyperscalers prioritize non-China supply chains [2]. Inspire Impact Score of 29 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based allocation.
The bear case centers on GAAP profitability, customer concentration, and capital intensity. Despite non-GAAP profitability, trailing GAAP profit margin remains -9.57% and ROE is -5.45%, with free cash flow deeply negative at approximately -$887M on a trailing basis due to massive capacity buildout spending [4]. The 2017-2022 collapse from $100+ to $2 was caused by losing a single customer (Amazon) — the same structural concentration risk exists today with Microsoft and a small number of hyperscalers representing the majority of data center revenue [2]. Competition from Coherent, Lumentum, and Chinese manufacturers like InnoLight could pressure 800G/1.6T pricing as industry supply catches demand. Prior insider selling of $99M (CEO $19.9M, CFO $15.2M) at prices above $100 raises questions about leadership's assessment of near-term upside versus the long-term story [3]. Shares outstanding surged approximately 50% YoY from the $382M equity raise, creating dilution that offsets per-share economics. The beta of 3.84 means market-wide risk-off events would amplify downside disproportionately, and the Q3 guidance of $255-290M requires the new Texas facility to ramp on schedule — any production delays would create a significant earnings miss [4].
