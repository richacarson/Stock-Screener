Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
AAON, Inc. designs, manufactures, and sells premium commercial and industrial HVAC equipment including rooftop units, chillers, air handling units, condensing units, and data center cooling solutions. The BASX brand provides advanced liquid cooling systems for hyperscale AI data centers. Operates manufacturing facilities in Tulsa and Longview, Texas. Founded in 1988, headquartered in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

Investment Thesis
AAON is riding the most powerful secular tailwind in industrials: AI data center cooling demand. Q1 2026 delivered record revenue of $496.9M (54.3% YoY growth) driven by BASX segment revenue of $135.4M (up 104.5%), and total backlog surged 107.4% to a record $2.1B [1]. Management raised FY2026 guidance to 40-45% revenue growth (from 18-20%), signaling confidence in sustained demand. AI data centers require 15-30x the power density of traditional facilities, creating a structural demand tailwind for AAON's liquid cooling solutions that will persist as long as AI infrastructure buildout continues [2][3]. The company has a premium position in custom-engineered HVAC with 16.65% insider ownership providing management alignment, and the $7.77B market cap leaves room for significant upside if data center revenue scales to enterprise-grade consistency.
The primary risk is margin compression and valuation. Profit margin has declined from 14.0% (FY2024) to 7.3% (TTM) as BASX production scales at lower margins than legacy AAON products, and gross margin fell to 25.1% in Q1 2026 (guidance 27-28% for full year) [1][5]. At 39.38x forward earnings and 66.78x trailing PE, the stock prices in significant future growth, leaving minimal margin of safety against execution risk on a rapid production ramp. The $425M in total debt against essentially zero cash ($13K) creates balance sheet fragility if data center demand cycles or capacity investments take longer to generate returns [1]. Additionally, AAON's data center revenue concentration risk is increasing — if a major hyperscaler delays or cancels orders, the outsized backlog could unwind quickly. FY2025 net income actually declined YoY ($107.6M vs $168.6M in FY2024) despite 20% revenue growth, demonstrating that scaling BASX is dilutive to profitability in the near term [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record $2.1B backlog (+107.4% YoY) provides multi-year revenue visibility as AI data center cooling demand accelerates, with FY2026 guidance raised to 40-45% growth and BASX segment potentially surpassing legacy AAON revenue within 2-3 years
  2. Gross margin recovery toward 27-28% guidance range as BASX production scales and manufacturing efficiency improves at the expanded Longview facility, potentially restoring profit margins toward historical 12-15% levels
  3. AI infrastructure buildout represents a multi-decade secular trend — hyperscaler capital expenditure plans of $200B+ annually through 2030 imply sustained demand for advanced cooling solutions well beyond the current order cycle
Key Risks
  1. Profit margin compression from 14.0% to 7.3% demonstrates that BASX revenue growth is currently dilutive to earnings — FY2025 net income declined to $107.6M from $168.6M despite 20% revenue growth, and the margin recovery timeline is uncertain
  2. Forward PE of 39.38x and trailing PE of 66.78x price in aggressive growth assumptions that leave no room for execution missteps on the BASX production ramp, customer order delays, or hyperscaler capex pauses
  3. Near-zero cash position ($13K) against $425M total debt creates balance sheet vulnerability during a period of aggressive capacity expansion ($52.9M capex in Q1 alone), leaving limited financial flexibility if data center demand cycles unexpectedly
