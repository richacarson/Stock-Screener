Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Specialty Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Advance Auto Parts is one of the largest automotive aftermarket parts retailers in North America, operating approximately 4,700 stores and serving both do-it-yourself (DIY) consumers and professional (commercial) mechanics through its Advance Auto Parts, Carquest, and Worldpac brands. Under CEO Shane O'Kelly (since September 2023), the company is executing a multi-year turnaround focused on merchandising improvements, operational efficiency, and professional customer growth. Q1 FY2026 delivered the strongest comp sales growth in five years at 3.5%, with adjusted operating margin expanding 400bps to 3.8%.

The Forward PEG of 0.4 is the lowest in the batch, reflecting analyst expectations of 22% EPS growth as the turnaround gains traction under CEO O'Kelly's merchandising and operational initiatives. If AAP can close even a fraction of the margin gap with O'Reilly (which operates at ~20% operating margin vs. AAP's 3.8%), the stock has significant upside from current levels.
The bear case is substantial and reflected in the overall score of 33 (SELL). The dividend was slashed 83% from $1.50 to $0.25 per quarter, and the current 1.88% yield is below the 2% Dividend sleeve threshold. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): AAP's core business — selling physical auto parts to DIY consumers and professional mechanics — is protected from AI disruption by its fundamental physical-world requirements [1]. Cars need physical replacement parts (brake pads, oil filters, batteries, alternators), and AI cannot manufacture, stock, or install these components. The professional business requires relationship-driven sales, local inventory availability, and rapid delivery to repair shops — all physical-world operations with high switching costs. Some AI exposure exists in diagnostic tools (AI-powered vehicle diagnostics could change how mechanics identify needed parts) and inventory management (AI optimization could benefit competitors), but these are efficiency enhancers, not displacement threats. Analysts have not flagged AI as a significant risk to automotive aftermarket retail. AI Opportunity (0/1): AAP does not generate revenue from AI products or services and has no public AI deployment strategy.
