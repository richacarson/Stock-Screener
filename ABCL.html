Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Canada
AbCellera Biologics is a Vancouver-based, technology-driven biopharmaceutical company that uses an AI-powered discovery engine (microfluidics, high-throughput screening, genomics and machine learning) to find and develop antibody medicines. Founded by CEO Carl Hansen out of his University of British Columbia lab, it discovered bamlanivimab, the first COVID-19 antibody authorized for use (partnered with Eli Lilly), and has formed more than 100 partnered programs with 27 partners. It is now shifting from partner-funded discovery to developing its own clinical assets, led by ABCL635 for vasomotor symptoms and ABCL575, backed by $675M+ in available liquidity.

Investment Thesis
AbCellera is a well-funded, founder-led bet on whether its AI-powered discovery engine can produce its own blockbuster. With $675M+ of liquidity and minimal debt (D/E of 15%), the company can fund ABCL635, its non-hormonal candidate for menopausal hot flashes, and ABCL575 through key clinical milestones without near-term financing pressure [1]. New partnerships such as the Vertex collaboration and more than 100 partner programs provide optional milestone and royalty upside, and the platform's record (bamlanivimab in months) gives credibility [1][3].
The bear case is that the financial profile is that of an early-stage biotech. Q2 2026 revenue collapsed 76% to $4.1M, the quarterly net loss widened to $55.4M, and ROE, profit margin and forward earnings are all deeply negative, giving Infrastructure a score of 1/10 [1][4]. Clinical risk is binary, and AI-native antibody-design competitors threaten the economics of discovery services. A capital raise following the stock's rally also signals dilution risk while the company burns more than $50M per quarter [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. ABCL635 advancing toward later-stage trials for vasomotor symptoms, a large non-hormonal women's health market [1]
  2. Milestones and expansion of new partnerships (including Vertex) that rebuild licensing revenue from the $4.1M Q2 2026 trough [1]
  3. Start of ABCL575 clinical data and use of in-house GMP manufacturing to speed IND filings [1]
Key Risks
  1. Binary clinical risk: a setback for ABCL635 would remove most of the internal-pipeline value with no commercial product to fall back on
  2. Ongoing cash burn (Q2 2026 net loss of $55.4M, R&D of $46.0M) and possible further equity dilution [1][6]
  3. AI-native antibody-design competitors and pharma in-house AI groups compressing discovery-partnership economics [2]
