Company Profile

Above Food Ingredients is a Canadian regenerative ingredient company operating through two segments: Disruptive Agriculture (grain origination, processing, and sale of regeneratively grown crops) and Consumer Packaged Goods (proprietary and private-label consumer product formulations). Founded by Lionel Kambeitz, the company has undergone strategic restructuring including subsidiary receiverships and faces Nasdaq delisting proceedings. Headquartered in Regina, Saskatchewan.

Investment Thesis
Above Food Ingredients is a micro-cap ($3.1M market cap) Canadian regenerative agriculture company in acute distress. The stock has collapsed from $0.59 to $0.06 since April 2026, and the company faces Nasdaq delisting after failing to file its Form 20-F for FY2025 [1][4]. Management has raised FY2026 profit guidance to $40M+ from $30M, which if delivered would represent a dramatic turnaround from trailing losses of -$61M. The company claims to have eliminated all corporate debt through subsidiary receiverships and is pivoting toward Agri-Tech/Fin-Tech via a proposed merger with Palm Global Technologies. If management delivers on profitability and resolves the Nasdaq compliance issues, the stock offers extreme upside from a $3M market cap base.
The bear case is overwhelming. Revenue declined 45% YoY with deeply negative profit margins of -21%, and no audited financials have been filed for FY2025 — the Nasdaq has initiated delisting proceedings [1][4]. The $3.1M market cap and $0.06 stock price create existential risks including delisting, zero institutional coverage, and potential permanent capital loss. The pivot from food ingredients to 'Agri-Tech and Fin-Tech' via the Palm Global merger raises fundamental questions about strategic coherence and management credibility. The company's $40M+ profit guidance for FY2026 lacks credibility given no audited financials and ongoing delisting. The Altman Z-Score-equivalent financial profile suggests high bankruptcy risk. Investors face asymmetric downside risk — the company may cease to be a publicly traded entity before any turnaround materializes.
