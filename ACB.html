Aurora Cannabis Inc. produces, distributes, and sells cannabis and cannabis-derivative products in Canada and internationally. The company operates through three segments: Canadian Cannabis, International Cannabis, and Plant Propagation. Aurora is a leading medical cannabis company with operations in Germany, Poland, and other international markets, with medical cannabis comprising the majority of revenue.

Investment Thesis
Aurora Cannabis delivered its strongest fiscal year to date in FY2026 with CAD 321M net revenue (up 11%), adjusted EBITDA of CAD 54M (up 32%), and a clean balance sheet with $165M cash and minimal debt (D/E 17%) [1]. The international medical cannabis strategy is working — 55% of revenue now comes from international markets with double-digit growth in Germany and Poland. The PEG ratio of 0.49 suggests the market is pricing in significant earnings growth, and the company's pivot to high-margin medical products is generating genuine operational improvement. If GAAP profitability follows adjusted EBITDA, the stock could re-rate meaningfully from its $179M market cap.
The bull case requires ignoring an extraordinary track record of shareholder destruction. Aurora's cumulative 1:120 reverse stock splits represent over 99% value erosion, and the -42.41% GAAP profit margin shows the company continues to generate accounting losses even as adjusted metrics improve [4][5]. The FY2027 'reset year' warning — with Canadian medical reimbursement changes expected to weigh on revenue and margins — signals more volatility ahead [1]. Cannabis remains federally illegal in the US, limiting the company's TAM, and regulatory frameworks in international markets are evolving unpredictably. The 2.9/5 Glassdoor rating, frequent leadership turnover, and organizational instability suggest the operational foundation remains fragile. At 37.81x forward earnings, the stock is priced for a profitable future that has never materialized on a GAAP basis.
Key Catalysts
  1. GAAP profitability inflection following eight consecutive quarters of adjusted EBITDA growth, which would trigger institutional investor interest and potential index inclusion
  2. German recreational cannabis legalization expanding Aurora's addressable market in Europe's largest economy, leveraging existing medical distribution infrastructure
  3. US federal cannabis rescheduling or legalization opening the world's largest cannabis market to international operators with established pharmaceutical-grade capabilities
Key Risks
  1. GAAP profit margin of -42.41% and history of cumulative 1:120 reverse splits demonstrate persistent inability to generate sustainable shareholder returns — adjusted EBITDA improvements have not translated to bottom-line profitability
  2. FY2027 'reset year' guidance warns of Canadian medical reimbursement changes that could compress revenue and margins in Aurora's home market
  3. Cannabis industry faces ongoing regulatory uncertainty, banking restrictions, and stigma that limit institutional investment and create binary risk events around policy changes
