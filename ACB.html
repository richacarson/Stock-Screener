Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - Specialty & Generic ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Aurora Cannabis Inc. is a Canadian cannabis company focused on global medical cannabis production and distribution. Under CEO Miguel Martin, Aurora has pivoted from a diversified recreational/medical model to a medical-cannabis-first strategy, generating 55% of net revenue internationally with leading positions in Germany and Poland. FY2026 net revenue reached CAD 321M (+11% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of CAD 54M (+32% YoY) and adjusted gross margins of 60%. The company ended FY2026 with $165M in cash and minimal debt, but remains GAAP unprofitable with a -38% profit margin due to impairments and restructuring charges accumulated during years of value destruction from a peak market cap of ~$10B to ~$260M today.

Investment Thesis
Aurora Cannabis at $4.09 per share ($260M market cap) is a turnaround story with improving fundamentals but insufficient quality to warrant investment under the IOWN framework. FY2026 delivered CAD 321M revenue (+11%), adjusted EBITDA of CAD 54M (+32%), and 60% adjusted gross margins — demonstrating that the medical cannabis pivot is working operationally [1]. The company holds $165M cash with minimal debt (D/E 4.3%), providing runway, and its #1 global medical cannabis position with leadership in Germany and Poland creates a differentiated moat [4]. Disease-resistant cultivar research and EU market expansion provide growth vectors in a cannabis market expected to benefit from continued international legalization.
The bear case is substantial. Aurora remains GAAP unprofitable with a -38% profit margin, negative ROE, and a forward P/E of -37x indicating expected continued losses [4]. The stock has destroyed ~97% of shareholder value from its 2018 peak, and the $260M market cap reflects deep investor skepticism. Employee culture scores are poor (Glassdoor 2.6 culture, 2.4 career opportunities), and the company has no AI capabilities or competitive technology moats beyond regulatory licenses [2]. The cannabis industry remains federally illegal in the US (limiting Aurora's largest addressable market), pricing pressure continues in Canada, and the Inspire Impact Score of -38 (MISALIGNED) due to Cannabis: Cultivation and THC Retail creates a faith-alignment barrier [3]. Revenue growth metrics diverge between yfinance (-8.8%) and company reporting (+11% CAD), suggesting currency and timing complications. Until Aurora achieves sustained GAAP profitability and demonstrates a durable competitive advantage beyond medical cannabis licensing, the risk-reward profile remains unattractive.
Key Catalysts
  1. German cannabis legalization expansion could significantly increase Aurora's largest international market, with Aurora already holding GMP-certified production and distribution infrastructure as the market leader in Germany's medical cannabis segment [1]
  2. Continued adjusted EBITDA growth (CAD 54M, +32% YoY) could lead to GAAP profitability inflection as restructuring charges and impairments decline, potentially re-rating the stock from a loss-making cannabis company to a profitable medical cannabis leader [1]
  3. US federal cannabis reform (rescheduling or legalization) would open the world's largest cannabis market and could trigger significant revaluation of Canadian licensed producers with international GMP-certified operations like Aurora [1]
Key Risks
  1. Sustained GAAP unprofitability — profit margin of -38.3% and negative ROE of -9.4% indicate the company is still burning cash on a GAAP basis despite adjusted EBITDA positivity, and dilutive equity raises could further destroy shareholder value [4]
  2. Cannabis pricing compression in Canada and increasing competition in EU medical markets from new entrants could erode Aurora's margins and market share, particularly as German legalization attracts larger, better-capitalized competitors [1]
  3. US federal cannabis reform remains uncertain and could benefit US-based operators over Canadian LPs, while Aurora's $260M market cap limits its ability to compete for acquisitions or invest in new market entry against larger peers like Tilray [1]
