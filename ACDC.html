Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
ProFrac Holding Corp. operates as a vertically integrated energy services company providing hydraulic fracturing, proppant production, well stimulation, equipment manufacturing, and specialty chemistry services to upstream oil and gas companies in the United States. Operating through Stimulation Services, Proppant Production, Manufacturing, and Flotek Industries segments, ProFrac has grown through acquisitions including US Well Services, Flotek Industries, and Performance Proppants. Q2 2026 revenue of $498M (+11% sequential) and adjusted EBITDA of $69M showed improving trajectory, but TTM revenue of $1.79B remains down 18.9% YoY amid persistent oilfield services pricing pressure. Market cap ~$903M.

Investment Thesis
ProFrac offers a deeply discounted entry into vertically integrated North American hydraulic fracturing at $4.96/share (~0.5x TTM revenue, ~3.3x annualized Q2 EBITDA). Q2 2026 showed meaningful sequential improvement — revenue of $498M (+11% from Q1's $450M) and adjusted EBITDA of $69M (up 28% from $54M) — suggesting the trough may be behind the company [1]. Vertical integration across frac fleets, sand mines, manufacturing, and specialty chemistry provides structural cost advantages that should amplify operating leverage when completions activity recovers. The Wilks family's $10M+ insider buying at $4.00/share signals conviction from those closest to the business. Free cash flow of $67M on a TTM basis provides liquidity despite the heavy debt load.
The bear case remains severe. ProFrac is deeply unprofitable on a GAAP basis with -22.8% profit margin, -44.8% ROE, and consensus full-year 2026 EPS of -$1.03 to -$1.28 [1]. Debt of $1.22B against $18.8M cash creates significant financial fragility with D/E of 170%, and TTM revenue of $1.79B is down 32% from the 2023 peak of $2.63B. The dual-class share structure concentrates control with the Wilks family, and the CEO transition from Ladd Wilks to Matt Wilks in August 2026 maintains family governance rather than introducing independent leadership. Glassdoor ratings of 2.9/5 with frequent layoff reports indicate cultural dysfunction that limits talent retention in a skilled-labor industry [2]. The hydraulic fracturing market remains fragmented with excess fleet supply that suppresses pricing, and recovery timing depends on oil prices and E&P capital discipline beyond management's control. The Inspire Impact Score of 34 (ALIGNED) provides faith-based appeal but cannot offset fundamental financial weakness.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sequential revenue and EBITDA improvement — Q2 2026 revenue of $498M (+11% from Q1) and EBITDA of $69M (+28%) suggest early cyclical inflection that could accelerate if E&P operators increase completion activity in H2 2026 and into 2027 [1]
  2. Vertical integration margin expansion leveraging in-house proppant supply and equipment manufacturing to capture more value per frac stage during an upcycle, potentially driving EBITDA margins toward mid-cycle levels of 15-20%

AI Risk Protection (8/9): ProFrac's core business — hydraulic fracturing, proppant mining and production, and heavy equipment manufacturing — is inherently physical-world and structurally immune to AI displacement [1].
