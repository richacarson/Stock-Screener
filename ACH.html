Company Profile
Healthcare ·Home Health Care Services ·NYSE ·United States
Accendra Health, Inc. (formerly Owens & Minor) is a pure-play home-based care company operating through its Apria and Byram Healthcare brands, providing in-home care for diabetes treatment, respiratory therapy, sleep apnea (CPAP), and other durable medical equipment and supplies. The company completed its transformation in December 2025 by divesting the Products & Healthcare Services segment for $375M and rebranding from Owens & Minor.

Investment Thesis
Accendra Health is a post-transformation turnaround story with improving fundamentals. Q1 2026 beat EPS expectations, gross margins surged to ~46% from 19% pre-divestiture, and EBITDA margins reached ~13% — demonstrating the pure-play home-based care model can generate meaningful cash flow [1][4]. FY2026 guidance of $2.55-2.65B revenue and $335-355M EBITDA provides a path to stabilization. At a forward P/E of ~3x and PEG of ~0.6, the stock is priced for significant risk but offers substantial upside if the company achieves profitability on an increasingly asset-light, higher-margin business serving the secular tailwind of aging demographics and the shift from institutional to home-based healthcare.
The bear case is severe. The stock has declined from $9+ to ~$3 since the transformation began, reflecting market skepticism about execution. GAAP net margin remains -39.85% due to restructuring charges and heavy debt servicing on a negative-equity balance sheet (D/E of -4.67) [4]. The loss of a major commercial payer contract drove Q1 2026 revenue down 6.8% YoY, and the $2.55-2.65B FY2026 guidance implies continued revenue contraction [1]. Leadership instability — two executives departed post-divestiture, the executive team receives a 'C-' rating, and the CEO has presided over an 85%+ stock price decline — raises questions about whether this management team can execute the turnaround. The 'State Owned Enterprise' Inspire negative attribution appears to be a legacy data mapping from when the ACH ticker belonged to Aluminum Corporation of China.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2026 EBITDA guidance of $335-355M validates the post-divestiture margin expansion thesis, with potential for upside if the commercial payer contract loss is backfilled
  2. Aging U.S. demographics and Medicare policy shifts favoring home-based care over institutional settings provide a secular growth tailwind for Apria and Byram Healthcare brands
  3. Balance sheet deleveraging from divestiture proceeds and improved cash flow could reduce the negative equity position and re-rate the stock toward analyst target of $4.49 (60%+ upside)

  3. Leadership instability with two executive departures, a 'C-' executive team rating, and an 85%+ stock price decline under current CEO raises fundamental questions about management's ability to execute
