Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NASDAQ ·United States
Acadia Healthcare Company, Inc., based in Franklin, Tennessee, operates US behavioral health facilities including acute inpatient psychiatric hospitals, specialty treatment facilities, residential treatment centers and comprehensive treatment centers for opioid use disorder. Q2 2026 revenue was $865.8M, about flat YoY, and adjusted EBITDA fell to $149.2M from $201.8M on higher professional and general liability costs; full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $3.40-3.45B revenue and $590-615M adjusted EBITDA. The company faces a DOJ Criminal Division and SEC investigation into admissions, length of stay and billing, plus securities litigation that followed a 2024 New York Times investigation. Debbie Osteen has returned as CEO. Market cap approximately $2.8B at $29.96.

Investment Thesis
Acadia is a stabilization story in a sector with lasting demand. Q2 2026 beat consensus (adjusted EPS of $0.38 versus $0.33 expected, revenue of $865.8M versus $844.8M expected), and management raised full-year guidance to $3.40-3.45B revenue and $590-615M adjusted EBITDA [1][4]. Excluding the timing of state supplemental payments, same-facility revenue would have grown 3.2% [1]. At a forward P/E of 16.2 on forward EPS of $1.85, up from a 52-week low of $11.43, any clean resolution of the federal investigation could lift the valuation further [8].
The bear case is legal and reputational overhang plus leverage. The DOJ Criminal Division re-issued subpoenas in December 2024 on admissions, length of stay and billing, the SEC is also involved, and the internal investigation has reached no conclusions [3]. A securities class action covering February 2020 to September 2024 followed a New York Times investigation that hurt patient volumes [5], and patient-safety lawsuits and a CMS immediate-jeopardy citation at a youth hospital add risk [3]. Professional and general liability costs rose $39.3M in Q2 alone, adjusted EBITDA fell 26% YoY, and $2.59B of debt sits against $171M of cash with negative free cash flow [1][8].
Key Catalysts
  1. Resolution or narrowing of the DOJ Criminal Division and SEC investigations into admissions and billing practices [3]
  2. Same-facility growth returning to about 3% as state supplemental payment timing normalizes [1]
  3. Delivering on the raised 2026 guidance of $590-615M adjusted EBITDA and $1.45-1.60 adjusted EPS [1]
Key Risks
  1. Federal criminal and SEC investigation into length of stay and billing, plus securities class action and derivative suits, could lead to large penalties or Medicaid/Medicare consequences [3]
  2. Rising professional and general liability costs ($39.3M increase in Q2 2026, including a $28.6M reserve adjustment) compressing margins [1]
  3. Leverage of $2.59B debt with negative free cash flow and heavy reliance on Medicaid and state supplemental payments [1][8]
