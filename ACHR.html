Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Archer Aviation is a next-generation aerospace company developing the Midnight eVTOL (electric vertical takeoff and landing) aircraft for urban air mobility and expanding into defense through the strategic acquisition of three Boeing-owned companies (Wisk, InSitu, and SkyGrid). The Midnight aircraft completed a piloted round trip between two California airports in late July 2026, marking a key milestone toward FAA certification expected by late 2026. Archer reported Q2 2026 revenue of $5M (+213% QoQ), year-to-date revenue of $6.6M, and maintains $1.6B in liquidity. The InSitu acquisition brings a profitable defense drone business with $200M+ in annual revenue. Archer was selected for the U.S. eVTOL Integration Pilot Program and has a 100-person AI team developing the Zee aviation AI foundation model in partnership with NVIDIA. CEO and Founder Adam Goldstein leads a team of approximately 1,100 employees.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 7 /10
Archer Aviation is a young company with strong infinite game characteristics — a genuine Just Cause centered on zero-emission air mobility, courageous long-term bets on a technology that is years from commercial viability, and a willingness to fundamentally expand the business through the Boeing acquisition. Founder-CEO Adam Goldstein has maintained the mission through years of pre-revenue development, capital raises, and regulatory uncertainty, demonstrating the kind of cause-driven persistence Sinek values. However, the company is too young (founded 2018) to have been tested through the leadership transitions, market crises, and multi-decade consistency that separate authentic infinite game players from well-intentioned startups. The Defense expansion (InSitu, Halo UAS with Anduril) introduces tension between the zero-emission civilian purpose and military applications.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Archer's core business is fundamentally physical — designing, manufacturing, certifying, and operating electric aircraft requires aerospace engineering, FAA certification, physical production facilities, battery technology, and flight operations infrastructure that AI cannot substitute [1]. The eVTOL industry is one of the most capital-intensive and regulation-heavy sectors in the economy, creating structural barriers that no AI application can circumvent. AI is overwhelmingly an enabler for Archer: the company IS building AI into aviation (Zee foundation model, NVIDIA partnership, autonomous flight capabilities from Wisk acquisition), making it a builder of AI-powered physical infrastructure rather than a company vulnerable to AI disruption [2]. The InSitu defense drone business benefits directly from AI integration for autonomous operations, making AI a revenue driver rather than a threat. The only theoretical AI risk is that autonomous drone technology from competitors could challenge Archer's defense business, but Archer's own AI capabilities (Zee, Wisk autonomous eVTOL, NVIDIA partnership) position it to be the disruptor rather than the disrupted. AI Opportunity (1/1): Archer generates AI-embedded value through the Zee aviation AI foundation model (announced 2026), the NVIDIA IGX Thor partnership for next-generation aviation AI, the Wisk autonomous eVTOL platform (which depends on AI for pilotless operation), and InSitu's defense drone autonomy capabilities [2]. While AI-specific revenue is not yet separately quantified, the company's AI team of 100 and its partnerships represent measurable AI investment that will be core to commercial eVTOL and defense drone revenue streams.
