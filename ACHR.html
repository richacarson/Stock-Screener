Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYQ ·United States
Archer Aviation is a next-generation aerospace company developing the Midnight eVTOL (electric vertical takeoff and landing) aircraft for urban air mobility and expanding into defense through the strategic acquisition of three Boeing-owned subsidiaries — Wisk (autonomous eVTOL), InSitu ($200M+ annual defense drone revenue), and SkyGrid (airspace management). Q2 2026 revenue was $5M (+213% QoQ), YTD revenue $6.6M, with $1.6B in liquidity. The company has a 100-person AI team developing the ZEE aviation AI foundation model in partnership with NVIDIA. CEO and Founder Adam Goldstein leads approximately 1,160 employees from San Jose, California.

Investment Thesis
Archer Aviation at $5.03 ($3.87B market cap) represents a high-conviction bet on the convergence of eVTOL commercialization, defense drone technology, and aviation AI — with $1.56B in liquidity providing multi-year runway [1]. The Boeing acquisition transforms Archer from a single-product startup into a diversified aerospace platform: InSitu brings $200M+ in profitable annual defense drone revenue immediately, Wisk adds autonomous eVTOL technology, and SkyGrid provides airspace management software. The Midnight aircraft's piloted round trip between airports (July 2026) and FAA certification path create near-term catalysts, while the ZEE aviation AI model and NVIDIA IGX Thor partnership position Archer as a technology leader in physical AI for aviation [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 60 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes this one of the most faith-alignment-friendly aerospace investments available.
The bear case centers on execution risk and pre-commercial financials that dominate the Infrastructure score of 2. Archer has $6.6M in YTD revenue against an adjusted EBITDA loss of $177M in Q2 alone — ROE is -44.5%, profit margin is negligible, and every profitability metric is at the floor [1]. FAA certification for eVTOL aircraft is unprecedented, and any delay could significantly impact the thesis and require additional dilutive capital raises. The stock has declined 25% from $6.72 to $5.03, reflecting market skepticism about commercialization timelines. The Boeing acquisition, while strategically compelling, adds integration complexity and defense-sector exposure. The eVTOL market itself is unproven — no company globally has achieved commercial passenger eVTOL operations, and consumer adoption, pricing economics, and infrastructure requirements remain uncertain. Competition from Joby Aviation and potential entrants from traditional aerospace creates pressure. At $3.87B market cap, the stock prices in significant success that has not yet been demonstrated [1][2].
