Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Grocery Stores ·NYSE ·United States
Albertsons Companies operates one of the largest food and drug retail chains in the United States with approximately 2,270 stores across 34 states under banners including Albertsons, Safeway, Vons, Jewel-Osco, Shaw's, and ACME. The company also operates in-store pharmacies, fuel centers, and a growing digital commerce platform. Under CEO Susan Morris, the company is pursuing an organic growth strategy centered on AI-driven operational efficiency after the failed $24.6B Kroger merger. Q1 FY2026 net sales of $24.9B (+0.2% YoY) with identical sales declining 0.8%. TTM revenue of $83.2B. Founded in 1860 and headquartered in Boise, Idaho.

Investment Thesis
Albertsons at $12.54 with a $6.1B market cap trades at a deeply discounted 6.78x forward P/E with a 5.42% dividend yield, offering potential value if GAAP earnings normalize from merger-related charges that depressed trailing profit margin to 0.08% [1]. The company operates 2,270 stores generating $83.2B in annual revenue providing essential, recession-resistant grocery demand, and CEO Susan Morris is investing in AI-driven operational efficiency with quantified results including 10% digital cart size increases from the 'Ask AI' tool built on Google Vertex AI [3]. Forward EPS of $1.85 versus trailing EPS of $0.17 suggests massive earnings recovery ahead, and the recent 13% dividend increase to $0.68/share signals management confidence in cash flow sustainability. Free cash flow of $1.75B comfortably covers the approximately $350M annual dividend cost.
The bear case is severe and centers on balance sheet distress. D/E of 974% represents existential financial risk — $15.7B in total debt against just $308M in cash creates extreme vulnerability to any sustained margin compression from grocery price wars, inflation, or economic downturn [1]. Q1 FY2026 showed alarming stagnation with revenue growing just 0.2% YoY and identical sales ex-fuel declining 0.8%, suggesting the post-merger organic growth thesis is failing to gain traction against intensifying competition [1]. ROE of 2.7% reflects genuinely weak returns on equity, not just charge distortion. The grocery competitive landscape continues to intensify with Walmart's AI-powered supply chain optimization, Amazon Fresh and Whole Foods expansion, and Aldi/Lidl opening hundreds of new US stores — all better-capitalized competitors against which Albertsons lacks a clear structural moat [1][4]. The lowered FY2026 EPS guidance to $1.75-$1.85 (versus initial $2.22-$2.32) signals deteriorating near-term fundamentals. Shareholder activists have flagged inadequate food waste disclosure and human rights supply chain policies relative to Kroger [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Normalized GAAP earnings recovery as $200M+ in Kroger merger-related charges roll off, potentially restoring profit margins toward the historical 1.5-2% range and closing the gap between trailing PE (73.8x) and forward PE (6.78x) [1]
  2. AI-driven operational efficiency gains across four strategic pillars — digital experience, merchandising intelligence, supply chain automation, and labor productivity — could expand operating margins by 20-50bps in an industry where every basis point is material [3]
  3. Regional consolidation from 11 divisions to 4 creates centralized merchandising efficiency and cost synergies that should begin to flow through the P&L in the second half of FY2026 [3]
Key Risks
  1. Debt-to-equity of 974% with $15.7B total debt against $308M cash creates severe financial vulnerability — any sustained margin compression from grocery price wars or recession could threaten covenants and force dividend cuts or asset sales [1]
  2. Q1 FY2026 identical sales ex-fuel declined 0.8% with total revenue growing just 0.2% YoY, and full-year EPS guidance was lowered to $1.75-$1.85 from $2.22-$2.32, signaling organic growth is stalling post-Kroger merger collapse [1]
  3. Intensifying competitive pressure from better-capitalized Walmart (AI supply chain), Amazon (Fresh/Whole Foods), Costco (membership expansion), and Aldi/Lidl (aggressive US store buildout) in a structurally low-margin industry where Albertsons lacks a defensible moat [4]
