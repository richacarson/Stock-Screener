Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto & Truck Dealerships ·NYSE ·United States
ACV Auctions operates a digital wholesale marketplace for used vehicles in the United States, connecting dealers and commercial consignors through online auctions and data-driven inspection, plus marketplace services including ACV Transportation, ACV Capital and AI-powered tools such as MAX inventory management and ClearCar consumer appraisal. CEO George Chamoun has led the company since 2016. Q2 2026 revenue was a record $213.9M (+10% YoY), with marketplace GMV roughly flat; full-year 2026 guidance calls for $845M-$855M revenue (+11-13%). Q3 2026 results had not been reported as of the screen date (guidance $219M-$225M). Market cap approximately $1.8B.

Investment Thesis
ACV is the scaled digital disruptor in U.S. wholesale used-vehicle remarketing. Revenue has compounded at 29.5% a year, from $208.4M in 2020 to $759.6M in 2025, Q2 2026 revenue was a record $213.9M (+10%), and 2026 guidance of $845M-$855M implies 11-13% growth even with flat marketplace GMV [1][4]. The mix is improving toward higher-attach marketplace services (41% of revenue, +17% YoY), while AI inspection, ClearCar and MAX deepen dealer engagement [1][2]. Insider open-market buying in late 2025 and early 2026 and an Inspire Impact Score of 46 (ALIGNED, no negative attributions) add support [5].
The bear case is profitability and volume. ACV remains GAAP-unprofitable (ROE -15.1%, margin -7.9%, 2025 net loss $66.1M), and at 33.9x forward earnings the stock needs margin expansion that has so far been slow [4][6]. Revenue growth depends on fees and services rather than units, with Q2 2026 GMV roughly flat, so a weak used-vehicle cycle could stall growth [1]. Q3 2026 results, guided to $219M-$225M revenue and $21M-$24M adjusted EBITDA, were still pending at the screen date, and the stock has rallied above its 50-day average (about $8.96) to $10.50, raising the bar [1][6]. Ongoing antitrust litigation, both as plaintiff against Manheim and ADESA and as defendant in a bid-rigging class action, adds legal uncertainty [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results against guidance of $219M-$225M revenue (+10-13%) and $21M-$24M adjusted EBITDA; a beat would support the $845M-$855M full-year outlook [1]
  2. Continued growth of marketplace services (ACV Transportation and ACV Capital), which grew 17% YoY to 41% of revenue in Q2 2026 and lift revenue per vehicle [1]
  3. Adoption of AI data products (MAX inventory management, ClearCar consumer appraisal) by dealers, which could raise revenue per dealer and stickiness [2]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent GAAP losses: a 2025 net loss of $66.1M and Q2 2026 loss of $8.2M leave little cushion if used-vehicle volumes weaken [4]
  2. Flat marketplace GMV in Q2 2026 shows dependence on take-rate and service attach rather than share gains; incumbent auction operators such as Manheim and ADESA could cap volume growth [1][3]
  3. Legal exposure from the Western District of New York bid-rigging class action and ACV's own antitrust suit against Manheim and ADESA [3]

AI Risk Protection (7/9): ACV's marketplace rests on physical work AI cannot replace: in-person vehicle condition inspections, title and arbitration processes, vehicle transportation and dealer floor-plan financing, which make up the fast-growing marketplace-services segment [1][2]. AI mostly strengthens the model by improving inspection accuracy and pricing.
