Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto & Truck Dealerships ·NYSE ·United States
ACV Auctions Inc. operates the leading digital wholesale marketplace for used vehicles in the United States. The company provides dealers, commercial partners, and OEMs with AI-powered tools for vehicle inspection, valuation, and remarketing through its online auction platform and ACV MAX inventory intelligence suite. CEO George Chamoun has positioned ACV as the AI disruptor in the $200B+ wholesale auto market, with predictive pricing models accurate within $38 at retail. The company reported record Q2 2026 revenue of $214M (+10% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $21M, with FY2026 revenue guidance of ~$849M.

Investment Thesis
ACV Auctions is the leading AI-powered digital disruptor in the $200B+ wholesale auto market, leveraging proprietary data from millions of vehicle inspections to power predictive pricing (accurate within $38 at retail) and the ACV MAX inventory intelligence platform [1][3]. Q2 2026 revenue of $214M (+10% YoY) and record adjusted EBITDA of $21M demonstrate the company can grow and expand margins simultaneously, even as dealer wholesale volumes declined 6% in the quarter — proving market share gains over physical auction incumbents [1]. FY2026 guidance of $849M revenue and $73-77M adjusted EBITDA (+27% YoY) provides visibility to approaching GAAP profitability. PEG ratio of ~0.50x and 5-year revenue CAGR of ~23% suggest the market is undervaluing the growth trajectory at the current $1.3B market cap.
The bear case centers on revenue deceleration, persistent unprofitability, and competitive risk. Revenue growth has slowed from 16.3% (FY2025) to 10% (Q2 2026), and pro forma dealer wholesale volumes declined 6% — suggesting ACV is swimming against a macroeconomic tide that could intensify in a recession [1]. TTM net losses of -$62M and ROE of -14.3% reflect a company still burning cash on a GAAP basis, and the $18.7M bankruptcy charge in Q4 2025 highlights customer credit risk in a cyclical industry [4]. Manheim (Cox Automotive) and ADESA (Carvana) are well-capitalized incumbents with deep dealer relationships who are accelerating their own digital and AI capabilities — if either closes the technology gap, ACV's share gain narrative weakens materially. Stock has declined ~55% over 52 weeks, suggesting the market is skeptical of the current growth-to-profitability timeline. Glassdoor rating of 3.2/5 with leadership concerns raises questions about organizational health [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Viper AI inspection system scaling to 100-200 deployed units by year-end 2026, opening a new service lane acquisition channel that meets dealers where vehicles already are and could accelerate unit growth independent of wholesale market volume [3]
  2. Adjusted EBITDA margins expanding toward double digits (9% in FY2026) as operating leverage improves on fixed-cost AI and platform infrastructure, with potential GAAP profitability inflection in FY2027 [1][2]
  3. ACV MAX platform driving higher revenue per dealer through inventory intelligence features, data analytics, and remarketing tools that increase switching costs and expand the addressable market beyond auctions [3]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration from 16.3% (FY2025) to 10% (Q2 2026) amid 6% dealer wholesale volume declines — macroeconomic weakness or recession could further compress used vehicle transactions and slow ACV's share gain trajectory [1]
  2. Manheim (Cox Automotive) and ADESA (Carvana) accelerating digital and AI capabilities to defend their combined ~60% wholesale market share, potentially neutralizing ACV's technology differentiation advantage [3]
  3. Continued GAAP unprofitability (ROE -14.3%, TTM net loss -$62M) and customer bankruptcy risk ($18.7M charge in Q4 2025) may test investor patience if the path to sustainable profitability extends beyond FY2027 [4]
