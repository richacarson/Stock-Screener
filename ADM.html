Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Farm Products ·NYSE ·United States
Archer-Daniels-Midland is one of the world's largest agricultural supply chain managers and processors. It originates, transports and processes oilseeds, corn, wheat and other crops into food and feed ingredients, vegetable oils, biofuels and industrial products, reporting through Ag Services & Oilseeds, Carbohydrate Solutions and Nutrition. Led by Chair and CEO Juan Luciano, ADM has paid dividends for 94 consecutive years and raised them for more than 50, but its reputation was damaged by the 2024 Nutrition-segment accounting investigation and the resulting SEC settlement.

Investment Thesis
ADM's earnings are recovering. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.84 nearly doubled year over year on revenue of about $22.7B, and management raised full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $5.15-$5.60 from $4.15-$4.70 on strong crush and ethanol margins [1]. Biofuel policy (45Z credits, higher RVOs) supports soybean crush demand, and the $500-750M cost-savings program is on track (about $200M delivered in 2025) [2]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E of 39%), the forward P/E is 13.4, and the dividend record is one of the longest in the market: 94 years paid, 53 years of increases, a 2.7% yield and 7% 5-year dividend growth [7].
Profitability is still thin and cyclical. TTM profit margin is 2.2% and ROE is 7.6%, and earnings swing with crush spreads, ethanol margins and trade policy, including U.S.-China soybean flows [6]. Governance risk has not been resolved: the 2018-2023 Nutrition accounting misstatements, the SEC settlement, Hunterbrook's allegations about ADM's Xinjiang disclosures, and a Sustainalytics controversy level of 3 ('Significant') all weigh on trust [5][8]. Q3 2026 results on November 2 (consensus about $21.6-21.8B revenue, $1.52 EPS) will test whether the margin recovery lasts [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Execution on raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.15-$5.60, with Q3 results on November 2, 2026 [1]
  2. Final U.S. biofuel policy (45Z, RVOs) supporting soybean crush margins and renewable diesel feedstock demand
  3. Delivery of the remaining $300-550M of the $500-750M cost-savings program [2]
Key Risks
  1. Governance and reputation: the 2018-2023 Nutrition accounting misstatements, the SEC settlement and Xinjiang disclosure allegations could bring further regulatory, legal or ESG-fund exclusion consequences [5][8]
  2. Commodity margin cyclicality: crush and ethanol spreads could normalize, given 2.2% TTM profit margin and 7.6% ROE [6]
  3. Trade-policy shocks (U.S.-China tariffs on soybeans and grains) disrupting origination and export volumes
