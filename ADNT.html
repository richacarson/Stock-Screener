Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto Parts ·NYSE ·Ireland
Adient is the world's largest automotive seating supplier, designing, developing, manufacturing, and marketing complete seating systems, seat mechanisms, frames, foams, head restraints, armrests, and trim covers for passenger cars, commercial vehicles, and light trucks. Spun off from Johnson Controls in 2016, the Ireland-domiciled, Michigan-operated company runs approximately 200 manufacturing facilities across roughly 30 countries, serving nearly every major global OEM under CEO Jerome Dorlack.

Investment Thesis
Adient is a deep-value, self-help turnaround story: Q3 2026 revenue grew 5% YoY to $3.9B with China sales up ~33%, and management raised full-year revenue guidance to ~$14.8B while maintaining ~$130M FCF guidance [1]. At a 7.1x forward P/E and 0.29 Forward PEG, the stock prices in minimal recovery, and the company's roughly one-in-three global vehicle seating content underpins durable, if thin-margin, cash generation once cost actions land. Management's explicit cost-cutting focus under CEO Jerome Dorlack has begun stabilizing adjusted EBITDA margin (up 80bps excluding one-time items) despite flat headline EBITDA [1][6].
The bear case is substantial and shows up across every non-financial dimension of this score. Adient's mission language is generic and undifferentiated from peers, Glassdoor reviews describe a toxic, poorly-led culture, and the company faces a WARN Act investigation for allegedly shortchanging 320 laid-off employees on notice — the kind of evidence that drove Inspiration and Infinite Game scores to the bottom of the range [4][5]. D/E of 114% and profit margin near zero (0.32%) leave little cushion for a cyclical auto-production downturn, and the company's history includes a $3.5B debt-funded dividend that left it over-levered relative to earnings — a red flag for financial discipline that still colors its balance sheet today [6]. With a Just Cause score of zero and a Trusting Teams score of zero, Adient reads as a company optimizing for near-term survival rather than one built to endure.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued adjusted EBITDA margin recovery toward the ~$885M FY2026 guidance as temporary cost headwinds roll off and the underlying 80bps margin improvement compounds
  2. China sales momentum (+33% YoY in Q3 2026) diversifying revenue away from mature North American and European OEM programs
  3. Resolution of the WARN Act investigation and demonstrated improvement in Glassdoor sentiment as a leading indicator of reduced operational/legal risk
Key Risks
  1. Structural low-margin tier-1 supplier model (0.32% profit margin, 5.7% adjusted EBITDA margin) leaves almost no cushion against an auto-production downturn or OEM cost-down pressure
  2. Documented toxic-culture signals (Glassdoor, WARN Act investigation) suggest execution risk in a workforce-intensive manufacturing business spanning ~200 plants in ~30 countries
  3. 114% debt-to-equity, inherited in part from a historical debt-funded dividend payout, limits financial flexibility if the current cost-cutting turnaround stalls
