Company Profile
Industrials ·Farm & Heavy Construction Machinery ·NASDAQ ·Switzerland
Aebi Schmidt Holding AG manufactures specialty vehicles and equipment for municipal infrastructure maintenance, winter road services, airport operations, and agricultural applications. The company offers snow plows, spreaders, sweepers, implement carriers, truck bodies, and emergency lighting under brands including Aebi, Schmidt, Monroe, Towmaster, Utilimaster, Swenson, Meyer, and Spartan RV Chassis. Founded in 1883 in Switzerland, the company transformed its scale through the 2025 acquisition of The Shyft Group, doubling revenue to $1.53B in FY2025 and positioning for $1.95-2.15B in FY2026 guidance. H1 2026 sales reached $952M with Shyft contributing $397M. Record order backlog of $1.2B+ and 2030 revenue target of CHF 3B.

Investment Thesis
Aebi Schmidt at 9.67x forward P/E ($930M market cap) is a transformed industrial compounder with a clear path to margin expansion and revenue growth. The 2025 Shyft acquisition doubled revenue to $1.53B, H1 2026 sales reached $952M, and record backlog of $1.2B+ provides strong visibility for FY2026 guidance of $1.95-2.15B in net sales with $175-195M in adjusted EBITDA [1]. The company's 143-year operating history, niche leadership in winter maintenance and specialty vehicles, and strong brand portfolio (Monroe, Swenson, Meyer, Schmidt, Utilimaster) create durable competitive advantages in markets with high switching costs and regulatory barriers. AI resilience is exceptional — physical specialty vehicle manufacturing is structurally immune to AI disruption [2]. Forward P/E of 9.67x versus trailing P/E of 38.7x reflects the dramatic earnings inflection expected from Shyft integration synergies and margin normalization. Insider ownership of 53% provides exceptional management alignment.
The bear case centers on integration execution risk and thin current margins. Profit margin of just 1.1% and ROE of 3.6% are well below industrial peers, reflecting the early stage of Shyft integration where the combined entity is absorbing transaction costs, operational overlap, and cross-continental complexity [1][4]. D/E of 95.4% with $787M in total debt creates balance sheet risk if integration stalls or end markets soften. The company operates in cyclical markets — winter equipment demand varies with weather patterns, municipal budgets are politically driven, and airport equipment purchases follow aviation investment cycles. NASDAQ-listed Swiss holding company trading at ~$12 with 363K average daily volume creates some liquidity constraints for institutional investors. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) with no attributions provides no faith-alignment signal. Five-year revenue CAGR of 22.6% is almost entirely acquisition-driven, and organic growth rates for the legacy Aebi Schmidt business are unclear.
