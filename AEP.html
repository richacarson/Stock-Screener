Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NASDAQ ·United States
American Electric Power is one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, serving approximately 5.6 million customers across 11 states through regulated subsidiaries. AEP owns and operates approximately 40,000 miles of transmission lines — the largest in the US — and generates, transmits, and distributes electricity from a diversified fleet. The company increased its five-year capital plan to $78 billion, driven by 63 GW of incremental load commitments (90% data centers) expected by 2030.

Investment Thesis
AEP is a premier regulated electric utility positioned at the intersection of two powerful secular tailwinds: the clean energy transition and AI-driven data center demand. Q1 2026 operating EPS of $1.64 on $6.02B revenue beat estimates, and the company reaffirmed $6.15-$6.45 FY2026 operating EPS guidance while increasing its five-year capital plan to $78 billion [1]. The incremental load pipeline surged to 63 GW by 2030, with 90% tied to data centers — AEP Texas alone has 41 GW of commitments [2]. The 9% EPS CAGR guidance through 2030, 2.74% dividend yield with 116 years of uninterrupted payments and 16 consecutive years of increases, and an expanding rate base driven by data center interconnections make AEP a compelling income-plus-growth compounder [4]. The $75B market cap is supported by irreplaceable physical infrastructure — 40,000 miles of transmission, the largest network in the US.
The bear case centers on execution risk, valuation, and capital market dependency. The $78 billion capital plan requires sustained access to debt and equity markets at reasonable rates — rising interest rates would compress AEP's regulated return spreads and increase financing costs on a balance sheet already carrying 154% D/E [4]. Forward P/E of 21.6x is above the utility sector average and above the 20x IOWN target, pricing in the data center growth narrative with limited margin of safety. Regulatory risk persists across 11 state jurisdictions — any adverse rate case outcome could impair returns on the massive capital deployment program. The data center demand surge creates grid reliability risks, with the Department of Energy warning that extreme power fluctuations from AI model training are a 'high likelihood, high impact' threat to grid stability [2]. Coal stranded asset risk remains — legacy generation capacity faces accelerated retirement timelines that could trigger write-downs. Additionally, AEP's negative Inspire score of -67 reflects social activism positions that may be relevant to faith-aligned investors [3].
