Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NASDAQ ·United States
American Electric Power Company, Inc., headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, is one of the largest US electric utilities, operating regulated generation, the nation's largest 765-kV transmission system, and distribution across 11 states. Data-center demand in Ohio and Texas is driving unprecedented load growth: CEO Bill Fehrman (CEO since August 2025) expects total generation needs to nearly double by 2030, and Jefferies counted 17.9 GW of contracted data center load at the end of Q1 2026, the largest among the utilities it covers. Q3 2026 reportedly brought adjusted EPS of $2.23 versus a $2.04 estimate, 9.8% load growth at the vertically integrated utilities, and a five-year capital plan expanded to $100B through 2031. Market cap approximately $70.9B at $130.21.

Investment Thesis
AEP is the most direct regulated utility play on AI data-center electricity demand. Contracted data center load of 17.9 GW is the largest disclosed among large- and mid-cap utilities, incremental contracted load is expected to reach 84 GW by 2031, and the capital plan has grown to a reported $100B through 2031, which drives rate-base growth [1][4]. Q3 2026 reportedly beat with adjusted EPS of $2.23 versus $2.04 expected and 9.8% load growth, after management had already raised 2026 operating EPS guidance to $6.25-6.55 [1][3]. The 2.98% yield is supported by 16 consecutive years of annual dividend growth (5-year CAGR 5.66%), and Infrastructure scores 9/10 [8].
The bear case is balance-sheet strain and political risk. Debt-to-equity is 162%, total debt is $55.1B against $670M of cash, and free cash flow is about -$7.8B, so a $100B capital plan will need substantial new debt and equity [8]. With a PEG of 2.8 and a payout ratio of 62%, the valuation already reflects much of the growth [8]. Grassroots backlash in Ohio over data centers and bills, combined with criticism of the CEO's $37M pay, raises the risk of less favorable rate cases [4]. About 10,200 MW of coal capacity also carries environmental compliance costs and early-retirement risk [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Conversion of contracted data center load (17.9 GW at Q1 2026, 84 GW incremental expected by 2031) into rate base under the expanded capital plan [1][4]
  2. Favorable rate-case outcomes in Ohio and Texas that support recovery of the transmission and generation build-out [4][5]
  3. Continued guidance raises after the Q3 2026 beat (adjusted EPS of $2.23 versus $2.04 consensus) [1]
Key Risks
  1. Financing a capital plan of up to $100B with negative free cash flow (-$7.8B) and 162% debt-to-equity could require dilutive equity issuance [8]
  2. Political and regulatory backlash over rising residential bills tied to data centers, made worse by criticism of executive pay [4]
  3. Coal fleet of about 10,200 MW exposed to compliance costs and early retirement, despite the EPA's partial repeal of the 2024 GHG standards [5]
