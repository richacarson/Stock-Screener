Company Profile
Utilities ·Regulated Electric Utilities ·NASDAQ ·United States
American Electric Power Company is one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, providing electricity to approximately 5.6 million customers across 11 states through regulated subsidiaries. The company owns approximately 27,000 miles of transmission lines — the largest network in the U.S. — and is experiencing unprecedented load growth driven by AI data centers and hyperscalers, with 69 GW of new load additions identified through 2030. AEP has a $54B five-year capital plan and raised its FY2026 operating earnings guidance to $6.25-$6.55/share, reflecting an expected operating EPS CAGR of greater than 9% through 2030. The company has paid dividends for 116 consecutive years.

Investment Thesis
American Electric Power is a direct and compounding beneficiary of the AI revolution — the company has identified 69 GW of new data center load through 2030, the SoftBank partnership will build facilities requiring power equivalent to one-third of Ohio's generation capacity, and the $54B five-year capital plan supports nearly 11% annual rate-base growth [1][2]. The raised FY2026 operating earnings guidance of $6.25-$6.55/share and >9% EPS CAGR through 2030 represent growth rates historically reserved for technology companies, not utilities. The 116-year unbroken dividend streak with 15 consecutive years of increases provides income reliability, and the 3.11% yield with 56.5% payout ratio offers both current income and growth potential. Revenue growth of 10% YoY and a 5-year CAGR of 7.74% demonstrate that AI-driven electricity demand is already translating into financial results [1][4].
The bear case focuses on execution risk, leverage, and valuation. AEP's D/E of 154% is elevated and will increase further as the company deploys $54B in capital over five years — any interest rate increases or regulatory disallowances could compress returns below the cost of capital [4]. The forward P/E of 20.86x is above the utility sector average and prices in significant data center growth that may face headwinds from power availability constraints, permitting delays, or hyperscaler capex pullbacks. ROE of 12.49% is below the 15% target, suggesting regulatory returns have not yet reflected the growth investments. The 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 5.66% is slightly below the 6% target, and the 56.5% payout ratio limits dividend acceleration. Operating across 11 state regulatory jurisdictions creates execution complexity — each jurisdiction can challenge rate increases, delay capital recovery, or impose conditions on data center agreements. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Data center load materialization as 69 GW of identified demand through 2030 converts to executed service agreements and rate-base investment — each GW of new load supports billions in transmission and distribution capital that earns regulated returns [1][2]
  2. SoftBank partnership expansion and additional hyperscaler agreements (Amazon, Google already active) could accelerate the load growth trajectory beyond current 69 GW pipeline [2]
  3. Regulatory approvals for new tariff structures requiring data centers to meet load expectations would de-risk revenue visibility and protect existing customers from subsidy risk [1]
Key Risks
  1. Data center demand deferral or cancellation if hyperscaler capital spending slows, power availability constraints emerge, or alternative energy arrangements (on-site generation, nuclear SMRs) reduce reliance on utility-provided power [1]
  2. Multi-state regulatory risk across 11 jurisdictions where rate case outcomes, capital recovery mechanisms, and data center interconnection policies could diverge unfavorably [1]
  3. Balance sheet stress from $54B capital deployment at D/E of 154% — rising interest rates, regulatory lag, or construction cost overruns could compress earned ROE below authorized levels [4]
