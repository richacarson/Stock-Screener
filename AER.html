Company Profile
Industrials ·Rental & Leasing Services ·NYSE ·Ireland
AerCap Holdings N.V. is the world's largest aircraft leasing company, managing a portfolio of approximately 3,500 owned, managed, or on-order aircraft, engines, and helicopters. The company leases, finances, sells, and manages commercial flight equipment to airlines and operators worldwide. Founded in 1995 and headquartered in Dublin, Ireland. Q2 2026 revenue of $2.17B (+15% YoY), raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to ~$16.80. Book value per share reached $119.21 as of June 30, 2026 (+16% YoY). Market cap ~$22.5B.

Investment Thesis
AerCap is the world's largest aircraft lessor trading at 7.85x forward earnings and 0.8x PEG despite raising FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to ~$16.80 — dramatically above the initial $12-13 range and well above the $15.37 achieved in record 2025 [1]. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $2.17B (+15% YoY) with continued strong demand across passenger, cargo, and engine segments. Book value per share grew 16% YoY to $119.21, compounding at 68% since 2022. ROE of 18.7%, profit margin of 37.9%, and $5.6B in operating cash flow demonstrate best-in-class capital efficiency for the aviation leasing industry. The new $1B buyback program and dividend increase (from $0.25 to $0.40/quarter) signal management confidence in sustained cash generation. Inspire Impact Score of 36 (ALIGNED) with 8 positive ESG attributions and named 2026 Sustainalytics Industry ESG Leader [3].
The bear case centers on leverage and cyclicality. D/E of 232.7% and $42.8B total debt are structural for aircraft leasing but create vulnerability to interest rate increases and credit market disruptions [1]. Q2 2026 EPS of $4.65 (down from $7.24 YoY, though Q1 2026 EPS of $5.06 was up 41% YoY) shows earnings volatility driven by timing of aircraft sale gains, raising questions about whether the $16.80 full-year guidance reflects sustainable run-rate earnings or front-loaded disposal profits. The aviation industry remains cyclical and exposed to geopolitical disruption (conflict, pandemics, sanctions), fuel price spikes, and airline bankruptcies — the Russia-Ukraine conflict already required significant fleet write-downs. The stock's modest 1.12% dividend yield and only 3-year payment history offer minimal income protection. At $143/share vs. book value of $119, the stock trades at 1.2x book — a premium that may compress if the earnings trajectory normalizes. Competition from sovereign wealth-backed lessors (SMBC, BOC Aviation) could pressure lease rates.
Key Catalysts
  1. Raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to ~$16.80 from initial $12-13, with Q1-Q2 combined delivering ~$9.71 adjusted EPS — pacing above the full-year target and suggesting further upside [1]
  2. Book value per share compounding at 16-19% annually ($119.21 at June 30, 2026), with 68% cumulative growth since 2022, suggesting intrinsic value continues to compound well above the market's pricing [1]
  3. Expansion into engines, cargo, and helicopters diversifying the $60B+ asset base beyond passenger aircraft, while the $1B buyback program accelerates shareholder returns at 7.85x forward earnings
Key Risks
  1. 232.7% D/E ratio and $42.8B total debt create significant interest rate sensitivity — structural leverage is asset-backed but credit market disruptions could impact refinancing costs and aircraft asset values
  2. Earnings volatility from aircraft sale gains — Q2 2026 EPS of $4.65 vs. Q1's $5.06 demonstrates timing-driven variability that could disappoint if gains normalize, making the $16.80 guidance partially non-recurring
  3. Aviation industry cyclicality including recession-driven traffic decline, geopolitical disruption (conflict, sanctions, pandemic), or fuel price spikes could trigger lessee defaults and aircraft oversupply

AI Risk Protection (9/9 — IMMUNE): AerCap's business of owning, leasing, and managing physical aircraft is fundamentally immune to AI disruption [1]. Commercial aviation requires physical aircraft that must be manufactured, maintained, and operated according to stringent FAA/EASA regulatory standards — AI cannot substitute for physical planes carrying passengers and cargo. The aviation leasing industry's $60B+ asset base, multi-decade aircraft useful lives, and deep regulatory moats create structural immunity from technological disruption. AI acceleration may actually increase demand for air travel through increased business activity, supply chain complexity, and economic growth, thereby increasing aircraft demand. No analyst has flagged AI as a risk to aircraft leasing.
