Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance - Life ·NYSE ·United States
Aflac Incorporated is the largest provider of supplemental health and life insurance in the United States and Japan, operating through its Aflac Japan and Aflac U.S. segments. Products include cancer, medical, disability, critical illness, hospital indemnity, dental, vision, and life insurance policies sold to individuals and groups through independent agents and brokers. The company returned $1.3B to shareholders in Q1 2026 through buybacks and dividends. Founded in 1955, headquartered in Columbus, Georgia.
12,716 Employees ·www.aflac.com

Investment Thesis
Aflac is a best-in-class supplemental insurance franchise with dominant market positions in both the US and Japan, delivering Q1 2026 total revenue of $4.3B (up 27.9% YoY) and returning $1.3B to shareholders through buybacks and dividends [1]. The dividend profile is exceptional — 43 consecutive years of increases, 2.0% yield, conservative 26.9% payout ratio, and 8% 5yr growth CAGR place Aflac among the most reliable income generators in the S&P 500 [2]. At 16.3x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.18, the stock offers reasonable value for a company generating consistent mid-teens ROE and 25%+ profit margins. The $62.8B market cap is supported by disciplined capital allocation and a fortress balance sheet with D/E of only 48%.
The primary concern is the quality of revenue growth and Japan exposure. Q1 2026's headline 27.9% revenue growth was driven by net investment gains rather than operational expansion — adjusted earnings were flat YoY [1]. The 5yr revenue CAGR of approximately -5% reflects structural headwinds from yen depreciation and Japan's aging demographics, which pressure Aflac Japan's policyholder base. While Aflac is actively deploying AI for claims efficiency and exploring generative AI applications through its Hatch lab, the company's innovation pace is incremental rather than transformative [3][4]. The insurance industry faces longer-term questions about AI-enabled distribution and underwriting that could compress margins for incumbent players. Net insider selling by multiple directors in 2026 suggests management may view the stock as fairly valued near its 52-week high of $124.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued aggressive share repurchase program ($1.0B in Q1 2026 alone) driving EPS growth above underlying earnings growth, potentially accelerating if the stock pulls back from 52-week highs [1]
  2. AI-driven claims automation via Code-Based Processing expanding into new product lines, with potential for quantified cost savings that improve adjusted operating margins [3][4]
  3. Stabilization of Aflac Japan revenue if yen appreciates or operational growth offsets currency headwinds, which would improve the 5yr revenue growth trajectory
Key Risks
  1. Yen depreciation and Japan demographic headwinds could continue to pressure Aflac Japan revenue, which represents roughly 70% of total premium income — the 5yr revenue CAGR of -5% reflects this structural challenge
  2. Q1 2026 headline revenue growth of 27.9% was inflated by net investment gains; underlying adjusted earnings growth was essentially flat, suggesting limited organic momentum [1]
  3. Net insider selling pattern with multiple directors executing 10b5-1 sales near the 52-week high signals that leadership may not see significant upside from current levels [5]
