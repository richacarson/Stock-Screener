Company Profile
Financials ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Affirm Holdings operates a payment network that enables consumers to pay for purchases over time through its buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) platform, offering transparent, no-late-fee installment plans. Founded by PayPal co-founder Max Levchin, the company partners with merchants including Amazon, Shopify, and Walmart across the U.S. and internationally. Q3 FY2026 delivered $1,039M revenue (+33% YoY) and $102.9M net income, with GMV of $11.6B (+35%). FY2026 guided at $4.18-$4.21B revenue with 8.9-9.4% operating margin. Levchin envisions Affirm as the 'modern answer to American Express' — evolving beyond BNPL into a comprehensive financial services platform with AI-powered consumer protection. Market cap approximately $25.8B.
2,206 Employees ·www.affirm.com

Investment Thesis
Affirm has executed a remarkable transition from unprofitable BNPL startup to a rapidly scaling, profitable payment network. Q3 FY2026 delivered $1,039M revenue (+33% YoY) with $102.9M net income and 8.5% operating margin, while FY2026 is guided at $4.18-$4.21B revenue with operating margin approaching 9.4% [1]. TTM revenue of $3.97B with $382M net income and $787M free cash flow demonstrates the profitability inflection is durable, not a one-quarter anomaly. At forward P/E of 21.18x and PEG of 0.83, the valuation reflects strong but not excessive growth expectations for a company delivering 33%+ revenue growth with expanding margins [4]. The merchant network spanning Amazon, Shopify, and Walmart creates a distribution moat, while ~23M active consumers with $0 in late fees ever build brand loyalty. Levchin's vision of AI-powered consumer protection positions Affirm at the intersection of fintech and AI [2].
The primary concern is competitive intensity in consumer lending and credit cycle vulnerability. Apple Pay Later, Klarna, PayPal Pay Later, and traditional bank installment products are all targeting the BNPL market, and AI-driven underwriting — Affirm's core advantage — is increasingly replicable by well-funded competitors [2]. D/E of 240% reflects substantial funding debt inherent to the lending model, creating credit cycle vulnerability if consumer delinquencies spike during an economic downturn — Affirm has never operated through a recession as a public company. Profit margin of 9.63% is below the 20% target and may face pressure from competitive pricing dynamics [4]. The trailing P/E of 72.78x prices in significant earnings growth that must materialize. Insider selling and the absence of insider buying signal that management, while operationally committed, is not aggressively backing the stock with personal capital. The path from BNPL operator to 'modern American Express' is ambitious but requires expansion into adjacent financial services where incumbents have deep advantages.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2026 revenue and operating margin exceeding guidance ($4.18-$4.21B, 8.9-9.4% margin) would demonstrate sustainable profitability scaling, with Q4 guided at $1,080-$1,110M revenue and 9.5-11.5% operating margin [1]
  2. Margin expansion toward 20%+ as operating leverage kicks in — RLTC (revenue less transaction costs) growing faster (41%) than revenue (33%), and adjusted operating margin reaching 27% in Q3 FY2026 [1]
  3. Strategic evolution beyond BNPL into AI-powered financial advisory services — Levchin's 'smarter older sister' vision leveraging AI agents for consumer protection could expand TAM and diversify revenue beyond installment lending [2]
Key Risks
  1. Credit cycle vulnerability — Affirm has never operated through a recession as a public company, and consumer delinquency spikes could rapidly erode profitability given the lending-dependent model and 240% D/E ratio [4]
  2. Intensifying competition from Apple Pay Later, Klarna, PayPal, and traditional bank installment products threatens market share and pricing power in the increasingly commoditized BNPL category [2]
  3. Trailing P/E of 72.78x prices in significant continued earnings growth — any deceleration from the current 33% revenue growth rate or margin expansion stall could trigger meaningful multiple compression [4]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (5/9): Affirm operates in a sector where AI is both a core competence and a competitive threat. The proprietary AI-driven credit underwriting engine is central to its value proposition, enabling real-time approval decisions that traditional FICO-based systems cannot match [2]. However, BNPL and installment lending are increasingly competitive, with Apple Pay Later, Klarna, PayPal Pay Later, and traditional banks deploying sophisticated machine learning for consumer credit. Affirm's merchant network (Amazon, Shopify, Walmart) and ~23M consumer relationships create meaningful but not insurmountable moats [1]. The lending model faces structural AI risk as LLMs and alternative data enable new entrants to build competitive underwriting without Affirm's historical data advantage. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI is foundational to Affirm's business model. Levchin envisions AI agents that proactively protect consumers — finding optimal loans, flagging predatory terms — and states AI is 'unequivocally accretive to the bottom line' [2].
