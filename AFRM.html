Company Profile
Financials ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Affirm Holdings operates a payment network that enables consumers to pay for purchases over time through its buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) platform, offering transparent, no-late-fee installment plans. Founded by PayPal co-founder Max Levchin, the company partners with merchants including Amazon, Shopify, and Walmart across the U.S. and internationally. FY2026 (ended June 2026) delivered $4.26B revenue (+26% YoY), $50.2B GMV, and $1.93B net income (includes $1.45B one-time tax benefit from deferred tax asset release). Q4 FY2026 revenue was $1.17B (+33% YoY), the 10th consecutive quarter of 30%+ GMV growth. Active consumers grew 21% to 27.8M. Levchin envisions Affirm as the 'modern answer to American Express' — evolving beyond BNPL into a comprehensive financial services platform with AI-powered consumer protection. Market cap approximately $23.8B.
2,206 Employees ·www.affirm.com

Investment Thesis
Affirm has completed a remarkable transformation from unprofitable BNPL startup to a profitable payment network at scale. FY2026 delivered $4.26B revenue (+26% YoY) with $50.2B GMV, and Q4 revenue of $1.17B (+33% YoY) beat consensus by ~5%, marking the 10th consecutive quarter of 30%+ GMV growth [1]. Revenue less transaction costs — Affirm's preferred unit economics measure — grew 39% to $589M in Q4, demonstrating improving economics. At forward P/E of 14.06x, the stock is attractively valued for a company delivering 33%+ revenue growth with expanding margins and 27.8M active consumers growing 21% YoY [4]. The merchant network spanning Amazon, Shopify, and Walmart creates a distribution moat, while $0 in late fees across 27.8M consumers builds differentiated brand loyalty. Levchin's vision of AI-powered consumer protection positions Affirm at the intersection of fintech and AI [2].
The primary concern is competitive intensity in consumer lending and credit cycle vulnerability. Klarna, PayPal Pay Later, and traditional bank installment products are all targeting the BNPL market, and AI-driven underwriting — Affirm's core advantage — is increasingly replicable by well-funded competitors [2]. D/E of 182.8% reflects substantial funding debt inherent to the lending model, creating credit cycle vulnerability if consumer delinquencies spike during an economic downturn — Affirm has never operated through a recession as a public company. TTM profit margin of 45.3% is highly misleading, inflated by a one-time $1.45B tax benefit from deferred tax asset release; normalized margin is approximately 11%, well below the 20% target [1][4]. The CFPB opened an inquiry into BNPL practices including Affirm's, and a securities class action (Kusnier v. Affirm) alleges misleading investors about consumer protection risks [3]. The path from BNPL operator to 'modern American Express' is ambitious but requires expansion into adjacent financial services where incumbents have deep advantages.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2027 revenue growth sustaining above 25% would confirm the 10-quarter GMV growth streak is durable, with RLTC (revenue less transaction costs) growing 39% to $589M in Q4 FY2026 demonstrating improving unit economics [1]
  2. Margin expansion toward 15%+ normalized operating margin as operating leverage kicks in — RLTC growing faster (39%) than revenue (33%), with the company's most profitable quarter ever (ex-tax benefit) in Q4 FY2026 [1]
  3. Strategic evolution beyond BNPL into AI-powered financial advisory services — Levchin's 'smarter older sister' vision leveraging AI agents for consumer protection could expand TAM and diversify revenue beyond installment lending [2]
Key Risks
  1. Credit cycle vulnerability — Affirm has never operated through a recession as a public company, and consumer delinquency spikes could rapidly erode profitability given the lending-dependent model and 182.8% D/E ratio reflecting substantial loan funding debt [4]
  2. CFPB regulatory risk — the bureau opened an inquiry into BNPL practices in 2021 covering Affirm and peers, and a securities class action (Kusnier v. Affirm) alleges misleading investors about consumer protection adherence, creating ongoing regulatory and litigation overhang [3]
  3. Normalized profit margin of approximately 11% (ex-$1.45B one-time tax benefit) remains well below the 20% target, and competitive intensity from Klarna, PayPal, and traditional bank BNPL products threatens margin expansion trajectory [1][4]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Affirm operates in a sector where AI is both a core competence and a competitive threat. The proprietary AI-driven credit underwriting engine is central to its value proposition, enabling real-time approval decisions that traditional FICO-based systems cannot match [2]. However, BNPL and installment lending are increasingly competitive, with Apple Pay Later, Klarna, PayPal Pay Later, and traditional banks deploying sophisticated machine learning for consumer credit. The core technology — AI underwriting — is replicable by well-funded competitors. Affirm's merchant network (Amazon, Shopify, Walmart) and ~23M consumer relationships create meaningful but not insurmountable moats [1]. The lending model faces structural AI risk as LLMs and alternative data enable new entrants to build competitive underwriting without Affirm's historical data advantage. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI is foundational to Affirm's business model. Levchin envisions AI agents that proactively protect consumers — finding optimal loans, flagging predatory terms — and states AI is 'unequivocally accretive to the bottom line' [2]. The company chose to use AI to enhance employee productivity rather than reduce headcount, generating $786.8M in free cash flow through AI-enabled operational leverage [4].
