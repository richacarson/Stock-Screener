Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Canada
Alamos Gold is a Canadian intermediate gold producer operating the Island Gold District (Island Gold and Magino, combined through the 2024 Argonaut Gold acquisition) and the Young-Davidson underground mine in Ontario, plus the Mulatos District in Sonora, Mexico. Its development pipeline includes the Island Gold Phase 3+ expansion and the Lynn Lake project in Manitoba. Co-founder John McCluskey has led the company as CEO since 2003. 2026 production guidance is 510,000-560,000 ounces, lowered after a seismic event at Young-Davidson.

Investment Thesis
Alamos combines a fortress balance sheet (D/E of 4.6%) with strong profitability (46.7% net margin, 23.6% ROE) and one of the clearest organic growth profiles among mid-tier producers [3]. Q3 2026 revenue rose 10% to $510.4M at a realized gold price of $4,258/oz (+27%), and earnings from operations were $268.7M [1]. The Island Gold Phase 3+ expansion and Lynn Lake are targeted to lift output toward about 900K-1M oz by 2030 from a Canadian, low-jurisdiction-risk base, and the stock trades at a forward P/E of about 10 after falling from a 50-day average of $34 [1][3].
Operations have weakened. Q3 2026 ounces sold fell to 118,361, AISC rose to $1,956/oz on inflation and lower Island Gold grades, and full-year guidance was cut to 510-560K oz after a seismic event at Young-Davidson [1]. Earnings are highly leveraged to the gold price, so a retreat from record levels near $4,000+/oz would compress margins quickly, and large expansion projects carry cost-overrun and schedule risk. The dividend yields only about 0.55%, and the Inspire Impact score is a neutral 0.
Key Catalysts
  1. Island Gold Phase 3+ shaft commissioning and ramp-up of the combined Island Gold-Magino operation, lifting production and lowering AISC [2]
  2. Lynn Lake construction progress toward first gold, adding a third Canadian growth asset [2]
  3. Recovery of Young-Davidson production after the seismic event, restoring output toward pre-event guidance [1]
Key Risks
  1. Operational: AISC rising to $1,956/oz in Q3 2026 and lower Island Gold grades, plus seismic risk at deep underground mines like Young-Davidson [1]
  2. Gold price exposure: earnings and the 46.7% margin depend on gold above $4,000/oz [3]
  3. Execution risk on simultaneous large capital projects (Phase 3+, Lynn Lake) during cost inflation
