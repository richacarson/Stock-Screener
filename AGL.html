Company Profile
Healthcare ·Healthcare Plans ·NYSE ·United States
agilon health operates a technology-enabled value-based care platform that empowers community-based physicians to transition to a total-care delivery model for senior patients. Through long-term partnerships with primary care physician groups, agilon assumes global risk capitation contracts with Medicare Advantage payers and ACO models, providing technology, analytics, capital investment, and clinical program support. As of Q2 2026, agilon serves approximately 437,500 Medicare Advantage members and 110,000 ACO REACH members. The company appointed Tim O'Rourke as CEO in May 2026, raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $5.78-5.86B and adjusted EBITDA guidance to $85M at the midpoint following a return to profitability in H1 2026.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
agilon health operates with a genuine healthcare mission but lacks the organizational maturity and leadership continuity to score as an infinite game player. Founded in 2016 and public since 2021, the company has not yet proven its purpose through adversity or multi-decade consistency. The CEO transition — from founder Steven Sell to interim Executive Chairman Ronald Williams to new CEO Tim O'Rourke in just 10 months — reveals a company in survival mode, not one playing an infinite game. The decision to exit unprofitable contracts shows pragmatic discipline, but the underlying failures (medical cost overruns, unprofitable growth) suggest the company previously prioritized scale over sustainability.

Investment Thesis
agilon health's turnaround is accelerating with H1 2026 demonstrating sustained profitability — Q1 net income of $49M (EPS $2.94) and Q2 net income of $18M (EPS $1.04) mark the company's first consecutive profitable quarters since IPO [2][3]. Q2 2026 medical margin of $197M (+16% YoY) and raised FY2026 guidance to $5.78-5.86B revenue and $85M adjusted EBITDA midpoint validate the strategic shift from growth-at-all-costs to sustainable unit economics [3]. The pristine balance sheet ($303M cash vs $32M debt, D/E 14.7%) provides operational runway, and Wells Fargo's decision to double its price target to $141 from $72 signals institutional recognition of the improving trajectory [5]. The value-based care model benefits from structural tailwinds as CMS continues expanding Medicare Advantage and ACO programs, and agilon's physician partnership platform creates meaningful switching costs once integrated into practice workflows.
The bear case centers on revenue decline, leadership uncertainty, and execution risk. Revenue is declining approximately 2% YoY (FY2026 guidance of ~$5.82B vs FY2025 ~$5.93B) as membership drops from 605K to ~537K, and the company must prove it can reignite profitable growth rather than just cut its way to positive EBITDA [2][3]. The CEO transition to Tim O'Rourke — the third leader in 10 months after founder Sell and interim chairman Williams — introduces strategic uncertainty at a critical inflection point [5]. TTM ROE of -78.9% and profit margin of -3.9% reflect that profitability is nascent and unproven across a full annual cycle [4]. The competitive landscape intensifies as Optum Health, CVS/Oak Street, and Walgreens/VillageMD deploy far greater capital into value-based primary care. The stock required a 1-for-25 reverse split to maintain NYSE listing compliance, and while shares have surged 223% in one year on turnaround momentum, a reversal in medical margins from flu severity, utilization spikes, or V28 risk adjustment changes could erase fragile gains [11].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained H2 2026 profitability demonstrating medical margin above $350M full-year guidance, validating that contract exits and clinical programs produce durable unit economics across seasonal variation [3]
  2. New CEO Tim O'Rourke establishing strategic credibility with a clear multi-year plan for returning to profitable membership growth, potentially unlocking re-rating from turnaround to growth story [5]
  3. Expansion of ACO REACH model (currently 110,000 beneficiaries) as a diversification lever beyond Medicare Advantage dependence, with lower risk profile and growing CMS support [2]
Key Risks
  1. FY2025's $296M EBITDA loss demonstrates the fragility of the global risk capitation model — a single year of elevated medical costs or adverse coding adjustments can produce catastrophic losses on thin margins [1]
  2. Leadership instability with three CEOs in 10 months (Sell, Williams interim, O'Rourke) during a critical turnaround creates execution risk and potential strategic inconsistency [5]
  3. Medicare Advantage membership continues declining (-12% YoY to ~437K) with no clear floor, and physician partner defections to competing platforms could accelerate the decline beyond management's control [3]
