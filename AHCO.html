Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
AdaptHealth distributes home medical equipment (HME), medical supplies, and home healthcare services across the United States through four segments: Sleep Health, Respiratory Health, Diabetes Health, and Wellness at Home. The company services Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial insurance beneficiaries with CPAP machines, oxygen concentrators, insulin pumps, CGMs, wheelchairs, and other HME devices. Q2 2026 revenue of $740.3M (+12.7% YoY) with organic growth accelerating to 15.9%, but profitability has deteriorated significantly with a $144.2M goodwill impairment and adjusted loss of $0.99 per share. FY2026 guidance reduced to $2.85-2.89B revenue and $490-520M EBITDA. Market cap approximately $760M.

Investment Thesis
AdaptHealth's Q2 2026 organic growth of 15.9% — the highest quarterly rate in over a year — validates CEO Foster's pivot from acquisition-driven expansion to operational efficiency and capitated care [1]. At $5.70 per share with ~$2.9B guided revenue, the stock trades at a fraction of healthcare peer multiples, offering deep-value turnaround optionality if Foster can execute the margin recovery. The physical HME distribution model is structurally immune to AI disruption, and AdaptHealth's AI-powered operational tools (myAPP, AI chatbots) are generating measurable efficiency gains [2]. The $19.9M insider purchase by OEP VII at $9.73 signals institutional conviction in the turnaround thesis [5].
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. Q2 2026 operating loss of $137.8M including a $144.2M goodwill impairment — and adjusted loss of -$0.99 per share against analyst expectations of $0.15 profit — represents a dramatic miss that has cratered the stock from $9.74 to $5.70 [1]. FY2026 guidance was slashed from $3.45-3.52B to $2.85-2.89B revenue (18% reduction) and from $680-730M to $490-520M EBITDA (29% cut) following strategic portfolio changes [1]. D/E of 149% with $2.0B+ debt constrains flexibility during the transformation. A July 2026 data breach put patient information at risk, creating litigation exposure and reputational damage [6]. Glassdoor has deteriorated to 2.9/5 (declining 12% year-over-year) with complaints about constant leadership turnover, overwork, and management politics [4]. Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement rate pressure from federal budget constraints could further compress margins on the majority of revenue.
