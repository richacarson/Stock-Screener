Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
American Healthcare REIT, Inc. is a self-managed REIT that acquires, owns, and operates a diversified portfolio of clinical healthcare real estate properties, focusing primarily on senior housing communities, skilled nursing facilities, and outpatient medical buildings across the United States, United Kingdom, and the Isle of Man. Operates through four segments: integrated senior health campuses, outpatient medical, triple-net leased properties, and SHOP (Senior Housing Operating Portfolio). Market cap approximately $10.4B.

Investment Thesis
AHR delivered a strong Q1 2026 with revenue of $650.8M and its ninth consecutive quarter of double-digit same-store NOI growth (12.1% YoY), driven by 19.7% growth in senior housing operating properties and 14.5% growth in integrated senior health campuses [1]. NFFO of $0.50/share grew over 30% YoY, and the company raised full-year 2026 guidance to $2.03-$2.09 NFFO/share and 9-12% same-store NOI growth [1]. The secular aging demographic trend (baby boomers entering peak senior housing age) provides a multi-decade demand tailwind, and AHR's inclusion in major FTSE Russell Large Cap indices in 2026 broadens institutional investor access.
The primary concern is valuation relative to GAAP earnings. At 83.80x forward P/E and 5.95 PEG ratio, AHR appears extremely expensive on traditional metrics, though these are distorted by REIT accounting where depreciation depresses GAAP earnings far below cash generation [5]. The $1.00 annual dividend (1.86% yield) represents a significant cut from the pre-IPO $1.60 level, and the 173.72% GAAP payout ratio signals that GAAP earnings do not cover the dividend. Customer concentration risk exists with reliance on third-party operators, and rising interest rates could pressure cap rates and acquisition economics. The company's relatively short public track record (IPO 2024) limits visibility into management's ability to navigate downturns.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued double-digit same-store NOI growth driven by senior housing occupancy recovery and rate increases as the aging baby boomer demographic enters peak demand years
  2. FTSE Russell Large Cap index inclusion broadening institutional investor access and potentially driving passive fund inflows into a $10.4B market cap stock
  3. International portfolio expansion in the UK and Isle of Man providing geographic diversification and access to aging demographics in developed markets
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 83.80 and PEG of 5.95 leave minimal margin of safety on GAAP metrics, creating vulnerability to any deceleration in NOI growth or NFFO trajectory
  2. Dividend was cut from $1.60 to $1.00 pre-IPO, and the 173.72% GAAP payout ratio signals ongoing pressure on income sustainability even as NFFO covers the distribution
  3. Reliance on third-party property operators creates execution risk outside management's direct control, particularly in skilled nursing facilities facing persistent staffing challenges and regulatory scrutiny
