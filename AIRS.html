Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NasdaqGM ·United States
AirSculpt Technologies, Inc. is the parent company of Elite Body Sculpture, providing proprietary minimally invasive body contouring procedures across the United States. The company's patented AirSculpt technology removes fat through a small entry point using a proprietary device, without the use of needles, scalpels, or stitches. Services include AirSculpt (fat removal and transfer), AirSculpt+ (enhanced skin tightening), and AirSculpt Smooth (cellulite removal). Founded in 2012 by Dr. Aaron Rollins, headquartered in Miami Beach, Florida.
330 Employees ·www.airsculpt.com

Investment Thesis
AirSculpt Technologies stabilized its business in Q1 2026 with $39.4M revenue matching the prior year and same-center sales turning positive for the first time in over two years [1]. Under new CEO Yogi Jashnani (appointed January 2025), the company is focused on operational improvement — voluntarily prepaying $10M on its term loan to reduce total debt to $44.8M and guiding for $151-157M in 2026 revenue [2]. The company operates in the structurally attractive cosmetic surgery market ($83B+ and growing), owns a patented minimally invasive technology that differentiates from traditional liposuction, and is immune to AI disruption given the inherently physical nature of surgical procedures [4]. Revenue per case stability at approximately $12,780 and positive same-center sales growth suggest the business may be bottoming after two years of decline.
The bear case is severe: AirSculpt has been unprofitable since its 2021 IPO with ROE of -12.63%, profit margin of -7.39%, and Forward P/E of -216.5, reflecting persistent operating losses with no clear timeline to profitability [1]. Revenue growth is 0.0% YoY against a $4.33 stock price that has collapsed from post-IPO highs, and the 2026 revenue guidance of $151-157M represents essentially flat performance for the second consecutive year [2]. The company diluted shareholders with a $14.6M ATM equity raise while simultaneously burning cash. GLP-1 weight loss drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro) represent a secular threat to elective body contouring demand as patients choose pharmaceutical weight loss over surgical fat removal. With only 330 employees across approximately 19 centers and a new CEO with retail and auto parts experience rather than healthcare leadership credentials, execution risk on the turnaround strategy is meaningful.
Key Catalysts
  1. Same-center sales growth acceleration in Q2-Q4 2026 confirming the Q1 inflection was the start of a sustained recovery rather than a one-quarter anomaly
  2. New CEO Jashnani successfully implementing revenue optimization strategies from his aesthetics industry experience at Ideal Image, driving margin expansion toward positive net income
  3. Potential partnership or expansion into complementary aesthetic procedures beyond body contouring that broadens the revenue base and reduces single-procedure concentration risk
Key Risks
  1. GLP-1 weight loss drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro) gaining mainstream adoption as a pharmaceutical alternative to surgical body contouring, structurally reducing demand for AirSculpt's core procedures
  2. Persistent unprofitability with -7.39% profit margins and no clear path to breakeven — continued cash burn may require additional dilutive equity raises beyond the $14.6M ATM program already executed
  3. Single-procedure business model concentration means any disruption to body contouring demand — regulatory, competitive, or pharmaceutical — threatens the entire revenue base with limited diversification cushion

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): AirSculpt's core business — performing minimally invasive body contouring surgery — is inherently physical and requires skilled surgeons operating proprietary devices on patients in clinical settings. AI cannot substitute for the physical act of fat removal, skin tightening, or body contouring [4]. The service is regulated by state medical boards, requires in-person consultation and procedure, and depends on surgeon expertise that is structurally beyond AI's capability to replace. AI is being adopted in cosmetic surgery primarily for consultation tools (3D imaging, predictive outcome visualization) and practice management — these are enhancements that could benefit AirSculpt, not threats [4]. The only meaningful AI-adjacent risk is that AI-driven marketing and patient acquisition tools could lower barriers for competitors. AI Opportunity (0/1): AirSculpt does not generate any revenue from AI products or services. The company has not disclosed any AI-related operational initiatives or cost savings.
