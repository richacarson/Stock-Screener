Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT — Residential Development ·NYSE ·United States
Apartment Investment and Management Company (Aimco) is a Denver-based multifamily real estate company that is in liquidation. Stockholders approved a Plan of Sale and Liquidation on February 6, 2026, under which Aimco is selling all remaining apartment, development and land assets, winding down operations and dissolving, with completion targeted within 24 months. It has paid liquidating distributions of $1.45 (March 2026) and $1.30 (June 2026) per share after $2.83 of special dividends in 2025; net assets in liquidation were $514.6M at June 30, 2026 with $430.1M of property, construction and bridge debt still outstanding. Management's March 2026 estimate of total liquidating distributions was $5.75-$7.10 per share, versus a share price of $1.57.

Investment Thesis
AIV is now a liquidation arbitrage, not an operating investment. Management's March 2026 estimate put total liquidating distributions at $5.75-$7.10 per share, of which $2.75 has been paid ($1.45 in March and $1.30 in June), implying roughly $3.00-$4.35 of remaining value against a $1.57 share price [2][3]. Net assets in liquidation attributable to Aimco were $493.0M at June 30, 2026, or about $3.39 per share on 145.2M shares [1]. If Aimco closes its remaining development, lease-up and land sales near its estimates, holders could receive materially more than the current price within the plan's roughly 24-month window [2][5].
The bear case is that the market is pricing large shortfalls versus management's estimates: the shares have fallen from a 52-week high of $6.04 to $1.57 (only partly explained by distributions), the 50-day average is $2.08, and no further distribution has been declared since April 2026 [3][4]. The largest bucket of estimated value — $2.30-$3.30 per share from land holdings, development and lease-up properties — is the hardest to sell at full price, while $430.1M of property, construction and bridge debt and $145.2M of estimated wind-down costs rank ahead of equity [1][2]. Execution, timing and buyer risk are high, there is no operating business to fall back on, and the company's dissolution makes long-term ownership impossible.
Key Catalysts
  1. Aimco's Q3 2026 report and any updated estimate of remaining liquidating distributions, which would test the gap between the $1.57 share price and the ~$3.39 per share of net assets in liquidation at June 30, 2026 [1]
  2. Declaration of a third liquidating distribution funded by closings from the ~$680M, 12-property sale program and other asset sales [2][3]
  3. Sales of the larger land, development and lease-up assets (estimated $2.30-$3.30 per share) within the plan's ~24-month timeline [2][5]
Key Risks
  1. Valuation shortfall: buyers for development land and lease-up assets may pay well below management's $2.30-$3.30 per share estimate, and the share price decline to $1.57 suggests the market already expects this [2][4]
  2. Liabilities ahead of equity: $430.1M of property, construction and bridge debt, $33.5M of mezzanine participation sold and $145.2M of estimated wind-down costs as of June 30, 2026 [1]
  3. Timing: no distribution has been declared since April 2026; delays stretch the wind-down, add carrying costs and reduce the time value of distributions [3]
