Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Akebia Therapeutics, Inc. is a biopharmaceutical company focused on developing and commercializing therapeutics for patients with kidney diseases. The company markets Vafseo (vadadustat), an oral HIF-PHI therapy for the treatment of anemia due to chronic kidney disease in adult patients on dialysis, and Auryxia (ferric citrate) for iron deficiency anemia and hyperphosphatemia. Akebia aims to become a leading, fully integrated nephrology-focused biopharmaceutical company. Founded in 2007, headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

Investment Thesis
Akebia is at a critical inflection point. FY2025 total revenue of $236.2M (+47% YoY) was driven by the successful Vafseo U.S. launch ($45.8M) and strong Auryxia performance ($181.5M), and net loss narrowed dramatically to $5.3M from $69.4M in 2024 [2]. Vafseo revenue grew 32% YoY in Q1 2026 to $15.8M, demonstrating commercial momentum as the first oral HIF-PHI therapy for dialysis anemia [1]. With $184.8M in cash and operations expected to fund the current plan for at least two years, the company has adequate runway [2]. The $240M market cap trades at roughly 1x trailing revenue, providing limited downside if Vafseo continues to gain share as an oral alternative to burdensome injectable ESAs in the 550K+ U.S. dialysis patient population.
The dominant risk is the Auryxia exclusivity loss. Auryxia generated 77% of FY2025 revenue ($181.5M) but lost market exclusivity in March 2025, opening the door to generic competition that will erode revenue materially over the next 12-18 months [2]. Analysts expect total 2026 revenue to decline 8.5% to $216.2M, and per-share losses to widen to $0.13 [1]. Vafseo must ramp fast enough to offset Auryxia's decline — a demanding commercial execution challenge for a ~300-person company. D/E of 599% ($195.2M debt on $32.6M equity) creates balance sheet vulnerability if the Vafseo launch underperforms expectations. The TDAPA reimbursement window for Vafseo creates a time-limited opportunity that will transition to bundle payment in 2027, potentially pressuring pricing. At $1.11/share, the stock's micro-cap status and negative profitability create liquidity and financing risk — any secondary offering would be significantly dilutive. The pipeline beyond Vafseo and Auryxia is thin, leaving the company as essentially a two-product franchise with one product declining.
Key Catalysts
  1. Vafseo revenue acceleration beyond $15.8M quarterly as oral HIF-PHI therapy gains market share against injectable ESAs in the 550K+ U.S. dialysis patient population
  2. TDAPA reimbursement window for balance of 2026 providing favorable economics for Vafseo adoption at dialysis centers before bundle entry in 2027
  3. Path to sustained profitability — net loss narrowed to $5.3M in FY2025 from $69.4M in FY2024, with Vafseo growth potentially offsetting Auryxia generic erosion
Key Risks
  1. Auryxia exclusivity loss (March 2025) — generic competition will erode the $181.5M/year revenue stream that generated 77% of FY2025 total revenue, creating a revenue cliff Vafseo must offset
  2. D/E of 599% with $195.2M debt on $32.6M equity creates balance sheet vulnerability — any Vafseo commercial shortfall could force dilutive capital raises at the $1.11 stock price level
  3. TDAPA-to-bundle transition in 2027 creates reimbursement uncertainty for Vafseo pricing, potentially compressing margins during the critical early adoption phase
