Company Profile
Industrials ·Rental & Leasing Services ·NYSE ·United States
Air Lease Corporation purchases new commercial jet aircraft and leases them to airlines worldwide. Founded in 2010 by industry pioneer Steven Udvar-Hazy, the company owns ~496 aircraft and manages 45 additional. AL completed a $7.4B merger with Sumitomo Corporation on April 8, 2026, becoming a subsidiary of Sumisho Air Lease Corporation; common stock delisted from NYSE on April 18, 2026. Shareholders received $65.00 per share in cash. SMBC Aviation Capital acquired the outstanding order backlog. The company continues to file with the SEC as Sumisho Air Lease and reported Q2 2026 total revenue of $641.1M.

Investment Thesis
Air Lease's investment thesis concluded with the April 2026 Sumitomo merger at $65.00/share, valuing the company at $7.4B ($28.2B including assumed debt) [1]. The merger validated the underlying asset quality: a 496-aircraft fleet of exclusively modern, fuel-efficient aircraft generating $3.0B in annual revenue with ~34% profit margins [2]. Pre-merger metrics showed compelling value at 12.52x forward P/E with a PEG of 0.69, reflecting a supply-constrained aircraft market where Boeing and Airbus backlogs extend 5+ years [2]. For analytical purposes, the fundamentals remain strong — Q1 2026 rental revenue grew 4% to $674M, and the chronic aircraft supply shortage gives lessors unprecedented pricing power on lease rates and extensions.
The investment in AL is complete — stock delisted April 18, 2026, and shareholders received $65.00/share in cash [1]. For forward-looking analysis: D/E of 253% with $19.9B in total debt exposes the business to rising interest rates under Sumitomo ownership. Q2 2026 total revenue declined 12.4% YoY to $641.1M with net income of just $7.2M, heavily impacted by $65M in merger transaction costs and $26M in accelerated stock-based compensation [5]. The airline industry remains cyclical — a deep recession could trigger lease deferrals or airline bankruptcies. Boeing's ongoing production challenges could constrain fleet growth. The order backlog was transferred to SMBC Aviation Capital, potentially changing the fleet expansion dynamics. As a private subsidiary, Air Lease will no longer provide the public market transparency that supported its premium valuation.
