Company Profile
Industrials ·Rental & Leasing Services ·NYSE ·United States
Air Lease Corporation purchases new commercial jet aircraft and leases them to airlines worldwide. Founded in 2010 by industry pioneer Steven Udvar-Hazy, the company owns 496 aircraft, manages 45, and has 218 on order with manufacturers. AL operates a buy-new, lease, sell strategy focused on modern, fuel-efficient aircraft. Completed merger with Sumitomo Corporation on April 8, 2026, becoming Sumisho Air Lease Corporation and a wholly owned subsidiary; stock delisted from NYSE.

Investment Thesis
Air Lease offers compelling fundamental value at 12.52x forward P/E with a PEG of 0.69, ~34% profit margins, and a 496-aircraft fleet with 100% placement through 2026 and 218 aircraft on order [1][2]. TTM revenue of $3.02B grew 10.3% with Q1 2026 rental revenue up 4% to $674M, demonstrating steady recurring lease income even after stripping out the prior-year $331.9M Russia insurance settlement [1]. The Sumitomo merger (April 2026) provides permanent capital from Japan's largest trading house, potentially lowering funding costs and extending the fleet growth runway indefinitely. The chronic aircraft supply shortage — Boeing and Airbus backlogs extending 5+ years — gives lessors unprecedented pricing power on lease rates and extensions. At 0.86x book value pre-merger, the stock priced in risk that the merger removes.
The bear case centers on the post-merger structure and cyclical exposure. As a wholly owned Sumitomo subsidiary (delisted April 8, 2026), Air Lease is no longer investable as a public equity [1]. For existing shareholders who received the $65.00 merger price, the investment is complete. For analytical purposes: D/E of 253% with $19.9B in total debt exposes the business to rising interest rates, where each 100bp increase in funding costs compresses margins on the existing fleet [1]. The airline industry remains cyclical — a deep recession could trigger lease deferrals or airline bankruptcies. Boeing's ongoing production challenges could delay the 218-unit order backlog. Q1 2026 normalized net income of $114.8M ($458M annualized) represents a meaningful step down from the settlement-inflated 2025 earnings, and forward ROE of 9.22% is below the company's historical levels [2]. The 1.35% dividend yield provided minimal income cushion.
Key Catalysts
  1. Aircraft supply shortage — Boeing and Airbus production backlogs extending 5+ years give lessors unprecedented pricing power on lease extensions and new placements, with rental revenue growing 4% in Q1 2026 [1][2]
  2. Sumitomo permanent capital — the merger provides access to Sumitomo Corporation's $120B+ balance sheet, potentially lowering Air Lease's composite cost of debt (4.29% in Q1) and enabling accelerated fleet growth [1]
  3. Fleet modernization premium — 496-aircraft fleet composed entirely of new-technology, fuel-efficient aircraft commands higher lease rates and attracts the strongest airline credits, generating durable recurring revenue [2]
Key Risks
  1. Interest rate exposure — $19.9B in debt at 253% D/E means rising funding costs directly compress lease rate margins; even with Sumitomo backing, the business remains fundamentally leveraged to the credit environment [1]
  2. Airline credit risk — cyclical airline industry creates counterparty risk; a deep recession could trigger lease payment deferrals, airline bankruptcies, or aircraft repossessions requiring costly remarketing [2]
  3. Earnings normalization — Q1 2026 net income of $114.8M ($458M annualized) is a significant step down from the settlement-inflated $1.09B FY2025, and forward ROE of 9.22% raises questions about returns on Sumitomo's $65/share acquisition price [1][2]

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Air Lease's core business — owning and leasing physical aircraft to airlines — is structurally immune to AI disruption. Aircraft are tangible assets that must be manufactured by Boeing and Airbus, maintained under FAA/EASA regulations, and physically operated by airlines; AI cannot substitute for an A321neo or 787 Dreamliner [2]. The company's competitive moat rests on fleet strategy, airline relationships, manufacturer order slots (218 aircraft on order), and balance sheet capacity — none of which AI can replicate. Aircraft supply remains chronically constrained with manufacturer backlogs extending 5+ years, further protecting lessors' pricing power [2]. If anything, AI-driven airline efficiency gains could stimulate demand for air travel. AI Opportunity (0/1): While industry reports suggest GenAI could enhance residual value forecasting and lease structuring, Air Lease has not disclosed specific AI-generated revenue or quantified AI-driven operational savings [3].
