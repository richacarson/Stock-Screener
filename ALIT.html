Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
Alight, Inc. is a cloud-based human capital technology and services provider that helps employers and employees navigate health, wealth, and wellbeing decisions. The company serves approximately 70% of the Fortune 100, administering benefits, payroll, and HR programs for large enterprises. Originally spun out of Aon in 2017 and taken public via SPAC in 2021, Alight is undergoing a CEO transition with Rohit Verma replacing Dave Guilmette on January 1, 2026. FY2025 revenue declined to $2.3B amid operational challenges and a massive goodwill impairment charge, though Q1 2026 showed early stabilization with revenue of $534M beating estimates by 6.2%.

Investment Thesis
Alight at 12.16x forward earnings offers deep value optionality on a turnaround story under new CEO Rohit Verma, who delivered four consecutive years of record revenue growth in his prior role at Crawford & Company [2]. Q1 2026 revenue of $534M beat consensus by 6.2% and free cash flow grew 20% YoY to $53M, suggesting early operational stabilization [1]. The company serves ~70% of the Fortune 100 with deeply embedded benefits administration platforms that create high switching costs, and new business and renewal activity improved versus prior year. The Alight Assistant AI deployment positions the company at the intersection of HR tech and enterprise AI, earning Fortune Most Innovative Companies recognition in 2026. D/E of 69% is manageable with $500M+ liquidity, and the stock's 48% decline may overstate structural risk relative to the recurring revenue base.
The bear case is substantial and multi-faceted. Revenue has declined approximately 23% from ~$3.0B at IPO to $2.3B in FY2025, with recurring revenue still contracting -4% YoY in Q1 2026 — the turnaround thesis requires reversing a multi-year organic revenue decline that has persisted through two CEOs and multiple strategic pivots [1]. The massive goodwill impairment in FY2025 and simultaneous CFO departure signal that prior management overpaid for acquisitions and lost financial discipline. ROE of -29.09% reflects the impairment-driven destruction of shareholder equity [5]. Glassdoor feedback describes a toxic culture with mass talent exodus, stretched employees breaking down, and leadership lacking courage to advocate for staff — this cultural rot could take years to repair under Verma and will constrain execution in the interim [4]. AI disruption risk is non-trivial: benefits enrollment, claims processing, and HR administration are precisely the knowledge-worker tasks that LLMs are automating, and larger competitors (ADP, Workday) have deeper AI investment budgets. The Q2 guidance of $490-505M revenue and $80-90M adj EBITDA suggests continued margin compression.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO Rohit Verma's $100M innovation and operational excellence investment, combined with his track record of four consecutive years of record revenue growth at Crawford, could accelerate turnaround execution if deployed effectively against the AI-enabled platform strategy [2]
  2. Alight Assistant AI platform scaling beyond pilot to broad enterprise deployment during 2026-2027 enrollment seasons could differentiate versus competitors and drive retention improvements in the 25-30% of the book up for renewal this year [2]
  3. Q1 2026 beat and improved new business activity suggest the commercial execution problems that plagued FY2025 may be stabilizing — if recurring revenue growth turns positive, the 12.16x forward P/E could re-rate significantly toward HR tech peer multiples of 20-25x [1]
Key Risks
  1. Multi-year organic revenue decline from ~$3.0B to $2.3B has persisted through two CEOs and multiple strategic pivots — there is no evidence yet that Verma's leadership can reverse the structural contraction, and recurring revenue remains in decline at -4% YoY [1]
  2. Toxic organizational culture described by Glassdoor reviews — mass talent exodus, employee burnout, culture of fear toward senior leadership — will constrain execution capability and may take years to remediate, creating a vicious cycle of declining service quality and client attrition [4]
  3. AI disruption risk from LLMs automating benefits enrollment, claims processing, and HR administration tasks — larger competitors (ADP, Workday) have deeper AI investment capacity and could commoditize Alight's core service delivery model faster than Alight can adapt [2]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Alight's core business of benefits administration, payroll processing, and HR services faces meaningful AI substitution risk. AI chatbots and LLM-powered assistants are increasingly capable of handling benefits enrollment inquiries, claims processing guidance, and routine HR administration tasks that comprise a significant portion of Alight's service delivery model [2]. Competitors like ADP and Workday are also deploying AI to automate these processes. However, Alight's deep enterprise integrations (70% of Fortune 100), complex regulatory compliance requirements across health/retirement benefits, and the sensitivity of employee data create meaningful switching costs and regulatory moats that protect against rapid displacement. The risk is not elimination but margin compression as AI automates lower-value service layers. AI Opportunity (1/1): Alight generates early AI-embedded revenue through the Alight Assistant, which delivers personalized benefits support during enrollment periods and was recognized by Fortune as one of America's Most Innovative Companies in 2026 [2]. The company is actively monetizing AI capabilities within its existing service model, though specific AI revenue figures are not separately reported. The $100M innovation commitment includes substantial AI investment.
