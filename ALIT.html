Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYQ ·United States
Alight, Inc. is a cloud-based human capital technology and services provider that helps employers and employees navigate health, wealth, and wellbeing decisions through its Alight Worklife platform. The company serves approximately 70% of the Fortune 100, administering benefits, payroll, and HR programs for 30M+ people. Originally spun from Aon in 2017 and taken public via SPAC in 2021. New CEO Rohit Verma took over January 1, 2026. The stock has declined from ~$20 to $8.92 amid a $3.1B goodwill impairment in FY2025 and persistent revenue decline, though Q1 and Q2 2026 both beat estimates.

Investment Thesis
Alight at a Forward P/E of 1.71x on $8.92 offers deep value optionality if new CEO Rohit Verma can stabilize the business. Q1 2026 revenue of $534M and Q2 of $511M both beat estimates, with free cash flow of $53M (+20% YoY) in Q1 demonstrating underlying cash generation despite GAAP distortions from the $3.1B impairment [1]. Full year 2026 guidance of $2.08-2.10B revenue with $400-415M adjusted EBITDA and $200-220M free cash flow shows a business generating substantial operating cash even as GAAP metrics reflect write-down damage. The company serves ~70% of the Fortune 100 with deeply embedded benefits administration platforms creating high switching costs, and the $100M AI investment (Alight Assistant) earned Fortune Most Innovative Companies 2026 recognition [2].
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. Revenue has declined persistently from $2.39B (FY2023) to $2.08-2.10B (FY2026 guidance), with recurring revenue still contracting -4% YoY — the turnaround thesis requires reversing organic attrition that has persisted through two CEOs [1]. The $3.1B goodwill impairment in FY2025 vaporized shareholder equity, producing -97.3% ROE and -90.9% GAAP profit margin. D/E of 205.5% with $2.1B in total debt against only $219M in cash creates financial fragility if operating performance deteriorates further. Glassdoor reviews describe a toxic culture with mass talent exodus, employee burnout, and leadership creating a 'culture of fear' — this organizational rot constrains execution capability and may take years to repair [4]. AI disruption risk is non-trivial: benefits enrollment, claims processing, and HR administration are precisely the knowledge-worker tasks LLMs are automating, and ADP and Workday have deeper AI investment budgets. The stock's 56% decline from $20.25 to $8.92 reflects market skepticism about whether the business can stabilize [1][4].

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (5/9): Alight's core business of benefits administration, payroll processing, and HR services faces meaningful AI substitution risk. AI chatbots and LLM-powered assistants are increasingly capable of handling benefits enrollment inquiries, claims processing, and routine HR tasks that comprise a significant portion of Alight's service delivery [2]. Competitors ADP and Workday are deploying AI to automate these same processes. However, Alight's deep enterprise integrations (70% of Fortune 100), complex regulatory compliance across health/retirement benefits, and sensitivity of employee data create switching costs and regulatory moats that protect against rapid displacement. The risk is margin compression as AI automates lower-value service layers rather than complete elimination. AI Opportunity (1/1): Alight generates early AI-embedded revenue through the Alight Assistant, an AI-powered benefits assistant recognized by Fortune's Most Innovative Companies 2026 [2]. The $100M innovation commitment includes substantial AI investment deployed across personalized enrollment support, context-aware recommendations, and operational efficiency. Specific AI revenue figures are not separately reported but the company is actively monetizing AI within its existing service model.
