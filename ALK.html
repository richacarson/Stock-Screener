Company Profile
Industrials ·Airlines ·NYSE ·United States
Alaska Air Group is a U.S. airline holding company operating Alaska Airlines, Hawaiian Airlines (acquired September 2024) and Horizon Air, serving North America, Hawaii, and transpacific markets from hubs including Seattle, Portland, Anchorage, and Honolulu. FY2025 revenue reached $14.24B (+21% YoY on the first full year of Hawaiian), and Q3 2026 revenue rose 14% to $4.31B, but a 59.5% jump in quarterly fuel expense drove a GAAP net loss of $147M [1][2]. Diversified revenue (premium cabins, loyalty, cargo) now represents 54% of operating revenue [1].

Investment Thesis
Alaska's top line is working: Q3 2026 revenue rose 14% to $4.31B on only 2.4% more capacity, RASM climbed 11.7%, premium revenue grew ~25%, managed corporate revenue +30%, and loyalty cash remuneration rose 21% to $721M, so 54% of revenue now comes from higher-margin diversified streams [1]. At $35.71 the stock trades at 0.26x sales, 1.13x book, and 11.0x forward earnings, so any normalization of fuel costs could produce sharp operating leverage on a $14B+ revenue base that has grown at a 31.9% CAGR since 2020 [2][5][6].
The bear case is the balance sheet and fuel. Q3 fuel expense jumped 59.5% to $1.21B, management cited a $1.8B full-year fuel headwind, the company posted a $147M GAAP loss, and the nine-month operating result swung to a $365M loss from $228M of income [1]. TTM ROE is -10.5%, free cash flow is negative (FCF yield -16%), the current ratio is 0.46, and net debt is roughly 11x TTM EBITDA, leaving little cushion if a demand slowdown arrives before fuel relief [6]. Hawaiian integration of labor groups and systems remains an execution risk, and no dividend has been paid since the COVID suspension.
Key Catalysts
  1. Jet fuel normalization: the $1.8B 2026 fuel headwind is the main gap between today's GAAP losses and the 11x forward P/E; a sustained fuel decline would flow largely to operating income [1]
  2. Continued premium and loyalty mix shift, with premium revenue +25% and loyalty cash remuneration +21% in Q3 2026, raising unit revenue on a slow-growing capacity base [1]
  3. Hawaiian network synergies and corporate travel recovery (managed corporate +30% in Q3 2026) lifting yields on transpacific and West Coast routes [1]
Key Risks
  1. Persistently high fuel prices extending GAAP losses into 2027 against a 0.46 current ratio and roughly 11x net debt/EBITDA, raising refinancing and dilution risk [1][6]
  2. Hawaiian integration missteps, including combining seniority lists, labor contracts, and reservation systems, that could disrupt operations or labor relations [3]
  3. Negative free cash flow (FCF per share -$5.82 TTM) as capex runs at 1.9x depreciation, limiting the ability to deleverage from 213% D/E [5][6]
