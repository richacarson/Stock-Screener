Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application (Digital Banking) ·NASDAQ ·United States
Alkami Technology provides a cloud-based, multi-tenant digital banking platform that enables community banks, credit unions, and regional financial institutions to compete with large national banks on user onboarding, engagement, and revenue generation. Founded in 2009 and headquartered in Plano, Texas, the company expanded its platform in 2024 through the acquisition of MANTL, adding digital account-opening capability. Alkami reported $493.6 million in annual recurring revenue as of Q1 2026 across its financial-institution client base.

Investment Thesis
Alkami delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $126.1 million, up 28.9% YoY, with ARR reaching $493.6 million and adjusted EBITDA nearly doubling to $22.3 million on client expansion and the integration of the MANTL acquisition, which added digital account-opening capability [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance of $527.1-$530.9 million in revenue implies continued mid-20s growth, and Alkami remains one of the few independent, cloud-native digital banking platforms serving community banks and credit unions that need to compete with megabank digital experiences [1]. The core business benefits from high switching costs once integrated into a financial institution's core banking stack, providing durable revenue visibility via multi-year contracts.
The bear case centers on execution and culture risk layered on top of persistent unprofitability: net loss widened to $10.0 million in Q1 2026, ROE and profit margin are both deeply negative, and Glassdoor reviews describe rampant C-suite turnover (CEO, CRO, CHRO, and CMO all under 18 months tenure) alongside declining morale and politically-driven decision-making [1][4]. Forward P/E of 19.9x is reasonable for the growth rate, but the combination of unproven path to sustained profitability and a leadership team still finding its footing keeps this at a WATCH rather than a BUY — investors should look for evidence of margin inflection and leadership stability before increasing conviction.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued MANTL cross-sell into the existing client base of community banks and credit unions, expanding ARR beyond the current $493.6 million run-rate
  2. Path to non-GAAP profitability as adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled YoY in Q1 2026, which could re-rate the stock if margin expansion continues
  3. Consolidation among community banks and credit unions seeking modern digital banking platforms to compete with megabank user experience
Key Risks
  1. Persistent GAAP unprofitability (ROE -14.1%, profit margin -10.6%) with no clear disclosed timeline to sustained net income
  2. Leadership instability — CEO, CRO, CHRO, and CMO have all joined within the last 18 months, raising execution and strategic-continuity risk
  3. Employee morale trending down per Glassdoor reviews, with reports of politically-motivated rather than mission-driven decisions, which could affect client delivery quality and retention
