Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Alumis Inc. is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on the development and commercialization of transformative medicines for autoimmune disorders. The company's lead asset is envudeucitinib, a tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor in Phase 3 for plaque psoriasis and Phase 2b for systemic lupus erythematosus. Pipeline includes A-005 (CNS-penetrant TYK2 inhibitor), lonigutamab (anti-IGF-1R for thyroid eye disease), and an IRF5 program. Founded in 2021, headquartered in South San Francisco, California.

Investment Thesis
Alumis represents a high-conviction clinical-stage biotech play on the next generation of TYK2 inhibitors for autoimmune diseases. Envudeucitinib's Phase 3 psoriasis data — PASI 90 rates of 59.9-68.0% — is potentially best-in-class versus Bristol-Myers' Sotyktu (PASI 90 ~37% at Week 16), positioning the company for a significant share of the $15B+ psoriasis market upon anticipated NDA filing in Q4 2026 [1][2]. The $569.5M cash position provides approximately 2.5 years of runway, and the TYK2 franchise strategy encompasses multiple large autoimmune indications including SLE (Phase 2b data Q3 2026), neuroinflammatory diseases, and thyroid eye disease. Ten analysts cover the stock with a strong buy consensus and $40.10 median price target, reflecting substantial upside from the current $29.27 [4].
The primary risk is binary clinical and regulatory execution — the company has no commercial revenue and will burn ~$350-400M annually through the NDA process, leaving approximately 1.5-2 years of cash beyond the planned Q4 2026 filing [1]. Revenue of $8.4M TTM is negligible collaboration income, and estimated FY2026 revenue of $7.8M represents a 67.5% decline as collaboration milestones lapse. The competitive landscape is intensifying with multiple TYK2 programs in development from established pharma companies, and the company's $3.7B market cap prices in substantial commercial success. Any clinical setback in the SLE program (Q3 2026 data) or regulatory delay for the psoriasis NDA could severely compress the valuation, as there is no revenue floor to cushion a miss [1][4].

  3. Revenue decline of 67.5% expected for FY2026 as collaboration milestones lapse, and $3.7B market cap requires substantial commercial success upon approval to justify current valuation
