Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Alumis Inc. is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company developing transformative medicines for autoimmune disorders. Lead asset envudeucitinib is an allosteric TYK2 inhibitor with Phase 3 data in plaque psoriasis (PASI 90 rates of 59.9-68.0%, potentially best-in-class vs Sotyktu) and NDA submission planned Q4 2026. The Phase 2b LUMUS trial in systemic lupus erythematosus failed to meet primary endpoints on September 1, 2026, causing a ~58% stock decline. Pipeline includes A-005 (CNS-penetrant TYK2), lonigutamab (anti-IGF-1R for thyroid eye disease), and an IRF5 program. Founded 2021, headquartered in South San Francisco. Market cap approximately $1.47B.
225 Employees ·www.alumis.com

Investment Thesis
Alumis at $11.36 (down ~58% from September 1 SLE trial failure) represents a high-risk revaluation opportunity anchored by potentially best-in-class psoriasis data. Envudeucitinib's Phase 3 PASI 90 rates of 59.9-68.0% significantly exceed Bristol-Myers' Sotyktu (~37%), and the NDA filing remains on track for Q4 2026 targeting a $15B+ psoriasis market [1][2]. The $377.7M cash position provides approximately 1.5-2 years of runway through the NDA process. Analyst consensus remains 'Buy' with a $32.33 median price target implying 185% upside, though this predates the full SLE failure impact [6]. Insider buying of $9M+ by CEO Babler and directors at higher prices demonstrates leadership conviction in the long-term franchise value [3]. The LUMUS SLE trial did show 'robust efficacy' in the IFNGS-high biomarker subgroup, preserving potential for a biomarker-enriched registrational trial in a $5B+ SLE market [5]. Inspire Impact Score of 28 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based portfolio inclusion.
The bear case is severe and explains the 58% stock collapse. The Phase 2b LUMUS SLE trial failed to meet primary and secondary endpoints in the overall population on September 1, 2026, eliminating what was the company's second-largest commercial opportunity and triggering multiple analyst downgrades [5]. With $438.96M in TTM net losses on just $7.4M collaboration revenue (-63.1% YoY), Alumis is burning cash at ~$100M/quarter with only ~1.5-2 years of runway remaining [1][6]. The $1.47B market cap still requires substantial commercial success in psoriasis to justify — even with best-in-class data, ALMS must navigate FDA review, commercial launch against established competitors (Sotyktu, Skyrizi, Tremfya), and payer access negotiations without commercial infrastructure. Any delay in the Q4 2026 NDA filing or unexpected FDA questions could force a dilutive capital raise at depressed prices. The competitive TYK2 landscape is intensifying with multiple programs from established pharma companies possessing superior sales forces and payer relationships. Infrastructure scores at 2/10 reflecting the pre-commercial financial reality.
