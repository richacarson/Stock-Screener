Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Altimmune is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on developing pemvidutide, a novel dual-action GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist, for metabolic and liver diseases. Lead programs include PERFORMA Phase 3 in MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis), RECLAIM Phase 2 in Alcohol Use Disorder (positive topline data July 2026), and ALD (alcohol-associated liver disease) studies. The company pivoted from its original vaccines platform to metabolic disease. With only 57 employees operating through external partners, Altimmune holds $519M in cash (Q2 2026) and is led by CEO Jerry Durso (effective January 2026) following the departure of founder Vipin Garg.

Investment Thesis
Altimmune offers a differentiated entry into the GLP-1/metabolic disease megatrend through pemvidutide's unique dual-action GLP-1/glucagon mechanism that preserves lean mass while reducing liver fat — addressing limitations of pure GLP-1 agonists that cause muscle loss in 30-40% of weight. The RECLAIM Phase 2 positive topline data in Alcohol Use Disorder (July 2026) opens an entirely new indication beyond MASH/obesity, expanding the addressable market [1][2]. PERFORMA Phase 3 in MASH has been initiated with potential accelerated approval by 2029. The $519M cash position provides runway through multiple clinical catalysts. At $3.11 per share ($605M market cap), the stock trades at a deep discount to the multi-billion-dollar peak sales estimates for pemvidutide if approved in MASH alone. Insider buying by the CEO and CFO with zero insider selling signals leadership conviction [4].
The bear case is dominated by competitive and clinical risk. Pemvidutide competes against Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound, $15B+ revenue), Novo Nordisk's semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), and Amgen's MariTide — companies with 100-1000x Altimmune's resources and clinical infrastructure [3]. The PERFORMA Phase 3 MASH trial has not yet reported topline results, and the failure rate for Phase 3 MASH trials is historically high (multiple competitors have failed). With only $31K in TTM revenue and -$91.8M in annual losses, the company is entirely dependent on capital markets for survival — any Phase 3 disappointment could be terminal [5]. The CEO transition from founder Vipin Garg to Jerry Durso in January 2026 introduces leadership risk during the most critical phase of clinical development. The Inspire Impact Score of -62 (MISALIGNED) due to embryonic stem cell involvement may limit allocation for faith-based investors. The 57-person workforce relying on external partners creates operational fragility if key CRO relationships deteriorate.
Key Catalysts
  1. PERFORMA Phase 3 MASH topline results — the pivotal efficacy readout that could validate pemvidutide against established GLP-1 therapies and support accelerated FDA approval by 2029 [1]
  2. RECLAIM Phase 2 AUD positive data (July 2026) opens a novel indication with less competition than MASH/obesity, potentially generating partnership interest from larger pharma [2]
  3. Insider buying pattern (9 purchases, 0 sells, CEO buying at $2.95 in August 2026) combined with $519M cash runway signals leadership confidence at current depressed valuations [4]
Key Risks
  1. Phase 3 MASH failure risk — historically high failure rate in MASH trials, and any negative PERFORMA result would be devastating given the single-asset nature of the company [1]
  2. Overwhelming competition from Eli Lilly ($15B+ tirzepatide revenue), Novo Nordisk (semaglutide franchise), and Amgen (MariTide) with resources to out-execute on clinical development, commercial launch, and physician education [3]
  3. Pre-revenue company burning $91.8M annually with $31K in TTM revenue — entirely dependent on capital markets, and any capital raise at $3.11 would be severely dilutive to existing shareholders [5]
