Company Profile

Alto Ingredients produces, distributes, and markets specialty alcohols, renewable fuel, and essential ingredients. The company operates production facilities in Pekin, Illinois and the Western U.S., serving health and beauty, food and beverage, industrial, agricultural feed, and fuel markets through its Marketing and Distribution, Pekin Campus Production, and Western Production segments.

Investment Thesis
Alto Ingredients is a confirmed turnaround story with Q1 2026 delivering $4.0M net income ($0.05 EPS, beating the $-0.05 consensus by $0.10), a $16.0M improvement from Q1 2025's $12.0M loss [1]. The stock has surged 274% over the past 52 weeks as the market prices in sustained profitability. At 12.4x forward P/E with improving ROE (12.63%, up from 5.67% last screen) and a conservative D/E of 37%, the valuation is attractive if the profitability trajectory holds [5]. The Pekin Campus specialty return percentage improved to 54% (from 48%), demonstrating the mix-shift toward higher-margin specialty alcohols. $11.5M in 45Z transferable tax credits on the balance sheet and ~$15M expected full-year provide non-operational earnings support [1]. Improved crush margins ($0.17/gallon vs $0.02) and corn cost reductions ($4.58/bushel vs $4.81) create a more favorable operating environment. Analysts rate the stock a Strong Buy with a $9.00 target (94% upside).
The bear case remains substantial despite the turnaround. Profit margin of 3.20% is thin for any business and provides virtually no buffer against ethanol/corn crush spread compression — the Q1 2026 improvement was driven by favorable commodity pricing that can reverse quickly [5][1]. Revenue has declined from $1.34B in FY2022 to $916M TTM, a negative 5yr CAGR of -3.5%, reflecting a structurally shrinking business rather than a growth story. Production volume declined to 59.1M gallons from 62.6M in Q1, signaling capacity underutilization [1]. The $45Z tax credit dependency ($11.5M on balance sheet, ~$15M expected full-year) creates political risk — any policy changes could eliminate a significant earnings component. The ethanol industry faces long-term structural headwinds from electric vehicle adoption and declining gasoline demand. At $359M market cap with 390 employees, Alto lacks scale to invest in meaningful differentiation. Despite a 274% stock rally, insiders have not purchased meaningful amounts of stock with personal capital, raising questions about long-term conviction at the leadership level [2].
