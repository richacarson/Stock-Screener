Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment & Materials ·NASDAQ ·United States
Applied Materials, Inc. is the world's largest semiconductor equipment company, providing manufacturing equipment, services, and software to the global semiconductor and display industries. Core products include chemical vapor deposition, physical vapor deposition, etch, chemical mechanical planarization, metrology, and inspection systems. The company also provides advanced packaging solutions for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and AI chip interconnects. Q3 FY2026 delivered record revenue of $9.12B (+25% YoY) driven by AI infrastructure spending. The $5B EPIC R&D center anchors CEO Gary Dickerson's 'Materials to Systems' strategy. Founded in 1967, headquartered in Santa Clara, California.

Investment Thesis
Applied Materials is the world's largest semiconductor equipment company and a direct, structural beneficiary of the AI infrastructure buildout, with its deposition, etch, metrology, and advanced packaging tools required at every major fabrication node [1][2]. Q3 FY2026 delivered record revenue of $9.12B (+25% YoY) with non-GAAP EPS of $3.50 (+41%), representing dramatic acceleration from Q2's 11% growth and validating the 'AI giga-cycle' thesis [1]. Management raised the CY2026 semiconductor systems growth outlook to >30%, and 13 consecutive quarters of YoY gross margin expansion to 50.4% demonstrates pricing power in an oligopolistic market. The balance sheet is pristine: $14.5B in liquid assets against $6.5B total debt, 41% ROE, and 29.3% profit margin [5]. At $486 (down from $584 in June), the stock trades at 28.1x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.6 — dramatically more attractive than the 53.5x forward P/E two months ago, reflecting valuation normalization while fundamentals accelerated.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, geopolitics, and the duration of the AI capex cycle. While the forward P/E has improved to 28.1x from 53.5x, it still embeds sustained earnings growth that could disappoint if hyperscaler capex decelerates or semiconductor inventory normalizes [2]. The $252.5M export control settlement in February 2026 exposed compliance failures, and further US restrictions on semiconductor equipment exports to China (27% of revenue) could materially reduce AMAT's addressable market [4]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only ~8.5% reminds investors that the current AI-driven acceleration follows a period of near-flat growth in FY2023-2024, and semiconductor equipment spending is inherently cyclical. The stock's 52-week range of $154-$740 reflects extreme volatility. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors. While the current growth trajectory is exceptional, the risk of multiple compression on any growth deceleration remains the key concern at this valuation level.
