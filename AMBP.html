Company Profile
Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·Luxembourg
Ardagh Metal Packaging S.A. is a global supplier of infinitely recyclable beverage cans, producing aluminum and steel beverage packaging for major brands including Coca-Cola, AB InBev, and PepsiCo. Operations span North America, Europe, and Brazil. Spun off from Ardagh Group in 2021 as a pure-play metal packaging company with $5.5B in FY2025 revenue.

Investment Thesis
AMP delivered FY2025 revenue of $5.5B (up 12% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $739M, and raised FY2026 EBITDA guidance to $775-790M after strong Q2 2026 results showing 14% EBITDA growth [1][3]. The company benefits from a powerful secular tailwind: the global shift from single-use plastic to infinitely recyclable aluminum beverage cans, supported by regulatory bans on plastics and consumer sustainability preferences. With global beverage can demand growing 3-5% annually and AMP positioned as one of the world's largest producers, the revenue runway is visible and durable. Forward P/E of approximately 15x is undemanding if the earnings recovery thesis materializes.
The balance sheet remains the critical concern: $5.5B in long-term debt against negative equity of -$0.22B produces near-zero net margins of 0.23% as interest expense consumes operating profit [5]. Revenue growth of 12% annually is flattered by aluminum input cost pass-through — underlying volume growth was only approximately 3% in FY2025 [1]. The dividend of $0.40/share appears unsustainable at approximately 500% of trailing earnings. ROE of -103.6% reflects negative equity rather than operating efficiency. Any macro slowdown reducing beverage consumption would pressure both volumes and debt service capacity, creating existential refinancing risk if credit markets tighten simultaneously. At $4.34 per share, AMBP trades on faith in the deleveraging thesis rather than current fundamentals.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2026 EBITDA guidance raised to $775-790M from original $750-775M, reflecting operational momentum with potential for further upgrades if global beverage demand holds through H2 2026 [3]
  2. Secular shift from plastic to aluminum packaging accelerating through regulatory bans and sustainability mandates across Europe and North America, providing structural volume tailwind of 3-5% annual growth
  3. Deleveraging progress reducing interest expense burden, with potential to materially improve net margin from current 0.23% and restore earnings visibility as EBITDA grows toward $800M
Key Risks
  1. $5.5B debt load with negative shareholder equity creates existential refinancing risk if credit markets tighten or operating performance deteriorates below debt covenant thresholds
  2. Input cost pass-through masks modest underlying volume growth of approximately 3% in FY2025; a beverage consumption slowdown would expose the leveraged balance sheet to margin compression and covenant pressure [1]
  3. Commodity aluminum price volatility creates revenue and cost uncertainty, with limited pricing power beyond pass-through mechanisms in a competitive oligopoly market against better-capitalized Ball Corporation and Crown Holdings
