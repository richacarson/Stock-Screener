Company Profile
Technology ·Robotics & Automation ·NASDAQ ·United States
AMC Robotics Corporation (formerly AMCI Acquisition Corp) manufactures and sells autonomous robots and AI-driven security and safety technology solutions. Products include the Kyro quadruped robotic platform for inspection, security, and patrol; NovaArm robotic material handling systems; and logistics automation. The company originated from the YI smart security camera portfolio and went public via SPAC merger with AlphaVest Acquisition Corp. Established manufacturing subsidiary in Vietnam for Kyro production.

Investment Thesis
AMC Robotics is a micro-cap ($110M market cap) autonomous robotics company in early commercial stage, transitioning from declining YI security camera revenue to AI-powered quadruped robots (Kyro) and robotic material handling (NovaArm). FY2025 adjusted net income of $0.7M and adjusted EBITDA of $0.8M demonstrate the core business can generate margin when non-cash warrant charges are excluded [2]. The Kyro platform showcased at CES 2026 and NovaArm targeted for Q2 2026 commercial launch represent the growth runway, while the Vietnam manufacturing subsidiary provides cost-efficient scale production capability. The AI robotics market is projected to grow rapidly, and AMC's $4.87 stock price reflects maximum pessimism.
The bear case is severe: revenue has declined from $17.89M (FY2022) to $5.98M (FY2025), a 67% cumulative decline over three years, as the legacy camera business collapses faster than robotics revenue materializes [2][3]. GAAP net loss of $24.8M in FY2025 (adjusted for non-cash items to +$0.7M) and only $6.6M cash provide minimal runway — the $8M PIPE financing buys months, not years. The stock fails IOWN's liquidity floor ($870K average daily dollar volume vs. $1M minimum), making position sizing impractical for any institutional portfolio. The company competes in autonomous robotics against Boston Dynamics (backed by Hyundai's $90B+ resources), Unitree, Ghost Robotics, and dozens of well-funded startups — with $6M annual revenue against competitors spending hundreds of millions on R&D. The risk-reward is unfavorable given the capital constraints and competitive intensity.
Key Catalysts
  1. NovaArm commercial launch targeted Q2 2026 could provide the first meaningful new product revenue and validate the pivot from cameras to robotics
  2. Vietnam manufacturing subsidiary enables lower-cost Kyro quadruped production at scale, potentially improving gross margins from the 48% level and enabling competitive pricing against larger robotics competitors
  3. AI robotics sector tailwinds as enterprise customers adopt autonomous inspection and security solutions, with Kyro targeting industrial and commercial applications worth billions annually
Key Risks
  1. Revenue has declined 67% from $17.89M to $5.98M over three years as legacy YI camera business erodes — no evidence that robotics products will generate replacement revenue at sufficient scale or pace
  2. Cash position of $6.6M plus $8M PIPE financing provides less than 12 months of runway at current burn rates, creating near-term dilution or insolvency risk if robotics commercialization stalls
  3. Fails IOWN liquidity floor with $870K average daily dollar volume, and competes against Boston Dynamics, Unitree, and dozens of well-funded robotics companies with 10-100x more capital for R&D and go-to-market
