Company Profile
Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·Australia
Amcor plc is the global leader in responsible packaging solutions, operating across flexible packaging, rigid containers, and specialty cartons for food, beverage, healthcare, personal care, and industrial end markets. Following the $8.4B all-stock acquisition of Berry Global completed in April 2025, Amcor operates approximately 400 facilities with 75,000 employees across ~140 countries. FY2026 revenue reached $23.5B (+57% YoY, acquisition-driven) with adjusted EPS of $2.39 (+48.8%). Berry synergies of $285M were 10% ahead of initial targets, with $650M total identified. Incorporated in Australia and dual-listed on NYSE and ASX.

Investment Thesis
Amcor at 11.37x forward earnings with a 5.40% dividend yield offers deep value in the newly consolidated global packaging leader, now with a full fiscal year of Berry integration results to validate execution [2]. FY2026 delivered revenue of $23.5B, adjusted EPS of $2.39 (+48.8%), and Berry synergies of $285M — 10% ahead of initial targets — with $650M total identified [1]. Adjusted EBIT margin expanded 180bps to 12.3% (13.3% excluding noncore businesses), and Q4 volume growth turned modestly positive for the first time, improving 200bps sequentially across food service, pet care, and protein [1]. The PEG ratio of 0.21 signals extreme undervaluation relative to the double-digit EPS growth guided for CY2027. The packaging business is structurally resilient — essential consumer and healthcare packaging demand is non-discretionary and AI-proof — while the AA MSCI ESG rating and Inspire Impact Score of 69 (ALIGNED) provide strong faith-alignment credentials [3].
The bear case centers on balance sheet leverage and organic growth quality. D/E of 128% remains elevated post-Berry, and while improved from 144% two months ago, significant debt limits flexibility if macro conditions deteriorate [5]. The 109% GAAP payout ratio is still above 100% — while double-digit EPS growth in CY2027 should bring it below 100%, any sustained earnings miss could force a dividend cut devastating to the income-oriented shareholder base [1]. The 57% reported revenue growth is entirely acquisition-driven; organic volume growth only just turned positive after quarters of decline. ROE of 9.40% remains well below the 15% target, suppressed by Berry goodwill and integration costs. The packaging industry faces long-term headwinds from sustainability regulations mandating costly material transitions, and concentrated CPG customer bases exert pricing pressure. CEO Konieczny, while executing well on integration, is still in his early tenure as permanent CEO. The 5-year dividend growth CAGR of only 2.04% significantly lags inflation, limiting total return for income investors.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Amcor's core business — manufacturing physical packaging products including flexible pouches, rigid containers, bottles, closures, and specialty cartons — is fundamentally immune to AI substitution [1]. AI cannot replace a pharmaceutical blister pack, a food-grade flexible pouch, or a beverage bottle. The company's advantages are rooted in physical-world assets: 400 manufacturing facilities, deep customer co-development relationships, regulatory-compliant processes (particularly in healthcare packaging), and global supply chain scale [2]. These moats are structural and cannot be replicated by software. The primary AI-adjacent risk is that AI-powered procurement optimization by large CPG customers could increase pricing pressure, but Amcor's scale and co-development model mitigate this.
