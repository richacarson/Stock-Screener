Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
AMETEK, Inc. is a leading global manufacturer of highly differentiated electronic instruments and electromechanical devices operating through two segments: Electronic Instruments Group (EIG) and Electromechanical Group (EMG). The company serves process, aerospace, defense, power, medical, and semiconductor markets with mission-critical precision instruments, sensors, embedded computing, thermal management, and specialty metals. Founded in 1930 and headquartered in Berwyn, Pennsylvania, AMETEK employs a serial acquisition strategy ('four growth strategies') that has built a portfolio of 40+ niche technology businesses. Q2 2026 revenue grew 15% YoY to $2.04B with adjusted diluted EPS up 17% to $2.09, and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $8.20-$8.30.

Investment Thesis
AMETEK is a best-in-class industrial compounder delivering Q2 2026 revenue of $2.04B (+15% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $2.09 (+17%) and raised FY2026 EPS guidance to $8.20-$8.30 [1]. The company's 'four growth strategies' — operational excellence, strategic acquisitions, global expansion, and new products — have produced a remarkable track record of compounding returns. Profit margin of 20% is exceptional for an industrial company, D/E of 20.5% provides balance sheet capacity for continued acquisitions, and $1.49B TTM free cash flow funds the acquisition pipeline [4]. AMETEK is well-positioned for secular AI infrastructure tailwinds through its power management, thermal systems, semiconductor equipment, and precision instrument portfolios. The Q1 2026 record orders and Q2 broad-based growth signal strong demand across end markets [1].
The bear case centers on valuation and acquisition dependency. At 24.8x forward earnings and a 2.7 PEG ratio, AMETEK trades at a significant premium to the industrial sector, leaving limited margin of safety if growth decelerates [4]. The serial acquisition model requires a continuous pipeline of quality targets at reasonable valuations — competition for niche industrial technology companies has intensified, potentially compressing acquisition returns. ROE of 14.6% narrowly misses the 15% target, and without acquisitions the organic growth rate of 5% (Q1 2026) is solid but not exceptional for a premium-valued industrial [1]. Glassdoor feedback suggesting the company is 'focused on stock market performance, not people' could impair talent retention in a competitive labor market for engineers and scientists [2]. The 0.57% dividend yield is negligible for income investors. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~7.4% reflects moderate organic growth supplemented by acquisitions, and any pause in deal-making would expose the slower underlying organic growth trajectory.
