Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Residential ·NYSE ·United States
American Homes 4 Rent is one of the largest single-family rental REITs in the United States, owning and operating more than 54,000 homes across 22 states and 40 markets. The company both acquires existing properties and develops new build-to-rent communities through its AMH Development program. Q2 2026 revenue was $470.1M (+2.8% YoY) with same-home occupancy of 96.0%, core FFO per share of $0.48 (+5.2% YoY), and adjusted FFO per share growth of 8.3%. The company raised its full-year 2026 core FFO guidance midpoint by $0.03 to $1.95 per share.

Investment Thesis
AMH offers stable, inflation-protected income with a 3.86% dividend yield backed by 54,000+ physical homes across 22 states with 96.0% occupancy [1]. The single-family rental market benefits from structural tailwinds: elevated mortgage rates making homeownership unaffordable for millions of families, millennial family formation driving demand for suburban rental homes, and limited new single-family housing supply. Core FFO per share grew 5.2% in Q2 2026 with management raising full-year guidance to $1.95 midpoint, demonstrating steady operational execution [1]. The build-to-rent development program creates higher-quality, lower-maintenance homes at attractive yields. The Inspire Impact Score of 54 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes AMH suitable for faith-based income portfolios. Conservative D/E of 69% provides balance sheet safety.
The bear case centers on growth deceleration, REIT-specific valuation challenges, and governance concerns. Revenue growth of only 2.8% YoY and same-home rent growth decelerating suggest AMH is maturing — the explosive post-pandemic growth in SFR demand has normalized [1]. Forward P/E of 46.03x (GAAP) appears expensive though this metric is distorted by REIT depreciation — the more relevant FFO multiple is approximately 17.5x, which is fair but not cheap for a low-growth REIT. The GAAP payout ratio of 104.21% exceeds 100%, though on an FFO basis the payout is sustainable at roughly 68% of core FFO [1]. Blended lease spreads of 2.8% barely exceed inflation, limiting organic growth. The activist criticism from Land & Buildings (2022) highlighted governance and ESG weaknesses, and the executive team's bottom-35% Comparably rating suggests leadership quality concerns [3]. Rising property insurance costs and maintenance expenses in aging Sun Belt markets could compress margins. PEG of 12.37 implies investors are paying a steep premium for modest FFO growth.
Key Catalysts
  1. Build-to-rent development program delivering higher-quality homes at attractive yields (6-7% stabilized yields vs 4-5% acquisition cap rates), creating a pipeline of premium rental communities that could accelerate FFO growth beyond the current 5% trajectory [1]
  2. Mortgage rate persistence above 6% extending the housing affordability crisis, driving continued demand for single-family rentals from families who would traditionally be homebuyers — each year of elevated rates expands the addressable tenant pool [1]
  3. Full-year 2026 core FFO guidance raised to $1.95/share midpoint (+4.3% YoY), with potential for further upside if blended lease spreads accelerate from the July preliminary 2.8% level as seasonal demand peaks [1]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration to 2.8% YoY with blended lease spreads barely above inflation suggests the post-pandemic SFR boom has normalized, and AMH may struggle to grow faster than 3-5% annually without acquisitions or accelerated development spending [1]
  2. Governance and leadership concerns — executive team rated in bottom 35% of peers on Comparably, activist criticism of ESG oversight, and the passing of founder B. Wayne Hughes in 2022 removing the founding visionary without evidence of cultural succession [3]
  3. Rising property insurance and maintenance costs in Sun Belt markets (Florida, Texas, Arizona — AMH's largest geographies) could compress NOI margins, particularly as climate-related risks increase frequency and severity of weather events [1]
