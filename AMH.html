Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Residential ·NYSE ·United States
American Homes 4 Rent is one of the largest single-family rental REITs in the United States, owning and operating more than 61,000 homes across 22 states and 40 markets. The company both acquires existing properties and develops new build-to-rent communities through its AMH Development program. H1 2026 revenue was $942.1M (+2.8% YoY) with same-home occupancy of 96.0%, core FFO per share of $0.49 in Q2 (+5.2% YoY), and full-year 2026 core FFO guidance raised to $1.95/share midpoint.

Investment Thesis
AMH at $30.89 with a $12.7B market cap offers a 4.0% dividend yield backed by 61,000+ physical single-family homes across 22 states with 96.0% occupancy [1]. The single-family rental market benefits from structural tailwinds: elevated mortgage rates making homeownership unaffordable, millennial family formation driving demand for suburban rentals, and limited new single-family housing supply. H1 2026 revenue grew 2.8% to $942.1M with core FFO per share of $0.49 in Q2 (+5.2% YoY) and management raised full-year guidance to $1.95 midpoint [1][2]. The build-to-rent development program creates higher-quality, lower-maintenance homes at attractive yields versus acquisition. Conservative D/E of 68.9% provides balance sheet safety, and the Inspire Impact Score of 54 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes AMH suitable for faith-based income portfolios.
The bear case centers on growth deceleration, REIT-specific valuation challenges, and leadership concerns. Revenue growth of only 2.8% YoY and same-home rent growth decelerating in H2 2026 with flat new-lease rate growth suggest AMH is maturing — the explosive post-pandemic SFR demand has normalized [2]. Forward P/E of 45.7x (GAAP) appears expensive; the more relevant FFO multiple of ~17.5x is fair but not cheap for a low-growth REIT. The GAAP payout ratio of ~99% exceeds sustainability on an earnings basis, though on an FFO basis the payout is sustainable at roughly 68% of core FFO [1][5]. The executive team's bottom-35% Comparably rating and the 2022 Land & Buildings activist criticism of ESG oversight raise governance questions [4]. Rising property insurance costs and maintenance expenses in aging Sun Belt markets could compress margins, and markets like Atlanta and Tampa show weakening rental fundamentals [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Build-to-rent development program delivering higher-quality homes at attractive yields (6-7% stabilized yields vs. 4-5% acquisition cap rates), creating a pipeline of premium rental communities that could accelerate FFO growth beyond the current 5% trajectory [1]
  2. Mortgage rate persistence above 6% extending the housing affordability crisis, driving continued demand for single-family rentals from families who would traditionally be homebuyers — each year of elevated rates expands the addressable tenant pool
  3. Full-year 2026 core FFO guidance raised to $1.95/share midpoint, with potential for further upside if blended lease spreads accelerate as seasonal demand peaks [2]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration to 2.8% YoY with flat new-lease rate growth expected in H2 2026, and weakening fundamentals in key markets like Atlanta and Tampa suggest the post-pandemic SFR boom has normalized [2]
  2. Leadership and governance concerns — executive team rated in bottom 35% of peers on Comparably, 2022 activist criticism of ESG oversight from Land & Buildings, and CEO transition from Singelyn to Bryan Smith (January 2025) introduces execution uncertainty [3][4]
  3. Rising property insurance and maintenance costs in Sun Belt markets could compress NOI margins, particularly as climate-related risks increase frequency and severity of weather events in AMH's largest geographies (Florida, Texas, Arizona) [1]
