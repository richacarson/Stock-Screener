Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment & Materials ·NASDAQ ·United States
Amkor Technology is one of the world's largest outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) providers, operating advanced packaging and test facilities across South Korea, Vietnam, Japan, the Philippines, Portugal, and China. The company specializes in advanced packaging technologies including high-density fan-out (HDFO), 2.5D, system-in-package (SiP), and flip-chip, serving semiconductor companies across computing, communications, automotive, and industrial end markets. Q2 2026 revenue reached a record $1.9B (+26% YoY) with Q3 2026 guidance of $1.95-$2.05B, and CEO Kevin Engel assumed leadership in January 2026.

Investment Thesis
Amkor at $41.89 with a 14.97x forward P/E and 0.76 PEG ratio offers compelling value exposure to the AI infrastructure buildout. Record Q2 2026 revenue of $1.9B (+26% YoY) with Q3 guided to $1.95-2.05B demonstrates sustained demand, and the computing segment is expected to accelerate ~30% sequentially in Q3 driven by AI chip packaging [1]. The 10-year TSMC partnership and multi-year NVIDIA collaboration provide exceptional multi-year revenue visibility rarely available for semiconductor services companies [2]. D/E of 57% provides balance sheet conservatism with $2.5B cash against $2.7B debt, and the company is investing $2.5-3.0B in capex to expand advanced packaging capacity for the AI era. The stock has pulled back sharply from its 52-week high of $96.68, creating an attractive entry point at less than half of peak valuation [5].
The bear case centers on margin structure, customer concentration, and execution risk. Profit margin of 7.45% is thin for a company with 30,800 employees and massive capital requirements — OSAT companies structurally operate at low margins due to customer bargaining power, and free cash flow is negative $379M despite record revenue as $2.5-3.0B capex absorbs cash [5]. Top 10 customers represent ~72% of sales, creating concentration risk. The planned SiP production migration from South Korea to Vietnam is expected to disrupt results through H1 2027, while the 5-year revenue CAGR of only ~6% reflects the semiconductor cyclicality that makes Amkor's revenue inherently volatile — the current AI-driven boom could moderate as prior cycles (2022-2024 downturn) demonstrated [1]. Beta of 2.22 means the stock amplifies market moves in both directions, and the 57% decline from the 52-week high illustrates the volatility risk. CEO Kevin Engel took over in January 2026 and has not yet navigated a downturn, adding leadership transition risk [3].
