Company Profile
Healthcare ·Staffing & Employment Services ·NYSE ·United States
AMN Healthcare Services is the largest healthcare staffing and workforce solutions company in the United States, providing travel nursing, allied health staffing, physician and leadership search, language services, and technology-enabled workforce management through its WorkWise AI platform. The company connects healthcare professionals with hospitals and health systems nationwide and serves as a critical labor supply intermediary during healthcare workforce shortages. Founded in 1985, headquartered in Dallas, Texas.
2,664 Employees ·www.amnhealthcare.com

Investment Thesis
AMN Healthcare is the undisputed U.S. healthcare staffing leader at a cyclical inflection point. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.378B doubled YoY, driven by healthcare labor disruptions and strikes, with $62M net income ($1.59 EPS) beating analyst estimates by 32% [1]. Travel nurse volume grew for the first time since 2022, and the aging U.S. population combined with a structural nurse shortage projected to reach 200,000+ by 2030 creates secular demand tailwinds. At $35.77 per share with a $1.39B market cap, AMN trades at PEG of 1.16, and the WorkWise AI platform positions the company to capture technology-enabled workforce management revenue as hospitals seek to optimize labor costs [3].
The bull case is severely undermined by the unsustainable nature of Q1 2026 results — Q2 guidance of $620-635M revenue with breakeven operating margin reveals the revenue doubling was strike-driven and non-recurring [1]. The underlying business declined 8.5% in FY2025 as post-COVID travel nurse rate normalization compressed revenue from the $5.24B peak (2022) to $2.73B. D/E of 232% creates financial fragility, ROE is negative at -14.2%, and profit margin is approximately breakeven on a trailing basis [2]. AI disruption risk is material: the staffing intermediary function of matching, credentialing, and placing healthcare workers is precisely the type of marketplace that AI-powered platforms can disintermediate. AMN's divestiture of Smart Square for $75M suggests shrinking rather than expanding its technology footprint [6]. Analysts are split: 3 buy, 5 hold, 1 sell ratings.
Key Catalysts
  1. Healthcare labor disruptions and nurse strikes creating episodic demand surges that could sustain above-trend revenue through 2026 if labor tensions persist across major health systems
  2. WorkWise AI platform adoption for predictive scheduling and workforce optimization, potentially converting one-time staffing revenue into recurring technology subscription revenue [3]
  3. Structural U.S. nurse shortage projected to exceed 200,000 by 2030 as baby boomer retirements accelerate, creating secular demand for travel nursing and allied health staffing
Key Risks
  1. Q1 2026 revenue doubling was strike-driven and non-recurring — Q2 guidance of $620-635M with breakeven margin reveals the underlying business remains in cyclical downturn with continued rate normalization pressure [1]
  2. D/E of 232% creates balance sheet vulnerability in a declining revenue environment, with negative ROE (-14.2%) and near-zero profit margins limiting ability to invest through the cycle [2]
  3. AI-powered workforce management platforms could disintermediate the staffing agency model by enabling hospitals to directly match with and manage temporary healthcare workers, compressing AMN's intermediary margins [3][6]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
5
AI Risk Protection (5/9): AMN's core business as a healthcare staffing intermediary faces meaningful AI disruption risk. While the physical presence of nurses, therapists, and physicians at patient bedsides cannot be replaced by AI, the staffing agency intermediary function — matching, credentialing, placement, and scheduling — is the type of information-processing marketplace that AI platforms can automate [3]. AI-powered workforce management tools could enable hospitals to directly source and manage temporary healthcare workers, bypassing staffing agencies. AMN's divestiture of Smart Square scheduling software to symplr for $75M underscores competitive pressure from specialized technology players [6]. However, regulatory complexity (state licensing, credentialing, Joint Commission compliance) and relationship-intensive crisis staffing provide meaningful switching costs and moat. AI Opportunity (0/1): WorkWise platform incorporates AI for predictive scheduling and workflow automation, and the Jaide Health acquisition adds AI-enabled medical translation, but no specific AI revenue figures are quantified in public disclosures [3].
