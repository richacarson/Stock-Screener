Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NYSE ·United States
Amprius Technologies designs and manufactures ultra-high energy density lithium-ion batteries built on a proprietary silicon nanowire anode platform, targeting weight-sensitive mobility markets including drones, electric aviation, defense, and light EVs. The company has been in commercial production since 2018 and reported record Q2 2026 revenue of $34.0 million, up 126% year-over-year, driven by a new multi-year contract with Stark Future expected to exceed $100 million and roughly $500 million in new orders from U.S. defense customers across multiple military branches. Tom Stepien became CEO effective January 1, 2026, succeeding founder Dr. Kang Sun.

Investment Thesis
Amprius is converting a decade of silicon-anode battery R&D into rapidly scaling commercial revenue — Q2 2026 revenue of $34.0M grew 126% YoY with gross margin expanding to 27% from 20% in Q1, and management raised full-year guidance to at least $140M while reiterating a path to positive Adjusted EBITDA of $4M-plus [1]. The order book is unusually concrete for a small-cap hardware name: a Stark Future contract expected to exceed $100M, ~$500M in new U.S. defense orders across military branches, and a $24M European drone order provide multi-year revenue visibility in categories (defense robotics, drones, eVTOL) where energy density is a genuine competitive differentiator and demand is compounding alongside AI-enabled autonomous systems [1][2]. A clean balance sheet (D/E of 0.05) gives the company runway to fund the DIU-backed factory expansion without near-term dilution pressure.
The bear case starts with credibility: a May 2026 Manatee Research short report alleged Amprius exaggerated customer orders, overstated its contract manufacturing network, and leaned on aggressive bill-and-hold revenue recognition, and Block & Leviton has opened a securities investigation — the company's April 2026 auditor switch from BDO to Deloitte adds to governance scrutiny even absent a confirmed finding [3]. The stock remains deeply unprofitable (ROE -36%, profit margin -33%) and trades at a Forward P/E of 576.76, pricing in flawless execution on guidance the company only recently raised. Insiders have been exclusively net sellers over the past several months, including a $23.9M share sale by a director in April 2026, which cuts against the growth narrative even as the new CEO settles in. Glassdoor reviews citing favoritism and limited technician advancement raise questions about whether the organization can scale manufacturing discipline as fast as its order book is growing.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued ramp of the Stark Future contract (expected to exceed $100M) and additional defense orders following the ~$500M already booked across U.S. military branches, which would validate the raised $140M FY2026 revenue guidance [1]
  2. Gross margin expansion trajectory (27% in Q2 2026, up from 20% in Q1) continuing toward the company's 28%+ full-year target as manufacturing scale improves unit economics [1]
  3. Resolution of the Manatee Research short-seller allegations and ongoing securities investigation in the company's favor, which could remove a valuation overhang if disclosures are confirmed accurate [3]
Key Risks
  1. Active securities fraud investigation (Block & Leviton) following short-seller allegations that Amprius exaggerated customer orders and used aggressive bill-and-hold revenue arrangements — an adverse finding would be a credibility-destroying event for a story stock trading on unproven guidance [3]
  2. Extreme valuation (Forward P/E of 576.76) with persistent net losses (-33% profit margin, -36% ROE) leaves no margin for execution missteps on the newly raised revenue guidance
  3. Customer concentration risk given that a large share of near-term growth is tied to a small number of contracts (Stark Future, a handful of defense awards), making revenue vulnerable to any single contract delay or cancellation [2]
