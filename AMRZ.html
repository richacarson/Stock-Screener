Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Building Materials ·NYSE ·Switzerland
Amrize is the largest cement producer in North America and a major supplier of aggregates, ready-mix concrete, and building-envelope products (commercial and residential roofing, insulation), spun off from Holcim in June 2025. It operates two segments: Building Materials (Q3 2026 revenue $2.84B, 33.0% segment EBITDA margin) and Building Envelope (Q3 2026 revenue $929M) [1]. Chairman and CEO Jan Jenisch, former Holcim and Sika CEO, leads the company; Q3 2026 revenue rose 2.2% to $3.77B and FY2026 revenue guidance is $12.2-12.4B [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Amrize is North America's largest cement producer and trades at 15.7x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.09, low leverage (D/E 54.8%, 1.8x net leverage), and strong pricing: Q3 2026 aggregates prices rose 6.9% and the Building Materials margin expanded to 33.0% even as weather cut cement volumes 4.2% [1][5]. Net income grew 3.3% to $558M in Q3, nine-month revenue grew 5.0% to $9.45B, and the company returned about $1.1B to shareholders through Q3, including a $1B buyback completed October 28 [1]. Data-center, energy, and infrastructure construction give multi-year demand support for a business with no AI substitution risk [1].
The bear case is slow organic growth and cash conversion. Q3 revenue grew only 2.2% (helped by $77M of acquisitions), adjusted EPS was flat at $1.05, and FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $2.95-3.05B implies little growth [1]. Building Envelope margin fell 410bps to 19.6% on freight and raw-material inflation, and management pushed price-cost recovery into 2027. Residential shingle demand is weak due to fewer storms and slow existing-home sales [1]. Free cash flow was negative $551M through nine months while the company spent $750M on buybacks and $467M on acquisitions, so continued returns depend on Q4 cash generation and debt capacity [1]. ROE of 9.7% and a 3.3% three-year revenue CAGR show a mature, cyclical business [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q4 2026 seasonal cash generation reversing the $551M year-to-date free-cash-flow use, which would support the up-to-$0.44 annual dividend and further buybacks after the completed $1B program [1]
  2. Data-center and energy-project construction lifting cement and commercial roofing volumes, which management names as long-term demand 'mega-trends' [1]
  3. Building Envelope price-cost recovery in 2027 restoring segment margin from 19.6% toward prior-year levels near 23.7% [1]
Key Risks
  1. Continued cement volume weakness (down 4.2% in Q3 2026) if weather or a construction slowdown persists, with fixed-cost kilns magnifying the margin impact [1]
  2. Building Envelope margin compression from freight and raw-material inflation, with residential shingle demand tied to storm activity and existing-home sales [1]
  3. Debt-funded capital returns and acquisitions (net debt $5.55B) while year-to-date free cash flow is negative, reducing flexibility in a cyclical downturn [1]
