Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NYSE ·United States
Amentum Holdings is a global leader in advanced engineering and technology solutions serving the U.S. federal government and allied nations across defense, intelligence, energy, space, and commercial sectors. Formed through the 2024 merger of legacy Amentum and Jacobs Critical Mission Solutions, the company operates with approximately 53,000 employees in more than 70 countries. Amentum reported Q3 FY2026 revenue of $3.5B (+1% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $290M (+6% YoY, 8.3% margin) and adjusted EPS of $0.67 (+20% YoY). The company holds a $48B total backlog with TTM book-to-bill of 1.3x. FY2026 revenue guidance was revised to $13.8B-$13.95B (down due to NASA insourcing and contract protests), but EBITDA and EPS guidance were raised on margin expansion. CEO John Heller has led the company since March 2022.

Investment Thesis
Amentum at 9.01x forward earnings with a forward PEG of approximately 0.50 is a deeply undervalued government services leader trading at a significant discount to peers despite delivering 20% adjusted EPS growth in Q3 FY2026 and holding a massive $48B backlog with TTM book-to-bill of 1.3x [1]. The company's post-merger scale ($14B+ revenue, 53,000 employees, 70+ countries) creates competitive advantages in pursuing large-scale, complex government contracts across defense, intelligence, energy, and space. Funded backlog grew 10% YoY to $6.2B, providing near-term revenue visibility, while adjusted EBITDA margin expansion to 8.3% (+40bps YoY) demonstrates operational improvement [1]. The FY2027 outlook anticipates mid-single-digit growth excluding NASA headwinds, suggesting the near-term revenue pressures are temporary. The appointment of a Chief AI Architect and investment in enterprise-wide AI capabilities positions Amentum to capture growing DoD and intelligence community AI spending [2]. The company's structurally high AI resilience (score 9) reflects the physical-world, clearance-protected nature of its core services.
The bear case focuses on thin margins, revenue headwinds, and merger integration risk. GAAP profit margin of only 1.04% leaves minimal room for error — government services contractors operate on thin margins where cost overruns, contract disputes, or pricing pressure can quickly turn profits into losses [4]. FY2026 revenue guidance was revised downward to $13.8-13.95B from higher levels due to NASA insourcing (a structural headwind as the agency brings work in-house) and contract protest delays, suggesting vulnerability to government budget and procurement decisions [1]. ROE of only 4.46% reflects dilutive post-merger capital structure and the inherently capital-light but low-return nature of government services contracting. The 2024 merger integration is ongoing, creating execution risk as two large organizations combine cultures, systems, and operations across 70+ countries. The company has limited track record in its current form — only two years of combined operating history provides insufficient data to assess long-term strategic capability. Legacy controversies including human trafficking and contract fraud allegations from the AECOM subsidiary era (now Amentum) create reputational risk [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of 5 (MIXED) with only one positive attribution (Customer Privacy) provides minimal appeal for faith-based investors.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Amentum's core business — providing mission-critical engineering, technology, and support services to defense, intelligence, energy, and space clients — involves physical-world operations that AI structurally cannot displace [1]. The company's services require on-site presence at military bases, nuclear facilities, space launch complexes, and intelligence operations centers. Security clearances at the SECRET and TOP SECRET/SCI levels create regulatory moats that AI tools cannot obtain. Government contracting involves complex procurement processes, compliance requirements, and relationship-based business development that are deeply human-intensive. Environmental remediation, facility management, and weapons system maintenance are physical operations that AI cannot perform. The only exposure is that AI could increase efficiency of some support functions (IT services, data analytics) potentially reducing the scope of some contracts, but the core mission-critical services are structurally immune. AI might also disrupt some back-office functions that Amentum performs for clients. AI Opportunity (1/1): Amentum is actively developing AI capabilities for government clients, appointing a Chief AI Architect (Dr. Sam Nazari) in May 2026 to drive enterprise-wide AI integration [2]. The company is building AI-powered solutions for defense and intelligence customers, a market where demand for AI applications is rapidly growing as the DoD and intelligence community invest in AI adoption. While specific AI revenue is not separately disclosed, AI-enabled services represent a meaningful and growing portion of the company's pipeline and backlog.
