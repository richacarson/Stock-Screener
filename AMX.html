Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·Mexico
América Móvil, S.A.B. de C.V. is the largest telecommunications company in Latin America, operating wireless, fixed-line, broadband, and pay-TV services across 25 countries in the Americas and Europe through brands including Telcel, Claro, and TracFone. Controlled by the Slim family, the company serves over 300 million wireless subscribers and is investing heavily in 5G and fiber broadband infrastructure. Founded in 2000, headquartered in Mexico City, Mexico.
177,545 Employees ·www.americamovil.com

Investment Thesis
América Móvil delivered strong 2025 results with revenue up 8.6% to Ps.943.6B, net income of Ps.88.1B, and continued momentum in Q1 2026 with EBITDA margin expanding to 40% and net income rising 25% [1][2]. At a forward P/E of 13.74 with 20.5% ROE, AMX offers an attractive valuation for the dominant LatAm telecom franchise with 300M+ wireless subscribers across 25 countries. The 2.14% dividend yield, deleveraging trajectory toward 1.3x net debt/EBITDA, and MXN 21B share buyback authorization provide shareholder-friendly capital return. The $7B annual capex program positions AMX for 5G and fiber broadband growth as Latin American data consumption accelerates.
The bear case centers on structural growth limitations and emerging market risk. Revenue growth of 2-4% CAGR in local currency terms reflects a maturing telecom market, and the 2% five-year USD revenue CAGR is dragged down by peso depreciation and competitive pricing pressure [5]. D/E of 173% reflects the capital intensity inherent in maintaining and upgrading infrastructure across 25 countries, many with currency and political instability. Profit margin of 8.78% is modest and vulnerable to inflationary cost pressures, regulatory mandates on pricing, and spectrum auction costs. The Slim family's controlling ownership introduces governance risk for minority shareholders — capital allocation decisions, related-party transactions, and strategic direction are concentrated in a single family. The 'Sexually Explicit' negative attribution from Inspire Insight relates to content distribution through AMX's media and broadband platforms.
Key Catalysts
  1. 5G rollout acceleration across Mexico, Brazil, and Colombia driving incremental data revenue and premium service pricing as smartphone penetration deepens
  2. Continued EBITDA margin expansion toward 42%+ through network efficiency gains, AI-driven operational optimization, and fixed-cost leverage on growing data volumes
  3. Deleveraging below 1.3x net debt/EBITDA combined with MXN 21 billion share buyback program creating a compelling total return story at 13.74x forward earnings
Key Risks
  1. Currency risk — Mexican peso and Brazilian real depreciation against the USD compresses AMX's dollar-denominated returns and ADR value, with limited natural hedging across the portfolio
  2. Regulatory and political risk across 25 countries, including spectrum auction costs, price regulation, and antitrust enforcement that could constrain margins and growth
  3. Slim family controlling ownership creates governance risk for minority ADR holders, with limited board independence and potential related-party transaction conflicts

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): América Móvil's core business — owning and operating physical telecommunications infrastructure (cell towers, fiber networks, spectrum licenses, data centers) across 25 countries — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. AI cannot replace physical network infrastructure; it requires it. The massive capital requirements ($7B annual capex), regulatory licenses, and decades of deployed infrastructure create insurmountable barriers. Furthermore, AI is a demand catalyst: generative AI applications, cloud computing, IoT, and autonomous vehicles all require increased bandwidth and network capacity, directly increasing demand for AMX's connectivity services [3]. The risk is limited to AI potentially commoditizing some value-added services (customer support chatbots, content recommendations) and enabling more efficient competitors. AI Opportunity (0/1): América Móvil uses AI internally for network optimization and customer service automation but does not generate quantifiable revenue from AI products or services sold to customers [3].
