Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·Mexico
América Móvil is the largest telecommunications company in Latin America, controlled by the Carlos Slim family, providing wireless and fixed-line voice, data, broadband, and pay TV services across 25 countries. The company operates under brands including Telcel, Telmex, Claro, and TracFone, serving approximately 300 million wireless subscribers. AMX reported FY2025 revenue of Ps.943.6B (~$50.1B USD) with net profit of Ps.88.1B (~$4.7B), driven by postpaid subscriber growth, fiber broadband expansion, and enterprise cloud services growing at 12.3% YoY.

Investment Thesis
América Móvil offers exposure to Latin America's digital transformation at an attractive valuation (12x forward earnings) with a well-covered 2.3% dividend growing at 6.8% annually. The company's dominant market positions across 25 countries, accelerating fiber and 5G buildout, and emerging enterprise cloud services division (growing 12.3% YoY) provide multiple growth vectors [1][2]. With ROE of 20.5% and a payout ratio under 40%, the company generates strong returns while maintaining capacity for continued infrastructure investment [6].
The bear case centers on regulatory overhang in Mexico, where AMX faces ongoing antitrust restrictions that limit pricing power and require infrastructure sharing with competitors [4]. Debt-to-equity of 173% reflects heavy capital intensity, and LatAm currency exposure creates earnings volatility — a 10% peso depreciation against the dollar materially impacts reported USD results. Revenue growth of 3.2% YoY is modest for a company operating in markets with significant digital adoption upside, and profit margins of 8.8% lag global telecom peers, suggesting limited operating leverage from scale [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Accelerating 5G monetization across Mexico, Brazil, and Colombia as network coverage expands and postpaid mix shifts higher, with service revenue already growing 5.3% YoY
  2. Enterprise cloud and IT services division growing at 12.3% as corporate networks modernize, representing a higher-margin revenue diversification beyond traditional connectivity
  3. Fiber-to-the-home expansion with 45M+ homes passed, driving broadband ARPU increases as subscribers upgrade from DSL to fiber
Key Risks
  1. Mexican antitrust regulations require infrastructure sharing and prohibit interconnection charges, structurally capping AMX's pricing power in its largest and most profitable market
  2. Latin American currency volatility — peso, real, and Colombian peso depreciation directly compresses USD-reported revenue and earnings, with limited natural hedging across the portfolio
  3. Capital intensity of simultaneous fiber and 5G buildouts across multiple countries strains free cash flow, with D/E already at 173% and annual capex running ~$8-9B
