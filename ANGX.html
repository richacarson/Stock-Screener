Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NYSE ·United States
Angel Studios is a values-based media and technology company that produces and distributes faith-based and family-friendly films and television series, including Sound of Freedom, The King of Kings, DAVID, Tuttle Twins, Homestead, and The Wingfeather Saga. Founded in 2013 as VidAngel (a content-filtering service shut down by a 2017 studio lawsuit), the company reinvented itself around the 'Angel Guild' — a crowdfunded membership model in which more than 2.85 million paying members across 180+ countries vote on which projects get greenlit and marketed. Angel began trading on the NYSE under ticker ANGX on September 11, 2025 after completing a SPAC merger with Southport Acquisition Corp [1][3].

Investment Thesis
Angel Studios is a high-growth, high-risk story built on a genuinely novel distribution model: Q2 2026 revenue grew 27.5% YoY to $111.7M, driven by Guild membership nearly doubling to 2.85M paying members and Guild revenue growth of 93.8% YoY, while operating cash flow turned positive at $16.9M and the adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed from $17.5M to $11.7M [2]. Marketing efficiency is genuinely improving — Guild sales and marketing expense fell from 71.6% to 52.8% of Guild revenue even as membership additions accelerated (390,000 in Q2 2026 vs. 230,000 a year earlier) — evidence the audience-funded flywheel is gaining real operating leverage [2]. The Angel Guild crowdfunding model is a structurally differentiated moat: it converts audience trust into both financing and marketing, a mechanism traditional studios cannot easily replicate, and full-year adjusted EBITDA loss guidance of under $25M implies a credible path toward breakeven if growth persists [2].
The bear case is substantial and centers on balance-sheet fragility, unresolved litigation, and unproven AI resilience. The company carries negative shareholders' equity (D/E of -2.63x) and a current ratio of just 0.65, signaling real near-term liquidity risk despite $48M in cash on hand [4]. GAAP losses are widening, not narrowing — net loss grew to $23.8M in Q2 2026 from $15.7M a year earlier, and gross margin compressed from 69% to 54% as the revenue mix shifts toward lower-margin Guild subscriptions away from higher-margin theatrical releases like King of Kings [2]. Governance overhang is real: Angel lost The Chosen to Disney in arbitration, faces an active lawsuit from DAVID filmmakers alleging an attempted 'hostile takeover,' and Sound of Freedom's promotional narrative around Tim Ballard was undercut by subsequent sexual-misconduct allegations against him — all of which raise questions about whether the studio's stated values are consistently honored in its business dealings [5]. Finally, no company-specific analysis of generative-AI risk to Angel's production/distribution model was found in research, leaving a meaningful blind spot on how AI-native content competitors could pressure the studio's cost structure and differentiation over time.
