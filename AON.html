Investment Thesis
Aon is a best-in-class global insurance broker trading at 18.2x forward earnings with a 21.5% profit margin, 46.9% ROE, and approximately $3B+ in annual free cash flow [6]. Full-year 2025 revenue of $17.2B grew 9% with 6% organic growth for the second consecutive year, and Q1 2026 delivered $5.03B revenue (+6.4%) with net income of $1.21B (+26%) [1][2]. The asset-light brokerage model generates exceptional returns on invested capital, and the $13B NFP acquisition expanded the addressable market into the mid-market segment. CEO Case's 21-year leadership and $282M personal stock ownership provide strategic continuity and alignment, while the $1.3B+ AI investment positions Aon as a technology-enabled advisor in an increasingly complex risk landscape [3][5].
The bear case centers on leverage, valuation, and macro sensitivity. D/E of 168% with approximately $16B in debt reflects aggressive financial engineering from the NFP acquisition — while serviceable given $3B+ FCF, rising rates increase carry costs [6]. The estimated PEG of approximately 2.5 suggests mid-single-digit organic growth is largely priced into the current multiple, limiting upside unless Aon accelerates beyond guidance. Organic revenue growth of 5-6% is modest for a stock trading at premium multiples. Insurance brokerage revenue is cyclical and correlated with premium rates — a soft insurance market could compress organic growth below guidance. Integration risk from the $13B NFP acquisition remains meaningful, and the prior failed Willis merger highlights execution uncertainty around large-scale M&A. The 0.83% dividend yield provides minimal income support for a $76B market cap company.
Key Catalysts
  1. 2026 guidance for mid-single-digit organic growth with 70-80 bps margin expansion and double-digit FCF growth, with Q2 2026 results expected to confirm sustained momentum [1]
  2. NFP acquisition integration creating mid-market cross-selling opportunities and expanding the addressable market for Aon's data analytics and risk advisory platform
  3. $1.3B+ AI and analytics investment generating measurable productivity gains across 60,000 employees, potentially accelerating organic growth and margin expansion beyond guidance [3]
Key Risks
  1. Elevated leverage at 168% D/E with approximately $16B debt from the NFP acquisition creates refinancing risk in a rising rate environment and constrains strategic flexibility [6]
  2. Insurance market cyclicality — a soft premium environment could compress organic revenue growth below mid-single-digit guidance, pressuring the valuation multiple
  3. Integration execution risk from the $13B NFP acquisition with potential for margin dilution during the multi-year integration period, following the precedent of the failed $30B Willis merger
