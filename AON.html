Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance Brokers ·NYSE ·Ireland
Aon plc is a global professional services firm providing risk management, insurance brokerage, reinsurance, and human capital consulting. Operating through Risk Capital and Human Capital segments, the company serves clients in 120+ countries with commercial risk solutions, health and wealth advisory, and data analytics-driven decision tools. Aon completed the $13.4B acquisition of NFP in April 2024, significantly expanding into middle-market insurance brokerage. H1 2026 revenue grew 4% reported (5% organic) with Q1 net income up 26% YoY. CEO Greg Case has led the firm since 2005. Market cap is approximately $63B.

Investment Thesis
Aon at 14.6x forward earnings offers compelling value for a global #2 insurance broker generating $3.2B in free cash flow with 5% organic revenue growth and expanding margins. H1 2026 revenue grew 4% reported with Q1 net income surging 26% to $1.21B, while full-year guidance was reaffirmed for mid-single-digit organic growth with 70-80 bps margin expansion [1]. The $13.4B NFP acquisition transforms Aon's addressable market by adding a middle-market brokerage channel, and AI-powered products (Broker Copilot, Claims Copilot) backed by $1.3B+ investment create differentiated client offerings that deepen relationships [2]. ROE of 44.7% and profit margin of 22.3% demonstrate capital-efficient operations in an asset-light model. Greg Case's 21-year tenure provides strategic consistency rare in professional services.
The bear case centers on leverage, organic growth limitations, and integration risk. D/E of 163.5% reflects $15.9B in total debt from NFP financing against only $1.1B cash, limiting financial flexibility and increasing sensitivity to interest rate movements [4]. Reported revenue growth of just 2.2% YoY (vs. 5% organic) masks FX headwinds and confirms that the organic growth engine is mid-single digits at best — far below the 10%+ that would reclassify this as a growth compounder. The PEG ratio of 2.32x suggests the market sees limited earnings acceleration. NFP integration is still early, and large professional services acquisitions carry meaningful execution risk around client retention, talent retention, and culture integration — the precedent of the failed $30B Willis merger highlights M&A uncertainty. Insurance brokerage revenue is cyclical and correlated with premium rates — a soft insurance market could compress organic growth below guidance. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors despite strong ESG practices across 14 positive attribution categories [3].
