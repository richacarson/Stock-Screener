Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Apogee Therapeutics is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing next-generation biologics for immunological and inflammatory disorders. Lead candidate zumilokibart (APG777) is a half-life extended anti-IL-13 monoclonal antibody in Phase 2/3 for atopic dermatitis, with additional programs in asthma, eosinophilic esophagitis, and COPD. AbbVie announced acquisition of Apogee for $135.11 per share ($10.9B) in June 2026, with shareholder vote scheduled August 11, 2026. Founded 2022, CORE values: Caring, Original, Resilient, Egoless.

Investment Thesis
Apogee is being acquired by AbbVie for $135.11 per share in cash ($10.9B total), with the shareholder vote scheduled for August 11, 2026 and closing expected Q3 2026 [3]. The stock trades at $134.84, a $0.27 (0.2%) discount to deal price, reflecting high deal certainty. Prior to the acquisition announcement, Apogee had built a compelling clinical-stage platform — zumilokibart showed 75-86% sustained response at Week 52 in atopic dermatitis with best-in-class 3-month dosing, and the pipeline expanded to 5+ candidates across immunology and respiratory [2]. Cash of $1.3B with runway into 2029 and the $1.3B Blackstone non-dilutive financing validated the platform. AbbVie's $10.9B valuation represents a significant premium for a pre-revenue biotech.
With the stock trading at 99.8% of the $135.11 deal price, the investment thesis is now purely a merger arbitrage calculation rather than a fundamental biotech assessment. The remaining risk is deal break — if the August 11 shareholder vote fails or regulatory clearance is denied, the stock could decline substantially from $134.84 to pre-announcement levels (the stock traded at ~$24.80 earlier in 2026 before pipeline catalysts and the acquisition) [3]. For investors not already holding APGE, the risk-reward is asymmetric: <1% upside to deal close vs. potentially 50%+ downside on deal break. The AbbVie acquisition is expected to be EPS-dilutive through 2031 with accretion beginning in 2032, suggesting AbbVie is paying a full price that leaves minimal upside for deal-arb investors [3]. The IOWN framework scores this stock on its fundamental merits as a pre-revenue biotech — the infrastructure score of 3 reflects mechanical limitations of scoring pre-revenue companies, not the underlying quality of the platform that attracted AbbVie's $10.9B bid.
