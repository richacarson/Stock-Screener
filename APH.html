Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYQ ·United States
Amphenol designs, manufactures, and markets electrical, electronic, and fiber optic connectors across three segments — Communications Solutions, Harsh Environment Solutions, and Interconnect and Sensor Systems. The company serves automotive, aerospace, defense, IT datacom, industrial, and mobile device markets globally. TTM revenue reached $29.0B with 60.9% YoY growth, significantly boosted by the Q1 2026 CommScope acquisition. Record Q2 2026 orders of $10.7B (+94% YoY) with 1.23:1 book-to-bill. Founded in 1932 and headquartered in Wallingford, Connecticut. A 2-for-1 stock split was executed in September 2026.

Investment Thesis
Amphenol at $86.96 (post-split) with a $214B market cap is one of the purest plays on AI physical infrastructure: record Q2 2026 sales of $8.76B (+55% YoY, +30% organic) were driven by IT datacom up 63% organically, record orders of $10.7B (+94% YoY, 1.23:1 book-to-bill), and record backlog of $10.1B, with AI-related revenue at a $10.5-11B annualized pace [1][2]. The CommScope integration is exceeding expectations — outlook raised to $4.60B from $4.10B with EPS accretion doubled to $0.30. ROE of 38.1%, record adjusted operating margin of 29.8%, a 5-year revenue CAGR of 23.2%, and a Forward PEG of 0.99 demonstrate exceptional capital efficiency fairly priced relative to the growth trajectory. The decentralized intrapreneurship model with 130+ autonomous business units enables rapid market-by-market capture without central bottlenecks [3][6].
The bear case centers on growth sustainability and valuation risk. At 43.5x trailing and 26.5x forward earnings, Amphenol prices in continued 55%+ growth that becomes mathematically harder to sustain as the AI infrastructure buildout matures and CommScope revenue annualizes [1][2]. Management has explicitly acknowledged that current AI server market share levels 'may not be sustainable,' and the copper-versus-optics technology transition presents positioning risk [4]. D/E of 120.4% reflects CommScope acquisition debt that must be deleveraged through earnings, creating vulnerability if AI spending decelerates. The electronic components industry is historically cyclical — revenue stagnated from $12.6B (2022) to $12.6B (2023) before the AI boom rescued growth. Profit margin of 17.7% remains below the 20% target, and with 170,000 employees across 130+ semi-autonomous units, execution risk increases as the organization scales rapidly [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued AI datacenter capex from hyperscalers sustaining or accelerating the 63% organic IT datacom growth rate, with the $10.1B record backlog and 1.23:1 book-to-bill providing several quarters of revenue visibility [1]
  2. CommScope acquisition integration exceeding expectations with management raising full-year revenue outlook to $4.60B from $4.10B and doubling EPS accretion to $0.30, expanding addressable market into enterprise networking and outdoor wireless [1][2]
  3. Forward PEG compression to 0.99 (from 1.17 at last screen) as analyst EPS estimates catch up to execution, supporting multiple sustainability if growth continues [2]
Key Risks
  1. AI server interconnect market share may not be sustainable at current levels — management has explicitly flagged this risk as fast-evolving technology cycles and copper-versus-optics transition create positioning uncertainty [4]
  2. Heavy revenue concentration in AI-driven IT datacom growth (63% organic in Q2 2026) creates vulnerability to any deceleration in hyperscaler AI infrastructure capital spending, which would compress the 26.5x forward multiple
  3. D/E of 120.4% from the $6.5B CommScope deal must be deleveraged through earnings, and prior cycles show revenue can stagnate when end-market demand softens ($12.6B in both 2022 and 2023) [1][2]
