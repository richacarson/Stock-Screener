Company Profile
Technology ·Data Center Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Applied Digital designs, develops, and operates next-generation AI data center infrastructure ('AI Factories') with purpose-built high-performance computing facilities featuring large-scale liquid cooling systems. The company has secured approximately $36 billion in contracted base-term lease revenue across five AI Factory campuses totaling 1.41 GW of critical IT load, serving U.S.-based hyperscalers under 15-year lease agreements. CEO Wes Cummins and co-founder/President Jason Zhang (appointed January 2026) led the company's pivot from crypto mining hosting (as Applied Blockchain) to AI infrastructure beginning in 2022. FY2026 revenue reached $611.3M (+167% YoY), with Q4 delivering $258.7M (+407%). The company is approaching GAAP breakeven with Q4 adjusted net income of $12.9M. Market cap approximately $7.6B.

Investment Thesis
Applied Digital is a pure-play AI infrastructure company at an inflection point — FY2026 revenue of $611.3M (+167% YoY) with Q4 delivering $258.7M (+407%) and adjusted net income of $12.9M, demonstrating the operating leverage as hyperscaler leases ramp [1]. The company has secured $36B in contracted base-term lease revenue ($86B including renewal options) across five AI Factory campuses totaling 1.41 GW of critical IT load, with the most recent Polaris Forge 3 deal worth $7.5B from a U.S.-based investment-grade hyperscaler [2]. At $25.38 per share ($7.6B market cap), APLD trades at roughly 0.21x contracted base-term revenue, a significant discount to established data center operators. The secular AI compute demand trajectory — driven by frontier model training and inference scaling — provides a multi-year tailwind. The Inspire score of 55 (ALIGNED) with 13 positive attributions supports faith-based investment compatibility.
The bear case centers on profitability risk, leverage, and execution concentration. APLD lost $250.3M in FY2026 on $611M in revenue, and the path to sustained GAAP profitability depends entirely on completing campus buildouts and ramping lease revenue — any construction delays, cost overruns, or power procurement issues could extend the loss period and strain the balance sheet [1][4]. The ~$2.8B debt load creates significant financial risk in a tightening capital environment. Customer concentration is critical — a small number of hyperscaler tenants represent substantially all contracted revenue, and losing or renegotiating a single lease could materially impair the business [2]. Competition from better-capitalized incumbents (Digital Realty, Equinix, QTS/Blackstone) intensifies as AI data center demand attracts more capital into the sector. The stock has declined from its 52-week high to $25.38, reflecting market repricing of execution risk and broader AI infrastructure sector rotation. Insider selling by CEO Cummins (~$6M with zero purchases) raises questions about conviction at current valuation [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Revenue ramp across five AI Factory campuses as construction completes — each campus reaching full capacity adds billions in annualized lease revenue, with 1.41 GW of total contracted capacity providing a multi-year runway toward $1B+ annual revenue [1][2]
  2. Additional hyperscaler lease announcements beyond the five currently contracted campuses, potentially expanding the $36B contracted base and re-rating the stock from its current 0.21x contracted revenue multiple [2]
  3. Path to sustained profitability as lease revenue ramps against a largely fixed-cost infrastructure base — Q4 FY2026 adjusted net income of $12.9M demonstrates operating leverage is beginning to materialize, with cash flow positivity expected by late FY2027 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Construction execution risk across five simultaneous campus projects — any material delays in power procurement, supply chain, or buildout could delay revenue recognition and trigger covenant issues on the ~$2.8B debt facility [1][2]
  2. Extreme customer concentration with a small number of hyperscaler tenants creating binary outcomes — any lease renegotiation, termination, or credit event could materially impair revenue visibility and debt serviceability [2]
  3. Balance sheet vulnerability with ~$2.8B in debt and -$250M annual losses — if capital markets tighten or AI infrastructure spending decelerates, APLD's ability to fund remaining construction and service debt could be severely impaired [4]
