Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Hotel & Motel ·NYSE ·United States
Apple Hospitality REIT is one of the largest hospitality REITs in the U.S., owning a diverse portfolio of 217 upscale, rooms-focused select-service hotels with approximately 29,583 rooms across 86 markets in 37 states and Washington D.C. The portfolio is concentrated in Marriott, Hilton, and Hyatt-branded properties (Residence Inn, Hampton, Hilton Garden Inn, Courtyard). Pays a monthly dividend. Founded by Glade Knight, led by CEO Justin Knight since 2014. Market cap approximately $3.9 billion.

Investment Thesis
Apple Hospitality REIT owns 217 upscale select-service hotels (~29,583 rooms) under premium brands (Residence Inn, Hampton, Hilton Garden Inn, Courtyard) and yields 5.79% via a monthly $0.08 dividend ($0.96 annualized) [1][2]. Q1 2026 showed meaningful improvement with revenue of $337.7M (+3.1% YoY), comparable RevPAR of $114.61 (+2.2% YoY), and adjusted hotel EBITDA of $108.5M (32.2% margin). Management raised full-year 2026 RevPAR guidance by 100 bps, citing resilient demand and FIFA World Cup tailwinds, with preliminary April data showing RevPAR accelerating to over 4% [1]. Conservative balance sheet with D/E of 54%, $1.57B total debt at 4.65% weighted average rate, and forward-purchase agreements in Anchorage and Las Vegas demonstrate prudent growth. CEO Justin Knight's 25+ year tenure provides governance stability.
The IOWN framework structurally underweights hotel REITs due to limited innovation, modest organic growth, and the absence of a transcendent purpose beyond financial returns. Revenue growth has been anemic on a pre-COVID basis (~1% CAGR from 2019 to 2026), and the GAAP payout ratio of 132% — while distorted by REIT depreciation — highlights that the monthly dividend ($0.96 annualized) consumes more than reported earnings [2]. The dividend has not recovered to pre-COVID levels ($1.20/year in 2019 vs. $0.96 today), resulting in zero consecutive years of dividend growth and a negative 5-year dividend growth CAGR. ROE of 5.4% reflects the capital-intensive nature of hotel ownership, and the Forward P/E of 23.86 provides limited valuation cushion. A recession compressing leisure and business travel would pressure RevPAR, margins, and dividend coverage simultaneously.
Key Catalysts
  1. RevPAR growth accelerating to 4%+ in April 2026, with FIFA World Cup in 2026 expected to boost occupancy and ADR in select markets where APLE has significant hotel presence
  2. 5.79% monthly dividend yield ($0.96 annualized) provides reliable income stream for dividend-focused investors, with conservative D/E of 54% supporting dividend sustainability
  3. Forward-purchase hotel agreements in high-growth markets (Anchorage, Las Vegas) and ongoing portfolio recycling creating incremental value through disciplined capital allocation
Key Risks
  1. Dividend has not recovered to pre-COVID levels ($0.96/year vs. $1.20 pre-COVID), resulting in zero consecutive years of growth and a negative 5-year dividend CAGR, with GAAP payout ratio of 132% signaling thin coverage
  2. Organic revenue growth of 3.1% and pre-COVID 7-year CAGR of approximately 1% reflect a fundamentally low-growth business model dependent on hospitality cycle dynamics and acquisition activity
  3. Recession risk would compress both leisure and business travel simultaneously, pressuring RevPAR, occupancy, and hotel EBITDA margins that currently stand at 32.2% — a level that could deteriorate rapidly in a downturn

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Hotel ownership is a textbook IMMUNE business — physical real estate that AI cannot replace. Travelers must physically occupy rooms, and the select-service hotel segment is driven by location, brand quality, and operational execution rather than information services that AI could substitute [1]. AI may even boost travel demand through AI conferences, tech industry expansion, and productivity-driven business travel growth. The brand operators (Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt) handle AI deployment for revenue management and guest personalization, so APLE benefits from AI efficiency gains without bearing the development cost or facing substitution risk. Analysts have not flagged AI as a disruption risk for hotel REITs. AI Opportunity (0/1): No direct AI revenue stream — APLE is a passive owner of physical real estate assets that relies on third-party brand operators for technology deployment and operational management.
