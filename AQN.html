Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Diversified ·NYSE ·Canada
Algonquin Power & Utilities operates regulated electric, water, wastewater, and natural gas utilities across the United States, Canada, Bermuda, and Chile through its Liberty Utilities subsidiary. The company is undergoing a strategic transformation from a diversified power and utility company to a pure-play regulated utility under CEO Rod West (appointed March 2025, former 25-year Entergy executive). AQN reported Q1 2026 revenue of $792.4M (+14.4% YoY) with adj net earnings of $99.6M. Guidance targets 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.35-$0.37 with 5-6% rate base CAGR through 2028. The quarterly dividend of $0.065/share ($0.26 annualized) reflects the 2023 cut from $0.72/year.

Investment Thesis
Algonquin Power at $5.25 (12.9x forward earnings) offers a turnaround utility story with downside protection from regulated rate-of-return operations and a 4.95% dividend yield. Q1 2026 revenue grew 14.4% to $792.4M with adjusted net earnings of $99.6M, and management guides 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.35-$0.37 with 5-6% rate base CAGR through 2028 [1]. The pure-play regulated utility transformation under CEO Rod West (25-year Entergy veteran) simplifies the business model and could trigger a valuation re-rating toward regulated utility peer multiples of 15-18x forward earnings. At $5.25, the stock trades at the lowest P/E among North American diversified utilities, implying the market has not yet credited the restructuring progress. AI-driven data center electricity demand growth provides a secular tailwind for regulated utility rate base expansion [4].
The bear case centers on execution risk, weak profitability, and a severely damaged dividend track record. ROE of 2.49% is among the lowest in the utility sector, reflecting dilutive returns from years of poorly-timed acquisitions and overleveraged expansion [5]. Revenue growth has slowed to 3.1% YoY (from 14.4% in Q1), and earnings growth of -95.4% demonstrates the business is not yet generating consistent returns. The dividend cut of 64% from $0.72 to $0.26/year in 2023 will take years to repair — income investors have long memories, and the payout ratio of ~72% leaves limited room for increases without earnings acceleration. Three CEO changes in three years (Banskota, Huskilson, West) and Starboard Value's activist campaign reveal governance instability that could resurface [2]. D/E of 133.5% limits financial flexibility for growth investment. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) with no attributions reflects minimal ESG differentiation despite the company's sustainability heritage.
Key Catalysts
  1. Pure-play utility transformation completion — successful divestiture of remaining non-regulated assets could trigger valuation re-rating toward regulated utility peer multiples of 15-18x forward earnings from current 12.9x [3]
  2. Rate base growth of 5-6% CAGR through 2028 from regulated utility capital investment programs, providing predictable earnings growth that supports eventual dividend increases [1]
  3. AI-driven data center electricity demand in AQN service territories could accelerate rate base investment and earnings beyond current projections as hyperscalers seek utility partnerships for power supply
Key Risks
  1. Severely damaged dividend credibility after the 64% cut from $0.72 to $0.26/year in 2023 and 5yr dividend growth CAGR of -18% — rebuilding a consecutive growth track record will take 5-10 years, and income investors may avoid AQN until the record is re-established [5]
  2. ROE of 2.49% suggests the regulated utility asset base is generating inadequate returns on invested capital, potentially indicating regulatory headwinds, operational inefficiency, or stranded asset risk from the strategic transformation [5]
  3. Leadership and governance instability — three CEO transitions in three years and Starboard Value's board campaign demonstrate organizational fragility that could resurface if the turnaround stalls [2]
