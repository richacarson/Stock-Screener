Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
Accuray Incorporated designs, develops, and sells radiation therapy systems for the treatment of tumors and other medical conditions. Products include the CyberKnife robotic radiosurgery system and the Radixact/TomoTherapy image-guided radiation therapy platform. Serves hospitals and cancer treatment centers worldwide. Revenue includes product sales (systems) and service contracts. Founded 1990, headquartered in Sunnyvale, California.

Investment Thesis
Accuray generates $428.6M in annual revenue from an installed base of CyberKnife and Radixact radiation therapy systems with recurring service contracts, trading at a severely depressed $41.6M market cap — just 0.1x revenue [5]. New CEO Steve La Neve brings 40 years of medical device experience from Globus Medical, Becton Dickinson, and Medtronic, and the TCW Group's $15M equity investment plus $40M debt-for-equity exchange signals institutional belief in the transformation plan [3][4]. Service business provides predictable recurring revenue, and radiation therapy demand is structurally supported by rising global cancer incidence.
The $41.6M market cap on $428.6M revenue reflects severe financial distress, not opportunity. D/E of 350.5% with $173.8M debt against $49.6M equity, -101% ROE, -10.8% profit margin, and -$46.1M TTM net losses describe a company consuming value, not creating it [5]. The all-time low of $0.23 (July 24, 2026) followed withdrawn FY2026 guidance citing Middle East geopolitical disruptions that delayed product installations and service revenue [2]. The TCW convertible preferred at $0.50/share with 8% dividend creates a senior claim that subordinates common equity, and Q3 product revenue declined 26% YoY as capital equipment orders weakened globally [2]. With 118.96M shares outstanding and potential dilution from TCW conversion, recovery requires a fundamental operational turnaround that new management has not yet demonstrated.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO La Neve's transformation plan driving service business growth and margin expansion could stabilize revenue and return the company to profitability
  2. TCW Group's $15M equity investment and debt restructuring reduce near-term balance sheet risk and signal institutional support for the turnaround thesis
  3. Resolution of Middle East geopolitical disruptions could release delayed product installations and restore backlog conversion timing
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 350.5% with $173.8M debt, all-time low stock price of $0.23, and withdrawn guidance create existential financial risk — any further revenue decline could trigger covenant violations or insolvency
  2. Product revenue declined 26% YoY in Q2 FY2026 as global capital equipment spending weakened, and Middle East geopolitical uncertainty continues to delay installations
  3. TCW convertible preferred at $0.50/share with 8% dividend subordinates common equity holders, and conversion would dilute existing shareholders by approximately 80M shares (67% dilution)
