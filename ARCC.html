Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management — Business Development Company ·NMS ·United States
Ares Capital Corporation is the largest publicly traded business development company (BDC) in the United States, providing direct lending and other financing solutions to middle-market companies. Managed by Ares Management, ARCC invests primarily in first and second lien senior secured loans, mezzanine debt, and equity to private companies across a variety of industries. Q2 2026 core EPS was $0.47 (down 6% YoY), revenue ~$768M, and the company moved five borrowers to non-accrual status. The stock trades at 0.96x book value with a 10.3% dividend yield.

Investment Thesis
Ares Capital at $18.56 trades at 0.96x book value with a 10.3% dividend yield, offering the highest-quality income stream in the BDC space backed by a $27B+ diversified portfolio of middle-market loans, dominant origination scale, and the backing of Ares Management's $450B+ AUM platform [1][2][3]. The Commercial Paper program launched in June 2026 reduces funding costs and demonstrates ARCC's unique ability to access capital markets unavailable to smaller BDCs. Core EPS of $0.47 in Q2 2026 covers the $0.48 quarterly dividend at near-100%, and the 67+ consecutive quarter track record of stable or growing distributions provides income visibility. The Inspire Impact Score of 37 (ALIGNED) makes this suitable for faith-based income portfolios.
The bear case centers on earnings deterioration as the rate cycle turns. Trailing EPS of $1.34 — sharply below the $1.93 forward estimate — reflects GAAP net losses from unrealized markdowns, and Q2 2026 core EPS declined 6% YoY from $0.50 to $0.47, with five new borrowers moved to non-accrual [1][2]. Earnings growth of -54.1% and revenue growth decelerating from 8% to 3.1% YoY signal the golden era of high-rate BDC earnings is fading [3]. The ~$1B in AI-exposed software portfolio holdings creates indirect credit risk if AI disruption accelerates defaults among technology borrowers [7]. The GAAP payout ratio of 143% — while structurally inherent to the BDC model — leaves zero retained earnings buffer for credit losses in a recession, and any dividend cut from this yield level would trigger a severe sell-off. At 9.6x forward earnings, the stock prices in continued NII compression through the rate cutting cycle.
Key Catalysts
  1. Commercial Paper program reducing funding costs by an estimated 50-100bps vs. traditional BDC credit facilities, partially offsetting net interest margin compression from lower base rates [1]
  2. Potential for increased deal flow as private equity sponsors resume M&A activity in the middle market, driving origination volume growth and portfolio expansion [2]
  3. Dividend sustainability supported by 67+ consecutive quarters of stable or growing distributions and near-100% NII coverage of the $0.48/quarter payout [5]
Key Risks
  1. Interest rate compression in 2026-2027 could squeeze net investment income by 10-15% as floating-rate loan yields decline faster than funding costs adjust — Q2 2026 core EPS already declined 6% YoY [1][2]
  2. Approximately $1 billion in software portfolio investments face medium AI disruption risk per Ares Management's own stress test, creating potential credit deterioration in 5-8% of the portfolio [7]
  3. BDC regulatory structure requires 90%+ income distribution, leaving minimal retained earnings buffer for credit losses — five new non-accrual borrowers in Q2 2026 signal potential credit deterioration in the broader middle-market lending environment [2]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (6/9): ARCC's core business of direct lending to middle-market companies involves relationship-driven origination, credit underwriting judgment, and bespoke deal structuring that AI cannot readily automate — providing meaningful protection against disruption [7]. Parent Ares Management stress-tested its portfolio and found 86% at low AI disruption risk and 13% at medium risk. However, ARCC disclosed that nearly $1 billion of its software portfolio investments face medium AI disruption risk, creating indirect exposure through potential credit deterioration in AI-vulnerable borrowers [7]. CEO Schnabel acknowledged technology obsolescence as the top risk in the software lending space, and Ares CEO Arougheti described the AI fear narrative as 'odd and frustrating,' noting software is only ~6% of total exposures. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI-driven revenue generation. Parent Ares deploys ~25 AI use cases internally for operational efficiency, but these do not generate revenue for ARCC.
