Arcos Dorados is the world's largest independent McDonald's franchisee, operating approximately 2,400 restaurants across 21 countries and territories in Latin America and the Caribbean. The company holds exclusive rights to own, operate, and sub-franchise McDonald's restaurants in the region, serving millions of customers daily. Investment Thesis
Arcos Dorados offers compelling value as the world's largest independent McDonald's franchisee, trading at 7.53x trailing earnings with a 3.17% dividend yield [5]. Record Q1 2026 results — revenue of $1.22B (+12.9%), adjusted EBITDA of $118M (+29.3%), and comparable sales up 16% — demonstrate strong operational execution [2]. From 2019 through 2025, revenue grew nearly 60% and net income more than tripled in USD terms [1]. The digital transformation driving 64% of revenue through digital channels with 30.4M loyalty members provides structural competitive advantages and margin improvement potential. Plans for 105-115 new restaurant openings in 2026 with $275-325M capex target underpenetrated Latin American markets with 650M+ consumers. The positive Inspire score of 65 and industry-leading ESG governance — first LatAm restaurant company to tie ESG to executive compensation — appeal to faith-based and responsible investors [4].
The bear case centers on leverage, FX risk, and franchise dependency. D/E of 293% with $2.26B in total debt creates meaningful refinancing risk as Latin American interest rates remain elevated, and the thin 4.53% profit margin leaves minimal cushion for cost inflation [5]. Revenue growth of 12.9% in Q1 2026 was partially driven by inflation pass-through — systemwide comparable sales of 16% were 'in line with blended inflation,' suggesting limited real volume growth. As a franchisee, Arcos Dorados lacks control over menu pricing strategy, brand direction, and royalty rates — McDonald's Corp could unilaterally change franchise terms or economics. FX translation risk across 21 Latin American currencies creates persistent earnings volatility that is difficult to hedge. The $1.7B market cap and $8.20 share price reflect small-cap status with limited institutional coverage and potential liquidity constraints during regional political or economic crises.
Key Catalysts
  1. 105-115 new restaurant openings in 2026 with $275-325M capex driving revenue growth across underpenetrated Latin American markets, with 19 openings already completed in Q1 [1][2]
  2. Digital revenue at 64% and loyalty program expansion to 30.4M members creating margin improvement through operational efficiency, customer retention, and data-driven personalization [2]
  3. Attractive valuation at 7.53x trailing P/E with 3.17% dividend yield provides downside protection and re-rating potential as adjusted EBITDA growth of 29.3% in Q1 2026 demonstrates margin expansion [2][5]
Key Risks
  1. High leverage at 293% D/E with $2.26B debt creates refinancing risk in elevated Latin American interest rate environments, with only $422M cash on hand [5]
  2. FX translation risk across 21 Latin American currencies — comparable sales growth of 16% was 'in line with blended inflation,' indicating limited real growth beyond currency pass-through [1][2]
  3. Franchise dependency on McDonald's Corp for brand strategy, menu innovation, and royalty terms limits strategic autonomy and exposes shareholders to unilateral changes in franchise economics [1]
