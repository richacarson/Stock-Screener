Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Ardent Health, Inc. owns and operates a network of hospitals and clinics providing healthcare services across the United States, including general and specialty services such as internal medicine, general surgery, cardiology, oncology, orthopedics, women's services, neurology, and emergency care. The company operates 30 acute care hospitals and approximately 280 sites of care with over 1,800 affiliated providers across six states. New CEO Dave Caspers was elevated from COO in 2026, replacing Marty Bonick. Ardent is a subsidiary of EGI-AM Investments. Q1 2026 revenue grew 7% YoY to $1.60B, but Q2 2026 revenue declined 1.4% to $1.62B. FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at $6.4-$6.7B revenue.
19,500 Employees ·www.ardenthealth.com

Investment Thesis
Ardent Health trades at a deeply discounted 9.0x forward P/E with $6.3B in TTM revenue across 30 hospitals and 280 sites of care, offering deep value exposure to a resilient healthcare services business [1]. Q1 2026 showed encouraging momentum with revenue up 7% YoY and adjusted EBITDA up 26.3% (15% excluding a $10.9M investment gain), demonstrating disciplined cost management can drive margin expansion [1]. New CEO Dave Caspers brings operational focus from his COO tenure and is driving the IMPACT program targeting margins and care transformation. Insider buying by director DeMichiei and CFO Lumsdaine at $8.81 signals internal confidence at depressed valuations [3]. The healthcare delivery model is structurally protected from AI disruption, and the Inspire ALIGNED score of 45 confirms governance quality.
The bear case centers on accelerating margin compression, accounting concerns, and execution risk. Q2 2026 profitability deteriorated sharply — profit margin fell to approximately 1.0%, net income dropped to $17M from $73M a year earlier, and revenue declined 1.4% YoY as the company lapped two quarters of New Mexico directed payment benefits recognized in the prior year [1]. Ardent faces a securities class action covering investors from July 2024 to November 2025 over a $43M revenue reduction tied to collectability estimates and a $54M professional liability reserve increase that triggered a 33.8% stock decline [3]. D/E of 130% with $2.27B debt represents meaningful leverage for a business generating only 1.22% net margins, leaving minimal buffer against further deterioration. Hospital operators face structural headwinds from rising professional fees, nurse staffing shortages, payer mix pressure, and government reimbursement uncertainty. The CEO transition during the most challenging margin environment since the IPO introduces additional execution risk.
