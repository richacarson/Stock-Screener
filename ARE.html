Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Alexandria Real Estate Equities is the pioneering and dominant life science REIT, owning and operating purpose-built lab/office campuses in major biopharma clusters including Greater Boston, San Francisco, San Diego, New York City, Seattle, and Research Triangle. Founded by Joel Marcus 32 years ago, ARE has established four strategic verticals: real estate, venture investments, thought leadership, and corporate responsibility. 39% of the 44 novel therapies approved by the FDA in 2025 were developed or commercialized by ARE tenants. The company reported Q2 2026 FFO as adjusted of $1.73 per share, but faces significant headwinds with occupancy declining to 86.9% and revenue down approximately 13% YoY amid a life sciences real estate downturn.

Investment Thesis
Alexandria represents an asymmetric setup in irreplaceable life science real estate — the stock trades at depressed levels (5.4% dividend yield, P/FFO of ~8.3x) reflecting a severe biotech funding drought that has driven occupancy to 86.9% and compressed near-term earnings [4][6]. The long-term thesis remains intact: ARE owns purpose-built lab campuses in the most valuable biopharma clusters in the world, tenants developed 39% of 2025 FDA novel therapies, and the physical infrastructure required for drug development cannot be replicated or displaced [1]. If biotech funding normalizes, occupancy recovers, and leasing activity resumes, the stock has significant re-rating potential from current trough-cycle valuations.
The bear case is that the life science real estate downturn may persist — public biotech companies signed just 6% of Q2 2026 leases, and 35% of 2026 expiring leases remain unsigned [4]. Revenue has declined approximately 13% YoY, FFO is expected to drop 29.3% in FY2026, and the dividend was cut 43% from $5.08 to $2.88 annually, resetting years of consecutive growth [6]. Immigration restrictions limit access to international research talent, creating structural headwinds for the U.S. biomedical industry that ARE depends on [4]. The negative ROE of -5.86% and GAAP losses exceeding $1B signal genuine financial stress, not just REIT accounting distortion. If occupancy continues declining below 85%, further dividend cuts and capital raises may be necessary.
Key Catalysts
  1. Biotech funding recovery driving improved leasing activity and occupancy stabilization — historical cycles show strong rebounds in life science real estate demand following funding droughts
  2. AI-driven drug discovery accelerating the pharmaceutical pipeline, increasing demand for physical wet lab and clinical development space that only purpose-built life science campuses can provide
  3. Development pipeline delivery at trough-cycle construction costs, positioning ARE to capture rising rents when the market recovers with limited new competitive supply
Key Risks
  1. Prolonged biotech funding drought — if venture capital and IPO markets for life science companies remain depressed through 2027, occupancy could decline below 85% and force further dividend cuts
  2. Immigration restrictions limiting access to international research talent could structurally reduce demand for U.S. life science lab space, particularly in ARE's key Boston and San Francisco clusters
  3. Rising interest rates and wider credit spreads increase ARE's cost of capital for new development projects while compressing cap rates on existing properties, squeezing investment returns
