Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Alexandria Real Estate Equities, Inc. is an S&P 500 real estate investment trust (REIT) and the preeminent owner, operator, and developer of collaborative life science, agri-tech, and advanced technology Megacampus ecosystems in AAA innovation cluster locations. The company's 41.7 million RSF portfolio serves the world's leading pharmaceutical, biotech, and technology companies. Alexandria Venture Investments is among the most active early-stage life science investors. Founded in 1994 by Joel S. Marcus, headquartered in Pasadena, California.

Investment Thesis
Alexandria Real Estate Equities is the irreplaceable franchise in life science real estate — 32 years of pioneering execution, 41.7M RSF of purpose-built Megacampus ecosystems in AAA innovation clusters, and a venture investment platform that deepens tenant relationships and creates proprietary deal flow [3]. The current share price of $47.88 reflects a trough year: FFO guidance of $6.40/share (down from $9.01 in 2025) due to $2.9B in planned dispositions, but this is a deliberate strategy to strengthen the balance sheet with $4.17B of liquidity and a 10-year weighted average debt term [1][2]. At 7.5x P/FFO, the stock is pricing in severe distress for a company with 30 consecutive years of dividend payments and 90.9% occupancy. Executive Chairman Marcus's personal purchases during the downturn signal management conviction in the recovery. The 5.78% dividend yield on the reduced payout provides income support while waiting for earnings normalization in 2027-2028.
The bear case is real and reflects both cyclical and structural headwinds. Revenue declined 11.5% YoY in Q1 2026, the 45% dividend cut signals financial stress, and GAAP ROE of -5.86% includes meaningful impairment charges that question historical asset valuations [1][5]. The biotech funding environment — Alexandria's primary demand driver — remains uncertain, with venture capital deployment to life science companies running below 2021 peak levels. Occupancy of 90.9% is below the company's historical 95%+ norm, and the first half of 2026 faces additional lease expirations and tenant wind-downs. The forward P/E of 47.50 on GAAP earnings (depressed by REIT depreciation and write-downs) makes traditional valuation comparison difficult, and the negative profit margin of -12% reflects accounting realities that mask operational performance. Infrastructure scoring penalizes ARE under the GAAP-based framework, though FFO-based metrics are more appropriate for REIT valuation.
