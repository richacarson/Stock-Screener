Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Ares Management Corporation is a global alternative investment manager operating across credit, real estate, private equity, infrastructure, and secondaries asset classes. With $671.3 billion in assets under management and approximately $410 billion in fee-paying AUM as of Q2 2026, Ares is one of the largest alternative asset managers worldwide and a leading player in private credit. Q2 2026 delivered $1.43B revenue (+17% YoY), record quarterly fundraising of $36.4B, fee-related earnings of $491M (+20% YoY), and realized income of $522M (+31% YoY). Founded in 1997 by Michael Arougheti, David Kaplan, John Kiss, and others, headquartered in Los Angeles.

Investment Thesis
Ares Management delivered an exceptional Q2 2026 with $1.43B revenue (+17% YoY, beating estimates by 7.5%), record quarterly fundraising of $36.4B, FRE of $491M (+20% YoY), and realized income of $522M (+31% YoY), lifting AUM to $671.3B [1]. The forward P/E of 16.2 with a PEG of 0.86 suggests the market is pricing in robust earnings growth at a reasonable valuation. Management fees exceeded $1B quarterly (+14% YoY), providing durable recurring revenue, while the 3.95% dividend yield with 23% five-year dividend growth CAGR offers compelling income with growth [6]. The secular shift from public to private markets — with alternatives still representing only 15-20% of institutional allocations — provides a multi-decade tailwind. Ares's diversified platform across credit ($335B+), real estate, private equity, infrastructure, insurance (Aspida), and AI venture (BootstrapLabs) reduces single-strategy concentration risk and positions the firm to capture share across multiple capital channels.
The primary risk is the gap between GAAP earnings and distributable earnings, reflected in the 62.8x trailing P/E and ~227% GAAP payout ratio — metrics that appear alarming until adjusted for non-cash items endemic to alternative asset manager accounting [6]. If distributable earnings growth decelerates from the current 20-31% trajectory, the dividend could come under scrutiny despite the ~56% cash payout ratio. The rapid AUM growth from $197B to $671B in five years has been fueled by private credit's explosive popularity, but increasing competition from banks re-entering direct lending and rival managers (Blackstone, Apollo, Blue Owl) scaling credit platforms could compress management fee rates and returns. The D/E ratio of 163% is elevated relative to the 70% target, and ROE of 13.5% falls short of the 15% benchmark. Significant insider selling by CEO Arougheti ($90.7M in sales over six months, pre-planned under 10b5-1 plans) warrants monitoring despite substantial remaining ownership [5]. Regulatory scrutiny of private credit markets, potential credit cycle deterioration across the $335B+ credit portfolio, and founder-CEO key-man risk without a publicly identified successor represent additional concerns. Key Risks

  3. Founder-CEO key-man risk — Michael Arougheti has led Ares since its 1997 founding with no publicly identified successor, and cumulative insider sales significantly exceeding purchases ($1.16B vs $19M) signal estate planning dynamics that could precede a transition [5]

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Ares's core business of alternative asset management is well-protected from AI disruption. The fundamental activities — institutional fundraising relationships built over decades, complex deal origination, bespoke credit underwriting requiring deep sector expertise, and portfolio management of illiquid assets — are relationship-intensive, judgment-dependent, and heavily regulated by the SEC [1]. Managing $671B in AUM requires institutional trust that AI cannot replicate. The private credit business involves bespoke deal structuring and borrower relationships that resist commoditization. However, AI tools could partially automate credit analysis, document review, and portfolio monitoring, and AI-powered fintech competitors could emerge in simpler direct lending categories [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): While Ares acquired BootstrapLabs (an AI-focused venture platform) in 2024, hired a Head of Data and AI, and is deploying AI across four focus areas (investment portfolio, process, experience, operations), no quantifiable AI-related revenue from Ares's own AI products or services has been reported [3]. The BootstrapLabs acquisition generates management fees from investing in AI companies, but Ares itself does not sell AI products to external customers.
