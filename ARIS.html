Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Canada
Aris Mining Corporation is a Canadian gold mining company focused on South America, operating the Segovia and Marmato underground gold mines in Colombia. Formerly GCM Mining Corp., the company produced 148,000 ounces of gold in H1 2026 and reaffirmed full-year guidance of 300,000-350,000 ounces. Q2 2026 delivered gold revenue of $321M at an average realized price of $4,450/oz with adjusted EBITDA of $179M and adjusted EPS of $0.47. TTM adjusted EBITDA reached a record $690M. Aris also holds the Toroparu project in Guyana and settled its ICSID arbitration with Colombia through a long-term security and sustainability agreement.

Investment Thesis
Aris Mining is a high-growth gold producer delivering exceptional results: Q2 2026 gold revenue of $321M with adjusted EBITDA of $179M and adjusted EPS of $0.47, driving TTM adjusted EBITDA to a record $690M [3]. At $20.30/share with a trailing P/E of just 7.52x, the stock trades at a significant discount to gold producer peers despite 27% H1 gold sales growth YoY and a clear production pathway from 148,000 oz in H1 2026 to 300,000-350,000 oz for the full year [1][3]. The balance sheet is pristine with $426M cash and near-zero net debt, providing full self-funding capability for the Marmato CIP plant (Q4 2026 commissioning) and Toroparu construction decision (early 2027) [3]. The ICSID arbitration settlement with Colombia removes a key legal overhang and establishes a long-term security and sustainability partnership [4]. D/E of 36%, ROE of approximately 19%, and profit margin of 23% reflect a company in peak financial form.
The bear case centers on gold price dependence, jurisdiction risk, and execution complexity. Aris realized $4,450/oz in Q2 2026 — a pullback to $2,500/oz would compress margins substantially given combined AISC near $2,000/oz, and the stock has declined from April highs despite record operating results, suggesting the market is pricing in gold price reversion risk [3]. Colombia presents ongoing security, regulatory, and political risks; the Soto Norte Temporary Reserve Area imposed by the Ministry of Environment suspends environmental licenses for two years, demonstrating how quickly government policy can shift [4]. The pathway to 1M oz long-term requires flawless execution across two complex underground mines, successful Marmato CIP commissioning, favorable Toroparu economics, and navigating ongoing COO leadership transitions. The stock's Growth sleeve classification means no dividend income for investors (the company retains capital for expansion). Currency risk (Colombian peso) and energy cost inflation could pressure AISC. Despite the strong financial profile, this remains a single-country mining operation concentrated in Colombian underground mines.
