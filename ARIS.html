Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Canada
Aris Mining Corporation is a Canadian gold mining company focused on South America, operating the Segovia and Marmato underground gold mines in Colombia. Formerly GCM Mining Corp., the company produced 257,000 ounces of gold in 2025 and is executing expansion projects targeting 500,000 ounces in 2026 and 1 million ounces long-term. Aris also holds the Toroparu project in Guyana.

Investment Thesis
Aris Mining is a high-growth gold producer delivering exceptional results: TTM revenue of $1.14B (104% YoY growth), TTM adjusted EBITDA of $610M, and Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.60 (up 275% YoY from $0.16) on the combination of 36% production growth and elevated gold prices above $4,800/oz [1][3]. The growth trajectory is clearly defined: 2026 production target of ~500K oz (vs. 257K in 2025), with Segovia's second mill ramping and Marmato's CIP plant commissioning in Q4 2026, and a long-term objective of 1M oz [3]. At $14.90/share with a trailing P/E of 17.7x, the stock trades at a meaningful discount to gold producer peers. The balance sheet is pristine with near-zero net debt and $472M cash, providing full self-funding capability for expansion [3]. The ICSID arbitration settlement with Colombia removes a key legal overhang [4].
The bear case centers on gold price dependence, jurisdiction risk, and execution challenges. The stock has declined 27% from its April highs despite record operating results, suggesting the market is pricing in gold price reversion risk — a pullback to $2,000/oz would severely compress margins given combined AISC of $1,963/oz [3]. Colombia presents ongoing security, regulatory, and political risks; the Soto Norte environmental license suspension demonstrates how quickly government policy can shift [4]. The pathway to 500K oz in 2026 and 1M oz long-term requires flawless execution across two complex underground mines, successful Marmato CIP commissioning, and favorable Toroparu economics. Currency risk (Colombian peso) and energy cost inflation could pressure AISC. Despite the improved profit margin of 15.2%, ROE of 12.2% remains below the 15% target, reflecting the capital-intensive growth phase where returns lag investment.
Key Catalysts
  1. Marmato CIP plant (5,000 tpd) commissioning expected Q4 2026, adding 35-50K oz annual production and unlocking the pathway to 500K oz and ultimately 1M oz [3]
  2. Segovia second mill ramp-up producing 66,600 oz in Q1 2026 at owner-mining AISC of $1,492/oz — well below $1,700-1,800/oz guidance — with continued production acceleration expected [3]
  3. Near-zero net debt with $472M cash provides firepower for Toroparu construction decision (early 2027) and potential accretive acquisitions without equity dilution [3]
Key Risks
  1. Gold price dependence — combined AISC of $1,963/oz means profitability compresses rapidly below $2,500/oz; the 27% stock decline from April highs signals market skepticism on sustained gold prices [3]
  2. Colombia jurisdiction risk — Soto Norte environmental license suspension, ongoing security challenges, and potential regulatory shifts could disrupt operations or block expansion [4]
  3. Execution risk on multi-mine expansion — pathway to 1M oz requires successful Marmato CIP commissioning, Toroparu development, and sustained Segovia ramp-up across complex underground operations [3]
