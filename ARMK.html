Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NYSE ·United States
Aramark provides food and facilities services to education, healthcare, business and industry, sports, leisure, and corrections clients in the United States and internationally. The company operates through two segments: Food and Support Services United States, and Food and Support Services International. Following the 2023 spin-off of its Uniform business (Vestis), Aramark focuses on contract food service, facilities management, and the recently launched Aramark Nexus for hyperscale AI data center services. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $5.06B (+9.3% YoY) with organic revenue guidance raised to 9-10% and adjusted EPS growth of 20-25%. Revenue of ~$19.9B with 278,390 employees across 19 countries. Founded in 1959, headquartered in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

Investment Thesis
Aramark is delivering strong operational execution as a focused food and facilities services company post-Vestis spin-off. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $5.06B (+9.3% YoY) with organic revenue guidance raised to 9-10% and adjusted EPS growth of 20-25% demonstrates sustained momentum [1]. Adjusted EPS grew 30% in Q3 to $0.52 with operating income expanding on record new business wins and >98% client retention [2]. At 17.46x forward earnings with a 1.31 PEG ratio, the stock offers reasonable value relative to its growth trajectory. The Aramark Nexus initiative positions the company to benefit from hyperscale AI data center buildout, potentially opening its highest-growth vertical. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 12.05% reflects strong secular trends in outsourced food services as institutions choose to contract out rather than self-operate.
The fundamental challenge is that Aramark is a low-margin business (1.93% net) carrying elevated debt (214% D/E) — any revenue softness will disproportionately impact earnings and debt service capacity [4]. The company's labor practices create ESG and regulatory risk: documented controversies include worker strikes over wages and benefits, corrections facility food safety allegations, and reliance on corrections operations for approximately 12% of revenue [3]. Innovation and Inspiration scores of 5 and 3 respectively highlight the limited differentiation in what remains a commodity services business. Organic growth decelerated from 12% in Q2 to 9.3% in Q3, and the Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) restricts allocation for faith-based investors. Competition from Compass Group, which consistently operates at higher margins and organic growth, remains the primary competitive threat.
Key Catalysts
  1. Aramark Nexus AI data center services initiative with a top global hyperscaler expected to become the company's largest client, opening a new high-growth vertical with structural tailwinds from massive AI infrastructure buildout [1]
  2. FY2026 organic revenue guidance raised to 9-10% with adjusted EPS growth of 20-25%, supported by record new business wins and >98% client retention across education, healthcare, and sports verticals [1][2]
  3. Margin expansion from Culinary Co-Pilot AI deployment and post-Vestis operational focus could push profitability beyond the historical 2% net margin ceiling, with adjusted EPS already growing 30% YoY in Q3 [2][3]
Key Risks
  1. Structurally thin net profit margin of 1.93% with D/E of 214% creates significant financial vulnerability — food cost inflation, labor shortages, or rising interest rates on variable-rate debt could compress already razor-thin earnings [4]
  2. Labor practice controversies including worker strikes, corrections food safety allegations, and corrections facility dependence (12% of revenue) create ESG, regulatory, and reputational risk that could trigger contract losses at sensitive institutional clients [3]
  3. Organic revenue growth deceleration from 12% in Q2 to 9.3% in Q3 at a time when the stock has reached all-time highs (~$62) leaves limited margin of safety if the growth trajectory continues to moderate [1][4]
