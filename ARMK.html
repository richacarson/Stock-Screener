Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NYSE ·United States
Aramark provides food and facilities services to education, healthcare, business and industry, sports, leisure, and corrections clients in the United States and internationally. The company operates through two segments: Food and Support Services United States, and Food and Support Services International. Following the 2023 spin-off of its Uniform business (Vestis), Aramark focuses on contract food service, facilities management, and the recently launched Aramark Nexus for hyperscale AI data center services. Revenue of $19.4B with 278,390 employees across 19 countries. Founded in 1959, headquartered in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

Investment Thesis
Aramark is delivering strong operational execution as a focused food and facilities services company following the 2023 Vestis spin-off. Q2 FY2026 revenue grew 14.7% to $4.91B with organic growth of 12%, driven by record new business wins across all segments — education, healthcare, business & industry, sports & leisure, and international [1]. EPS surged 64.8% and operating income rose 26.2%, demonstrating margin leverage as the company scales. The entry into hyperscale AI data center services through Aramark Nexus, with a multi-year engagement expected to become its largest client, opens a high-growth vertical with structural tailwinds [1]. At 0.97x PEG and 20.9x forward earnings, the stock is reasonably valued for a company delivering 20%+ EPS growth.
The fundamental challenge is that Aramark is a low-margin business (1.84% net) carrying $6.4B in debt (192% D/E) — any revenue softness will disproportionately impact earnings and debt service capacity [4]. The company's labor practices create ESG and regulatory risk: documented controversies include prison food safety violations, worker strikes over wages and benefits, and reliance on incarcerated labor for 12% of revenue [3]. Organic growth guidance of 7-9% for FY2026 is decelerating from recent quarters, and the trailing P/E of 41.9x reflects elevated expectations that leave little room for a miss. The food services industry faces structural margin pressure from food cost inflation and tight labor markets, while competition from Compass Group and Sodexo remains intense.
Key Catalysts
  1. Aramark Nexus AI data center services initiative with a top global hyperscaler expected to become the company's largest client, opening a new high-growth vertical with structural tailwinds from massive AI infrastructure buildout
  2. FY2026 earnings inflection with 23%+ EPS growth to $2.25 consensus, driven by operational leverage, record new business wins, and margin expansion from 4.6% operating margin toward peer levels
  3. International expansion in 19 countries providing diversified growth with Q2 showing broad-based revenue increases across all geographic segments
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 192% with $6.4B total debt creates significant financial risk — 1.84% net margin leaves minimal cushion against revenue softness, food cost inflation, or rising interest rates on variable-rate debt
  2. Labor practice controversies including worker strikes, prison food safety allegations, and incarcerated labor dependence (12% of revenue) create ESG, regulatory, and reputational risk that could lead to contract losses
  3. Trailing P/E of 41.9x and decelerating organic growth guidance of 7-9% leave limited margin of safety — any earnings miss could trigger multiple compression toward the 20x forward target

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Aramark's core business is fundamentally physical-world operations — cooking food, cleaning buildings, managing facilities, landscaping, and custodial services for 278,390 employees across 19 countries [1][2]. These labor-intensive, on-site services cannot be displaced by AI; institutions will always need physical food preparation, facility maintenance, and human service delivery.
