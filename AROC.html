Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Archrock is the largest owner and operator of natural gas compression equipment in the United States, with a nearly 70-year legacy of industry leadership. The company provides contract compression services and aftermarket parts/maintenance to oil and gas producers, processors, gatherers, and transporters through its fleet of 4.53 million operating horsepower. Founded in 1990 and headquartered in Houston, Texas.
1,350 Employees ·www.archrock.com

Investment Thesis
Archrock is the dominant U.S. natural gas compression provider riding a structural growth cycle fueled by rising natural gas demand from AI data centers, LNG exports, and domestic power generation. Q1 2026 delivered $373.8M revenue (+7.7% YoY), $221M adjusted EBITDA (+12%), and $73.8M net income with 95% fleet utilization at 4.53M operating HP [1][4]. Management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $865-915M with $250-275M in growth CapEx for continued fleet expansion. At 18.6x forward P/E with 22.7% ROE and 21.5% profit margin, AROC offers compelling valuation with a 2.43% dividend yield growing at ~8% CAGR [5]. Equipment lead times of 160 weeks create extreme supply-side barriers that protect pricing power and utilization. CEO Childers' 14+ year tenure and 4.5/5 Glassdoor rating reflect stable, culture-driven execution that is rare in energy services [2]. Leverage is declining (2.6x, down from 2.7x) with nearest bond maturity pushed to 2032 and ~$600M pro forma liquidity [4].
The bear case centers on growth deceleration, energy cycle dependence, and leverage. Revenue growth decelerated from 15.5% (FY2025, TOPS-driven) to 7.7% (Q1 2026) as the acquisition base effect normalizes — organic growth is more modest at mid-to-high single digits, and analyst forecasts project only 5.9% revenue growth over the next 3 years [5]. D/E of 157% with $2.39B in total debt creates interest rate sensitivity and limits financial flexibility during an energy downturn, despite the improving leverage trajectory [5]. Natural gas prices and drilling activity directly drive compression demand — a sustained gas price decline below $2/MMBtu would reduce utilization from the current 95% and compress pricing. EPS grew only 5% in Q1 2026 ($0.42 vs $0.40), well below the EBITDA growth rate of 12%, due to higher SGA costs ($45M vs $37M, including a $3.7M non-recurring item) [4]. Archrock's business is tied to fossil fuel infrastructure — while the energy transition timeline is long-dated, regulatory changes affecting natural gas drilling or methane emissions could increase compliance costs for the 1,350-employee company [3].

Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration from 15.5% (FY2025) to 7.7% (Q1 2026) as TOPS acquisition base effect normalizes — organic growth in the mid-single digits may not support the current valuation [1][5]
  2. D/E of 157% with $2.39B total debt creates interest expense headwinds, though leverage is declining (2.6x) and nearest maturity pushed to 2032 [4][5]
  3. Natural gas price and drilling activity cyclicality — a sustained gas price decline below $2/MMBtu would reduce the 95% fleet utilization that underpins Archrock's pricing power and profitability [4]
