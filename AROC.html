Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Archrock is the largest provider of contract natural gas compression services in the United States, operating through Contract Operations and Aftermarket Services segments. The company designs, owns, installs, operates, and maintains natural gas compression equipment for oil and gas producers, processors, gatherers, and transporters. Formerly Exterran Holdings, Archrock completed its transformative acquisition of TOPS in October 2024, approximately doubling its fleet to ~4.5 million operating horsepower. Q2 2026 revenue was $371.2M with contract operations growing 3% YoY to $329.3M, though total revenue declined 3.1% as aftermarket services softened. The company tightened FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $865M-$885M.
1,350 Employees ·www.archrock.com

Investment Thesis
Archrock at 14.0x forward earnings offers attractive value as the dominant U.S. contract compression services provider with structural natural gas demand tailwinds. The TOPS acquisition transformed Archrock into the clear market leader with ~$1.5B TTM revenue, $865M-$885M in guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA, and profit margins above 21% at 95% fleet utilization [1]. The dividend profile is compelling for income investors — 3.0% yield with 9.2% five-year growth CAGR and a manageable 46% payout ratio funded by $726M in operating cash flow [4]. Three secular demand drivers — AI data center power generation, LNG export capacity additions, and industrial electrification — create a multi-decade demand runway for natural gas compression that partially insulates the business from commodity price cyclicality [2]. Equipment lead times of ~160 weeks limit new supply, protecting pricing power for existing operators.
The bear case centers on debt, revenue deceleration, and energy cycle dependence. D/E of 151.9% with $2.36B total debt against just $2M cash creates meaningful financial risk and interest rate sensitivity [4]. Revenue declined 3.1% YoY in Q2 2026 ($371.2M vs $383.2M) with aftermarket services falling ~8%, and management tightened EBITDA guidance from $865M-$915M to $865M-$885M, signaling weaker-than-expected demand conditions [1]. Heavy insider selling — CFO Aron sold $10.6M+ in shares in 2026 — contrasts with the bullish demand narrative and may indicate management concern about near-term fundamentals. The stock has already declined 11% from 52-week highs near $34. The company remains fundamentally exposed to natural gas production levels — a sustained gas price decline below $2/MMBtu that curtails producer drilling activity would reduce fleet utilization from the current 95% [1]. ESG pressures on fossil fuel infrastructure may increase cost of capital over time, and the 1,350-employee workforce faces integration challenges from the TOPS merger [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center buildout driving structural natural gas demand for power generation, with management explicitly citing AI onshoring as a multi-year tailwind supporting compression fleet expansion at $250-275M annual growth CapEx [2]
  2. TOPS integration synergies reaching full run-rate, with the combined fleet offering scale advantages in utilization, maintenance efficiency, and customer coverage that competitors cannot replicate at Archrock's 4.5M+ HP scale [1]
  3. Dividend growth trajectory accelerating — from flat $0.58 annual (2020-2022) to projected $0.90+ in 2026 (9.2% 5yr CAGR) — with 46% payout ratio and $726M OCF providing substantial coverage and room for continued increases [4]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue deceleration from +7.7% (Q1 2026) to -3.1% (Q2 2026) with tightened EBITDA guidance ($865M-$885M vs prior $865M-$915M) suggests demand softening, particularly in aftermarket services which declined ~8% YoY [1]
  2. Elevated debt ($2.36B, D/E 151.9%) with minimal cash reserves ($2M) creates financial fragility — interest expense headwinds and reduced flexibility to weather an energy downturn or fund fleet maintenance CapEx [4]
  3. Heavy CFO insider selling ($10.6M+ in 2026) combined with the stock's 11% pullback from highs may signal management's assessment that near-term risk/reward has shifted despite the structural demand thesis [1]
