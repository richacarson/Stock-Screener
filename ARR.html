Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Mortgage ·NYSE ·United States
ARMOUR Residential REIT is a Vero Beach, Florida-based mortgage REIT that invests mainly in U.S. agency residential mortgage-backed securities financed with repurchase agreements and hedged with interest-rate derivatives, paying monthly dividends. In Q3 2026 book value per share fell about 17% to $14.55 as mortgage spreads widened late in the quarter and the Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike since 2023; the company posted a GAAP net loss of $(327.0)M, or $(2.26) per share, against distributable earnings of $0.71 per share [1]. It pays $0.24 per month ($0.72 per quarter) [1].

Investment Thesis
ARMOUR offers a very high monthly income stream: $0.24 per month ($2.88 a year, about a 23% yield at $12.45) that Q3 2026 distributable earnings of $0.71 per share nearly covered [1][4]. The stock trades at about 0.86x the $14.55 September 30 book value and 4.35x forward earnings, so a narrowing of agency mortgage spreads from their late-Q3 wides could quickly rebuild book value and support the payout [1][6]. The company has paid dividends every year since 2010 [4].
The bear case is structural. With D/E of about 825% and financial leverage near 9.6x, a modest move in spreads wiped out 17% of book value in one quarter (total economic return -12.9%) and produced a $(327.0)M GAAP loss [1][6]. The dividend has been cut repeatedly, from $6.00 in 2020 to $2.88 in 2025 split-adjusted, and Q3 distributable EPS of $0.71 fell short of both the $0.73 estimate and the $0.72 quarterly dividend [1][4]. ATM issuance of 12.4M shares in the quarter dilutes holders, a newer management team (1.7-year average tenure) is still settling in, and further Fed tightening would squeeze repo-funded spreads [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Agency MBS spread tightening after Q3 2026's late-quarter widening, which would rebuild book value from $14.55 [1]
  2. A stable or lower repo funding curve if the Fed pauses after its first hike since 2023, improving distributable-earnings coverage of the $0.24 monthly dividend [1]
  3. Accretive capital deployment if ATM issuance and buybacks are timed against the stock's discount or premium to book value [1]
Key Risks
  1. Further spread widening or rate volatility at roughly 9.6x leverage, which could cause another double-digit book-value loss like Q3 2026's 17% decline [1][6]
  2. Another dividend cut: Q3 distributable EPS of $0.71 already trails the $0.72 quarterly dividend, and the payout has fallen more than 50% since 2020 [1][4]
  3. Dilution from continued at-the-market equity issuance (12.4M shares, $205.7M in Q3 2026) if shares trade below book value [1]
