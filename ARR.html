Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Mortgage ·NYSE ·United States
ARMOUR Residential REIT, Inc. invests in residential mortgage-backed securities (MBS) in the United States, primarily securities issued or guaranteed by U.S. Government-sponsored entities (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) and Ginnie Mae, backed by fixed rate, hybrid adjustable rate, and adjustable-rate home loans. Externally managed by ARMOUR Capital Management LP. Q2 2026 delivered GAAP net income of $111.5M ($0.86/share), distributable earnings of $93.2M ($0.72/share), and net interest income of $76.8M. Book value per share was $17.53 at June 30, 2026. Market cap approximately $2.1B. Shares outstanding grew 67.7% over the past year through equity issuance.

Investment Thesis
ARMOUR Residential REIT delivers a compelling 17.74% dividend yield paid monthly ($0.24/share), backed by a portfolio of agency MBS guaranteed by the U.S. government (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Ginnie Mae), meaning zero credit risk on the underlying securities [1][4]. Q2 2026 marked a strong recovery with GAAP net income of $111.5M ($0.86/share), distributable earnings of $93.2M ($0.72/share), and book value per share of $17.53 — a meaningful improvement from Q1's GAAP loss [1]. ROE improved to 20.33%, profit margin stands at 87.82%, and the stock trades at a 6.6% discount to book value ($16.38 vs $17.53), offering potential valuation upside. The business is structurally immune to AI disruption (AI Resilience score 8), and the ALIGNED faith alignment score (Inspire Impact +43) adds a values dimension for faith-based investors [5]. Forward PE of 5.63 reflects deep value pricing for a high-yield income vehicle.
The bear case centers on dividend sustainability, structural dilution, and interest rate sensitivity. Distributable earnings of $0.72/share in Q2 exactly matched the quarterly dividend of $0.72 ($0.24/month × 3), leaving zero margin of safety — any earnings deterioration would immediately threaten the distribution [1]. The 67.7% growth in shares outstanding over the past year through aggressive equity issuance massively dilutes per-share value, and the 5-year dividend CAGR of -10.27% reflects serial cuts from over $1.00/month to the current $0.24/month [4]. The 757% D/E ratio inherent to the leveraged mREIT model amplifies interest rate sensitivity — an unexpected rate spike or mortgage spread dislocation similar to 2022-2023 could again produce negative revenue and significant book value erosion, with a 5% decline in MBS portfolio value potentially wiping out ~40% of equity [4]. Insiders have been net sellers, with two officers disposing of 87-95% of their direct holdings at prices above the current level [6]. The externally managed structure creates inherent agency conflicts, with management fees based on portfolio size (incentivizing dilutive equity issuance) rather than per-share value creation.
