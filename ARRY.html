Company Profile
Industrials ·Solar Equipment & Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Array Technologies, Inc. is a leading manufacturer of ground-mounting systems used in solar energy projects, including single-axis tracker systems that optimize energy production by automatically adjusting panel orientation. The company surpassed 100 GW in cumulative tracker deliveries across 30+ countries and is evolving from a pure-play tracker company into a technically integrated energy infrastructure platform through five new product launches spanning foundations, wire management, controls, software, and AI. Array reported Q2 2026 revenue of $342M and holds a record $2.5B orderbook (+37% YoY).

Investment Thesis
Array Technologies is executing an operational recovery with Q2 2026 revenue of $342M (+53% sequentially) and a record $2.5B orderbook (+37% YoY) providing 6.9 quarters of revenue visibility at the FY2026 guidance midpoint [1][3]. The company reaffirmed revenue guidance of $1.4-1.5B and raised adjusted EPS to $0.68-0.75, representing a clear path from GAAP losses to adjusted profitability. At just 6.7x forward adjusted earnings, Array is one of the cheapest ways to play the secular solar demand cycle driven by AI data center buildout, IRA incentives, and global decarbonization [5]. The strategic evolution into five new product lines (foundations, wire management, controls, software, AI) with the hire of First Solar's Charlie Wickersham as SVP Technology positions Array for higher-margin, more diversified revenue. The 100 GW installation milestone validates market leadership.
The bear case centers on balance sheet strain, GAAP unprofitability, and execution risk. D/E of 285% is near the 300% floor, reflecting acquisition-related debt from APA Solar that limits financial flexibility — any delay in the earnings recovery could pressure debt service [5]. GAAP profit margin of -7.25% and ROE of -22.65% mean the company is still losing money on a reported basis, with adjusted metrics relying on add-backs that may not be sustainable [5]. The stock has declined from ~$12 to ~$5 over the past year despite improving fundamentals, suggesting the market questions the quality of earnings. Management instability with 1.9-year average tenure and net insider selling of $1.6M raises execution concerns during a period requiring operational excellence. IRA incentive policy uncertainty under potential political changes could reduce domestic solar installations, and commodity steel price volatility directly impacts gross margins. The $755M market cap on $1.45B guided revenue (0.5x P/S) prices in significant uncertainty about the transition to sustained profitability.
