Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Artiva Biotherapeutics is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing natural killer (NK) cell-based therapies for patients with autoimmune diseases and cancers. Lead program is AlloNK (AB-101), an allogeneic, off-the-shelf, non-genetically modified, cryopreserved NK cell therapy candidate. The company has reached an inflection point with positive clinical data across multiple autoimmune indications and FDA alignment on a single Phase 3 registrational trial design in refractory rheumatoid arthritis [1]. Founded in 2019 by Thomas J. Farrell, headquartered in San Diego, CA. Market cap approximately $433M with $86.8M cash (Q1 2026) providing runway into Q2 2027 [2].

Investment Thesis
Artiva Biotherapeutics stands at a clinical inflection point with AlloNK, its off-the-shelf NK cell therapy, advancing toward a Phase 3 registrational trial in refractory rheumatoid arthritis with FDA alignment on trial design [1]. The autoimmune cell therapy market is nascent but potentially transformative — RA alone affects 1.3M Americans, and AlloNK's allogeneic, non-genetically modified approach addresses the scalability and cost limitations that have constrained autologous CAR-T therapies. At $8.91 per share with $86.8M cash ($3.93/share net cash), the market is pricing in significant clinical risk while offering option value on a Phase 3-ready asset in a rapidly growing therapeutic category [2]. Insider buying from 10% shareholder GC Corp ($16.5M) and RA Capital ($7M+) signals sophisticated biotech investor conviction [4]. Analyst price targets of $23-$41 suggest 160-360% upside if clinical execution continues.
The bear case is dominated by clinical and financial execution risk. Artiva has zero revenue, an accumulated deficit of $354.1M, and $86.8M cash providing runway only into Q2 2027 — insufficient to fund a full Phase 3 trial without additional capital raises that will dilute shareholders [2]. The cash burn rate increased 43% year-over-year, accelerating the financing timeline. Phase 3 trials in autoimmune diseases are expensive ($100M+) and carry substantial failure risk — even with FDA alignment on trial design, clinical outcomes are uncertain and competitor NK cell and CAR-T programs could reach market first. CEO Fred Aslan selling $244K in shares while the company pursues transformative clinical milestones sends a mixed insider signal [4]. The stock's 52-week range of $1.47 to $14.53 reflects extreme volatility inherent in clinical-stage biotechs. Infrastructure scores are structurally depressed because pre-revenue biotechs have no earnings, revenue, or margins for the framework to evaluate — the WATCH rating is driven entirely by Innovation and AI Resilience scores, which cannot capture the binary clinical risk/reward profile that defines ARTV's investment case [2][5].

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): PROTECTED. Cell therapy development and manufacturing involve physical-world biological processes — isolating NK cells from donor cord blood, expanding them in bioreactors, cryopreserving them, and administering them to patients — that AI cannot substitute or replace [1]. Clinical trials require physical patient enrollment, dosing, monitoring, and regulatory compliance that are structurally immune to AI disruption. The FDA approval pathway involves complex biological product review that AI cannot automate. Artiva's competitive moat lies in its proprietary manufacturing platform for producing off-the-shelf NK cells at scale, which is a physical manufacturing capability. However, AI-powered drug discovery platforms could accelerate competitor development timelines, and AI-driven target identification could shift the competitive landscape in cell therapy. Analysts are not flagging AI as a risk for cell therapy companies. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI revenue. Artiva's therapeutic platform is biologically derived. While AI tools may be used internally for data analysis in clinical trials, the company does not generate revenue from AI products.
