Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment ·NASDAQ ·Netherlands
ASML Holding N.V. is the world's sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, the critical technology enabling the most advanced semiconductor chips at 5nm and below. The company also produces deep ultraviolet (DUV) lithography systems and metrology and inspection tools. ASML serves every major chipmaker globally, including TSMC, Samsung, Intel, and SK Hynix. With a monopoly on EUV technology and the emerging High-NA EUV platform, ASML is the fundamental enabler of Moore's Law continuation and the AI computing revolution. Q2 FY2026 revenue of approximately $10.65B (+21.3% YoY), FY2026 guidance raised to EUR43-45B revenue with 54-56% gross margin [1]. Founded in 1984, headquartered in Veldhoven, Netherlands.
44,000 Employees ·www.asml.com

Just Cause

Just Cause (3/3): ASML's purpose — enabling the advancement of semiconductor technology through lithography innovation — has been validated by 40 years of consistent execution [4]. The company spent over 20 years and billions of euros developing EUV lithography when many customers and industry observers doubted it would ever work. ASML persisted because it believed the semiconductor industry needed EUV to continue advancing. This purpose survived multiple leadership transitions (from Arthur del Prado through Peter Wennink to Christophe Fouquet) and the 2008 financial crisis. Strategic decisions — investing in High-NA EUV, expanding capacity by 30% annually, maintaining the open innovation model with Zeiss and TRUMPF — all trace directly to this purpose [1]. The purpose IS the competitive advantage: ASML's monopoly exists because no other company was willing to invest for 20 years without returns.

The transition from DUV to EUV was particularly dramatic, requiring entirely new light sources, optics, and manufacturing processes. Investment Thesis
ASML delivered Q2 2026 revenue of approximately $10.65B (+21.3% YoY) with EPS of $8.67, beating consensus for the fourth consecutive quarter, driven by Installed Base Management revenue of $3.15B as customers accelerated AI-related upgrades [1]. The company raised its FY2026 guidance for the second time to EUR43-45B revenue with 54-56% gross margin, explicitly attributing the raise to surging AI chip demand [1][3]. ASML holds a 100% monopoly on EUV lithography — the essential technology for manufacturing every advanced AI chip — with a backlog of approximately EUR38.8B, close to fully covered for 2027, and planning 30% annual EUV capacity increases [1]. Financial metrics are outstanding: 9% D/E (net cash of $6.38B), 53.94% ROE, 30.11% profit margin, ~25% YoY revenue growth, and ~22% 5-year revenue CAGR [5]. At $1,698 with a Forward P/E of 30.27, the valuation has compressed from 31.47x in July, while analyst consensus targets $2,159.50 (+27% upside) [5]. Memory revenue growing 75% in 2026 (driven by HBM for AI training) opens a large new market segment. The Inspire score of -49 (MISALIGNED) is a consideration for faith-aligned portfolios.
The bear case centers on valuation, cyclicality, and geopolitical risk. At 30.27x forward earnings and a PEG of 2.35, ASML is priced for near-perfect execution with limited margin of safety [5]. The semiconductor equipment industry is historically cyclical, and the current AI-driven supercycle could moderate if hyperscaler capital spending plateaus or chip overcapacity emerges in 2027-2028. Geopolitical risk has intensified: U.S. lawmakers introduced the MATCH Act proposing to ban DUV immersion lithography exports AND servicing of existing equipment in China, which would materially impact the approximately 20% of net sales ASML derives from China in 2026 [6]. ASML denied allegations that it breached EUV export controls regarding China, but the political environment is increasingly hostile to any ASML-China engagement. Former ASML engineers in China have reportedly partially reverse-engineered EUV technology, and Substrate, a $1B+ Peter Thiel-backed startup, is attempting to build rival lithography — though CEO Fouquet dismisses both threats [4][6]. ASML's 30%+ annual capacity expansion plans require significant capital investment and could create oversupply risk if demand decelerates. The stock has declined 6.4% from July's $1,815 to $1,698, reflecting these concerns.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued AI chip demand surge driving additional orders for 2027-2028, with ASML potentially raising guidance again as the EUR38.8B backlog continues to expand — management describes order intake as 'very strong' even after stopping quarterly bookings disclosure [1]
  2. High-NA EUV (EXE:5200) adoption accelerating as chipmakers move to 2nm and below process nodes, creating a higher-ASP product cycle ($350M+ per machine) that expands ASML's revenue per system delivered [1]
  3. Memory revenue growing 75% in 2026 as HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) for AI training requires EUV lithography, with DRAM makers TSMC, Samsung, and SK Hynix investing heavily in capacity — opening a large new revenue segment [3]
Key Risks
  1. Forward PEG of 2.35 and P/E of 30.27x price in substantial future growth with limited margin of safety — any deceleration in AI chip demand, hyperscaler capex reduction, or guidance miss could trigger significant multiple compression from the $659B market cap [5]
  2. Escalating U.S.-China technology export controls — the proposed MATCH Act would ban DUV immersion lithography exports AND servicing of existing equipment in China, potentially eliminating the approximately 20% of net sales ASML derives from Chinese customers in 2026 [6]
  3. Semiconductor equipment cyclicality — the AI-driven supercycle could moderate if overcapacity emerges or if the planned 30% annual EUV capacity increases create supply/demand imbalances in 2027-2028 [1]
