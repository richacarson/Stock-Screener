Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment ·NASDAQ ·Netherlands
ASML Holding N.V. is the world's sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, the critical technology enabling the most advanced semiconductor chips at 5nm and below. The company also produces deep ultraviolet (DUV) lithography systems and metrology and inspection tools. ASML serves every major chipmaker globally, including TSMC, Samsung, Intel, and SK Hynix. With a monopoly on EUV technology and the emerging High-NA EUV platform, ASML is the fundamental enabler of Moore's Law continuation and the AI computing revolution. 2026 revenue guidance raised twice to EUR43-45 billion. Founded in 1984, headquartered in Veldhoven, Netherlands.
44,000 Employees ·www.asml.com

The 40-year commitment to advancing lithography technology -- including the 20+ year, multi-billion-dollar development of EUV when the industry doubted it would ever work -- exemplifies playing to keep playing rather than playing to win this quarter. CEO Christophe Fouquet maintains the culture of collaboration, direct communication, and customer-first decision-making that has characterized ASML since its founding. The company's monopoly was built not through acquisition or market manipulation but through patient, persistent R&D investment in the hardest engineering problems in the world. Strategic decisions consistently trace back to the purpose of enabling semiconductor innovation.
Just Cause
10
Just Cause (3/3): ASML's purpose -- enabling the advancement of semiconductor technology through lithography innovation -- has been validated by 40 years of consistent execution [4][5]. The company spent over 20 years and billions of euros developing EUV lithography when many in the industry, including some customers, doubted it would ever work. The company persisted because it believed the semiconductor industry needed EUV to continue advancing. This purpose survived multiple leadership transitions (from Arthur del Prado through Peter Wennink to Christophe Fouquet) and the 2008 financial crisis. Strategic decisions -- investing in High-NA EUV, expanding capacity by 30% per year, maintaining the open innovation model with partners like Zeiss and TRUMPF -- all trace directly to this purpose [1][2]. The purpose IS the competitive advantage: ASML's monopoly exists because no other company was willing to invest for 20 years without returns.

Existential Flexibility

Existential Flexibility (1/2): ASML has evolved its technology platform significantly over 40 years -- from contact printers to steppers to scanners to DUV to EUV to High-NA EUV [1][2]. Each generation required substantial R&D investment and represented a meaningful technical evolution. The transition from DUV to EUV was particularly dramatic, requiring entirely new light sources, optics, and manufacturing processes. However, ASML has always been a lithography company -- the evolution, while technically transformative, has been along a consistent strategic path rather than a complete business model reinvention in the Sinek sense.

Investment Thesis
ASML reported EUR9.3 billion in Q2 2026 revenue and EUR2.9 billion in net income, raising its 2026 guidance for the second time to EUR43-45 billion in revenue with 54-56% gross margin [1]. The company holds a 100% monopoly on EUV lithography, the essential technology for manufacturing every advanced AI chip. Order momentum is 'extremely strong,' with ASML close to fully covered for 2027 Low-NA EUV and planning 30% capacity increases for both 2027 and potentially 2028 [1][2]. The backlog of approximately EUR38.8 billion provides extraordinary revenue visibility. Financial metrics are outstanding: 13% D/E, 53.94% ROE, 30.11% profit margin, 31% YoY revenue growth, and 21.74% 5-year revenue CAGR [7]. AI chip demand -- with memory revenue growing 75% in 2026 -- is driving a structural expansion in ASML's addressable market that shows no signs of slowing [1][3].
The bear case centers on valuation and cyclicality. At 31.47x forward earnings and a PEG of 2.54, ASML is priced for near-perfect execution with minimal margin of safety [6][7]. The semiconductor equipment industry is historically cyclical, and the current AI-driven supercycle could cool if hyperscaler capital spending moderates or chip overcapacity emerges. ASML's trailing P/E of 55.68 and forward P/E of 31.47 imply that the market is pricing in substantial 2027+ earnings growth that may not fully materialize if the AI buildout plateaus. Geopolitical risk is significant: US-China technology export controls affect ASML's ability to sell advanced systems to Chinese customers, and any escalation could reduce the total addressable market. ASML's capacity expansion plans (30%+ annually) require significant capital investment and could create oversupply risk if demand decelerates. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may concern faith-aligned investors [3][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued AI chip demand surge driving additional orders for 2027 and 2028, with ASML potentially raising guidance again as backlog continues to expand beyond EUR38.8 billion
  2. High-NA EUV adoption accelerating as chipmakers move to 2nm and below process nodes, creating a higher-ASP product cycle that expands ASML's revenue per system
  3. Memory revenue growing 75% in 2026 as HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) for AI training requires EUV lithography, opening a large new market segment for ASML's systems
Key Risks
  1. Forward PEG of 2.54 and forward P/E of 31.47 price in substantial future growth with minimal margin of safety -- any deceleration in AI chip demand or guidance miss could trigger significant multiple compression
  2. Semiconductor equipment is historically cyclical, and the current AI-driven supercycle could moderate if hyperscaler capital spending peaks or chip overcapacity emerges in 2027-2028
  3. US-China technology export controls restrict ASML's ability to sell advanced EUV systems to Chinese customers, and any escalation of geopolitical tensions could materially reduce the addressable market
