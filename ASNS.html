Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·OTC ·United States
Actelis Networks designs, develops, manufactures, and markets cyber-hardened and hybrid fiber networking solutions for IoT and telecommunication applications. The company serves governmental agencies and commercial customers in North America, Europe, the Middle East, and Asia Pacific with products for intelligent traffic systems, utilities, rail, telecom, defense, and campus networking. Founded in 1998 by CEO Tuvia Barlev and headquartered in Sunnyvale, California, the company was delisted from Nasdaq in April 2026 for failing minimum bid price requirements and now trades on OTC markets. H1 2026 revenue was $2.1 million (+26% YoY) with improving gross margins of 51%, and the company announced a binding term sheet to acquire Israel-based Exaware Routing Ltd. to enter the AI data center networking market. Market cap approximately $1.3 million.

Investment Thesis
Actelis Networks shows early signs of revenue recovery — H1 2026 revenue of $2.1M grew 26% YoY with Q2 gross margin expanding to 51% from 32%, driven by an improving software and services mix [1]. The planned Exaware Routing acquisition (binding term sheet March 2026) could position the company in the high-growth AI data center networking market with high-throughput routing and open networking platforms [2]. The MetaShield AI cybersecurity SaaS platform addresses the growing critical infrastructure protection market, and the company's cyber-hardened networking solutions serve government/defense customers with regulatory barriers to entry. With D/E of only 20.4% and manageable debt of $979K, the balance sheet is clean relative to revenue [4].
The bear case is severe and existential. Actelis was delisted from Nasdaq in April 2026 for failing minimum bid price requirements, now trading at $0.09 on OTC markets with a $1.3M market cap — a distress-level valuation for a 28-year-old company that has never achieved sustainable profitability [3]. Revenue has collapsed from $8.8M (FY2022) to an annualized $4.2M despite multiple strategic pivots, and the company continues to lose multiples of its revenue annually. The 39-employee company competes against Cisco ($200B+ market cap), Juniper ($14B), and Nokia ($24B) — an existential mismatch in R&D resources, sales reach, and brand recognition. The Exaware acquisition financing is uncertain given the company's nano-cap status, and even if completed, integration risk for a 39-person company is substantial. OTC listing severely restricts institutional investor access, reduces liquidity, and may trigger forced selling by funds with exchange-listing requirements. CEO Barlev's 28-year tenure without achieving commercial scale raises fundamental questions about the company's ability to compete in any market.
