Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail ·NASDAQ ·United States
Academy Sports and Outdoors, Inc. is a Texas-based sporting goods, outdoor recreation, and apparel retailer operating over 300 stores concentrated in the South and Southeast United States, led by CEO Steve Lawrence. The company reported fiscal 2025 (ended January 2026) sales growth of 2.0% alongside a 1.5% decline in comparable sales, opened 24 new stores, and guided fiscal 2026 net sales of $6.23-6.36 billion with comparable sales in a range of (1.0)% to 2.0% amid a soft discretionary spending environment.

Investment Thesis
Academy Sports trades at a deeply discounted 7.1x forward earnings and 0.60 PEG for a consistently profitable retailer generating 18.6% ROE, a valuation that appears to price in more distress than the fundamentals currently show [1][5]. Management continues to invest through the cycle -- opening 20-25 new stores in FY2026 and building out AI-driven personalization under CIO Sumit Anand -- while FY2025 EPS grew 17.3% even as comparable sales dipped, suggesting real operating leverage and cost discipline [1][2].
The bear case centers on stalled top-line growth and reputational/litigation overhang: comparable sales fell 1.5% in FY2025 and FY2026 guidance spans a wide (1.0)% to 2.0% range, reflecting genuine uncertainty about discretionary consumer spending on sporting goods and outdoor gear [1]. Academy's continued sale of firearms and ammunition carries ongoing litigation and reputational risk, illustrated by the high-profile Sutherland Springs church shooting lawsuits that were ultimately dismissed only on statutory retailer-immunity grounds rather than on the merits of the sale itself [4]. The cheap valuation may therefore reflect the market's structural discount for a low-growth, litigation-exposed specialty retailer rather than a clear mispricing, despite a strong Inspire Impact Score of 54 (ALIGNED) on general labor, sustainability, and affordability practices [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. A return to positive comparable sales growth, even modestly, would validate management's view that FY2025's 1.5% comp decline was cyclical rather than structural [1]
  2. Continued new-store expansion (20-25 planned in FY2026) into new or underpenetrated Sun Belt markets could re-accelerate total revenue growth even if comps remain soft [1]
  3. Scaling of the CIO-led AI personalization initiative could improve digital conversion and defend market share against larger e-commerce-driven competitors [2]
Key Risks
  1. Comparable sales have been negative to flat for multiple periods, and FY2026 guidance of (1.0)% to 2.0% shows management itself is uncertain about a near-term inflection in consumer demand [1]
  2. Ongoing sale of firearms and ammunition exposes Academy to recurring litigation and reputational risk, as demonstrated by the Sutherland Springs shooting lawsuits, even where courts have ultimately ruled in the retailer's favor on statutory grounds [4]
  3. As a physical, regional big-box retailer, Academy remains exposed to AI-driven price transparency and e-commerce competition from larger-scale players like Amazon and Dick's Sporting Goods that can out-invest in personalization technology [1][2]
