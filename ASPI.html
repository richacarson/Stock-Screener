Company Profile
Materials ·Specialty Chemicals / Nuclear Materials ·NASDAQ ·United States
ASP Isotopes is a development-stage advanced materials company focused on producing and commercializing enriched isotopes for nuclear medicine, healthcare, quantum computing, and clean energy using its proprietary Aerodynamic Separation Process (ASP) technology and Quantum Enrichment technology for HALEU (High Assay Low Enriched Uranium) nuclear fuel. The company restarted the first 18 stages of its Silicon-28 enrichment facility targeting Q3 2026 commercial shipments. H1 2026 revenue rose to $9.3M from $2.3M a year earlier, driven by specialist isotopes, radiopharmacy operations, and helium/LNG sales from the Renergen acquisition. Founder Paul Mann resumed the CEO role in January 2026 after a medical leave.

Investment Thesis
ASP Isotopes at a $613M market cap is a speculative play on the convergence of nuclear energy revival, quantum computing advancement, and medical isotope demand — three secular growth trends that could transform ASPI from a development-stage company into a major advanced materials producer. H1 2026 revenue of $9.3M (up 304% from $2.3M) demonstrates early commercial traction across specialist isotopes, radiopharmacy, and Renergen helium/LNG [1]. The Silicon-28 enrichment facility restart targeting Q3 2026 commercial shipments addresses a critical bottleneck for quantum computing — high-purity Si-28 wafers are essential for silicon-based qubits and supply is severely constrained. The $22M TerraPower loan for HALEU production and collaboration with South African Nuclear Energy Corporation position ASPI in the emerging advanced nuclear fuel supply chain as governments push for energy independence [3]. Analyst projections of breakeven by 2028 with $64.3M profit suggest a visible path to profitability if commercialization executes on schedule.
The bear case is development-stage execution risk and cash burn at a scale that dwarfs current revenue. ASPI lost $60.7M in H1 2026 on only $9.3M of revenue — the company burns ~$10 for every $1 it earns — and the path to profitability by 2028 requires successful commercialization of multiple unproven technologies simultaneously (ASP enrichment, Quantum Enrichment, Si-28 production) [1]. D/E of 98.09% and the company's reliance on equity dilution and project financing to fund operations create capital structure risk. The Fuzzy Panda Research short-seller report raised questions about the company's technology claims and governance, and while ASPI responded, the SEC scrutiny that typically follows such reports remains a risk [4]. CEO Paul Mann's medical leave and return, coupled with insider sales of 81,076 shares ($692K), introduce leadership stability concerns [2][5]. Nuclear regulatory approvals for HALEU production are uncertain and could face delays from NRC licensing, international safeguards requirements, or political opposition. The stock's decline from a 52-week high of $14.49 to $4.00 (-72%) reflects the market's skepticism about execution timelines.
