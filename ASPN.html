Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Chemicals — Aerogel Insulation ·NYSE ·United States
Aspen Aerogels designs, develops, manufactures, and sells aerogel-based insulation products for energy infrastructure and electric vehicle thermal barrier applications. The company operates through two segments: Energy Industrial (petrochemical, LNG, and power generation insulation) and Thermal Barrier (PyroThin products for EV battery thermal runaway protection). Q2 2026 revenue of $49.8M (+32% QoQ) shows accelerating recovery from the Q4 2025 facility incident, with thermal barrier revenue surging 81% sequentially to $29.5M and European revenue guidance raised to $20M-$30M for 2026 [1]. Founded in 2001, headquartered in Northborough, Massachusetts.

Investment Thesis
Aspen Aerogels is the undisputed global leader in commercial aerogel insulation technology, and Q2 2026 results demonstrate the recovery is accelerating — revenue of $49.8M (+32% QoQ), thermal barrier surging 81% sequentially to $29.5M, and European revenue guidance raised to $20M-$30M for 2026 [1]. Q3 guidance of $65M-$80M implies annualized revenue approaching $280M+, with management targeting EBITDA breakeven in H2 2026. The company's monopoly-like market position in aerogel thermal barriers — protected by proprietary manufacturing processes, an extensive patent portfolio, and years-long OEM qualification cycles — provides structural competitive advantage that the temporary production disruption has not diminished [3]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~21% reflects the secular growth trajectory. D/E of 49.4% and adequate cash reserves provide financial durability to fund the recovery. Energy Industrial targeting 20% growth and data center/grid qualification wins provide diversification beyond the EV segment.
The bear case remains substantial: FY2025 revenue of $271.1M represented a -40.1% collapse from FY2024's $452.7M peak, and the company generated deep losses with ROE of -75.1% and profit margin of approximately -86% on a trailing basis [2]. Even with Q2 2026 recovery, annualized revenue is still well below the pre-incident run rate, and the path from EBITDA breakeven to positive net income is uncertain. Single-site manufacturing concentration at East Providence remains unresolved — the Q4 2025 incident demonstrated that a single operational disruption can eliminate half the company's revenue and generate hundreds of millions in losses. The stock has declined ~90% from 2024 highs to $5.85, and at ~$450M market cap with no near-term path to positive earnings, the equity requires a leap of faith in management's ability to restore production. EV market deceleration risk is real — if global adoption slows or OEMs delay platform launches, thermal barrier demand could plateau. Glassdoor's 2.9/5.0 rating raises organizational health concerns [5]. The Inspire Impact Score of 59 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors but does not offset the financial recovery risks.
