Strive, Inc. (formerly Asset Entities) is a publicly traded Bitcoin treasury and asset management company formed through the September 2025 reverse merger of Strive Enterprises (founded by Vivek Ramaswamy in 2022) with Asset Entities. The company manages approximately $2B in AUM across anti-ESG ETFs and direct indexing, while accumulating Bitcoin through up to $1.5B in PIPE/warrant proceeds. Led by CEO Matt Cole (formerly 15 years at CalPERS), the company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $2.94M with EPS of -$3.65 and trailing 12-month losses of $697.96M. Market cap approximately $1.8B.

Investment Thesis
Strive occupies a unique niche as the first publicly traded asset management Bitcoin treasury company, combining approximately $2B AUM in anti-ESG ETFs with a Bitcoin accumulation strategy funded by $750M+ in PIPE proceeds [2]. The stock has risen 71% from $11.55 to $19.73 since the prior screen, reflecting Bitcoin price appreciation flowing through to the treasury position. CEO Matt Cole's 15-year CalPERS institutional background provides credibility, TD Cowen initiated with a Buy rating and $26 target [3], and the anti-ESG/pro-Bitcoin positioning aligns with current political tailwinds. The minimal D/E of 1% means the balance sheet is essentially unlevered, providing downside protection relative to leveraged Bitcoin proxy peers [4].
The bear case is severe and structural: trailing 12-month losses of $697.96M on $7.03M revenue make the $1.8B market cap almost entirely a function of Bitcoin holdings and speculative sentiment rather than fundamental business value [4]. The -591% profit margin and -125% ROE indicate no viable path to operating profitability from $7M annual asset management revenue alone — the company is functionally a Bitcoin ETF with an expensive management layer. Q2 2026 EPS of -$3.65 dramatically missed the -$0.95 estimate, and the anti-ESG thesis is a cyclical political trade rather than a durable competitive advantage — if ESG sentiment shifts, the brand positioning becomes a liability. Vivek Ramaswamy's political career creates headline risk, the founder's departure and two strategic pivots in three years raise governance concerns, and the 28-person team has limited capacity to execute across asset management, Bitcoin treasury, and media operations simultaneously [2].
