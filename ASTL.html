Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Algoma Steel is a Canadian integrated steel producer headquartered in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, manufacturing flat-rolled sheet and plate steel products for automotive, construction, defense, energy, and infrastructure end-markets. Founded in 1901, the company is undergoing a transformative $750M+ conversion from blast furnace to Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) steelmaking, with initial EAF production expected Q3 2026. Revenue has declined sharply during the transition — Q2 2026 revenue fell 55% YoY to $267.5M — while the company carries $448M market cap and negative earnings under new CEO Rajat Marwah (effective January 2026).

Investment Thesis
Algoma Steel is a deep-value turnaround optionality play centered on the EAF transformation — if the $750M+ electric arc furnace ramps successfully to 3.7M tonnes of annual capacity in H2 2026-2027, the company would transform from a high-cost blast furnace operator to a competitive, low-carbon steel producer with structural cost advantages (scrap-based, lower energy, ~70% carbon reduction) [1]. Record plate sales in Q2 2026 demonstrate strong demand from defense, infrastructure, and construction end-markets where Section 232 tariffs protect North American producers. Adjusted EBITDA of $13.8M in Q2 2026 showed the company can generate positive cash flow even at trough production volumes during the transition. The $448M market cap implies the market assigns minimal value to the EAF optionality, creating significant upside if execution succeeds.
The bear case is existential. Algoma is burning cash at an alarming rate — revenue declined 55% YoY to $267.5M in Q2 2026, profit margin is -71.6%, and D/E of 368% reflects the massive debt burden from EAF construction financing that dwarfs the company's equity base [4]. The EAF has not yet produced commercial steel, and initial production (expected Q3 2026) faces execution risk including ramp-up timeline, product quality validation with automotive customers, and working capital requirements. Forward EPS is negative ($-0.30), indicating analysts expect continued losses. The new CEO Rajat Marwah started in January 2026 during the most critical phase of the transformation, creating leadership transition risk at the worst possible time [2]. U.S. tariffs on Canadian steel add cost pressure for Algoma's cross-border shipments, and steel price volatility could compress margins even post-EAF. A fatal workplace incident in 2023 with pending OHSA charges creates legal and reputational risk [3]. If the EAF ramp falters or steel prices decline, the debt burden could become unserviceable.
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful EAF initial steel production in Q3 2026 and ramp-up toward 3.7M tonne annual capacity, which would transform the cost structure and re-rate the stock as a competitive low-carbon producer [1]
  2. Record plate demand from defense and infrastructure spending (U.S. IIJA, defense budgets) providing premium pricing and margin support during the EAF transition period [1]
  3. Green steel premium pricing as the EAF enables ~70% carbon reduction, potentially commanding 5-15% price premiums from automotive and construction customers with decarbonization mandates [3]
Key Risks
  1. EAF execution risk — initial production delays, product quality issues with automotive customers, or ramp-up timeline extension could extend the cash-burn period beyond the company's financial capacity with D/E already at 368% [4]
  2. Liquidity and solvency risk as the company generates negative margins (-71.6%), has deeply negative ROE (-139%), and carries debt exceeding its market cap, with refinancing dependent on successful EAF commissioning [4]
  3. Steel price cyclicality — a decline in steel prices during the 12-18 month EAF ramp-up period could compress margins below breakeven, potentially triggering covenant violations or requiring dilutive equity raises [1]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Steel manufacturing is structurally immune to AI disruption. Algoma's core business — melting scrap and iron ore into liquid steel, casting slabs, and rolling them into sheet and plate products — involves high-temperature physical-world operations, heavy capital equipment, decades of metallurgical expertise, and regulatory compliance that AI tools cannot replicate or displace [4]. The end products (structural plate for bridges, armored plate for military, sheet for automotive) are essential physical materials with no digital substitute. AI may increase demand for steel through data center construction, renewable energy infrastructure (wind towers), and defense spending. Steel pricing is driven by commodity cycles, trade policy, and supply/demand fundamentals that are independent of AI substitution risk. Minor exposure exists through potential AI-driven optimization by competitors reducing production costs, but this is an efficiency differentiator, not a substitution threat.
