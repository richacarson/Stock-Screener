Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
AST SpaceMobile is building the world's first space-based cellular broadband network designed to connect directly to standard unmodified smartphones via satellite. Founded by Abel Avellan, a serial space entrepreneur and ITU/UNESCO Broadband Commission commissioner. Backed by strategic investors including AT&T, Google, Vodafone, American Tower, and Rakuten. Q2 2026 revenue of $31.5M more than doubled Q1's $14.7M, with full-year 2026 guidance of $150-$200M. Targeting 45 satellites in orbit by early 2027 scaling to 90-100 for global coverage. Holds approximately $3.0B in cash and restricted cash. Market cap ~$29B.

Investment Thesis
AST SpaceMobile is building a first-of-its-kind space-based cellular broadband network with no direct competitor at comparable scale, backed by strategic partnerships with AT&T, Google, Vodafone, American Tower, and Rakuten [3]. Q2 2026 revenue of $31.5M more than doubled Q1, and full-year guidance of $150-$200M provides a trajectory toward meaningful scale [1]. The total addressable market is enormous — 5+ billion people lack adequate cellular coverage, and the company's direct-to-standard-smartphone technology eliminates the adoption barrier that has limited prior satellite phone services. With $3.0B in cash, the company has funded its expansion through the 45-satellite milestone targeted for early 2027, and the founder-CEO's $0 cash compensation signals deep alignment with long-term shareholders [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of 44 (ALIGNED) makes this a rare category-creating company accessible to faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on execution risk, capital intensity, and pre-profit valuation. Net loss widened to $230.9M in Q2 2026 (-$0.77/share) with operating margin of -440%, reflecting the massive cost of satellite production and launch ($610M in Q2 capex alone) against still-small revenue [1]. The $29B market cap values a company with $46M in TTM revenue at approximately 630x sales — an extraordinary premium that requires near-flawless execution to justify. The company's revenue is heavily milestone-dependent (government contracts, commercial gateway deliveries) and Q4-weighted, creating quarterly volatility risk [1][2]. The April 2024 shareholder class-action lawsuit alleging misleading satellite production timeline statements, along with the mid-2024 CFO departure, raise governance concerns [4]. Satellite technology risk persists — any launch failure, on-orbit malfunction, or spectrum interference issue could delay the entire constellation timeline. Competition from Starlink (SpaceX) and T-Mobile's satellite partnership could erode the first-mover advantage if direct-to-smartphone satellite becomes commoditized.
