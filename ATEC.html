Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
Alphatec Holdings (ATEC) is a spine-focused medical technology company offering an integrated procedural ecosystem: lateral and posterior approach systems (including PTP), implants and biologics, SafeOp neuromonitoring, EOS low-dose 3D imaging with AI-driven alignment measures, and the Valence navigation and robotics platform. FY2025 revenue was $764M (+25%). Q3 2026 revenue rose 13% to $221M with ~20% case growth, but management cut FY2026 revenue guidance to ~$862M (from $882M) on lower biologics attachment, weaker procedure volumes and an international supply constraint, and the stock fell roughly 36% to ~$7. Chairman and CEO Pat Miles has led the company since 2017.

Investment Thesis
ATEC is the fastest-growing scaled spine company, with revenue compounding ~39% annually from FY2020 to FY2025 and Q3 2026 case volumes still up ~20% [1][4]. The integrated ecosystem — PTP lateral access, SafeOp neuromonitoring, EOS imaging with AI-automated alignment, and Valence navigation/robotics with new FDA clearances for intraoperative alignment and the Contour 3D rod bender — creates surgeon switching costs that peers selling standalone implants lack [2]. Adjusted EBITDA rose 46% year-over-year to $38.3M in Q3 2026, showing operating leverage, and after a ~36% post-earnings drop the shares trade at ~$7.31, a forward P/E of 12.8 and a provider PEG of 0.48 [1][5]. The Inspire Impact Score of +48 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions supports faith-based suitability.
The bear case is that growth is getting more expensive and the balance sheet is thin. FY2026 revenue guidance was cut to ~$862M from $882M (and from ~$890M in January), with revenue per case down ~5% on lower biologics attachment, softer procedure volumes and an international supply constraint, and management declined to give 2027 guidance [1][2]. GAAP profit margin is still -10.3%, ROE is -308%, and D/E of 2,788% reflects equity nearly erased by cumulative losses, leaving limited cushion [5]. The CEO sold ~$2.2M of stock at ~$22 in December 2025, and a plaintiff firm opened a securities-claims investigation after Q1 2026 results — risks that could intensify after the Q3 miss [3][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Valence navigation/robotics commercial ramp following FDA clearances for intraoperative alignment and Contour 3D automated rod bending, increasing per-procedure revenue capture [2]
  2. EOS Insight adoption beyond the current 15-20% of the installed base, pulling through implant and biologics share with EOS-equipped surgeons [2]
  3. Continued adjusted EBITDA leverage (Q3 2026 +46% to $38.3M) moving the company toward sustained positive free cash flow [1]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue-per-case erosion (~-5% in Q3 2026) from lower biologics attachment and pricing, which drove the FY2026 guidance cut to ~$862M and a ~36% stock decline [1]
  2. Balance-sheet fragility: D/E of 2,788% and ROE of -308% leave little equity cushion if losses persist or refinancing is needed [5]
  3. Securities-litigation and credibility risk after repeated guidance reductions in 2026, compounded by CEO share sales [3][6]
