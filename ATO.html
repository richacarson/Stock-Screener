Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Gas ·NYSE ·United States
Atmos Energy Corporation is the largest fully regulated, pure-play natural gas distribution company in the United States, serving approximately 3.4 million residential, commercial, and industrial customers across eight states. Founded in 1906 and headquartered in Dallas, Texas, the company operates through Distribution and Pipeline & Storage segments, owning 76,000 miles of underground distribution mains and 5,700 miles of gas transmission lines. FY2026 YTD (nine months ending June 2026) EPS of $7.33 on $3.1B in capital expenditures with 85%+ focused on safety and reliability. Full-year FY2026 EPS guidance reaffirmed at $8.40-$8.50.
5,487 Employees ·www.atmosenergy.com

Investment Thesis
Atmos Energy at $164.74 with a $27.8B market cap is the premier pure-play regulated natural gas utility in the United States, serving 3.4 million customers across eight states with a 120-year operating history. FY2026 YTD EPS of $7.33 tracks toward reaffirmed full-year guidance of $8.40-$8.50, representing 8-10% EPS growth driven by rate base expansion from $3.1B in annual capital spending [1][2]. The company offers a 2.43% dividend yield with 38 consecutive years of payments, 40+ years of consecutive growth, and an 8.0% 5yr dividend growth CAGR — well above inflation [5]. D/E of 67.7% is conservative for a utility, profit margin of 28.5% reflects the regulated rate structure's profitability, and Forward P/E of 18.24 is reasonable for a defensive, AI-immune utility with predictable earnings growth [1][5].
The bear case centers on low growth, safety liabilities, and secular energy transition risk. Revenue grew just 4.8% YoY and the 3yr CAGR of 3.8% reflects the inherent growth ceiling of a regulated utility — Atmos cannot grow faster than its rate base and customer additions allow [1]. The NTSB investigation of the January 2024 Jackson, Mississippi gas explosions found that Atmos underrated known leaks, and a 2018 investigation documented dozens of home explosions across Texas since 2006, raising safety liability concerns and potential regulatory penalties [3]. Free cash flow is deeply negative at -$2.2B as capital spending ($3.1B YTD) dramatically exceeds operating cash flow ($2.0B), requiring continuous debt issuance to fund infrastructure investment [5]. The long-term electrification trend and municipal natural gas bans (though partially reversed) threaten demand for gas distribution infrastructure over a multi-decade horizon. ROE of 9.8% is below the cost of equity for most utility investors, reflecting regulatory lag in rate recovery.
Key Catalysts
  1. Rate base growth acceleration — $4.2B planned annual capital spending with 85%+ focused on safety and reliability drives predictable rate base expansion, supported by 13 pending regulatory filings seeking ~$600M in annualized operating income increases [1][2]
  2. AI data center energy demand — growing AI infrastructure requires significant electricity generation, much of it gas-fired, potentially increasing demand for Atmos's pipeline and storage operations, particularly in Texas and the Southeast [1]
  3. Dividend growth continuity — 40+ consecutive years of dividend increases with 8.0% 5yr CAGR and a 46.1% payout ratio provide substantial room for continued above-inflation dividend growth on expanding earnings [5]
Key Risks
  1. Pipeline safety liabilities — NTSB findings that Atmos underrated known leaks before the 2024 Jackson, Mississippi explosions, combined with a documented history of home explosions in Texas since 2006, expose the company to regulatory penalties, litigation costs, and reputational damage [3]
  2. Chronic negative free cash flow — capital spending of $3.1B YTD against operating cash flow of $2.0B creates a structural funding gap requiring continuous debt issuance, increasing leverage risk if interest rates remain elevated or regulatory cost recovery is delayed [5]
  3. Electrification and energy transition — long-term secular shift toward electric heating, municipal gas connection bans, and state-level climate legislation could erode gas distribution demand over a 10-20 year horizon, threatening the stranded asset risk for Atmos's 76,000 miles of gas mains
