Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
aTyr Pharma is a clinical-stage biotherapeutics company developing immunomodulatory medicines based on its proprietary tRNA synthetase biology platform. The lead candidate, efzofitimod, targets NRP2 to modulate immune responses in pulmonary sarcoidosis and systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease (SSc-ILD). The Phase 3 EFZO-FIT trial in pulmonary sarcoidosis failed its primary endpoint in September 2025, leading to an 83% stock decline and a securities class action lawsuit. The company plans a new global Phase 3 study with revised endpoints. Pipeline also includes ATYR0101 for fibrosis. Headquartered in San Diego, California.

Investment Thesis
aTyr Pharma holds $68.3M in cash (Q1 2026) against a $47M market cap, implying the market assigns negative value to the entire efzofitimod pipeline — a potential mispricing if the new Phase 3 design succeeds [1][5]. The company plans to submit a Phase 3 IND in June 2026 for a new global efzofitimod study in chronic, symptomatic pulmonary sarcoidosis using FVC as the primary endpoint, with KSQ-Lung as a key secondary endpoint at week 48 [1]. EFZO-CONNECT is completing enrollment in SSc-ILD, providing a second indication catalyst. The reduced Q1 operating expenses ($11.4M vs. $15.8M prior year) extend cash runway, and the tRNA synthetase biology platform (including preclinical ATYR0101 for fibrosis) provides pipeline diversification. With 9 analysts rating the stock a Buy with a $2.86 average price target — implying 496% upside from $0.48 — the risk/reward is asymmetric if the Phase 3 path materializes [5].
The bear case remains formidable. The Phase 3 EFZO-FIT trial definitively failed its primary endpoint in September 2025, triggering an 83% stock crash and a Hagens Berman securities class action lawsuit alleging materially false and misleading statements about efzofitimod's efficacy [4]. Daily dollar trading volume of $758,400 falls below the $1M liquidity floor, making institutional investment impractical and creating severe position-exit risk [5]. Cash of $68.3M declining at $12.6M/quarter gives approximately 5-6 quarters of runway before a dilutive capital raise becomes necessary at potentially catastrophic sub-dollar prices. The new Phase 3 design — using FVC instead of corticosteroid reduction as the primary endpoint — is unproven and may not satisfy the FDA. ROE of -108% and zero revenue confirm no path to profitability without drug approval, which historically occurs in fewer than 10% of Phase 3 re-attempts after initial failure. The securities litigation adds legal costs and management distraction to an already resource-constrained organization.
