Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·United States
Atlantic Union Bankshares is the holding company for Atlantic Union Bank, the largest regional bank headquartered in the lower Mid-Atlantic. Founded in 1902 and headquartered in Glen Allen, Virginia, the bank provides commercial and consumer banking, wealth management, treasury services, and mortgage lending through full-service branches across Virginia, Maryland, and North Carolina. Under CEO John Asbury (since 2016), the company completed a transformative acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp in 2024-2025, roughly doubling assets to over $35B and expanding its footprint into the Washington D.C. metro market. FY2025 revenue reached $1.23B (+60.6% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $3.44.

Investment Thesis
Atlantic Union Bankshares at 9.61x forward earnings offers a well-managed, acquisition-driven community bank at a reasonable valuation following the transformative Sandy Spring Bancorp integration. FY2025 revenue of $1.23B (+60.6%) and Q2 2026 net income of $158M demonstrate the earnings power of the combined franchise [1][2]. The bank is now the largest regional institution in the lower Mid-Atlantic with period-end loans of $27.7-28.0B, deposits of $30.8-31.0B, and a 2026 NIM target of 3.75-3.80%. The dividend yield of 3.82% with a 42.27% payout ratio provides income with room for continued dividend growth (6% 5-year CAGR). Integration cost savings from Sandy Spring should drive ROE improvement from the current 9.76% toward the bank's historical 11-13% range.
The bear case centers on acquisition integration risk, interest rate sensitivity, and limited growth visibility post-Sandy Spring. The headline EPS growth of 835% YoY is entirely due to depressed Q2 2025 earnings ($0.12 EPS) from integration charges, and normalized earnings growth is far more modest [2]. ROE of 9.76% is below the 15% target and below many regional bank peers, reflecting goodwill dilution from the acquisition. The stock has declined from its 52-week high of $43.62 to $38.78, suggesting the market is cautious about integration execution and the interest rate outlook. With period-end loans guided at $27.7-28.0B, organic loan growth (ex-acquisition) appears modest. Credit quality deterioration in the Washington D.C. office market — where Sandy Spring had significant CRE exposure — is a specific risk that could drive elevated provision expense. The Inspire Impact Score of 41 (ALIGNED) is favorable for faith-based investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sandy Spring integration cost savings — completion of core systems conversion and branch consolidation should drive efficiency ratio improvement toward the mid-40s target for 2026, expanding net income margins beyond the current 32% level [1]
  2. ROE recovery from 9.76% toward historical 11-13% range as acquisition-related goodwill dilution is offset by higher pre-provision net revenue from the combined $35B+ asset franchise [2]
  3. Net interest margin expansion — 2026 NIM guided at 3.75-3.80%, benefiting from the combined franchise's deposit base repricing and potential Fed rate cuts that would reduce deposit costs faster than asset yields decline [1]
Key Risks
  1. Commercial real estate credit quality deterioration — Sandy Spring's significant exposure to Washington D.C. metro office properties creates elevated provision risk as the office sector continues to face remote work structural headwinds, potentially requiring material reserve builds [1]
  2. Integration execution risk — merging two distinct banking cultures, technology platforms, and customer relationships is a multi-year process, and the 60.6% revenue jump masks whether organic growth in the legacy AUB franchise has stalled during management distraction [1][2]
  3. Interest rate sensitivity — as a net interest income-dependent bank (NII guided at $1.16-1.165B for 2026), AUB's earnings are directly exposed to yield curve movements, and a flattening or inverted curve would compress NIM below the 3.75-3.80% target [1]
