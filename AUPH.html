Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Aurinia Pharmaceuticals is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on autoimmune diseases. Lead product LUPKYNIS (voclosporin) is the first FDA-approved oral calcineurin inhibitor for active lupus nephritis. Pipeline includes aritinercept, a dual BAFF/APRIL inhibitor for autoimmune diseases. CEO Kevin Tang (since March 2026) took over from Peter Greenleaf following activist campaign. LUPKYNIS 2026 guidance: $305-315M net product sales. Headquartered in Edmonton, Canada with U.S. commercial operations. Market cap ~$2.2B.
128 Employees ·www.auriniapharma.com

Investment Thesis
Aurinia is executing a highly profitable single-product commercialization with LUPKYNIS generating $160.9M in H1 2026 revenue (up 21% YoY), Q2 net income of $37.4M, and a fortress balance sheet of $443M cash against $64M debt [1]. At a trailing P/E of 7.2 and forward P/E of 15.4, the stock trades at a steep discount to specialty pharma peers, reflecting single-product concentration risk. The 2026 guidance of $315-325M total revenue (11-15% growth) provides earnings visibility, while LUPKYNIS's extended patent exclusivity protects the franchise through the mid-2030s [3]. Aritinercept (dual BAFF/APRIL inhibitor) diversifies the pipeline beyond calcineurin inhibition. Operating cash flow of $175M TTM and $148M free cash flow support capital returns or business development. The Inspire Impact Score of 37 (ALIGNED) reflects positive marks across customer welfare, product access, and ethical practices.
The bear case centers on extreme single-product dependency, governance risk, and cultural deterioration. LUPKYNIS represents ~100% of revenue, and generic patent challenges could shorten exclusivity if courts rule unfavorably [3]. Roche's Gazyva (approved October 2025) and emerging CAR-T lupus therapies create competitive pressure that could erode market share gains. CEO Kevin Tang simultaneously serves as CEO, board chair, and controls ~30% of shares through Tang Capital — governance concentration that has driven Glassdoor ratings to 2.6/5, with employees describing a 'political' culture rewarding 'control rather than competence' [2][4]. The workforce was slashed 63% from ~350 to 128, raising questions about commercial execution capability at scale. Four of nine board directors failed majority shareholder support at the 2024 annual meeting, and the FDA controversy (lawsuit against ex-drug review chief) creates regulatory relationship risk [4]. The PEG ratio is unavailable, limiting growth-adjusted valuation assessment.
