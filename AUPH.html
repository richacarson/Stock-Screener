Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Aurinia Pharmaceuticals Inc. is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on autoimmune diseases. Its lead product LUPKYNIS (voclosporin) is the first FDA-approved oral therapy for active lupus nephritis in combination with background immunosuppressive therapy. Headquartered in Victoria, British Columbia, Canada, with U.S. commercial operations based in Rockville, Maryland.

Investment Thesis
Aurinia is executing a highly profitable single-product commercialization with LUPKYNIS generating $160.9M in H1 2026 revenue (up 21% YoY), net income of $71.8M (up 60%), and a fortress balance sheet of $443M cash against just $69M debt [1]. At a trailing P/E of 7.1 and forward P/E of 15.5, the stock trades at a steep discount to specialty pharma peers, reflecting the market's single-product concentration discount. The IgA nephropathy expansion (AUR101 Phase 3) could roughly double the addressable market, and 2026 guidance of $315-325M total revenue (up 11-15%) provides earnings visibility. The Tang-led management team has demonstrated ruthless capital discipline through the 2024 restructuring, converting Aurinia from a cash-burning biotech into a profitable commercial-stage pharma company.
The bear case centers on extreme single-product dependency and leadership risk. LUPKYNIS represents essentially 100% of revenue, and generic patent challenges filed in May 2025 could shorten exclusivity if courts rule unfavorably, though 30-month stays provide near-term protection [3]. Roche's Gazyva (approved October 2025) and emerging CAR-T therapies for lupus nephritis create competitive pressure that could erode market share gains [3]. Kevin Tang's consolidation of power — simultaneously serving as CEO, board chair, and controlling ~30% of shares through Tang Capital — creates governance concentration risk and has driven significant employee turnover, with Glassdoor reviews describing a deteriorating culture [2]. The halting of AUR200/AUR300 leaves no pipeline diversification beyond LUPKYNIS and the IgA nephropathy extension, creating binary risk on a narrow therapeutic franchise.
Key Catalysts
  1. IgA nephropathy (AUR101) Phase 3 data readout could significantly expand LUPKYNIS's addressable market beyond lupus nephritis, with regulatory filing expected in 2027
  2. 2026 revenue guidance of $315-325M with improving profitability supports continued margin expansion as LUPKYNIS scales toward peak sales, with H1 net margins already exceeding 44%
  3. Potential share repurchase program leveraging the $443M cash position and sub-$2B market cap could provide meaningful EPS accretion given the low trailing P/E of 7.1
Key Risks
  1. Generic patent challenges filed May 2025 with 30-month stays could shorten LUPKYNIS exclusivity if courts rule unfavorably, compressing the remaining patent-protected revenue window
  2. Roche's Gazyva and emerging CAR-T therapies create competitive pressure in lupus nephritis, potentially capping LUPKYNIS market share gains and peak sales estimates
  3. Kevin Tang's dual CEO/Chairman role and ~30% ownership creates governance concentration risk, while the resulting culture deterioration (Glassdoor 3.0 culture score) may impair commercial execution talent retention
