Investment Thesis
Aveanna Healthcare at 9.4x forward earnings offers compelling value in the secular home healthcare growth theme, with Q1 2026 revenue of $648M (up 15.9% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $84.4M (up 25.2%), leading management to raise FY2026 guidance to $2.56-2.58B revenue and $328-332M EBITDA [1]. Growth is broad-based: PDS +16.4%, HHH +17.4%, MS +7.4%. The preferred payer strategy is accelerating with four new agreements in Q1 bringing volume to 60%, and the Family First Homecare acquisition adds pediatric scale. The business is structurally immune to AI disruption — skilled nurses providing hands-on patient care cannot be automated. Aging demographics, Medicaid expansion, and the shift from institutional to home-based care create multi-year secular tailwinds [2].
The bear case centers on extreme leverage, Medicaid risk, and historical governance concerns. D/E of 776% reflects PE-backed origins with $1.51B total debt creating significant interest expense that constrains free cash flow and strategic flexibility [5]. Medicaid reimbursement risk is existential — CEO Shaner has warned proposed federal cuts would be 'detrimental,' and any rate reduction could severely impact the PDS segment serving Medicaid-eligible pediatric patients [2]. The company's history under PE ownership includes serious patient safety concerns (Bloomberg reported injury reports and seven child deaths linked to profit-maximization pressure) and multiple data breaches resulting in a $425K HIPAA settlement and ongoing class action litigation [4]. Truist lowered its price target to $5 while maintaining a Hold rating, signaling analyst caution [5]. Forward EPS of $0.70 vs. trailing $1.05 suggests FY2025 earnings included one-time items, and profit margin of 9.25% leaves little cushion for wage inflation.
