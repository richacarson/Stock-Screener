Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
Broadcom designs semiconductors and infrastructure software. Its Semiconductor Solutions segment supplies custom AI accelerators (XPUs) for hyperscalers, Tomahawk/Jericho Ethernet switching for AI clusters, plus wireless, broadband, and storage chips; Infrastructure Software is anchored by VMware Cloud Foundation, mainframe, and security software. Q3 FY2026 revenue grew 86% YoY to a record $29.6B, with AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B (+221%) now ~56% of the total. Led by CEO Hock Tan since 2006; headquartered in Palo Alto, California.

Investment Thesis
Broadcom is the clearest compounding story in AI infrastructure outside NVIDIA: Q3 FY2026 revenue rose 86% to $29.6B, AI semiconductor revenue grew 221% to $16.7B, Q4 AI revenue is guided to $21.7B, and management has outlined ~$115B of FY2027 and ~$230B of FY2028 AI revenue [1][2]. Despite that growth the stock trades at ~18x forward earnings and a 0.34 PEG, with a 42.9% profit margin, 44% ROE, deleveraging balance sheet, and a dividend that has grown ~12.6% annually for five years [8].
The bear case is concentration and durability: a handful of hyperscalers drive the AI business, and any capex digestion, in-sourcing of chip design, or share shift to rivals such as Marvell, MediaTek, or NVIDIA would hit a revenue base now ~56% AI-dependent [1]. AI growth is also dilutive to gross margin, and the VMware franchise faces escalating EU antitrust scrutiny and customer defections after aggressive licensing and partner-program changes [7]. Share price (~$354) is already well below the 50-day average (~$373), showing how sensitive sentiment is to AI-capex headlines [8].
Key Catalysts
  1. Delivery against the ~$58B FY2026 AI revenue target, including $21.7B in Q4, and conversion of record AI bookings (>$30B in a single quarter) into FY2027 shipments
  2. Ramp of additional custom-XPU customers and 2nm XPU/next-generation Tomahawk Ethernet switches supporting the ~$115B FY2027 AI revenue outlook
  3. Continued deleveraging from VMware debt plus double-digit dividend growth funded by record free cash flow
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration: a few hyperscalers account for most AI revenue, so capex pauses or in-house chip design could sharply cut growth
  2. EU antitrust action over the VMware Cloud Service Provider program termination (CISPE complaint, March 2026) and broader VMware customer attrition
  3. Gross-margin dilution as lower-margin AI systems become a majority of revenue, plus cultural and retention risk from aggressive integration practices
