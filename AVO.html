Company Profile

Mission Produce is a global leader in the sourcing, farming, packaging, marketing, and distribution of avocados, mangoes, and blueberries. The company operates through Marketing and Distribution, International Farming, and Blueberries segments. In May 2026, Mission completed its $430M acquisition of Calavo Growers, creating the dominant North American avocado platform with expanded prepared foods capabilities (guacamole, value-added products). Q2 FY2026 revenue was $290.9M (-24% YoY) as normalized avocado pricing offset 14% volume growth. Market cap approximately $950M.

Investment Thesis
Mission Produce is the world's dominant avocado platform following the transformative $430M Calavo Growers acquisition completed May 2026, consolidating North American avocado leadership and opening the prepared foods segment with $25M+ in expected annualized synergies [1][2]. The company trades at 22.74x forward earnings with a conservative pre-acquisition D/E of 32%, and vertically integrated farming operations in Peru, Colombia, and Mexico provide supply security and margin upside as trees mature. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~7% and 14% volume growth in Q2 FY2026 demonstrate underlying demand strength despite pricing headwinds. The business is structurally immune to AI disruption, and the Inspire Impact Score of 62 (ALIGNED) with 11 positive ESG attributions reflects responsible agricultural practices [3]. Global avocado consumption continues expanding in Europe and Asia, providing a secular tailwind.
The bear case centers on severe near-term revenue compression, integration risk, and thin margins. Avocado pricing normalized sharply from FY2025 peaks, driving Q1 and Q2 FY2026 revenue declines of -17% and -24% YoY respectively, with TTM revenue declining approximately -13% [1]. Profit margin of 1.91% is structurally thin for food distribution, and ROE of 5.69% indicates modest capital efficiency — these metrics will remain drags regardless of revenue trajectory [5]. The Calavo acquisition will materially increase leverage when reflected in Q3 FY2026 financials, adding balance sheet strain during a revenue downturn. Trailing P/E of 30.51x is elevated relative to depressed current earnings, and the forward P/E of 22.74x assumes meaningful earnings recovery. CEO John Pawlowski has been in the role only since April 2026, managing integration of the most complex acquisition in Mission's history with limited track record as CEO. Water scarcity in key growing regions and tariff uncertainty on Mexican avocado imports create structural risks.
Key Catalysts
  1. Calavo Growers integration delivering $25M+ in annualized synergies through combined sourcing, distribution, and overhead elimination, plus new prepared foods revenue stream from guacamole and value-added products [2]
  2. Avocado pricing recovery — current 24-36% price declines from FY2025 peaks reflect cyclically high Mexican supply; normalization would significantly leverage the combined company's expanded volume base with 14% organic volume growth [1]
  3. International farming operations in Peru and Colombia reaching maturity, providing owned-source fruit at better margins and reducing dependency on third-party sourcing, while European and Asian market expansion drives secular volume growth
Key Risks
  1. Commodity pricing exposure — avocado prices declined 36% YoY in Q2 FY2026 from higher Mexican supply, and Mission lacks pricing power in a commodity market; further price weakness would compress already-thin 1.91% profit margins to breakeven or below [1][5]
  2. Calavo integration execution risk — $430M acquisition during a revenue downturn materially increases leverage from the current D/E of 32%, and failure to achieve synergy targets would strain the balance sheet and delay margin improvement [2]
  3. Water scarcity and climate risk in key growing regions (Peru, Colombia, Mexico) could disrupt avocado supply; tariff uncertainty on Mexican fruit imports adds regulatory risk to the core supply chain

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Mission Produce's core business — growing, sourcing, ripening, packing, and distributing fresh avocados and other produce — is fundamentally physical and cannot be replicated or replaced by AI [1]. Agriculture requires land, water, climate, labor, and physical logistics infrastructure. Avocado ripening is a biological process requiring controlled environments and physical handling. The global supply chain from farms in Peru, Colombia, and Mexico to retailers in the U.S. involves customs, cold chain logistics, and perishable goods management that AI cannot substitute. No analysts have flagged AI as a disruption risk for fresh produce distribution. AI may modestly improve demand forecasting and supply chain optimization as efficiency gains, not business model threats. AI Opportunity (0/1): Mission Produce does not generate revenue from AI products or services. Any AI adoption would be for internal operational efficiency rather than revenue generation.
