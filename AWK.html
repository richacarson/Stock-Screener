Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities — Regulated Water ·NYSE ·United States
American Water Works is the largest regulated water and wastewater utility in the United States, serving approximately 3.6 million customer connections across 14 states. The company operates 80 surface water treatment plants, 520 groundwater treatment plants, 170 wastewater treatment plants, and 55,000 miles of transmission and distribution infrastructure. A pending merger with Essential Utilities will create a combined company serving 4.7 million water/wastewater connections and 740,000 gas connections across 17 states. Founded in 1886, headquartered in Camden, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
American Water Works is the gold standard of regulated water utilities — the largest in the US with 3.6M customer connections, a 140-year operating history, and one of the most defensible business models in the IOWN universe [1]. FY2026 EPS is projected at $6.08, reflecting 7.8% growth from FY2025's $5.64, with long-term 7-9% EPS and DPS growth targets anchored by $19-20B in 2026-2030 capital investment and regulated rate recovery mechanisms [1]. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.28B grew 11.1% YoY, showing acceleration [1]. The pending Essential Utilities merger ($40B combined enterprise value, Kentucky PSC approved April 2026, overwhelming shareholder approval February 2026) will create a 4.7M water/wastewater connection utility across 17 states plus 740K gas connections, accretive in year one [2]. The 2.51% dividend yield with 18 consecutive years of growth at 6.5% CAGR provides reliable income. AI data center buildout drives incremental water demand as a secular tailwind.
The bear case centers on valuation, leverage, and merger execution risk. At 22.0x forward earnings and PEG of 2.75, AWK is priced at a premium even for a high-quality utility [5]. D/E of 147% and $16B+ in total debt reflect the capital-intensive nature of water infrastructure — while standard for regulated utilities, rising interest rates increase cost of capital and could compress returns. ROE of 10.5% is below the 15% target, constrained by the regulatory earnings cap inherent to the utility model. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.01 missed estimates of $1.10 by 8%, and rising operating and depreciation costs compressed margins, with Q1 net income declining to $196M from $205M a year earlier [1]. The Essential Utilities merger faces regulatory approval risk across multiple state commissions — any conditions or delays could impact the timeline and accretion assumptions [2]. Negative free cash flow reflects heavy capital investment funded through debt and equity issuance, creating ongoing dilution risk.

  1. Essential Utilities merger (regulatory approvals progressing, Kentucky PSC approved April 2026) creating the dominant US water/wastewater/gas utility with 4.7M water and 740K gas connections, accretive in year one [2]

  3. Q1 2026 EPS miss ($1.01 vs $1.10 consensus) and margin compression from rising operating costs demonstrate that earnings growth is not automatic even with rate increases, and ROE of 10.5% remains below the 15% target for regulated utilities [1]

AI Risk Protection (9/9): American Water's business is structurally immune to AI disruption. The company operates physical water and wastewater infrastructure — treatment plants, pumping stations, pipelines, and dams — that AI cannot replicate or substitute [1]. Water provision is a regulated natural monopoly with government-granted franchise territories, creating an impenetrable competitive moat. AI data center buildouts actually increase demand for water (cooling), directly benefiting water utilities in service territories with data center growth. The aging US water infrastructure requires $1T+ in investment over the coming decades, further cementing the need for physical operators. Regulatory rate-setting mechanisms provide earnings visibility regardless of technological disruption in other sectors.
