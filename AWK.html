Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities — Regulated Water ·NYSE ·United States
American Water Works is the largest regulated water and wastewater utility in the United States, serving approximately 3.6 million customer connections across 14 states. The company operates 80 surface water treatment plants, 520 groundwater treatment plants, 170 wastewater treatment plants, and 55,000 miles of transmission and distribution infrastructure. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.61 (+8.1% YoY) beat estimates, and revenue of $1.36B (+6.2% YoY) was driven by authorized rate increases and acquisitions [1]. A pending merger with Essential Utilities will create a combined company serving 4.7 million water/wastewater connections and 740,000 gas connections across 17 states. Founded in 1886, headquartered in Camden, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
American Water Works is the gold standard of regulated water utilities — the largest in the US with 3.6M customer connections, a 140-year operating history, and one of the most defensible business models in the IOWN universe [1]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.61 (+8.1% YoY) beat consensus by 1.3%, with revenue of $1.36B (+6.2% YoY) driven by $216M in new authorized annualized revenues since January 2026 [1]. FY2026 EPS guidance of $6.02-$6.12 was affirmed, representing ~8% growth, with long-term 7-9% EPS and DPS growth targets anchored by $19-20B in 2026-2030 capital investment and regulated rate recovery mechanisms. The pending Essential Utilities merger ($40B combined enterprise value, Kentucky PSC approved April 2026) will create a 4.7M water/wastewater connection utility across 17 states plus 740K gas connections, accretive in year one with close expected by early 2027 [2]. The 2.82% dividend yield with 17 consecutive years of growth at 8.4% CAGR provides reliable and growing income. AI data center buildout drives incremental water demand as a secular tailwind.
The bear case centers on valuation, leverage, and merger execution risk. At 22.8x forward earnings and PEG of 4.76, AWK is priced at a substantial premium for a regulated utility growing EPS at 7-9% — any growth disappointment could trigger meaningful multiple compression [5]. D/E of 137% and $15.7B in total debt reflect the capital-intensive nature of water infrastructure — while standard for regulated utilities, rising interest rates increase the cost of capital and could compress returns. ROE of 10.1% is below the 15% target, constrained by the regulatory earnings cap inherent to the utility model. The Essential Utilities merger faces regulatory approval risk across multiple state commissions — any conditions, delays, or forced divestitures could impact the timeline and accretion assumptions [2]. The October 2024 cyberattack that forced billing pauses and system isolation exposed cybersecurity vulnerability in critical infrastructure that could recur with AI-enabled threats [3]. Negative free cash flow reflects heavy capital investment funded through debt and equity issuance, creating ongoing dilution risk. The Inspire Impact Score of 49 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (9/9): American Water's business is structurally immune to AI disruption. The company operates physical water and wastewater infrastructure — treatment plants, pumping stations, pipelines, and dams — that AI cannot replicate or substitute [1]. Water provision is a regulated natural monopoly with government-granted franchise territories, creating an impenetrable competitive moat. AI data center buildouts actually increase demand for water (cooling), directly benefiting water utilities in service territories with data center growth. The aging US water infrastructure requires $1T+ in investment over the coming decades, further cementing the need for physical operators. Regulatory rate-setting mechanisms provide earnings visibility regardless of technological disruption in other sectors. The October 2024 cyberattack demonstrated cybersecurity risk from AI-enabled threats, but this is an operational risk, not a substitution risk [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): American Water participates in water sector GenAI research through AWWA and is adopting operational AI for asset management, leak detection, and customer service, but these efforts do not generate identifiable AI revenue streams.
