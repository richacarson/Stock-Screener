Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NYSE ·United States
American Express Company operates as an integrated payments company worldwide, providing charge and credit card products, merchant acquisition and processing, network services, and travel-related services through a closed-loop network that serves as both card issuer and merchant acquirer. Founded in 1850, headquartered in New York City, Dow Jones Industrial Average component. Q2 2026 delivered $19.6B revenue (+10%, fourth consecutive double-digit quarter), EPS of $4.53 (+11%), record net card fees (+15.4%), and billings of $455.8B (+9%). Management raised full-year revenue guidance to 10% while holding EPS guidance at $17.30-$17.90 to reinvest upside into growth.

Investment Thesis
American Express delivered an exceptional Q2 2026 with $19.6B revenue (+10%, beating estimates), EPS of $4.53 (+11%), record net card fees (+15.4% — the fastest-growing revenue line), and billings of $455.8B (+9%), marking the fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit revenue growth [1]. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to 10% while holding EPS guidance at $17.30-$17.90 and choosing to reinvest the surplus into marketing and technology — a signal of confidence in sustainable long-term growth over short-term earnings optimization [1]. The closed-loop network model provides unique transaction-level data advantages that AI enhances rather than threatens, and the agentic commerce developer kit positions Amex as the payment infrastructure for AI-powered purchasing [5]. At 17.86x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.40, ROE of 34.38%, and improving credit quality (provision down to $1.1B with a $191M reserve release), the stock trades at a reasonable premium for a 175-year-old franchise delivering consistent double-digit growth. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway collects $576M annually in AXP dividends, reflecting decades of conviction in the business model [8].
The primary concern is the significant insider selling — $7.7M+ in executive sales following the Q2 2026 earnings beat, with CEO Squeri making 0 purchases and 8 sales over 5 years [7]. This raises questions about management conviction at current valuations despite strong operational performance. The D/E ratio of 172%, while improved from 221% at Q1, creates leverage risk if credit losses spike during a consumer downturn — Amex's concentration in affluent spending means a luxury and travel pullback could disproportionately impact revenue. Profit margin of 16.14% trails the 20% target, constrained by provision costs and the capital intensity of credit extension. The 1.13% dividend yield, despite 47 consecutive years of payment and a 16% increase this year, falls below the 2% threshold for Dividend sleeve classification. Competition from fintech players and Big Tech payment platforms (Apple Pay) could pressure the closed-loop model's merchant acceptance advantage over time.
