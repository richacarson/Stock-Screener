Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NYSE ·United States
American Express Company operates as a globally integrated payments company, providing charge and credit card products, merchant acquiring and processing, network services, and travel-related services through a closed-loop network in which it acts as issuer, acquirer and network. Founded in 1850 and headquartered in New York City, it is a Dow Jones Industrial Average component. Q3 2026 revenues net of interest expense rose 9% to $20.06B, billed business grew 8% to $454.0B, and adjusted EPS rose 13% to $4.68 (GAAP EPS $5.42 including a $982M pre-tax gain on the sale of its Global Business Travel Group stake); management reaffirmed ~10% full-year revenue growth and raised GAAP EPS guidance to $18.30-$18.60.

Investment Thesis
American Express delivered another strong quarter in Q3 2026: revenues net of interest expense rose 9% to $20.06B, billed business grew 8% to $454.0B, adjusted EPS rose 13% to $4.68, and provisions fell to $1.0B with a net write-off rate of 1.8%, below the 1.9% of a year earlier [1]. Management reaffirmed ~10% full-year revenue growth and raised GAAP EPS guidance to $18.30-$18.60, while average diluted shares fell 3% YoY to 674M [1]. At 15.8x forward earnings, a 0.97 forward PEG and a 35.9% ROE, investors pay a reasonable price for a premium franchise that is compounding fees (net card fees +17% in Q1) and positioning its network as trusted payment infrastructure for AI agents through the ACE developer kit [3][4][8].
The bear case centers on credit and concentration. D/E of 167.6% and a card-receivables balance sheet mean a consumer downturn would push provisions up sharply, and the Q3 reserve release could reverse [1][3]. Amex skews toward affluent travel and dining spend, which is discretionary, and Q1 airline volume had already 'softened' [4]. GAAP Q3 earnings were flattered by a one-time $982M pre-tax gain on the GBTG stake, the quarter carried a restructuring charge, and insiders have been net sellers [1][7]. Agentic commerce could also shift checkout control to AI platforms that commoditize card brands. The 1.15% yield keeps AXP in the Growth sleeve, and the Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) limits suitability for faith-based mandates.
Key Catalysts
  1. Commercialization of the Agentic Commerce Experiences developer kit and the Hypercard AI expense-management acquisition, with further AI products and new commercial cards slated for late 2026, could establish Amex as the trusted rail for AI-agent purchasing [4]
  2. Raised FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $18.30-$18.60 (adjusted $17.60-$17.90), with credit improving (1.8% net write-off rate) and 3% annual share-count reduction, sets up continued double-digit EPS growth into 2027 [1]
  3. Premium fee momentum (net card fees +17% to $2.8B in Q1 2026) from refreshed Platinum/Gold products and Gen Z/Millennial acquisition supports durable fee revenue that does not depend on spending [4]
Key Risks
  1. Credit-cycle reversal: with D/E of 167.6% and provisions recently helped by reserve releases, a rise in unemployment would compress earnings through higher write-offs and reserve builds [1][3]
  2. Discretionary-spend concentration: Amex's skew toward affluent travel, airline and dining spend (airline volume already 'softened' in Q1 2026) leaves it exposed to a premium-consumer pullback [4]
  3. Disintermediation by AI shopping agents or Big Tech wallets could weaken card-brand loyalty and merchant-fee economics if checkout decisions move to platforms Amex does not control [5]
