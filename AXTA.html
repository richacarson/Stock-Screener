Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·United States
Axalta Coating Systems, spun out of DuPont in 2013, is a global coatings maker. Its Performance Coatings segment (Refinish and Industrial) and Mobility Coatings segment (Light and Commercial Vehicle OEM) serve customers in 130+ countries. Q3 2026 net sales rose 2% to about $1.31B, and adjusted EBITDA fell 4% to $282M [1]. Shareholders approved an all-stock merger of equals with AkzoNobel on August 5, 2026. Regulatory clearance has been secured in about two-thirds of the required jurisdictions, and closing is targeted for early 2027 [2].

Investment Thesis
Axalta trades at 12.5x forward earnings. Mobility Coatings set records in Q3 2026 ($493M sales, +7%; segment EBITDA $94M at a 19.0% margin, up 100bps), Commercial Vehicle grew 24% on higher North American Class 8 production, and net leverage reached a record-low 2.1x [1][5]. The AkzoNobel merger has shareholder approval and about two-thirds of regulatory clearances, with closing targeted for early 2027. AXTA holders would own a share of a top-two global coatings company with cost-synergy potential [2]. FY2026 free cash flow guidance of about $450M (after about $80M of merger costs) gives the company room to finish the deal [1].
The bear case is decelerating earnings and deal risk. Q3 adjusted EPS fell 6% to $0.63, adjusted EBITDA fell 4% to $282M with margin down 130bps to 21.5%, and management cut its full-year outlook (FY adjusted EPS about $2.45, Q4 EBITDA $254-264M) as European Refinish volumes weakened [1]. Revenue has barely grown (1.8% YoY; 1.6% derived 3-year CAGR), Yahoo's PEG is 2.04, and trailing EPS growth is -13.7% [5]. The merger still needs about one-third of regulatory approvals and requires divestitures, and integration across 130+ countries could distract management and add more than the roughly $80M of 2026 M&A costs already budgeted [1][2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Closing of the AkzoNobel merger in early 2027 after the remaining regulatory clearances and the planned divestitures (Axalta's North America Building Products, AkzoNobel's European Refinish) [2]
  2. Continued Mobility Coatings strength, driven by Commercial Vehicle sales up 24% in Q3 on North American Class 8 production [1]
  3. European Refinish volume stabilization restoring Performance Coatings margin from 23.0% toward the 25.5% prior-year level [1]
Key Risks
  1. Regulatory delay or harsher remedies on the AkzoNobel merger, with about one-third of jurisdictions still to clear, which would extend merger costs (~$80M in 2026) and uncertainty [1][2]
  2. European Refinish weakness that has already forced a guidance cut and pushed Performance Coatings EBITDA down from $211M to $188M in Q3 [1]
  3. Low organic growth (1.8% YoY revenue) and declining EPS (adjusted EPS -6% in Q3) leaving valuation support dependent on deal synergies [1][5]
