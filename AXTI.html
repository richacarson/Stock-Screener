Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment & Materials ·NMS ·United States
AXT, Inc. designs, develops, manufactures, and distributes compound semiconductor substrates, primarily indium phosphide (InP), gallium arsenide (GaAs), and germanium (Ge) substrates. The company's InP substrates are critical components in optical transceivers used in AI data center interconnects, 5G communications, and silicon photonics. Founded in 1986 and headquartered in Fremont, California, AXT posted Q2 FY2026 revenue of $47.6M (up 165% YoY) with GAAP net income of $11.1M, 44.9% gross margin, and guided Q3 to $66M+. Market cap of $4.2B.

Investment Thesis
AXT at $64.63 with a $4.2B market cap is the purest-play beneficiary of AI data center interconnect buildout in the substrate market, with record Q2 2026 InP revenue of $30.7M driving total revenue to an all-time high of $47.6M — up 165% YoY [1]. Non-GAAP gross margins surged to 45% as volume leverage kicked in, and Q3 is guided to $66M+ with potential upside pending export permits [1]. The InP capacity expansion roadmap — tripling by end 2026 to $60M/quarter and doubling again to $130M/quarter by end 2027 — is backed by a backlog exceeding $100M [1]. With AI data centers requiring exponentially more optical transceivers (each needing InP substrates), AXTI sits at the narrow bottleneck of a secular demand curve. The $632M capital raise provides the funding to execute the expansion, and the balance sheet holds $748.8M in cash against $101.5M in debt [1][2].
The bear case centers on execution risk and valuation premium. At 28.7x forward earnings and a PEG of 12.09, the stock prices in flawless execution of a capacity expansion plan that has never been attempted at this scale by AXT — the company must successfully ramp crystal growth operations from $15M/quarter to $130M/quarter within 18 months while maintaining yield and quality [1]. Export permit uncertainty for China-manufactured substrates remains the largest near-term risk, with management acknowledging some Q3 revenue depends on permits not yet granted. The 2024 securities class action alleging environmental violations at Chinese subsidiary Tongmei introduces litigation overhang, and the $632M secondary offering diluted existing shareholders by approximately 50% [3]. The company's revenue history is deeply cyclical ($135M in 2021, collapsing to $62M by 2024), raising questions about whether the current InP supercycle is sustainable or another boom-bust peak. TTM profitability metrics remain depressed (ROE 1.01%, profit margin 3.23%) as the hyper-growth phase has yet to fully flow through trailing financial statements.
Key Catalysts
  1. InP capacity expansion to $60M/quarter by Q4 2026 and $130M/quarter by end 2027, enabling AXT to capture surging demand from hyperscaler 800G/1.6T optical transceiver deployments as AI data center construction accelerates globally [1]
  2. Q3 2026 revenue guided to $66M+ with potential upside, representing continued triple-digit YoY growth as the AI infrastructure spending cycle remains in early innings and InP substrate supply remains globally constrained [1]
  3. Gross margin inflection from 8.0% (Q2 2025) to 44.9% (Q2 2026) demonstrates powerful operating leverage as InP capacity utilization rises, with further margin expansion likely as scale effects and manufacturing optimization take hold [1]
Key Risks
  1. Export permit dependency for China-manufactured InP substrates creates binary revenue risk — management acknowledged some Q3 revenue requires permits not yet granted, and U.S.-China trade tensions could restrict AXT's ability to ship from its Tongmei subsidiary [1]
  2. Execution risk on unprecedented capacity ramp from $15M to $130M/quarter InP production — crystal growth is a precision process where yield degradation at scale could compress margins and delay revenue recognition [1]
  3. Securities class action lawsuit alleging environmental violations at Chinese manufacturing facilities introduces litigation costs and reputational risk, with potential regulatory scrutiny of Tongmei's operations [3]
