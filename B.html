Barrick Mining Corporation (renamed from Barrick Gold in May 2025) is one of the world's largest gold and copper mining companies, operating mines across Africa, the Americas, and the Middle East. Q1 2026 revenue reached $5.22B (+67% YoY) with net earnings up 256% and gold production of 719,000 oz beating guidance. TTM revenue is $20.65B. Mark Hill became President and CEO in February 2026 following Mark Bristow's departure in September 2025. The company announced a $3B+ stock buyback and a $4B agreement with Newmont resolving Nevada JV disputes and clearing the North American IPO path. Founded in 1983, headquartered in Toronto, Canada.

Investment Thesis
Barrick Mining at $40.23 with a $66B market cap is riding a record gold price environment into genuinely strong fundamentals — Q1 2026 revenue of $5.22B (+67% YoY), net earnings up 256%, gold production of 719,000 oz beating guidance, and operating cash flow of $2.55B (+111% YoY) [1]. The $4B Newmont agreement resolves a multi-year Nevada JV dispute and clears consent for a North American IPO, removing a significant overhang. Infrastructure metrics are outstanding: D/E of 12.7%, ROE of 27.0%, profit margin of 31.6%, and Forward P/E of 9.2 combine for a near-maximum score, while the $3B+ buyback and 26-year dividend history demonstrate capital discipline. The company's copper exposure through Reko Diq positions it for AI-driven data center demand, adding a structural growth story atop the gold price tailwind [1][3].
The bear case centers on commodity-price dependence and leadership continuity. Barrick's extraordinary growth (+67% revenue, +256% earnings in Q1 2026) is substantially a function of the gold price cycle rather than company-specific execution, and a gold price reversal would compress these metrics quickly given inherent mining operating leverage [1]. The PEG of 2.04 reflects this concern — the market doubts the growth rate is sustainable. CEO Mark Hill has been in role only since February 2026 following Bristow's abrupt September 2025 departure, an unusual succession event for a company managing complex, politically sensitive assets in Mali and Tanzania [2]. Historical ESG friction remains live — North Mara has faced documented allegations of police-related deaths and environmental violations, the Veladero mine in Argentina faced cyanide spill claims, and Mali production was disrupted by a 32% output decline in 2025 amid government disputes before recovering [4]. Jurisdictional risk in Barrick's highest-grade assets is structural, not incidental.
Key Catalysts
  1. Completion and integration of the $4B Newmont Nevada JV settlement, including progress toward the North American IPO which could unlock a valuation re-rating and institutional rebalancing [1]
  2. Continued gold price strength sustaining record revenue and cash flow, with Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $2.55B (+111% YoY) funding buybacks, dividends, and Reko Diq copper development simultaneously [1]
  3. Reko Diq copper-gold mine ramp-up in Pakistan providing commodity diversification and AI-driven data center copper demand exposure, positioning Barrick beyond pure gold dependence [3]
Key Risks
  1. Gold and copper price reversal — the bulk of Q1 2026's 67% revenue growth and 256% earnings growth reflects favorable commodity prices rather than structural company-specific improvement, and the PEG of 2.04 signals market skepticism about growth durability
  2. Leadership continuity risk — CEO Mark Hill has been in role only since February 2026 after Mark Bristow's unexpected September 2025 departure, creating governance uncertainty for a company operating in politically complex jurisdictions [2]
  3. Jurisdictional and ESG risk — documented allegations at North Mara (police-related deaths), Veladero (cyanide spill claims), and Mali (production disputes, 32% output decline in 2025) represent structural operating risks in Barrick's highest-grade assets [4]
