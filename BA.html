The Boeing Company is the world's largest aerospace company and a leading manufacturer of commercial jetliners, defense, space, and security systems, and provider of aftermarket support services. Excellence Evaluation — Think Like an Owner (50%)
Innovation WEAK
3
Innovation Track Record (0/3): Boeing has not successfully launched a meaningful new product in the last five years — the 737-7, 737-10, and 777X are all years behind schedule, Starliner faced repeated failures, and the company has been consumed by quality remediation rather than innovation [1][3]. AI Deployment (0/2): No evidence of specific AI-powered products shipping to customers or quantified internal AI-driven efficiency gains, though the defense segment includes autonomous systems research. Market Share Leadership (1/2): Boeing remains one of two global commercial aircraft duopolists with Airbus, but has been steadily losing market share — Airbus delivered 766 aircraft in 2025 vs. Boeing's ~650, and new order share has tilted toward Airbus in recent years [1]. Product Pipeline (1/2): The 737-7 has completed test flights with amended type certificate expected soon, the 737-10 recently completed its final test flight, and both target first deliveries in 2027 — specific products with approximate timelines but repeatedly delayed [1]. R&D Intensity (1/1): Boeing's R&D spending of ~$3.4B annually is above average for aerospace/defense as a percentage of revenue.

Community Impact (1/2): Boeing's charitable giving includes the Boeing Global Engagement portfolio and the employee Community Fund, supporting STEM education and community development programs, though recent financial distress has limited new commitments. Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Boeing's 100+ year history building aircraft that connect the world suggests infinite game DNA, but the last decade has been a case study in finite thinking — prioritizing share buybacks over engineering investment, quarterly EPS over product safety, and executive compensation over workforce trust. CEO Ortberg's back-to-basics turnaround is an attempt to restore infinite game principles, but the cultural damage from the McDonnell Douglas merger mentality remains deep.
Just Cause
7
Boeing's purpose — connecting, protecting, exploring, and inspiring the world through aerospace innovation — qualifies as a genuine Just Cause that has been partially lived for over a century [4]. The company's role in democratizing air travel, enabling space exploration (Apollo, ISS), and defending the free world is historically validated. However, the cause has been severely undermined by the 2018-2024 period of safety failures, financial engineering, and cultural decay. The Just Cause exists and is real but is being rebuilt rather than actively driving strategy [6][7].
Trusting Teams
2
Boeing's trust environment is deeply damaged — whistleblower retaliation against Ed Pierson and Sam Salehpour, the 2024 IAM machinists' strike over working conditions and compensation, five CEOs in 20 years creating leadership instability, and FAA findings of non-compliance in manufacturing quality control [6][7]. CEO Ortberg acknowledged Boeing's culture is 'very insular' with teams that 'don't work with each other as well as we could,' and warned the culture shift would be 'brutal to leadership' [5]. This is a low-trust organization in active recovery.
Worthy Rivals
10
Under Ortberg, Boeing's competitive language has shifted from defeating Airbus to fixing itself — the CEO's focus on internal quality, engineering-first culture, and operational discipline implicitly acknowledges that Airbus's strengths (execution consistency, production discipline) reveal Boeing's weaknesses [5]. The company is using Airbus as a benchmark for self-improvement rather than a target to destroy, reflecting an emerging infinite competitive mindset.
Existential Flexibility
2
Boeing has shown remarkable rigidity — clinging to the 60-year-old 737 platform through successive derivative variants rather than investing in a clean-sheet narrow-body aircraft, even as the 737 MAX crisis demonstrated the limits of incremental iteration. The company has not made a transformative strategic pivot in decades; the 787 Dreamliner (launched 2004) was the last genuinely new aircraft platform [1].
Courage to Lead
6
Ortberg's willingness to declare Boeing's culture 'insular' and warn that change would be 'brutal to leadership' shows nascent courage [5]. The decision to absorb a $280M VC-25B charge rather than paper over cost overruns signals honesty about program realities [1]. However, Boeing spent 2014-2024 doing the opposite — optimizing for quarterly EPS through $43B in share buybacks, cost-cutting engineering resources, and opposing safety improvements. One year of honesty does not erase a decade of cowardice.

Investment Thesis
Boeing is in the early innings of a multi-year turnaround under CEO Ortberg, with Q2 2026 delivering 171 aircraft (highest since 2018), generating positive free cash flow of $631M, and growing revenue 8% YoY to $24.6B [1]. The record $715B backlog provides unprecedented revenue visibility, the FAA has authorized Boeing to resume issuing airworthiness certificates for all 737 MAX and 787 variants, and the 737-7 and 737-10 certifications are imminent with first deliveries expected in 2027 [1]. Boeing remains one of only two companies on Earth capable of building commercial aircraft at scale — a duopoly moat that no competitor can realistically breach.
The bear case is formidable: $54.1B in total debt creates existential financial risk if the recovery stalls, and the forward P/E of 726x reflects how far Boeing is from normalized earnings [8]. The core loss of $0.76/share in Q2 2026 was worse than the $0.34 consensus estimate, driven by a $280M VC-25B charge that illustrates how defense fixed-price contracts continue to hemorrhage cash [1]. Boeing's safety culture remains under FAA scrutiny, whistleblower allegations are unresolved, and the Netflix documentary on whistleblower claims has renewed public skepticism [6][7]. With Airbus consistently outexecuting on deliveries and orders, Boeing risks becoming the permanent #2 in a duopoly where scale matters.
Key Catalysts
  1. 737-7 amended type certificate expected imminently, followed by 737-10 certification, unlocking two new delivery streams with combined backlog of 1,000+ aircraft and first deliveries in 2027
  2. Production rate increases toward 38/month on the 737 MAX line would dramatically improve per-unit fixed cost absorption and drive margin recovery toward historical 10-15% operating margins
  3. Record $715B backlog provides 7+ years of revenue visibility, supporting the case for sustainable free cash flow generation to deleverage the $54.1B debt load
Key Risks
  1. Total debt of $54.1B with near-zero profitability creates existential balance sheet risk — any production disruption, labor action, or demand slowdown could trigger a credit downgrade or dilutive capital raise
  2. Ongoing FAA quality scrutiny, unresolved whistleblower allegations, and potential for additional safety-related production slowdowns could derail the delivery ramp timeline
  3. Defense fixed-price contract losses continue to accumulate — the $280M VC-25B charge in Q2 2026 follows billions in prior charges on programs including the KC-46 tanker and T-7A trainer
