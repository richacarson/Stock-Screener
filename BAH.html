Company Profile
Technology ·IT Consulting & Other Services ·NYSE ·United States
Booz Allen Hamilton is the largest pure-play consulting and technology services provider to the U.S. federal government, with 98% of revenue from federal agencies including DoD, intelligence, and civil agencies. The firm is the prime digital integrator for the Department of Defense and the leading provider of AI to the U.S. federal government. Founded in 1914, headquartered in McLean, Virginia.

Investment Thesis
Booz Allen Hamilton trades at a deeply discounted 10.6x forward P/E after a 40%+ drawdown from 2024 highs, pricing in FY2026 revenue declining 6.4% to $11.2B as the civil segment faced unprecedented DOGE-driven headwinds [1]. Despite top-line pressure, profitability improved with adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 11%, free cash flow of $951M, and adjusted EPS of approximately $6.50 [1]. The AI business scaling toward $800M+, record backlog of $38B with 1.1x TTM book-to-bill, and the strategic OpenAI partnership position BAH as the premier AI-enabled government services franchise [3][7]. The 3.8% dividend yield backed by a 33% payout ratio and 14.89% 5-year dividend growth CAGR provides attractive income with substantial margin of safety.
The bear case centers on execution risk in a deteriorating federal spending environment. FY2027 guidance of 0-4% revenue growth with civil expected to decline high single digits signals continued near-term headwinds, and the Littlejohn data breach affecting 406,000 taxpayers triggered a $21M Treasury contract termination that could catalyze additional agency cancellations [1]. D/E of 373% — driven by share buybacks and an asset-light balance sheet — creates financial leverage risk if government contract flow deteriorates further. The 98% federal revenue concentration is unique single-customer risk, and generative AI threatens traditional billable-hour consulting economics even as BAH deploys AI for clients. The $62.68 stock price (vs. $93.77 average analyst target) reflects market skepticism about the pace of recovery [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI business scaling toward $1B+ annual revenue as defense and intelligence agencies accelerate AI adoption, with the OpenAI partnership providing differentiated national security AI capabilities
  2. Record $38B backlog converting to revenue as funded backlog increases and national security spending grows mid-single digits in FY2027, offsetting continued civil weakness
  3. Valuation re-rating from 10.6x forward P/E toward the 15-18x historical range as revenue growth inflects positive and $150M run-rate cost savings flow through to margins
Key Risks
  1. Littlejohn data breach fallout — Treasury's $21M contract termination could catalyze other agency cancellations, and 98% federal revenue concentration creates unique single-customer demand risk
  2. D/E of 373% creates financial leverage risk — interest expense consumes meaningful operating income, and DOGE-driven government efficiency initiatives could extend the revenue contraction beyond FY2027
  3. Generative AI threatens billable-hour consulting economics — even if contract volumes recover, AI-driven productivity gains could compress labor-arbitrage margins that underpin the services business model
