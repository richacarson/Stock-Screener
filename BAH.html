Company Profile
Industrials ·Consulting Services ·NYSE ·United States
Booz Allen Hamilton is a leading management and technology consulting firm providing analytics, digital, engineering, and cyber solutions to the U.S. government, including defense, intelligence, and civil agencies. The company is the #1 provider of AI services to the federal government with 2,500+ AI practitioners. BAH reported FY2025 (ending March 2025) revenue of $12.0B (+12.4% YoY) with record total backlog of $37.0B. FY2026 saw revenue decline to ~$11.3B amid government spending uncertainty, though the company maintained record backlog of $38B and adjusted EPS guidance of $5.95-6.15.

Investment Thesis
Booz Allen Hamilton offers a compelling combination of AI leadership, government mission criticality, and attractive valuation at 10.6x forward earnings with a 3.6% dividend yield growing at 9.5% annually [5]. As the #1 AI services provider to the federal government with 2,500+ AI practitioners and record total backlog of $38B, the company is positioned to benefit from secular growth in defense modernization, cyber security, and AI adoption across government agencies [2][3]. The FY2026 revenue decline to ~$11.3B from $12.0B reflects temporary government spending disruption, not structural deterioration — the record backlog and TTM book-to-bill of 1.1x signal continued demand [1][3].
The bear case centers on government spending uncertainty — FY2026 revenue declined ~5.5% amid a partial shutdown and DOGE-driven efficiency initiatives that could structurally reduce federal consulting spend [3]. D/E of 373% exceeds the scoring floor, reflecting a deliberately leveraged capital structure that amplifies both upside and downside risk. The Q3 FY2026 quarterly book-to-bill of 0.3x was alarmingly low, even if seasonal — funding was down 32% YoY, and a prolonged government austerity cycle would compress revenue further [3]. Profit margins of 7.6% leave limited cushion for pricing pressure if government agencies consolidate vendor relationships or build internal AI capabilities. The adjusted EPS guidance of $5.95-6.15 for FY2026 represents a ~4.7% decline from FY2025's $6.35, signaling near-term earnings pressure [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Government AI adoption acceleration as defense, intelligence, and civil agencies increasingly require AI deployment capabilities that only cleared, specialized providers like BAH can deliver at scale
  2. Defense budget growth driven by geopolitical tensions, with BAH's $38B backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility once government spending normalizes from FY2026 disruptions
  3. Margin expansion potential as the revenue mix shifts toward higher-margin AI and digital solutions versus traditional consulting staff augmentation
Key Risks
  1. Government spending austerity — DOGE-driven efficiency initiatives and potential continuing resolutions could structurally reduce federal consulting budgets, compressing revenue beyond the FY2026 dip
  2. Quarterly book-to-bill of 0.3x in Q3 FY2026 signals potential award flow deceleration — if new contract wins don't accelerate, the record backlog may mask deteriorating demand
  3. D/E of 373% creates meaningful financial risk in a rising rate environment, with interest expense consuming a growing share of operating income and limiting flexibility to invest in growth
