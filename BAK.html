Company Profile
Materials ·Chemicals ·NYSE ·Brazil
Braskem S.A. is the largest petrochemical company in Latin America, producing thermoplastic resins (polyethylene, polypropylene), basic chemicals, and specialty materials. The company operates manufacturing facilities across Brazil, the United States, Mexico, and Europe, processing naphtha and gas-based feedstocks into polymers used in packaging, automotive, construction, and consumer goods. Braskem is also a global leader in biopolymers through its I'm green bio-based polymer line derived from sugarcane ethanol and Wenew recycled resin products. Founded in 1972, headquartered in Camacari, Brazil. Market cap approximately $956 million. ADR trades on NYSE.

Investment Thesis
Braskem offers potential deep-value upside as a cyclical recovery play — Q1 2026 EBITDA surged 76% QoQ to $192M as resin pricing improved, and 2026 revenue guidance of $15.76B implies recovery from the current $12.79B TTM run-rate [1]. The company is the largest petrochemical producer in Latin America with real physical assets, a growing biopolymer franchise targeting 1 million tons by 2030, and a stock price ($2.67) near all-time lows that prices in near-worst-case scenarios [2]. If the global petrochemical cycle turns and Braskem successfully restructures its debt, the equity could re-rate significantly. Six analysts rate the stock 'Hold' with a $3.64 average target (36% upside).
The bear case is severe and centers on the company's distressed capital structure. Debt-to-equity of -423% and leverage of 16.81x EBITDA mean Braskem's equity is effectively an out-of-the-money call option on a petrochemical cycle recovery [3]. The company has sought creditor protection from a Sao Paulo court, signaling imminent risk of restructuring that could wipe out equity holders. Revenue declined 15.5% YoY driven by weak resin pricing and geopolitical headwinds increasing feedstock costs. The stock trades on the NYSE as an ADR, adding complexity for US investors, and Brazil's macroeconomic environment (currency volatility, political risk) introduces additional layers of uncertainty. Even in a cyclical recovery scenario, the massive debt burden means earnings accrue primarily to creditors rather than equity holders until leverage normalizes below 5-6x.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued petrochemical cycle recovery with resin pricing improvement extending the Q1 2026 EBITDA momentum ($192M, up 76% QoQ) through the remainder of 2026
  2. Successful debt restructuring that reduces leverage from 16.81x EBITDA toward a manageable 5-6x range, potentially converting some debt to equity or extending maturities
  3. Biopolymer franchise growth toward the 1 million ton target by 2030, with I'm green and Wenew commanding premium pricing over commodity resins and expanding margins
Key Risks
  1. Creditor protection proceedings and 16.81x EBITDA leverage create existential risk for equity holders — any restructuring could substantially dilute or eliminate common equity value
  2. Geopolitical tensions increasing feedstock costs (naphtha, ethane) combined with weak global demand for plastics could extend the petrochemical downcycle and delay EBITDA recovery
  3. Negative stockholders' equity and -423% debt-to-equity ratio mean the company cannot access traditional capital markets, leaving it dependent on creditor negotiations and asset sales for liquidity
