Company Profile
Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·United States
Ball Corporation is the world's largest manufacturer of recyclable aluminum packaging, producing beverage cans, cups, and aerosol containers across manufacturing facilities in North America, South America, Europe, Middle East, and Asia. The company divested its aerospace division in 2024 for $5.5 billion to focus exclusively on sustainable packaging, and is guided by its '30x30 Strategy' targeting $30 billion market cap by 2030 through operational excellence and sustainability leadership. Ball produces packaging for major global beverage brands and champions aluminum's infinite recyclability. Founded in 1880, headquartered in Westminster, Colorado. Market cap approximately $16.1 billion.

Investment Thesis
Ball Corporation delivered a strong Q1 2026 with $3.60B revenue (up 16.3% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.94 (up 22% YoY), exceeding analyst expectations by 8.1% on revenue [1]. The stock trades at 14.82x forward earnings and 1.10x PEG — reasonable valuations for the world's largest aluminum can manufacturer with improving fundamentals post-aerospace divestiture. Management guides for 10%+ comparable EPS growth and $900M+ free cash flow in 2026, with the 30x30 Strategy providing a clear path to double the current $16.1B market cap. The sustainability thesis is durable: aluminum is infinitely recyclable, regulatory tailwinds favor aluminum over plastic, and Ball's mission-driven focus positions it to capture growing demand from environmentally conscious brands and consumers [2][4].
The primary risk is that Ball remains a low-margin commodity manufacturer — profit margin of 6.86% and debt-to-equity of 139% leave limited room for execution error [3]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 2.4% reflects sluggish organic growth masked by the aerospace divestiture, and the stock is already trading above the analyst mean price target of $70.92. Raw aluminum pricing, energy costs, and beverage consumption volumes are all cyclical variables outside Ball's control. The 106.8% EPS growth rate is inflated by one-time gains from the aerospace sale and should not be annualized. Additionally, net insider selling over the last 90 days suggests that executives, while not bearish, are not adding to positions at current prices. The dividend yield of 1.32% is modest and offers limited downside support, and Ball's growth from here requires the aluminum sustainability thesis to translate into above-market volume growth — a plausible but unproven proposition.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued revenue acceleration above 10% driven by aluminum packaging volume growth as brands and regulators shift from plastic to infinitely recyclable aluminum containers globally
  2. Margin expansion through operational excellence, capacity utilization improvements, and raw material cost optimization driving profit margin from 6.86% toward the 8-10% range
  3. Achievement of the 30x30 Strategy milestones — de-leveraging below 3x EBITDA, share buyback continuation, and organic growth positioning Ball for a $30B market cap by 2030
Key Risks
  1. Commodity margin compression from volatile raw aluminum and energy costs squeezing the already-thin 6.86% profit margin, with limited pricing power in long-term beverage company contracts
  2. Debt-to-equity of 139% creates financial risk if the economic cycle turns — a recession-driven decline in beverage consumption would pressure both revenue and the ability to service debt
  3. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 2.4% reveals sluggish organic growth beneath the headline numbers, and the stock trading above analyst mean targets suggests limited near-term upside without a catalyst
