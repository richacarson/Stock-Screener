Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·Canada
Brookfield Asset Management Ltd. is one of the world's largest alternative asset managers with $603 billion in fee-bearing capital across infrastructure, renewable power, real estate, private equity, and credit strategies. Spun off from Brookfield Corporation (BN) in December 2022 as a pure-play, asset-light fee business, BAM earns management fees, incentive distributions, and carried interest from its fund vehicles. The firm raised a record $112 billion over the trailing twelve months and deployed $500 million into OpenAI's AI infrastructure platform. CEO Connor Teskey succeeded Bruce Flatt in February 2026 as part of a planned leadership transition. Headquartered in Toronto, Canada.

Investment Thesis
BAM is a world-class fee business with $603B in fee-bearing capital, 49.68% profit margins, and 21.48% revenue growth — a rare combination of scale and growth in alternative asset management [1][5]. Fee-related earnings grew 11% to $772M in Q1 2026, and the firm raised a record $35B in the quarter with infrastructure and private equity flagships on track to be their largest vintages ever [1]. CEO Teskey expects 2026 growth to exceed long-term targets. The $500M OpenAI partnership positions BAM at the intersection of infrastructure and AI, potentially creating a new secular growth vector [2]. The 4.10% dividend yield provides attractive income while the stock trades at 16.5% below analyst consensus price target of $56.86.
Valuation is the primary concern — at 24x forward earnings and 14.6x revenue for an asset management business, BAM is priced for continued exceptional fundraising and fee growth [5]. The GAAP payout ratio of 119% exceeds earnings, relying on distributable earnings to support the $1.88 annual dividend. BAM has only 3.5 years of history as an independent entity, and the smooth leadership transition from Flatt to Teskey remains to be validated through a full market cycle. Greenwashing allegations from environmental groups regarding contradictions between Brookfield's ESG pledges and Oaktree Capital's fossil fuel investments create reputational risk [4]. The stock has declined 16.5% over the past year despite strong operational performance, suggesting the market is concerned about alternative asset management fee compression or deployment pace.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record fundraising momentum with infrastructure and private equity flagships expected to be their largest vintages ever, driving fee-bearing capital toward $700B+ and accelerating fee-related earnings growth
  2. AI infrastructure buildout through the $500M OpenAI partnership and increasing data center investment demand, creating a multi-decade secular tailwind for BAM's infrastructure franchise
  3. Continued margin expansion in the asset-light fee model as scale effects compound — management fees on larger fund vintages generate operating leverage with minimal incremental cost
Key Risks
  1. Premium valuation at 24x forward earnings and 14.6x revenue leaves limited margin of safety — any deceleration in fundraising pace or fee growth would trigger meaningful multiple compression
  2. Greenwashing allegations and contradictions between ESG pledges and Oaktree Capital's fossil fuel investments could damage institutional investor relationships and ESG-mandated fund flows
  3. GAAP payout ratio of 119% exceeds reported earnings, and while distributable earnings support the dividend, any sustained earnings decline could force a dividend reduction that would damage the income investment thesis

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): BAM's core business — raising capital from institutional investors and deploying it into real assets (infrastructure, real estate, renewables) — involves deep client relationships, operational expertise, and physical asset management that AI cannot substitute [1][2]. CEO Teskey explicitly stated that BAM's exposure to software companies affected by AI is 'very minimal' and the firm 'mostly focuses on real assets where we don't take any technology risk' [2]. Data centers and energy infrastructure — key BAM investments — actually benefit from increased AI demand. The main AI risk is that passive/algorithmic investing could pressure active management fees over time, but alternatives have historically been resistant to this trend due to illiquidity premiums and operational value creation. AI Opportunity (1/1): The $500M investment in OpenAI's AI Deployment Company positions BAM as a direct beneficiary of AI infrastructure buildout [2]. BAM's portfolio companies are deploying AI for operational efficiency, and AI-driven data center demand is directly driving growth in BAM's infrastructure fund raising. Teskey expects AI to be 'a defining driver of productivity across the global economy' [2].
