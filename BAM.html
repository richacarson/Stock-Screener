Mindset: MIXED Overall: 7 /10
Brookfield is built for duration. It holds long-life real assets, manages permanent and long-dated capital, and has a decades-long culture of owner-operators that produced a four-year CEO succession [1][3]. Sinek would admire the patience and continuity, and the repeated reinvention from Brascan-era conglomerate to global alternatives manager. Investment Thesis
BAM is a high-quality fee compounder. Q2 2026 fee-related earnings rose 20% to $808M ($3.2B over the trailing twelve months, +19%), distributable EPS rose 15% to $0.44, fee-bearing capital grew 19% to $672B, and quarterly fundraising set a record $77B [1]. The Oaktree buy-in, the $40B Just Group mandate, and Angel Oak add about $200M of run-rate fee-related earnings [2]. The $5B AI infrastructure first close, the OpenAI partnership, and the $100B Paducah AI campus put BAM at the center of AI-era capital formation [1]. Metrics are excellent: ROE 26.8%, profit margin 48.9%, D/E 34.5%, PEG 0.86, and a 4.16% dividend yield [5].
The bear case is valuation, payout, and cyclicality in fundraising. At 22.4x forward and 27.7x trailing earnings, BAM is priced for continued high-teens fee growth, and Q3 fundraising is expected to fall well short of Q2's record $77B because $40B of that came from a single related-party mandate (Just Group, via Brookfield Wealth Solutions) [2][5]. The reported payout ratio of 108% leaves little room if fee growth slows. Related-party flows from Brookfield Corporation and Brookfield Wealth Solutions make headline growth hard to judge [1][5]. Reputational and ESG risks include the disputed net-zero claim and Westinghouse's tritium supply role, which has placed Brookfield on some European exclusion lists [4]. A CEO transition (February 2026) and a large AI-infrastructure concentration add execution risk if AI capex slows [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results (early November): fee-related earnings growth sustained near the 20% Q2 pace and confirmation of management's 'best year ever' outlook [1][2]
  2. Additional closes of the AI infrastructure strategy beyond the $5B first close, and progress on the $100B Paducah, Kentucky AI data-center campus [1]
  3. Full-year contribution from the Oaktree buy-in and the Just Group / Angel Oak mandates, adding about $200M of run-rate fee-related earnings [2]
Key Risks
  1. Fundraising normalization after the record $77B Q2 (of which $40B was a single related-party mandate), slowing fee-bearing capital growth from 19% [1][2]
  2. A dividend payout ratio of about 108% of reported earnings, which constrains dividend growth if fee-related earnings disappoint [5]
  3. Concentration in AI data-center and power investments that would be hurt if hyperscaler AI capex slows, plus ESG exclusion risk from Westinghouse's nuclear supply chain [1][4]
