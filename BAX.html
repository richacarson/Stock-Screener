Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices & Supplies ·NYSE ·United States
Baxter International is a Deerfield, Illinois-based medical device and supplies company providing sterile IV solutions, infusion systems and pumps, parenteral nutrition, inhaled anesthetics, and connected care technologies (smart beds, patient monitoring) following its 2021 acquisition of Hillrom. The company completed the divestiture of its Kidney Care business (now Vantive Health LLC) to Carlyle Group for ~$3.3B net proceeds in January 2025, and new CEO Andrew Hider has launched the Baxter Growth & Performance (GPS) operating system. Q2 2026 sales rose 5% to $2.96B and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $11.58-11.69B in sales and $1.95-2.15 adjusted EPS, though the company continues managing fallout from a 2025 Novum IQ Large Volume Pump safety recall.

Investment Thesis
Baxter's Q2 2026 results marked a turning point, with sales up 5% to $2.96B and management raising full-year 2026 guidance to $11.58-11.69B in sales and $1.95-2.15 in adjusted EPS, sending the stock up 16.8% on the print [1]. New CEO Andrew Hider — who previously nearly doubled adjusted revenue and tripled the stock price at automation firm ATS — has launched the Baxter Growth & Performance (GPS) operating system to drive continuous operational improvement, and the January 2025 divestiture of the Kidney Care/Vantive business for ~$3.3B net proceeds has sharpened the portfolio around core hospital products. A Forward P/E of 13.53x and a 2.77% dividend yield offer a reasonable entry point for a turnaround thesis, with adjusted operating margin expected to reach 13-14% for the full year despite near-term tariff and input-cost headwinds.
The bear case is substantial and centers on trust and balance sheet strain. Trailing GAAP ROE of -3.51% and profit margin of -5.20% reflect the financial damage from the 2025 Novum IQ Large Volume Pump recall, which involved 79 reports of serious injury and two patient deaths before Baxter paused shipments — triggering multiple securities class-action lawsuits alleging the company misrepresented the device's safety [2]. D/E of 152.42% is well above the Infrastructure target, and the 5-year dividend CAGR of roughly -20% reflects a two-thirds cut to the payout since 2015, meaning income investors have already been burned once by this name. Gross margin fell 210 basis points to 38.6% in Q2 2026 on higher-cost inventory and tariff impacts, and while guidance was raised, execution risk remains elevated given the company is simultaneously integrating a new CEO, remediating a major product safety failure, and digesting the Vantive divestiture.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full remediation and FDA clearance of the Novum IQ Large Volume Pump issue, which would remove the primary overhang on both margins and investor trust following the 2025 shipment pause [2]
  2. Continued execution of the Baxter Growth & Performance (GPS) operating system under new CEO Andrew Hider, whose track record at ATS included nearly doubling adjusted revenue over five years [1]
  3. Full-year 2026 guidance delivery ($11.58-11.69B sales, $1.95-2.15 adjusted EPS) with second-half margin expansion as tariff and input-cost headwinds ease, which would validate the turnaround narrative [1]
Key Risks

Multiple pending securities class-action lawsuits alleging Baxter made materially false and misleading statements about Novum IQ Large Volume Pump safety, with 79 reports of serious injury and two reported patient deaths tied to under-infusion risk, creating both financial and reputational exposure [2]
  2. Elevated leverage (152.42% D/E) combined with negative trailing GAAP profitability limits financial flexibility if the recall remediation costs or additional litigation exceed current expectations
  3. A 5-year dividend growth CAGR of approximately -20%, following a roughly two-thirds cut since 2015, signals the payout is not yet a reliable long-term income source despite the current 2.77% yield and single year of resumed growth
