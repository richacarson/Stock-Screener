Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NYQ ·United States
Baxter International provides a portfolio of hospital products across three segments: Medical Products & Therapies (sterile IV solutions, infusion systems, parenteral nutrition), Healthcare Systems & Technologies (smart beds, patient monitoring, surgical equipment), and Pharmaceuticals (injectable pharmaceuticals, inhaled anesthetics, drug compounding). The company completed the Vantive (Kidney Care) divestiture to Carlyle Group for ~$3.3B in January 2025 and has essentially eliminated its dividend. Q2 2026 sales rose 5% to $2.96B with management raising FY2026 guidance. CEO Andrew Hider (since September 2025) has launched the Baxter Growth & Performance (GPS) operating system. Founded in 1931, headquartered in Deerfield, Illinois.
37,500 Employees ·www.baxter.com

Investment Thesis
Baxter at $23.49 with a $12.1B market cap is a turnaround story showing early signs of operational improvement under new CEO Andrew Hider. Q2 2026 sales rose 5% to $2.96B with all segments growing, advanced surgery up 12%, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $11.58-11.69B in sales and $1.95-2.15 in adjusted EPS [1]. Forward P/E of 11.5 is undemanding if the turnaround gains traction and margins normalize from currently negative GAAP levels. The Vantive divestiture for ~$3.3B sharpened the portfolio around core hospital products (IV solutions, infusion systems, connected care), and Hider's track record at ATS Corporation (nearly doubled adjusted revenue, tripled stock price) lends credibility to the GPS operating system he's implementing. Forward EPS of $2.04 implies a dramatic swing from TTM losses to meaningful profitability [1][3].
The bear case is substantial and multi-dimensional. Trailing GAAP ROE of -14.4% and profit margin of -9.3% reflect significant financial damage from the 2025 Novum IQ Large Volume Pump recall (79 reports of serious injury, two patient deaths) and associated restructuring costs, with multiple securities class-action lawsuits creating ongoing financial and reputational exposure [2]. D/E of 156.4% is well above the Infrastructure target, reflecting the massive Hillrom acquisition debt that has not been adequately deleveraged, limiting financial flexibility. The near-elimination of the dividend (from $1.16 annually to $0.04, yield now 0.17%) signals the capital structure remains strained and represents a broken covenant with income investors [1]. PEG of 36.68 — while distorted by the earnings swing — suggests the market sees limited sustainable growth beyond the near-term recovery. Gross margin fell 210bps to 38.6% in Q2 2026 on higher-cost inventory and tariff impacts, and execution risk remains elevated as the company simultaneously integrates a new CEO, remediates a major safety failure, and digests the Vantive separation [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Full remediation and FDA clearance of the Novum IQ Large Volume Pump, which would remove the primary overhang on margins, investor trust, and potential securities litigation exposure [2]
  2. Delivery of FY2026 guidance ($11.58-11.69B sales, $1.95-2.15 adjusted EPS) with margin expansion in the second half as tariff headwinds ease, validating the GPS operating system and new CEO Hider's turnaround execution [1]
  3. Continued deleveraging from the 156.4% D/E level using Vantive divestiture proceeds and improving cash flow, which could eventually enable dividend restoration and re-rate the stock toward healthcare peer multiples [1]
Key Risks
  1. Multiple pending securities class-action lawsuits alleging Baxter made materially false and misleading statements about Novum IQ pump safety, with 79 reports of serious injury and two patient deaths tied to under-infusion risk, creating financial and reputational exposure of unknown magnitude [2]
  2. Near-elimination of dividend (yield 0.17%, down from 2.77% at last screen) signals the capital structure remains strained despite the Vantive proceeds, with D/E of 156.4% limiting flexibility for a healthcare company that historically offered reliable income
  3. Elevated execution risk as new CEO Hider simultaneously manages GPS implementation, Novum IQ remediation, post-Vantive separation integration, and tariff-driven margin pressure, any one of which could delay the turnaround trajectory [1][2]
