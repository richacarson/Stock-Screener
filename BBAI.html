Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
BigBear.ai provides AI-powered decision intelligence solutions primarily for U.S. defense, intelligence community, and homeland security agencies. The company's platforms include VANE (geopolitical risk analysis), ORION (force management), Ask Sage (generative AI for classified environments), and CargoSeer (cargo inspection and trade risk). CEO Kevin McAleenan, former Acting DHS Secretary, has led the company since January 2025. Q2 2026 revenue grew 13% to $36.7M with 20+ new contract wins and expanding margins. Market cap ~$1.5B.

Investment Thesis
BigBear.ai's Q2 2026 results demonstrate a genuine turnaround: revenue grew 13% to $36.7M with 20+ new contract wins, gross margins expanded 1,278bps to 34.0%, and full-year guidance of $135-165M (17% growth at midpoint) was reaffirmed [1]. The $281.9M backlog (+14% sequential in Q1) with book-to-bill well above 1.0x and a $53M sole-source classified IC contract provide multi-year revenue visibility [2]. CEO McAleenan (former Acting DHS Secretary) brings genuine national security credibility, and the company's defense AI products — Ask Sage for classified generative AI, VANE for geopolitical risk, ORION for force management — serve growing DoD modernization and AI adoption budgets. The balance sheet is exceptionally strong: $349-410M in cash against only $24M in debt (D/E of 3%), providing years of runway to invest through the growth ramp [4]. The Inspire Insight score of +31 (ALIGNED) and national security mission add appeal for faith-aligned investors.
The bear case remains significant. Despite Q2 improvement, BBAI is deeply unprofitable: TTM net loss of -$288.69M on $127.35M revenue produces a -226.7% profit margin, and the company has no near-term path to GAAP profitability [4]. Revenue grew 13% in Q2, but TTM revenue of $127M is still below 2021 levels ($145M), and the 5-year CAGR is negative — the company shrank during the biggest AI spending boom in history. The stock has fallen 55% from $6.72 to $3.03 over the past year despite improving fundamentals, suggesting the market remains skeptical of execution [4]. Competition from Palantir ($100B+ market cap, $3B+ revenue), Anduril, and tech giants (Google, OpenAI entering defense) creates structural disadvantages for a company with only $127M in annual revenue. Class action lawsuits add legal overhang. The Pentagon-OpenAI partnership demonstrated that the government can access comparable AI capabilities from larger, better-funded competitors [2]. The backlog-to-revenue ratio of ~9x quarters provides visibility but also signals capacity constraints and potential execution risk at current scale.
Key Catalysts
  1. Revenue growth re-acceleration — Q2 2026's 13% growth with 20+ new wins and full-year 17% growth target (if achieved) validates the McAleenan-era turnaround from the 19% 2025 decline [1]
  2. Gross margin expansion to 34.0% (from 21.3% prior year) signals improving revenue quality and mix shift toward higher-value AI products, creating a path toward eventual operating profitability [1]
  3. Defense AI spending tailwinds — growing DoD modernization budgets, border security investments, and bipartisan support for AI adoption across federal agencies expand BBAI's addressable market [2]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent unprofitability — TTM net loss of -$288.69M with no EBITDA guidance means the $349-410M cash position, while substantial, is being consumed, and additional dilutive capital raises remain possible [4]
  2. Competitive pressure from larger players — Palantir, Anduril, Google, and OpenAI entering defense AI with vastly greater resources could commoditize BBAI's products and limit pricing power despite classified-environment specialization [2]
  3. Execution risk at scale — $281.9M backlog-to-revenue ratio of ~9x quarters is very high, signaling potential capacity constraints that could delay revenue recognition and disappoint on the $135-165M guidance range [2]
