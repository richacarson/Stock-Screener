Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
BridgeBio Pharma is a biopharmaceutical company focused on discovering and delivering transformative medicines for genetic diseases and cancers. Its lead product ATTRUBY (acoramidis) treats transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) and has become a rapid commercial success, surging 211% YoY in Q2 2026. BEYONTTRA generates royalty revenue from EU and Japan sales. The company operates a hub-and-spoke model with autonomous teams focused on individual conditions. H1 2026 revenue reached $438.2M (up 93% YoY) with net loss narrowing to $0.78/share in Q2. Founded by CEO Neil Kumar in 2015.
700 Employees ·bridgebio.com

Investment Thesis
BridgeBio's ATTRUBY franchise continues to deliver exceptional commercial momentum — Q2 2026 total revenue of $243.7M (up from $110.6M in Q2 2025) brought H1 2026 revenue to $438.2M, with ATTRUBY net product revenue surging 211% YoY as the company expands its challenge to Pfizer's $3.5B+ tafamidis franchise in the large and underpenetrated ATTR-CM market [1][2]. BEYONTTRA adds $13.8M in royalty revenue from EU/Japan, diversifying the revenue base [2]. Net loss narrowed to $152.2M ($0.78/share) in Q2 from $181.9M ($0.95/share) in Q2 2025, demonstrating improving operating leverage as ATTRUBY scales. At ~$80/share with $15.6B market cap, the stock prices in significant ATTRUBY success but offers upside from pipeline readouts and international expansion. The Inspire Impact Score of 33 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions reflects responsible pharma practices.
The critical bear case centers on deep unprofitability and capital structure complexity. Despite $438.2M in H1 2026 revenue, BridgeBio reported a TTM profit margin of -98.45% — the company is spending nearly $2 for every $1 generated — and GAAP profitability remains years away even at current growth rates [2][6]. Cash of $587M against $1.86B in debt creates negative shareholder equity (D/E -133%), and the $1B preferred equity raise adds further dilution risk. ROE of -192% reflects accumulated losses that dwarf the current revenue trajectory. Concentration risk is acute: ATTRUBY represents over 90% of product revenue, making the entire enterprise highly binary on a single product's competitive trajectory against Pfizer [1][2]. Glassdoor reviews citing constantly shifting goals and board compensation controversies suggest governance risks. If ATTRUBY adoption decelerates or generic competition emerges sooner than expected, the leveraged capital structure offers limited margin for error.
