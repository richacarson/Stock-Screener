Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Recreational Vehicles ·NYSE ·United States
Brunswick Corporation is the world's largest recreational marine company, designing, manufacturing, and marketing marine propulsion systems (Mercury Marine), boats (Boston Whaler, Sea Ray, Lund, Bayliner, Crestliner), marine electronics (Navico Group: Simrad, Lowrance, B&G, C-MAP), and marine parts and accessories. Operates through Propulsion, Engine P&A, Navico Group, and Boat segments. Founded 1845, headquartered in Mettawa, Illinois. CEO David Foulkes since 2019 (Chairman since March 2025) leads the ACES Innovation Strategy (Autonomy, Connectivity, Electrification, Shared Access). Market cap ~$4.2B.

Investment Thesis
Brunswick is the global marine industry leader executing a technology-driven transformation through ACES while recovering from a cyclical trough. Q2 2026 delivered year-over-year sales growth across all four segments for the fourth consecutive quarter, with Mercury Marine gaining 200bps of U.S. outboard market share [1]. The stock's 19% decline from $80.85 to $65.03 has compressed the forward P/E to 12.0 and PEG to 0.72 — exceptional value for a dominant franchise with ~40% EPS growth guidance [5]. The company generates $523M operating cash flow and $366M free cash flow supporting both the 2.71% dividend yield (13 consecutive years of 15% annual growth) and share repurchases at cyclically depressed valuations. The Boating Intelligence/AutoCaptain AI platform positions Brunswick as the technology leader in a $170B+ global marine industry. The Inspire Impact Score of 65 (ALIGNED) with 16 positive ESG attributions and zero negatives makes this suitable for faith-aligned portfolios.
The bear case centers on cyclical vulnerability, leverage, and trailing losses. Brunswick is a deeply cyclical business — recreational marine demand correlates strongly with consumer confidence, housing wealth, and interest rates. The trailing net loss (profit margin -1.5%, ROE -4.8%) demonstrates how quickly earnings evaporate in a downturn [5]. D/E of 136.5% is elevated for a cyclical manufacturer, and the trailing payout ratio of 193% (distorted by one-time charges; forward payout ~33%) highlights dividend vulnerability in prolonged downturns. The initial 2026 guidance range of $3.80-4.40 adj EPS implies significant uncertainty, and the stock's 28% decline from its 52-week high of $90.25 suggests the market sees recovery risk [5]. Five-year revenue CAGR of ~0.5% reflects a secular growth challenge in recreational marine, with headwinds from aging boater demographics, rising insurance and marina costs, and potential tariff impacts on aluminum and components. The marine industry expects flat-to-slightly-up retail boat unit sales in 2026, limiting volume upside.
