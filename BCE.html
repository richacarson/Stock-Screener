Company Profile
Communication Services ·Integrated Telecommunication Services ·NYSE ·Canada
BCE Inc. is Canada's largest telecommunications company and parent of Bell Canada. BCE provides wireless (10.32M mobile subscribers), broadband Internet (4.89M high-speed subscribers), television, and enterprise communications services. Through Bell Media (CTV, Crave streaming at 5.07M subscribers) and the recently acquired Ziply Fiber in the US Pacific Northwest, BCE operates extensive fibre-optic, wireless, and data centre infrastructure. The company is pivoting toward AI-powered enterprise solutions through its Bell AI Fabric data centre initiative, targeting $2B in AI revenue by 2028. BCE cut its annualized dividend 56% from $3.99 to $1.75 in mid-2025 to fund accelerated capital investments. Market cap ~$21.9B.

Investment Thesis
BCE at 13.26x forward earnings offers a deeply discounted entry into Canada's dominant telecom infrastructure with a restructured 5.41% dividend yield and catalysts from fibre subscriber growth, AI data centre revenue, and the Ziply Fiber US expansion [5]. Q1 2026 revenue grew 4.0% to CAD $6,168M with Bell Business Markets up 9.7%, AI solutions surging 113%, and Crave streaming reaching 5.07M subscribers (+23% YoY) in Q2 [1][2]. The post-dividend cut payout ratio provides ample free cash flow for deleveraging from 3.5x toward the 3.0x net debt leverage target by 2030, while the 21.87% discount to analyst consensus target of $28.59 suggests the market has overpenalized the dividend reset [5].
The bear case centres on BCE's stretched balance sheet and unproven pivot economics. D/E of 173% with $29.4B in total debt and only $337M cash leaves BCE vulnerable to rising interest rates and limits financial flexibility if the $2B AI revenue target proves overly ambitious [5]. The 5yr revenue CAGR of just 1.5% reflects structural stagnation in legacy telecom segments — AI data centre revenue must deliver at scale to offset secular decline in traditional broadcasting and wireline telephony. The 56% dividend cut in 2025 destroyed BCE's identity as a reliable income stock; any further reduction or failure to resume dividend growth within 2-3 years could permanently impair the valuation [7]. Capital intensity surging to 20% (vs. 12-15% historically) means free cash flow is pressured even post-cut. Adjusted EPS is guiding down 5-11% for 2026, and the CRTC regulatory environment remains 'unsupportive' per management, with aggressive competitive pricing compressing margins [1].
