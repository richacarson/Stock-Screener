Company Profile
Communication Services ·Integrated Telecommunication Services ·NYSE ·Canada
BCE Inc. is Canada's largest communications company. Through Bell Canada it provides wireless, fibre broadband, TV and enterprise connectivity, and through Bell Media it owns CTV and the Crave streaming service; it entered the U.S. Pacific Northwest with the 2025 Ziply Fiber acquisition and is building Bell AI Fabric, which it describes as Canada's largest AI compute project. Q2 2026 operating revenue rose 1.5% to C$6.18B, adjusted EBITDA grew 1.0% to C$2.70B (43.8% margin) and adjusted EPS rose 3.2% to C$0.65, while free cash flow fell 9.5% as capex jumped 41.5%. 2026 guidance calls for 1-5% revenue growth, an 11% to 5% decline in adjusted EPS, free cash flow of C$2.1-2.3B and a held C$1.75 annual dividend.

Investment Thesis
BCE offers a 6.1% yield and trades at 10.8x forward earnings against a portfolio of essential Canadian connectivity assets [4][8]. Q2 2026 showed steady execution: revenue +1.5%, adjusted EBITDA +1.0% at a 43.8% margin, adjusted EPS +3.2% to C$0.65, operating cash flow +11.0%, Crave and fibre growth, and AI-powered solutions (Ateko and Bell Cyber) up 29% [1]. Management targets about C$2B of AI-powered revenue by 2028 and a 3.5x net-leverage ratio by end-2027, and Ziply Fiber gives it a U.S. fibre growth runway, so the rebased C$1.75 dividend could prove sustainable if the AI Fabric build-out converts to contracted revenue [1][3].
The bear case is weak growth and stretched cash flow. 2026 guidance calls for adjusted EPS to fall 5-11% and free cash flow of only C$2.1-2.3B (down 28-34%) as capital intensity rises to about 20%, so the C$1.75 dividend uses most of FCF [1]. D/E of 172.8% and a 5yr revenue CAGR of just 1.35% leave little room for error, and the 2025 dividend cut reset the growth streak to zero and turned the 5yr dividend CAGR negative (about -13%) [7][8]. Repeated layoffs (about 690 more in 2026) and Canadian regulatory pressure on pricing and wholesale fibre access add execution and reputational risk [5]. The Inspire Impact Score of -62 (MISALIGNED, Sexually Explicit content through its media assets) is a significant concern for faith-based investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Bell AI Fabric data-centre capacity, including the planned 300 MW Saskatchewan build, signing hyperscaler or sovereign-AI tenants and moving AI-powered revenue toward the C$2B 2028 target [3]
  2. Capex peaking in 2026 (capital intensity about 20%) followed by FCF recovery in 2027, supporting the 3.5x net-leverage target and dividend coverage [1]

Key Risks
  1. Dividend coverage: 2026 FCF guidance of C$2.1-2.3B leaves thin coverage for about C$1.6B of annual dividends, and a second cut is possible if AI Fabric returns lag or rates stay high [1]
  2. Leverage of 172.8% D/E with rising capex limits flexibility; a credit-rating downgrade would raise refinancing costs on a large debt stack [4][8]
  3. Regulatory and competitive pressure in Canadian wireless pricing and CRTC wholesale fibre-access rules, plus labour and reputational fallout from repeated layoffs, could erode margins [5]
