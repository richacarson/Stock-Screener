Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NYSE ·United States
Becton, Dickinson and Company (BD) is a global medical technology company that manufactures and sells medical devices, instrument systems, and reagents. Operating through BD Medical, BD Life Sciences, and BD Interventional segments, the company produces syringes, blood collection systems, medication management solutions, diagnostic instruments, and surgical products. BD completed the $4.2B acquisition of Edwards Lifesciences' Critical Care product group in September 2024, adding AI-enabled clinical monitoring solutions. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $5.0B grew 4.4% FX-neutral with raised full-year EPS guidance of $12.62-12.72. Founded in 1897, headquartered in Franklin Lakes, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
BD offers a compelling combination of defensive healthcare positioning, dividend reliability, and accelerating operational momentum at a reasonable valuation. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $5.0B grew 4.4% FX-neutral (accelerating from Q2's 2.6%), with all four segments delivering solid growth led by Interventional +5.5% and BioPharma Systems +5.2% [1]. Management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $12.62-12.72, and YTD free cash flow surged 45% to $1.7B, demonstrating improving cash conversion. At forward P/E of 13.83x with a 2.29% dividend yield backed by 54 consecutive years of increases, BDX trades at a significant discount to medtech peers [5]. The Edwards Critical Care acquisition adds AI-enabled clinical monitoring to BD's dominant positions in blood collection, medication management, and diagnostics, creating a connected care platform with long-term cross-selling potential [2].
The bear case centers on margin compression and M&A integration risk. GAAP profit margin of 4.19% (vs 24.9% adjusted operating margin) reflects heavy acquisition-related amortization, and ROE of 6.61% is well below the 15% target — indicating the M&A-heavy strategy has not yet translated into adequate shareholder returns [5]. Tariff headwinds reduced adjusted operating margin to 24.9% in Q3, and management continues to invest heavily in commercial and R&D capabilities [1]. Total debt remains elevated from the Edwards acquisition, with D/E at 69%. The GAAP trailing PE of 53x is misleading (driven by amortization-depressed earnings), but the gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings highlights the quality-of-earnings question for acquisitive medtech companies. Revenue growth of 4.4%, while accelerating, remains below the high-single-digit rates of premium medtech peers. Legacy surgical mesh litigation remains an unquantified tail risk. The Inspire Impact Score of -75 (MISALIGNED) due to Embryonic Stem Cells and LGBT Activism attributions creates alignment concerns for faith-based investors.
