Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NYSE ·United States
Becton, Dickinson and Company (BD) is a global medical technology company that manufactures and sells medical devices, instrument systems, and reagents. Operating through BD Medical, BD Life Sciences, and BD Interventional segments, the company produces syringes, blood collection systems, medication management solutions, diagnostic instruments, and surgical products. BD completed the $4.2 billion acquisition of Edwards Lifesciences' Critical Care product group in September 2024, adding AI-enabled clinical monitoring solutions. Founded in 1897 and headquartered in Franklin Lakes, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
BD offers a compelling combination of defensive healthcare positioning, dividend reliability, and embedded transformation optionality at an attractive valuation. At a forward P/E of 11.5x with raised full-year EPS guidance of $12.52-12.72 and a 2.78% dividend yield backed by 54 consecutive years of increases, BDX provides rare valuation compression in a quality medtech franchise [1][5]. The $4.2B Edwards Critical Care acquisition adds AI-enabled clinical monitoring capabilities to BD's dominant positions in blood collection, medication management, and diagnostics, creating a connected care platform with long-term revenue synergy potential [2]. Q2 FY2026 delivered an EPS beat of 3.5% above consensus, demonstrating execution discipline through the integration period.
The primary concern is that BD's transformation carries significant financial burden — $17.28B in total debt against only $816M cash leaves the balance sheet leveraged at D/E of 71.6%, while net profit margin of 5.87% reflects integration costs, Alaris remediation expenses, and ongoing surgical mesh litigation that compress returns well below medtech peer averages [1][5]. ROE of 6.67% is less than half the 15% target, indicating that the M&A-heavy strategy has not yet translated into adequate shareholder returns. Revenue growth of 2.6% FX-neutral is below the medtech industry growth rate, raising questions about whether the Edwards acquisition will meaningfully accelerate organic growth or simply add revenue at the cost of balance sheet strain. The legacy surgical mesh product liability litigation represents an unquantified tail risk that could generate additional charges [3].
