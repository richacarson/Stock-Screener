Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
HeartBeam is a medical technology company developing and commercializing a cable-free, ambulatory 12-lead ECG system that captures the heart's electrical signals in three dimensions. The system consists of a credit card-sized 3D ECG recording device, patient app, physician portal, and cloud-based algorithms for arrhythmia assessment and cardiac monitoring outside healthcare facilities. The company received FDA 510(k) clearance in December 2024 and began limited U.S. commercial launch in 2026.

Investment Thesis
HeartBeam has progressed significantly since its December 2024 FDA 510(k) clearance, signing initial commercial agreements with ClearCardio and Atelier Health to establish flagship sites across four major U.S. markets, targeting the ~5M patient direct-pay healthcare segment [1][2]. The Q1 2026 net loss narrowed 15% to $4.7M with operating cash burn down 19% to $3.6M, and the full-year burn is guided below $16M [1]. The April 2026 offering of $11.5M provides approximately 3-4 quarters of runway for commercialization. The ALIGN-ACS heart attack detection pilot and extended-wear 12-lead ECG patch prototype expand the addressable market beyond arrhythmia to acute coronary events and continuous monitoring, while the Mount Sinai AI-ECG collaboration positions HeartBeam as an AI-powered cardiac intelligence platform [1][3]. At ~$33M market cap, successful commercial traction could deliver substantial upside.
The bear case centers on execution risk, capital needs, and liquidity constraints. HeartBeam remains pre-revenue after 10+ years of development with zero commercial track record, and the stock has declined 34% from $0.89 to $0.59 since the prior screening [4]. Cash of $2.0M (March 31, 2026) plus the $11.5M offering provides limited runway — if commercialization expenses exceed guided burn, additional dilutive raises will be necessary on a share base that has already grown to ~55.5M shares [1]. Daily dollar volume of ~$1.0M is near the liquidity floor, creating position-sizing constraints for institutional investors. Competition from established players (iRhythm's Zio with $500M+ revenue, AliveCor's KardiaMobile) with physician relationships, distribution networks, and reimbursement infrastructure creates market penetration risk. Reimbursement uncertainty — whether insurers will cover home 12-lead ECG monitoring — limits the near-term addressable market to self-pay concierge practices.
