KE Holdings Inc. (Beike) is China's largest integrated online and offline platform for housing transactions and services, operating through Lianjia (owned brokerage), Beike (open platform connecting agents and developers), and expanding home renovation/furnishing and rental services. Founded in 2001, headquartered in Beijing, KE Holdings facilitates existing home sales, new home sales, home renovation, and rental transactions across 400+ cities. Q2 2026 revenue was RMB24.5B (-5.7% YoY) while net income doubled to RMB2.6B on cost optimization, with existing home GTV rising 8% YoY to RMB630B.

Investment Thesis
KE Holdings at 15.35x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.51 offers compelling value for China's dominant housing platform, which processed RMB888B in combined GTV in Q2 2026 with existing home transactions growing 8% YoY [1]. The profitability transformation is the key narrative — net income doubled in Q2 2026 to RMB2.6B with non-GAAP net margin reaching a three-year high of 13%, demonstrating that cost optimization and operational leverage can drive earnings growth even through revenue headwinds [1]. The near-minimal-debt balance sheet (D/E 22%, more cash than total debt) provides financial resilience through China's property cycle, and the platform model creates structural advantages through network effects across 119,000+ employees and 45,000+ connected stores. The home renovation and rental services diversification reduces dependence on cyclical transaction revenue.
The fundamental risk is China's structural property market headwinds and revenue trajectory. Revenue declined 19% in Q1 and 5.7% in Q2 2026, and while management attributes this to revenue recognition changes in rental services and renovation business recalibration, the pattern signals underlying demand uncertainty in China's real estate market [1][2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately 3% reflects a property sector that may face secular decline as China's demographics shift (aging population, urbanization saturation). As a China-domiciled company listed via ADR, BEKE carries geopolitical and regulatory risk including potential delisting concerns and VIE structure vulnerability. The profit margin of 5.32% is thin for a technology platform, and ROE of 7.01% suggests the company is not generating adequate returns on its equity base. Founder Zuo Hui's death in 2021 removed the visionary leader, and the current leadership has demonstrated effective cost management but not yet proven it can drive the next phase of growth innovation. AI Risk Protection (7/9): KE Holdings' core business of facilitating physical real estate transactions in China involves property viewings, local market expertise, regulatory compliance (hukou systems, purchase restrictions by city), and deeply relationship-driven negotiations that AI cannot structurally replace [1]. The 45,000+ connected stores and agent network create a physical-world moat, and China's fragmented regulatory environment across 400+ cities creates complexity that automated systems cannot navigate. AI tools can enhance agent productivity (virtual tours, automated matching, pricing analytics) but cannot substitute for the physical inspection, legal documentation, and trust-building required in high-value property transactions. The home renovation and rental services similarly involve physical-world operations with local supplier networks. However, AI-powered property search and virtual tour technology could gradually reduce the number of agents needed per transaction, creating moderate efficiency pressure on commission-based revenue.
