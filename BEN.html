Company Profile
Financials ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Franklin Resources, Inc., operating as Franklin Templeton, is a global investment management firm with record $1.8 trillion in assets under management. The company offers mutual funds, ETFs, separately managed accounts, and alternative investments across public and private markets. Founded in 1947 by Rupert Johnson Sr. (named after Benjamin Franklin), headquartered in San Mateo, California. CEO Jenny Johnson represents fourth-generation family leadership. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $2.36B (+14% YoY) with adjusted operating income up 35% YoY and margins guided to approach 30% by Q4.
9,800 Employees ·www.franklintempleton.com

Investment Thesis
Franklin Templeton at 11.21x forward earnings with a 3.81% dividend yield offers value and income backed by record momentum — Q3 FY2026 delivered $2.36B revenue (+14% YoY), adjusted operating income up 35% YoY to $508.9M, and record $1.8T AUM with $18.4B in quarterly long-term net inflows and record $122B in gross long-term inflows [1]. The margin expansion trajectory (approaching 30% by Q4 2026, guided to 29-30% in FY2027) validates the Legg Mason and Putnam integration thesis after years of post-acquisition margin compression. Private markets fundraising of ~$40B (exceeding the $25-30B target) and a record $28.6B institutional pipeline provide higher-fee revenue diversification away from fee-compressed traditional active management. The Johnson family's 42% voting control ensures long-term alignment and protection against activist disruption.
The structural bear case centers on the secular decline of traditional active management — passive funds and ETFs continue capturing market share from active managers, compressing fees industry-wide and threatening Franklin Templeton's core revenue model. ROE of 7.89% remains well below financial services peers, suggesting the business is not generating adequate returns on its equity base despite $1.8T in AUM. The payout ratio of 89.76% consumes nearly all earnings, leaving minimal reinvestment capacity for organic growth — the 5-year revenue CAGR of just 3% reflects a business that has barely grown organically despite massive acquisitions. CEO Johnson's own warning that AI could disrupt investment management acknowledges a risk the firm has not yet structurally addressed [2]. The removal of weapons exclusions from ESG funds and shifting sustainability priorities could create reputational risks for ESG-sensitive allocators [3].
