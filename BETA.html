Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
BETA Technologies is an electric aerospace company designing, manufacturing, and certifying electric aircraft, propulsion systems, and charging infrastructure. Products include the ALIA conventional fixed-wing electric aircraft (CX300 approaching FAA certification), the MV250 hybrid-electric VTOL for military applications (developed with GE Aerospace and Sikorsky), and the eIPP charging network deployed across 26+ states. The company signed a 10-year, $1B deal to supply electric motors to Eve Air Mobility. Public benefit corporation and Certified B Corp. IPO raised $1.01B in November 2025. Founded in 2017 by CEO Kyle Clark, headquartered in South Burlington, Vermont with 1,008 employees. Market cap is approximately $4.9B.

Investment Thesis
BETA Technologies is a founder-led electric aviation platform company with $3.9B+ in aircraft backlog (991 units), a $1B Eve motor supply deal, $1.48B in cash, and a genuine B Corp climate mission validated by CEO Kyle Clark's TIME100 Climate recognition [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue of $14.7M grew 146% YoY with full-year guidance raised to $42-50M. The CX300 conventional fixed-wing aircraft is approaching FAA type certification by late 2026/early 2027 — the most critical catalyst in the company's history. Gross margin of 60% on eIPP charging demonstrates strong unit economics, and the diversification into motor supply (Eve), military applications (MV250 with GE/Sikorsky), and autonomous flight creates multiple revenue streams beyond aircraft sales. At $4.9B market cap with $1.48B cash, the enterprise value of ~$3.4B against $4.9B+ in backlog provides favorable backlog-to-EV coverage.
The bear case centers on extreme capital intensity, certification risk, and execution uncertainty. Operating cash burn of $306M annually against $45M revenue means the company consumes $7 for every $1 earned — the $1.48B cash position provides approximately 5 years of runway at current rates, but any certification delay or backlog cancellation could trigger a capital raise at unfavorable terms [3]. Adjusted loss per share of $0.64 in Q2 was wider than the $0.46 consensus estimate, showing profitability remains distant. The stock trades at $20.90, down 39% from its $34 IPO price, reflecting market repricing of execution risk. The $3.9B aircraft backlog represents pre-certification orders — no production aircraft have been delivered, and FAA certification timelines are notoriously uncertain. Nearly 700 companies are competing in the eVTOL space, many better-funded, and safety concerns about lithium-ion batteries in aircraft could delay industry adoption [2].
