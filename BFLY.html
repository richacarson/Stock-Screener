Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Butterfly Network, Inc. develops and commercializes handheld, semiconductor-based whole-body ultrasound imaging systems powered by a single silicon chip (Ultrasound-on-Chip). The company's Butterfly iQ+ probe connects to smartphones and tablets, making point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) accessible to clinicians in emergency rooms, rural clinics, and developing nations. Butterfly integrates AI-powered software for automated image guidance, clinical decision support, and gestational age measurement. The Ultrasound-on-Chip has evolved into a foundational semiconductor platform, with third parties like Midjourney Medical building full-body scanners using 40 Butterfly chips. Q2 2026 revenue reached a record $32.6M (+39.5% YoY) with raised FY2026 guidance to $119-123M.

Investment Thesis
Butterfly Network is executing on a compelling vision of democratizing medical imaging through AI-powered semiconductor ultrasound. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $32.6M (+39.5% YoY) with gross margin expanding to 71.4% (from 63.7%) and adjusted EBITDA loss narrowing 78% to just $1.4M — approaching breakeven [1][5]. The raised FY2026 guidance to $119-123M reflects strong Embedded segment momentum, where Butterfly's Ultrasound-on-Chip is becoming a platform that others build on — validated by Midjourney Medical's 40-chip full-body scanner [2]. FDA-cleared AI tools (Gestational Age, HeartFocus) differentiate Butterfly from traditional ultrasound incumbents by enabling non-expert clinicians to perform diagnostic imaging. The stock has appreciated 96% in the last 52 weeks as the market recognizes the improving trajectory, and the $9.77 price reflects optimism about the transition to profitability [4].
The fundamental challenge is that Butterfly remains deeply unprofitable at -66.75% profit margin and -35.46% ROE, with sustained net losses against $121M guided revenue [4]. While adjusted EBITDA is approaching breakeven, GAAP profitability remains distant — Q2 2026 net loss was $12.9M despite record revenue [1]. The market cap implies a significant premium relative to current revenue, requiring extraordinary growth and margin expansion to justify. Competition from GE HealthCare, Philips, and other established medical imaging companies in the AI-powered ultrasound space could intensify as the market opportunity becomes clearer. Cash runway, while adequate for the near term, could require additional fundraising if the path to profitability takes longer than expected — any equity raise at current prices would be dilutive. The Embedded platform's success depends on third-party adoption scaling meaningfully beyond early partnerships, and the regulatory approval process for new AI features introduces timeline uncertainty.
