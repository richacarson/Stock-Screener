Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Farm Products — Agribusiness ·NYSE ·United States
Bunge Global SA is one of the world's leading agribusiness and food companies, operating in the origination, processing, and distribution of oilseeds, grains, and agricultural commodities. Following the completion of its merger with Viterra on July 2, 2025, Bunge is the world's largest global oilseed processor and a top-3 grain merchandiser alongside Cargill and ADM, with operations spanning over 40 countries. CEO Greg Heckman declared the combined company 'stronger, more agile and better positioned than at any point in our 200-year history.' Headquartered in St. Louis, Missouri.

Investment Thesis
Bunge Global offers a compelling value proposition as the world's largest oilseed processor trading at 12.5x forward earnings on raised FY2026 guidance of $9.00-$9.50 adjusted EPS (up from $7.50-$8.00), with a 2.60% dividend yield and 25 consecutive years of payments [1][6]. The transformative Viterra merger — completed July 2025 — created unmatched global scale, with Q1 2026 revenue of $21.86B nearly doubling year-over-year and grain merchandising volumes tripling from the integration [1]. The March 2026 Investor Day established a mid-cycle EPS baseline of approximately $13 with a target of $15+ by 2030, signaling substantial earnings upside as integration synergies materialize [2]. At $115.23 per share, the stock offers essential food security exposure at an attractive multiple with growing income support.
The bear case centers on margin fragility, integration execution, and ESG controversy. Profit margin of 1.16% provides virtually no earnings cushion — a 50bps decline in agricultural processing margins could eliminate profitability on $70B+ revenue [6]. GAAP earnings remain depressed by integration charges (GAAP P/E of 27.9 vs adjusted forward P/E of 12.5), and ROE of 5.96% is well below the 15% target, reflecting the near-term costs of absorbing Viterra [6]. The dividend payout ratio jumped to 73.8% (from 56%) as GAAP earnings fell while the dividend held, creating sustainability concerns if earnings don't recover [6]. Deforestation controversies are structurally significant: Friends of the Earth and Global Witness have documented supply chain links to Cerrado deforestation with ~95% of deforestation risk concentrated there, and Bunge has resisted the Cerrado Manifesto's 2020 cutoff date despite pressure from Walmart, Tesco, and Unilever [4][5]. These ESG risks create regulatory, reputational, and customer-retention headwinds that could accelerate under tightening EU deforestation regulation.
Key Catalysts
  1. Viterra synergy realization — integration of the world's largest oilseed processing and grain trading network driving raised FY2026 guidance to $9.00-$9.50 adjusted EPS, with mid-cycle baseline of $13 and $15+ target by 2030 [1][2]
  2. Commodity margin normalization — soybean crush margins and grain trading spreads were depressed; recovery would disproportionately benefit Bunge's expanded processing capacity on a $80B+ revenue base [1]
  3. Renewable fuel demand — growing renewable diesel mandates increase demand for soybean oil and vegetable oil feedstocks, structurally benefiting Bunge's unmatched crush capacity [2]
Key Risks
  1. Margin fragility — profit margin of 1.16% provides zero cushion; commodity margin compression on $70B+ revenue could rapidly erode earnings, and the elevated 73.8% payout ratio creates dividend sustainability risk if GAAP earnings don't recover [6]
  2. Deforestation and ESG controversy — Friends of the Earth and Global Witness have documented Bunge's supply chain links to Cerrado deforestation; tightening EU deforestation regulation and customer pressure from Walmart, Tesco, and Unilever create escalating regulatory and reputational risk [4][5]
  3. Integration execution risk — the Viterra merger is the largest in Bunge's history, and GAAP earnings remain depressed by integration costs (P/E 27.9); failure to achieve the $13+ mid-cycle EPS baseline would question the deal's value creation [1][2]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Bunge's core business — originating, transporting, processing, and distributing physical agricultural commodities across 40+ countries — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. Soybean crushing, grain logistics, port operations, storage elevators, and crop origination from millions of farmers require massive physical infrastructure and deeply embedded supply chain relationships that AI cannot replicate or substitute. The Viterra merger further entrenched Bunge's physical-world moat with expanded processing plants and logistics assets globally [2]. AI may improve demand forecasting, logistics routing, and commodity risk management, but these are efficiency tools, not substitution risks. Analysts have not flagged AI as a disruption risk for agricultural commodity processors. AI data center expansion indirectly supports food demand as global economic activity grows.
