BHP Group is the world's largest diversified mining company by market capitalization, producing iron ore, copper, metallurgical coal, and potash across operations in Australia, the Americas, and globally. FY2026 (ending June 2026) delivered revenue of US$58.8B (+15% YoY), underlying EBITDA of US$33B (+27%), and underlying attributable profit of US$13.2B (+30%), with copper surpassing iron ore as the largest EBITDA contributor at 54% of group earnings [1]. CEO Mike Henry transitioned to successor Brandon Craig in mid-2026. Founded 1851, headquartered in Melbourne, Australia; listed on NYSE as an ADR.

Investment Thesis
BHP at $81.87 with a $208B market cap is the world's largest diversified miner executing a validated commodity pivot — FY2026 revenue rose 15% to US$58.8B with underlying EBITDA climbing 27% to US$33B as copper became the dominant earnings driver at 54% of group EBITDA on 35% higher realized prices [1]. The strategic repositioning toward future-facing commodities is generating tangible results: ROE of 24%, D/E of 50%, and Forward P/E of 17.1x offer reasonable value for a commodity leader positioned at the intersection of AI infrastructure demand (copper), energy transition (electrification), and food security (Jansen potash approaching production). The 4.2% dividend yield provides meaningful income, and operating cash flow of US$21.8B funds both capital returns and growth investment.
The bear case centers on commodity cyclicality and execution risk. BHP's FY2026 results were amplified by a 35% copper price surge and record iron ore production — tailwinds unlikely to repeat, with Chinese property sector restructuring threatening iron ore demand and global recession risk potentially compressing copper prices from elevated levels [1]. The variable dividend policy produced a negative 5-year CAGR of -8.5%, with payouts declining over 80% from FY2022 peak, making BHP unreliable as an income investment despite the current yield. The US$2.3B Jansen potash impairment in FY2026 highlights execution risk on the company's largest capital project [1]. The Samarco/Fundao litigation continues with a UK Stage 2 trial pending and 200,000+ remaining claimants, carrying multi-billion dollar tail risk [3]. The CEO transition from Henry to Craig introduces leadership uncertainty, and profit margin of 16.7% below the 20% target reflects the capital-intensive nature of mining.
Key Catalysts
  1. Copper demand tailwinds from AI data center construction, EV adoption, and grid electrification — BHP's ~2 million tonnes of annual copper production at 54% of group EBITDA positions it as a primary beneficiary of secular demand growth driven by the energy transition [1]
  2. Jansen Stage 1 potash approaching first production with Stage 2 approved, adding an entirely new commodity vertical addressing the US$20B+ global potash market for food security with a projected 50+ year mine life [1]
  3. Valuation re-rating potential as copper grows to 60%+ of earnings and thermal coal exits by 2030, potentially narrowing the commodity-discount multiple toward industrial metals peers trading at 20-25x forward earnings
Key Risks
  1. Commodity price cyclicality — the 35% copper price increase and record iron ore production in FY2026 may represent a near-term peak; Chinese property sector restructuring directly threatens iron ore demand, and global recession could compress both copper and iron ore simultaneously [1]
  2. Samarco/Fundao dam litigation tail risk — UK Stage 2 trial pending with 200,000+ remaining claimants could result in multi-billion dollar additional liability beyond the US$5.3B+ already settled, plus ongoing Australian tax and royalty disputes add further contingent exposure [3]
  3. Variable dividend unreliability — the negative 5-year dividend CAGR of -8.5% and 80%+ peak-to-trough payout decline demonstrate that BHP's commodity-linked policy delivers highly volatile income streams incompatible with consistent dividend investing expectations
