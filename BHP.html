Company Profile
Materials ·Mining — Diversified Metals ·NYSE ·Australia
BHP Group Limited is the world's largest diversified mining company by market capitalization, producing iron ore, copper, potash, metallurgical coal, and other commodities across major assets in Australia, the Americas, and globally. FY2026 (ending June 2026) delivered record results: revenue of US$58.76B (+15%), underlying EBITDA of US$33B (+27%, ~60% margin), underlying attributable profit of US$13B (+30%), and net operating cash flows of US$21.8B. The company declared a record four-year dividend of US$8.7B. CEO Mike Henry is transitioning to Brandon Craig. Headquartered in Melbourne, Australia; listed on NYSE as an ADR.

Investment Thesis
BHP delivered record FY2026 results — US$58.76B revenue (+15%), US$33B underlying EBITDA (+27%, ~60% margin), US$13B underlying profit (+30%), and US$21.8B operating cash flow — driven by record copper prices (+35%) and iron ore production [1]. At 13.70x forward earnings with a 3.74% dividend yield and net debt declining to US$8.7B, BHP offers exceptional value for a diversified mining leader positioned at the intersection of AI infrastructure demand (copper), energy transition (electrification), and global food security (Jansen potash reaching first production FY2027) [4]. The petroleum exit and copper pivot are now fully validated by results, ROE of 24.71% demonstrates strong capital efficiency, and the US$8.7B total dividend is the highest in four years [1][5]. The orderly CEO transition from Mike Henry to Brandon Craig provides continuity.
BHP's commodity exposure creates inherent cyclicality — the 15% revenue growth and 35% copper price tailwind in FY2026 may not be sustained, and iron ore prices face Chinese demand risk as property sector restructuring continues; the 5-year dividend CAGR of -0.9% demonstrates that shareholders bear the full volatility of commodity cycles through BHP's payout-ratio-linked policy [5]. The Fundao dam disaster litigation continues with a UK Stage 2 trial in late 2026 covering causation and quantum, which could result in multi-billion dollar additional liability alongside the 200,000+ remaining claimants [3]. The CEO transition from Henry to Craig introduces execution risk during major capital deployment (US$12.8B Jansen, copper expansions), and the Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects the absence of faith-alignment data for this Australian-domiciled miner. Profit margin of 18.97% is below the 20% target, and the trailing P/E of 20.79 suggests the market is cautious about earnings sustainability at peak commodity prices.
