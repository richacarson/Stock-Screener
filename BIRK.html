Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Footwear & Accessories ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Birkenstock Holding plc designs, manufactures, and sells footbed-based footwear products including sandals, closed-toe shoes, and skincare products across the Americas, EMEA, and Asia Pacific. Founded in 1774, the company owns its entire production in Germany and has a 250-year heritage of cork-latex footbed technology.

Investment Thesis
Birkenstock delivered Q2 FY2026 revenue of €618M (up 14% constant currency) with double-digit growth across all regions, confirming full-year guidance of 13-15% constant currency growth despite tariff and geopolitical headwinds [1]. The 250-year-old brand holds a monopoly position in premium footbed footwear with unmatched heritage and proprietary technology. Net profit margin expanded to 16.3%, and the company's German manufacturing commitment and refusal to discount create a durable competitive moat. At 16.45x forward PE, valuation is reasonable for a growing luxury/premium consumer brand.
The primary risk is the gap between reported (7.7%) and constant currency (14%) revenue growth, exposing significant FX headwinds that may persist. EPS declined 22.1% in the most recent period despite revenue growth, suggesting margin pressure from tariffs, production capacity expansion costs, or FX translation. The PEG of 1.26 is reasonable but not cheap, and the company's concentration in a single product category (footbed footwear) limits diversification. As a relatively new public company (IPO October 2023), the stock lacks a long public track record, and L Catterton's eventual exit could create share price pressure.
Key Catalysts
  1. APAC revenue growing 22% represents a significant untapped growth vector — Asia Pacific is underpenetrated relative to Americas and EMEA market potential
  2. Production capacity expansion enabling supply to match growing demand, with new manufacturing facilities increasing output for closed-toe and professional categories
  3. Full-year constant currency guidance of 13-15% revenue growth confirmed despite tariff headwinds, demonstrating brand pricing power and demand resilience
Key Risks
  1. FX headwinds creating a significant gap between constant currency (14%) and reported (7.7%) revenue growth, with EPS declining 22% despite topline growth
  2. Single category concentration — Birkenstock's business is overwhelmingly dependent on footbed footwear, limiting diversification if the category matures
  3. L Catterton (LVMH-affiliated) exit overhang — as a major post-IPO shareholder, eventual share sales could create price pressure
