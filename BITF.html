Bitfarms Ltd., rebranding to Keel Infrastructure (ticker changed to KEEL effective April 6, 2026), is a North American digital infrastructure and energy company pivoting from Bitcoin mining to high-performance computing (HPC) and AI data centers. The company re-domiciled from Canada to the U.S., relocated headquarters to New York, and is selling Bitcoin holdings to fund its AI infrastructure strategy. Investment Thesis
Bitfarms/Keel Infrastructure holds a 2.1 GW North American power portfolio and approximately $197M in Bitcoin, with concrete plans to convert sites for HPC/AI workloads targeting Nvidia Vera Rubin GPU hosting [2]. The Panther Creek expansion (350 MW) has secured $300M in project financing, and six experienced AI data center executives were hired in March 2026. At $3.29 per share ($2.0B market cap), the stock trades at a fraction of its power portfolio replacement value if the AI infrastructure pivot succeeds. The rebranding to Keel Infrastructure and U.S. re-domiciliation could unlock institutional investor interest.
The bear case is severe: Q1 2026 revenue declined 23% YoY to $37M with a net loss of $145.4M, operating loss of $98M, and profit margin of -230.59% [1]. Debt-to-equity of 316% and ROE of -75.64% reflect a deeply distressed balance sheet burdened by convertible debt obligations [4]. The HPC/AI pivot has generated zero revenue and competes against hyperscalers and established data center operators (Equinix, Digital Realty) with dramatically greater scale, capital, and customer relationships. The company is selling its Bitcoin holdings ($20M of BTC in Q1) to fund operations — a sign of capital constraint. Financial misclassification allegations regarding digital asset sales and warrant accounting raise governance concerns [3]. The serial identity changes (crypto miner to Keel Infrastructure) suggest a company searching for viability rather than executing against a proven strategy.
Key Catalysts
  1. Washington site HPC/AI conversion completion by Q4 2026 with Nvidia Vera Rubin GPU hosting could generate the company's first AI infrastructure revenue and validate the pivot thesis
  2. Panther Creek 350 MW expansion with $300M secured financing provides long-term growth capacity if hyperscaler or enterprise customer contracts materialize
  3. U.S. re-domiciliation and Keel Infrastructure rebranding could attract institutional investors who previously avoided Canadian-domiciled crypto mining stocks
Key Risks
  1. Zero HPC/AI revenue track record creates existential execution risk — the company must simultaneously build AI infrastructure, attract customers, and manage declining mining revenue during a cash-constrained transition period
  2. Debt-to-equity of 316% with convertible notes represents severe dilution risk if the stock price remains depressed and notes convert at unfavorable terms, further eroding existing shareholder value
  3. Financial misclassification allegations regarding digital asset sales and warrant accounting raise governance concerns about management transparency during a period requiring investor trust in an unproven strategic pivot
