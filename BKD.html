Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Brookdale Senior Living is the largest operator of senior living communities in the United States, operating 500+ communities across independent living, assisted living, memory care, and continuing care retirement. Headquartered in Brentwood, Tennessee. Under CEO Nikolas W. Stengle (since October 2025), the company is executing portfolio optimization and occupancy recovery. Q2 2026 occupancy reached 82.4% (+230 bps YoY) with RevPAR growth of 8.2%, net income of $23M (vs. -$43M loss YoY), and Adjusted EBITDA of $122M (+4.3%). Full-year 2026 guidance: 8-9% RevPAR growth, $502-516M Adjusted EBITDA. All 2027 maturities refinanced.
22,440 Employees ·www.brookdale.com

Investment Thesis
Brookdale Senior Living at $10.09 with a $2.4B market cap offers a demographic-driven turnaround thesis anchored by the most powerful secular trend in U.S. healthcare — Baby Boomers turning 80+ over the next decade [1]. As the largest U.S. senior living operator with 500+ communities, Brookdale is positioned to capture accelerating demand with occupancy recovering to 82.4% (+230 bps YoY), RevPAR growing 8.2%, and Q2 2026 net income of $23M versus a -$43M loss a year earlier [1]. The profitability inflection is materializing — Adjusted EBITDA reached $122M in Q2 (+4.3%) with full-year guidance of $502-516M, and all 2027 debt maturities have been refinanced. AI resilience is exceptionally strong at 8/10 — hands-on caregiving for elderly residents is one of the most AI-immune business models in the economy. The Inspire Impact Score of 31 (ALIGNED) adds appeal for faith-based investors.
The bear case is dominated by balance sheet risk and the long road to sustained GAAP profitability. Total debt of $5.49B against negative stockholders' equity (-$0.12/share book value) and $390M in cash places Brookdale in financially distressed territory [5]. The company remains near breakeven with -4.72% profit margin and forward P/E of 84x on just $0.12 expected EPS, making traditional valuation nearly impossible. Revenue declined 11.6% YoY in Q2 2026 to $718.6M as community dispositions shrink the portfolio, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately 0% indicates the company has failed to translate demographic tailwinds into revenue growth despite an aging population [1][5]. The stock's 16.7% decline from $12.12 to $10.09 since August 2026 reflects market skepticism about the turnaround trajectory. The senior living industry faces structural headwinds including chronic labor shortages driving wage inflation, rising insurance costs, and regulatory burden. CEO Stengle is the latest in a series of leadership changes, and many communities remain below optimal occupancy levels. Interest expense on $5.49B in debt consumes significant cash flow, and until Brookdale achieves sustained deleveraging and GAAP profitability, the equity remains speculative.
