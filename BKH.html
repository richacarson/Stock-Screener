Company Profile
Utilities ·Regulated Electric & Gas Utility ·NYSE ·United States
Black Hills Corporation is a regulated electric and natural gas utility serving approximately 1.3 million customers across eight states in the western United States, including South Dakota, Wyoming, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska, and Arkansas. Q2 2026 revenue was $452.8M (+3.1% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.54 (up from $0.38). FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance reaffirmed at $4.25-$4.45. The company has identified a data center pipeline exceeding 3 GW, with approximately 600 MW from Microsoft and Meta included in the current financial plan through 2030. A merger with NorthWestern Energy is advancing, with CEO Linn Evans planning to retire upon completion. Founded in 1941, headquartered in Rapid City, South Dakota.

Investment Thesis
Black Hills Corporation offers a compelling income investment as a Dividend King with 56 consecutive years of dividend growth and 83 years of consecutive payments, yielding 3.66% at a reasonable 16.89x forward earnings [1][4]. The 'Silicon Prairie' data center strategy — with a pipeline exceeding 3 GW and 600 MW from Microsoft and Meta in the five-year capital plan — provides a rare growth catalyst for a regulated utility [1]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.54 (+42% YoY) demonstrated rate recovery lifting operating results, and FY2026 guidance of $4.25-$4.45 was reaffirmed with management targeting the upper half of the 4-6% long-term growth rate [1]. AI resilience is exceptional: regulated utilities are structurally immune to AI disruption while directly benefiting from AI-driven electricity demand. The Inspire Impact Score of 62 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based investor allocation. The pending NorthWestern Energy merger would create scale and diversification advantages.
The bear case centers on below-average returns, merger risk, and utility-sector structural constraints. ROE of 8.0% significantly trails the 15% target, reflecting the regulated return environment where state commissions cap profitability [4]. D/E of 120.4% is elevated even by utility standards, constraining balance sheet flexibility for the capital deployment needed to serve data center loads. The NorthWestern Energy merger introduces significant execution risk — Q2 2026 already showed $0.04/share in merger-related charges, and CEO Evans's planned retirement means the combined entity will be led by NorthWestern's Brian Bird, introducing cultural and strategic uncertainty [1][3]. The 72.2% payout ratio limits room for dividend acceleration, and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 4.78% trails the 6% target. Innovation and inspiration scores are weak at 3/10 each, reflecting inherent limitations of a regulated utility. The data center pipeline of 3 GW is largely aspirational — only 600 MW is in the current plan, and hyperscale customers could shift locations if power costs or regulatory conditions change.
