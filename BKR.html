Investment Thesis
Baker Hughes is an energy technology transformation story validated by record-breaking order momentum — Q2 2026 orders surged 49% YoY to $10.5B with IET orders of $7.1B (2.2x book-to-bill), driving record RPO of $40.1B including record IET RPO of $37.1B [1]. IET EBITDA margin of 20.6% (up 16% YoY) demonstrates the profitability of the technology-focused strategy, and management raised the Horizon 2 (2026-2028) outlook to exceed $45B. H1 2026 net income of $1.6B (up 46% from $1.1B in H1 2025) and Q2 free cash flow of $1.1B (up from $239M YoY) show improving earnings quality. D/E of 81% is well-managed, ROE of 16.5% exceeds targets, and the dividend yield of 1.4% with a conservative 30% payout ratio leaves substantial room for growth investment. CEO Simonelli's 9-year tenure, 1,400+ patents, and $600M R&D budget provide strategic continuity and innovation depth.
The primary risk is the disconnect between surging orders and flat revenue. H1 2026 revenue of $13.3B was flat vs 2025, and Q2 revenue declined 2% YoY despite the explosive order growth [1]. Forward P/E of 23.5 and PEG of 2.66 price in future growth that has not yet materialized in the top line. OFSE revenue declined 5% YoY in Q2 as North American upstream spending moderates, and this segment remains exposed to oil price volatility and OPEC+ production decisions. The massive $37.1B IET backlog carries multi-year execution risk — LNG project timelines can shift based on permitting, financing, and geopolitical factors. Profit margin of ~12% remains below the 20% target, and while IET margins are expanding, OFSE margins contracted (17.5% EBITDA margin, down 11% YoY). Glassdoor reviews citing 'huge middle management problem' and 'frequent layoffs' suggest the organizational culture may struggle to execute the technology transformation at pace [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record IET orders of $7.1B (2.2x book-to-bill) and RPO of $37.1B in Q2 2026, with management raising Horizon 2 outlook to >$45B, provide exceptional multi-year revenue visibility for the energy technology transformation [1]
  2. IET EBITDA margin expansion to 20.6% (up 16% YoY) as the higher-margin technology business grows to represent >50% of earnings, potentially driving consolidated margin expansion as revenue converts from backlog [1]
  3. Free cash flow surged to $1.1B in Q2 (from $239M YoY) with conservative 30% payout ratio, enabling simultaneous dividend growth, share buybacks, and R&D investment in climate technology and industrial AI solutions
Key Risks
  1. OFSE segment revenue declined 5% YoY in Q2 with EBITDA down 11% — continued North American upstream spending moderation or OPEC+ production shifts could further pressure the traditional services business [1]
  2. Forward PEG of 2.66 prices in sustained growth that has not yet materialized in revenue (flat H1 2026 vs 2025) — any failure to convert the record $40.1B backlog into revenue growth could trigger valuation compression
  3. Massive IET backlog ($37.1B) carries multi-year execution risk — LNG project timelines can shift based on permitting, financing, geopolitical factors, and Glassdoor-cited organizational challenges with middle management and leadership accountability [2]
