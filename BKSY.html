Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
BlackSky Technology Inc. is a real-time geospatial intelligence and global monitoring company. It owns and operates a constellation of high-revisit, high-resolution small satellites and an AI-enabled analytics platform (Spectra) that provides real-time intelligence to government defense and intelligence agencies and commercial customers. The company's Gen-3 satellite constellation integrates on-board AI processing for automated target recognition, change detection, and battle damage assessment. Headquartered in Herndon, Virginia.
340 Employees ·www.blacksky.com

Investment Thesis
BlackSky is at a genuine inflection point as its Gen-3 satellite constellation transitions from development to revenue generation, with 2026 revenue guidance raised to $130-150M representing 22-41% growth over 2025's $106.6M [1][3]. The company has secured $160M+ in contract awards year-to-date through Q1, including a near-$30M Gen-3 Assured subscription with an international defense customer and new U.S. government R&D contracts for automated target recognition and battle damage detection [2][3]. Subscription-based space intelligence revenue carries ~80% gross margins, and the path to adjusted EBITDA breakeven ($12-24M guidance for 2026) is becoming visible. Six analysts rate the stock 'Strong Buy' with an average $40.50 price target, implying 85% upside from the current $21.86 [5].
The bear case centers on execution risk and capital intensity. Q1 2026 revenue of $20.8M annualizes to only ~$83M, meaning the company needs dramatic back-half acceleration to hit the $130-150M guidance range, likely dependent on Gen-3 satellite deployments proceeding on schedule and contract conversions materializing [1]. The -89% profit margin and 261% debt-to-equity ratio reflect a company still burning significant cash, and the $800M market cap against only $106.6M trailing revenue (7.5x P/S) assumes substantial growth materialization. Government budget uncertainty under evolving defense priorities, concentration risk in a small number of large contracts, and potential equity dilution to fund the Arrow program ($115.5M cash may be insufficient for full constellation buildout) all represent meaningful downside risks [3][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Gen-3 satellite constellation reaching full operational capability in 2026, enabling over 50% subscription revenue growth and path to positive adjusted EBITDA of $12-24M as guided [1][3]
  2. Conversion of early-access Gen-3 trials with government customers into long-term funded subscriptions, potentially replicating the $30M international defense contract across additional allied nations [2]
  3. U.S. government R&D contract awards for automated target recognition and battle damage detection creating pipeline for future operational contracts as Gen-3 AI capabilities mature [3]
Key Risks
  1. Q1 2026 revenue of $20.8M requires massive back-half acceleration to achieve $130-150M full-year guidance — any Gen-3 deployment delays or contract timing slippage would force a guidance cut [1]
  2. Continued cash burn at -89% profit margin with only $115.5M in cash may necessitate additional equity dilution, particularly if Arrow spacecraft development requires more capital than planned [5]
  3. Government budget uncertainty and potential shifts in defense spending priorities could delay or reduce contract awards, especially for a small contractor competing against larger primes for classified intelligence budgets [3]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): BlackSky IS AI infrastructure — its core business integrates artificial intelligence directly into satellite hardware and ground analytics. The company's Gen-3 satellites perform on-board AI processing for automated target recognition, change detection, and anomaly detection, making it a builder of AI capability rather than a potential victim of disruption [2][3]. Physical satellite constellations in orbit, ground station networks, and classified government contracts create structural moats that AI alone cannot replicate. Competitors would need to launch actual spacecraft, obtain security clearances, and build classified processing infrastructure. The primary risk is that larger defense primes or well-funded competitors could develop competing AI-enabled satellite systems, but BlackSky's head start in real-time AI-integrated tasking provides meaningful protection. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI is BlackSky's core product. The Spectra AI analytics platform and Gen-3 on-board AI processing generate direct, quantifiable revenue through defense subscriptions including the near-$30M Gen-3 Assured international defense contract [2][3].
