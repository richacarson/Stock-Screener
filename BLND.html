Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYSE ·United States
Blend Labs provides a cloud-based software platform powering the digital interface between financial services firms and consumers. The platform supports mortgages, home equity loans, personal loans, credit cards, and deposit accounts, and includes Autopilot, an agentic AI-powered loan origination agent that completes reviews in 15 seconds. Five lenders including Onity Mortgage are now commercial Autopilot customers, with 20% of customers adopting the technology within the first month. Founded in 2012 by CEO Nima Ghamsari, headquartered in San Francisco. Market cap ~$360M.
419 Employees ·www.blend.com

Investment Thesis
Blend Labs is approaching a profitability inflection point with accelerating non-GAAP margins. Q2 2026 revenue of $33.8M (+7% YoY) reached the high end of guidance with non-GAAP operating income surging 51% to $7.0M — a 21% non-GAAP operating margin [4]. Q1 demonstrated even stronger growth at $30.8M (+15% YoY) with 15 new deals including a top 20 bank e-close agreement [1]. Autopilot, the agentic AI that processes loan origination in 15 seconds, now has five commercial customers with 20% adoption in the first month, attacking the $11,000 per-mortgage cost structure [2][3]. At $1.52 per share with a clean balance sheet ($44.9M cash, $2M debt), Forward P/E of 23.68 and PEG of 1.18 on a 22.91% 3-year EPS growth forecast, the valuation is attractive if the profitability trajectory continues [5]. The Inspire Impact Score of 35 (ALIGNED) is positive for faith-based investors.
The bear case centres on Blend's small scale, mortgage cyclicality, and execution uncertainty. With 419 employees and $130M revenue, Blend lacks the resources to compete with ICE Mortgage Technology's AI investment and product breadth. The Mortgage Suite (56% of revenue) is directly exposed to rising mortgage rates — Fannie Mae reduced Q2 and full-year 2026 market outlooks, and management warned that a large customer volume roll-off could create ~100bps market share headwind [4]. Q2 revenue growth decelerated to 7% from 15% in Q1, and shares fell on guidance despite the margin beat [4]. The stock has declined 58% over the past year ($3.60 → $1.52), and the ~$360M market cap limits institutional interest. GAAP profitability remains marginal at 0.02% profit margin, and the 5-year revenue trajectory is distorted by the Title365 divestiture to ~0% CAGR. The ISS Governance QualityScore of 10 (highest risk decile) and multi-class share structure create governance concerns.

Key Risks

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Blend's core platform — digital loan origination and consumer banking workflows — occupies a defensible position within bank IT infrastructure. Mortgage origination requires extensive regulatory compliance (TRID, HMDA, ECOA), lender-specific workflow customization, and deep integrations with loan origination systems that create meaningful switching costs [1]. Banks cannot simply replace Blend with a generic AI tool because regulatory requirements demand specialized, validated software. However, generalized AI workflow automation tools and vertical fintech AI startups could gradually commoditize document processing and decisioning features.
