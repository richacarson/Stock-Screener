Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NasdaqCM ·United States
Blink Charging Co. owns, operates, and provides electric vehicle charging equipment and networked EV charging services in the United States and internationally. The company offers Level 2 and DC fast charging stations for residential, commercial, fleet, and public applications, generating revenue through hardware sales, charging services, network fees, and fleet management. FY2025 revenue was $103.5M (down from $124M in FY2024), but service revenue grew 44.7% to $49.3M and now represents 48% of total revenue. CEO Mike Battaglia succeeded founder Brendan Jones in February 2025. The SEC investigation (opened 2020) closed in January 2025 with no enforcement action recommended.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): EV charging is fundamentally a physical infrastructure business — AI structurally cannot replace the need for deployed chargers, electrical connections, permitting, site maintenance, and grid integration [2]. Autonomous vehicles will require more, not fewer, charging stations. The EV transition creates accelerating demand for physical charging infrastructure regardless of AI developments. Blink's network of deployed chargers, site agreements, and utility interconnections represent physical assets that cannot be replicated by software. The only AI-adjacent risk is that competitors with superior AI capabilities (Tesla's site optimization, ChargePoint's network intelligence) could achieve better site selection, demand forecasting, and dynamic pricing — but this is competitive advantage within the industry, not AI disruption of the business model itself. AI Opportunity (0/1): Blink's AI deployments — predictive maintenance, grid load-balancing, Stable Auto smart charging partnership — are basic operational efficiency tools that improve uptime and utilization but do not generate standalone AI revenue or create commercially distinctive AI products [3].
