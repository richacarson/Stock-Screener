Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·Canada
Brookfield Corporation is a global alternative asset manager with over $1 trillion in assets under management across infrastructure, renewable power and transition, private equity, real estate, credit, and insurance solutions. The company operates and invests in long-life, high-quality assets in more than 30 countries. CEO Bruce Flatt (24+ year tenure, transitioning to Chairman) has positioned Brookfield as a leading enabler of the AI-driven infrastructure buildout through data centers, renewable energy, and the Radiant compute platform. BAM (the listed asset management subsidiary) raised a record $77B in Q2 2026 with fee-related earnings of $808M (+20% YoY). Market cap is $93.75B.

Investment Thesis
Brookfield Corporation is uniquely positioned at the intersection of two structural mega-trends: the multi-trillion-dollar AI infrastructure buildout and the global energy transition. With over $1 trillion in AUM, fee-bearing capital of $672B (+19% YoY), and a $100B AI infrastructure investment program backed by NVIDIA, KIA, QIA, OpenAI, and Anthropic, Brookfield is one of the few firms globally that can integrate land, power, connectivity, and compute at hyperscale [1][2]. BAM raised a company record $77B in Q2 2026 alone, with fee-related earnings of $808M (+20% YoY) and distributable earnings of $707M (+15% YoY) [3]. Forward P/E of approximately 14x on distributable earnings and PEG of ~0.88 suggest the market significantly undervalues Brookfield's earnings trajectory. The company's plan value per share substantially exceeds the $41.97 stock price, and management has been repurchasing over $1B of shares year-to-date. The 2 GW+ of data center capacity, Radiant compute platform, and $5B Bloom Energy partnership generate tangible, growing AI revenue [2].
The primary risk is Brookfield's complex corporate structure, which obscures true economic earnings behind GAAP reporting. GAAP ROE of 2.26% and profit margin of 1.77% are structurally misleading for an asset manager that earns primarily through management fees, carried interest, and realized gains — but they make the stock screen poorly on quantitative metrics [5]. Total debt of $269B against $14.9B cash creates significant leverage, though most sits at the portfolio company level with limited recourse to the parent. Revenue has been volatile due to asset sales, mark-to-market effects, and corporate restructuring (estimated -3% 5-year CAGR). Net insider selling ($12.4M Cockwell, $5M Lawson vs. $125K Pollock) sends mixed signals despite the corporate buyback program. Greenwashing allegations from environmental groups in Maine regarding Kennebec River dams and scrutiny for environmental practices in Brazil complicate the ESG narrative [4]. Execution risk on the $100B AI infrastructure program is material — deploying capital of that scale requires sustained demand for AI compute over a multi-decade horizon, and any cyclical slowdown in hyperscaler capex could delay returns.
