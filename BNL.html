Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT — Diversified Net Lease ·NYSE ·United States
Broadstone Net Lease is an industrial-focused, internally managed net lease REIT that invests in, owns, and manages primarily single-tenant commercial real estate properties net leased on a long-term basis. The company owns 773 properties across 41.9 million square feet with 99.8% occupancy, with over 50% of annual base rent from industrial tenants. The company operates a disciplined acquisition and build-to-suit development strategy with a $382 million committed pipeline. CEO John Moragne was promoted internally from General Counsel (2016) through COO (2018) to CEO (2023). Headquartered in Rochester, New York.

Investment Thesis
Broadstone Net Lease offers a defensible 5.8% dividend yield backed by 99.8% occupancy across 773 properties, $454M in FY2025 revenue (up 5.2%), and AFFO growth guidance of $1.53-$1.57 per share for 2026 [1]. Q1 2026 exceeded expectations with AFFO of $0.38/share (5.6% YoY growth), EPS beating consensus by 38.7%, and $171.9M deployed across acquisitions and build-to-suit developments [1]. The industrial-focused portfolio (50%+ of rent) differentiates BNL from diversified net lease peers, and the $382M build-to-suit pipeline provides development-driven growth beyond pure acquisitions. At $21.09 per share and 13.6x FY2026 AFFO midpoint, the stock trades at a reasonable valuation for a mid-cap net lease REIT with improving operational momentum. The lean 62-person team with CEO Moragne's decade-long internal tenure demonstrates efficient execution and strong organizational culture.
The bear case centers on BNL's limited scale, modest growth trajectory, and REIT structural constraints. Revenue growth of 5.2% YoY and AFFO growth of ~5-6% trail higher-growth REIT peers, limiting total return potential beyond the dividend [1]. GAAP earnings fell 41% in FY2025 due to a large one-off loss, compressing GAAP net profit margin to 21.9% from 37.3% the prior year and inflating the GAAP P/E to 40x [5]. The 86.5% D/E ratio constrains balance sheet capacity for acquisitions, and rising interest rates would increase refinancing costs on the debt stack. GAAP ROE of 3.2% reflects the capital-intensive nature of the REIT model. The dividend growth rate of 3.1% barely keeps pace with inflation, and the dividend has only been paid for 6 years since the 2020 IPO — a short track record compared to established dividend aristocrats [5]. Competition for net lease properties from larger, better-capitalized REITs (Realty Income at $70B+ market cap) compresses cap rates and limits BNL's ability to acquire high-quality properties at attractive returns.
Key Catalysts
  1. Build-to-suit pipeline expansion to $382M providing development-driven growth with higher returns than traditional acquisitions, plus $500-625M in total 2026 investment guidance [1]
  2. Industrial portfolio repositioning — over 50% of annual base rent from industrial tenants benefiting from reshoring, e-commerce logistics growth, and manufacturing expansion [1][2]
  3. AFFO growth acceleration — Q1 2026 AFFO grew 5.6% YoY and exceeded forecasts, with same-store contractual rent escalators providing organic growth visibility [1]
Key Risks
  1. Interest rate sensitivity — rising rates increase BNL's refinancing costs on the debt stack (D/E 86.5%) and compress property valuations, constraining acquisition and development activity [5]
  2. Scale disadvantage versus larger net lease REITs (Realty Income at $70B+ market cap vs BNL at ~$4.3B) limits cost of capital, competitive positioning for acquisitions, and investor visibility [1]
  3. Tenant credit risk in a potential recession — net lease REITs depend on tenant solvency to maintain the 99.8% occupancy rate, and industrial and restaurant tenants face cyclical business pressure that could lead to lease defaults [1]
