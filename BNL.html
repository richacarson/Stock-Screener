Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT — Diversified Net Lease ·NYSE ·United States
Broadstone Net Lease is an industrial-focused, internally managed net lease REIT that invests in, owns, and manages primarily single-tenant commercial real estate properties net leased on a long-term basis. The company owns a $5.8B+ portfolio of 773 properties across 41.9 million square feet with 99.8% occupancy, with over 50% of annual base rent from industrial tenants. Q2 2026 FFO of $0.39/share beat estimates, revenue of $122.31M grew ~8.2% YoY, and 2026 AFFO guidance of $1.53-$1.57/share was reaffirmed [1]. CEO John Moragne was promoted internally from General Counsel (2016) through COO (2018) to CEO (2023). Headquartered in Rochester, New York.
62 Employees ·www.broadstone.com

Investment Thesis
Broadstone Net Lease offers a defensible 5.57% dividend yield backed by 99.8% occupancy across 773 properties, with Q2 2026 FFO of $0.39/share beating estimates and revenue of $122.31M growing approximately 8.2% YoY [1]. 2026 AFFO guidance of $1.53-$1.57/share was reaffirmed, representing approximately 5-6% AFFO growth driven by acquisitions, build-to-suit completions, and contractual rent escalators. The industrial-focused portfolio (50%+ of rent) differentiates BNL from diversified net lease peers and provides exposure to reshoring, e-commerce logistics, and manufacturing expansion tailwinds. At $21.02 per share and ~13.6x FY2026 AFFO midpoint, the stock trades at a reasonable valuation for a mid-cap net lease REIT with improving operational momentum. The lean 62-person team with CEO Moragne's decade-long internal tenure and the $382M build-to-suit pipeline provide visible growth and efficient execution.
The bear case centers on BNL's limited scale, modest growth trajectory, and REIT structural constraints. AFFO growth of ~5-6% trails higher-growth REIT peers, limiting total return potential beyond the dividend [1]. GAAP earnings were depressed in FY2025, compressing GAAP ROE to 3.2% and inflating the GAAP P/E to 40x — metrics that penalize the REIT in a mechanical scoring framework, though they reflect depreciation charges standard in real estate rather than operational weakness [6]. D/E of 89% constrains balance sheet capacity for acquisitions, and rising interest rates would increase refinancing costs on the $2.73B debt stack. The dividend growth rate of 3.1% barely keeps pace with inflation, and the dividend has only been paid for 6 years since the 2020 IPO — a short track record compared to established dividend aristocrats [6]. Competition for net lease properties from larger, better-capitalized REITs (Realty Income at $70B+ market cap) compresses cap rates and limits BNL's ability to acquire high-quality properties at attractive returns. The Inspire Impact Score of 50 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions is a positive for faith-based investors but the limited positive attributions (only 3) reflect a narrow ESG profile.
Key Catalysts
  1. Build-to-suit pipeline expansion to $382M providing development-driven growth with higher returns than traditional acquisitions, plus $500-625M in total 2026 investment guidance creating visible external growth [1][2]
  2. Industrial portfolio repositioning — over 50% of annual base rent from industrial tenants benefiting from reshoring, e-commerce logistics growth, manufacturing expansion, and AI-driven demand for distribution infrastructure [1]
  3. AFFO growth acceleration — Q1 2026 AFFO grew 5.6% YoY, Q2 2026 FFO beat estimates, and 99.8% occupancy with contractual rent escalators provides organic growth visibility through the existing portfolio [1]
Key Risks
  1. Interest rate sensitivity — rising rates increase BNL's refinancing costs on the $2.73B debt stack (D/E 89%) and compress property valuations, constraining acquisition and development activity and potentially putting downward pressure on the stock price [6]
  2. Scale disadvantage versus larger net lease REITs (Realty Income at $70B+ market cap vs BNL at ~$4.3B) limits cost of capital, competitive positioning for acquisitions, and investor visibility in institutional portfolios [1]
  3. Tenant credit risk in an economic slowdown — net lease REITs depend on tenant solvency to maintain the 99.8% occupancy rate, and industrial, restaurant, and healthcare tenants face cyclical pressures that could lead to lease defaults or renegotiations [1]

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Broadstone's core business — owning physical single-tenant commercial real estate with long-term net leases — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. AI cannot replace industrial warehouses, healthcare facilities, or restaurant buildings. The triple-net lease structure means tenants bear operating costs, leaving BNL as a pure real estate owner with contractual rent escalators that AI cannot displace. The 99.8% occupancy rate across 773 properties and diversified tenant base across 200+ tenants further insulate the business. AI-driven growth in e-commerce, industrial automation, and data center expansion increases demand for the industrial properties that comprise over 50% of BNL's portfolio. The only minor exposure is that AI-driven disruption to specific tenants' businesses could lead to lease defaults, but the diversified tenant base mitigates this concentration risk. AI Opportunity (0/1): BNL does not generate revenue from AI products or services; it is a passive landlord collecting contractual rent with a lean 62-person team.
