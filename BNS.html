Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Diversified ·NYSE ·Canada
The Bank of Nova Scotia (Scotiabank) is one of Canada's Big Five banks and the third-largest by assets, founded in 1832 in Halifax, Nova Scotia and now headquartered in Toronto. It serves over 25 million customers globally through Canadian Banking, International Banking, Global Wealth Management, and Global Banking and Markets segments, with roughly $1.5 trillion in assets. Under CEO Scott Thomson, the bank has been refocusing its historically broad Latin American and Caribbean international footprint toward a North American (Canada-US-Mexico) growth strategy.

Investment Thesis
Scotiabank delivered Q2 2026 net income of $2,632 million (up from $2,032 million a year earlier) with diluted EPS of $2.00 (up 35% YoY), revenue growth of 8% YoY to CAD $9.84 billion, and ROE of 13.2%, tracking toward the bank's 14%+ medium-term objective for fiscal 2027 [1]. Canadian Banking earnings rose 53% YoY and Global Wealth Management earnings rose 19% YoY, evidence that the North American refocusing strategy under CEO Scott Thomson is gaining traction. At 14.8x forward earnings with a 3.56% dividend yield backed by an unbroken 193-year dividend record dating to 1833, BNS offers a durable, moated income profile — banking is a regulated, license-protected business that AI cannot easily disrupt, and the bank has built explicit AI-governance discipline (a data ethics team embedded in its code of conduct) ahead of many peers [1][2].
The primary risks are execution and reputational. The 2024 divestment of Colombia, Costa Rica, and Panama concentrates the bank's international exposure in Canada and Mexico, reducing the diversification that once distinguished BNS from domestic-only peers. ROE of 11.0% (trailing) still lags top-tier Canadian bank peers, and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR (~3.5%) has been depressed by the multi-year OSFI-mandated pandemic dividend freeze, meaning real income growth has been modest. A 2025 governance failure — two stock analysts and three compliance staff terminated for failing to escalate a personal-trading violation — and a history of prior AML and commodities-manipulation penalties are reputational overhangs that warrant continued monitoring even though the underlying franchise economics remain strong [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. ROE trajectory toward the 14%+ medium-term target for fiscal 2027, supported by 53% YoY earnings growth in Canadian Banking and 19% growth in Global Wealth Management
  2. Continued North American strategic focus following the 2024 Latin American divestitures, simplifying the investment case and capital allocation
  3. 193-year unbroken dividend record with resumed dividend growth (since the 2023 end of the pandemic-era regulatory freeze) supporting the income thesis for dividend-focused investors
Key Risks
  1. Reduced geographic diversification following the exit from Colombia, Costa Rica, and Panama, concentrating exposure in Canada and Mexico where macro conditions are correlated
  2. Governance and compliance history — the 2025 analyst/compliance-staff terminations plus a track record of prior AML and commodities-manipulation penalties create ongoing reputational and regulatory risk
  3. Dividend growth still recovering from the multi-year pandemic-era freeze, with a payout ratio of 59.6% limiting near-term acceleration without further earnings growth
