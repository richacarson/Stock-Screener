Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYQ ·Bermuda
Borr Drilling Limited is an offshore drilling contractor operating one of the world's largest fleets of modern jack-up rigs. The company provides drilling services to oil and gas operators globally, with operations spanning the Middle East, Southeast Asia, West Africa, and the North Sea. Borr reported Q1 2026 revenue of $247M (down 4.8% QoQ) with a net loss of $29M and adjusted EBITDA of $88.5M. The fleet expanded through the acquisition of five rigs from Noble Corporation, and total Dayrate Equivalent Backlog stood at $1.13B as of mid-2026.

Investment Thesis
Borr Drilling at $4.80 with a $1.48B market cap operates one of the largest modern jack-up fleets globally, with a $1.13B backlog providing 4.6 quarters of revenue visibility and 21 contract commitments secured year-to-date totaling $541M in Dayrate Equivalent Backlog [1][2]. The bull case centers on management's expectation that market dynamics will improve in H2 2026 with a recovery in dayrates and earnings visibility into 2027, modern fleet premiums over legacy rigs as the global jack-up fleet ages (marketed utilization at 90.1% for modern rigs), and growing energy demand from AI data centers supporting long-term hydrocarbon production investment. Technical utilization of 98.4% in Q2 2026 demonstrates operational excellence in rig management [2].
The bear case is dominated by financial fragility and cyclical risk. D/E of 258.6% reflects the heavy debt burden from fleet acquisitions, ROE of -24.7% and profit margin of -24.0% show the company is destroying capital, and the Q1 2026 net loss of $29M included an $8.4M credit loss provision signaling counterparty risk [1]. Revenue declined 13.2% YoY and 4.8% QoQ, with management attributing weakness to lower reimbursable expenses, fewer operating days, and declining dayrates for some rigs. The Forward P/E of 59.3 prices in recovery that has not materialized. TTM book-to-bill of approximately 1.1 is adequate but not accelerating, and 2026 H2 coverage at only 48% versus 80% for H1 leaves significant revenue uncertainty [2]. The offshore drilling industry remains structurally cyclical, and any softening in oil prices or E&P capex budgets could extend the downturn.
Key Catalysts
  1. Management expects H2 2026 market improvement with dayrate recovery and earnings visibility into 2027, supported by Middle East and Southeast Asian tenders that could fill the 48% uncovered H2 2026 capacity [2]
  2. Modern jack-up fleet premium as legacy rigs retire — global marketed utilization at 87.4% for all jack-ups versus 90.1% for modern units creates a structural advantage for Borr's young fleet [2]
  3. Growing global energy demand from AI data center buildout could support sustained E&P capital expenditure and offshore drilling activity, benefiting modern fleet operators
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 258.6% and Q1 2026 net loss of $29M with $8.4M credit loss provision indicate financial stress from debt-funded fleet expansion that requires dayrate recovery to service — any prolonged cyclical downturn could pressure covenant compliance and liquidity [1]
  2. Revenue declining 13.2% YoY with only 48% H2 2026 contract coverage leaves substantial uncertainty about near-term earnings trajectory, and lower dayrates for some rigs suggest competitive pricing pressure in a softening market [1][2]
  3. Structural cyclicality in offshore drilling — E&P capex budgets are driven by oil prices and OPEC+ policy, both outside Borr's control, and any reduction in exploration spending disproportionately impacts leveraged drillers [1]
