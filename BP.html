BP p.l.c. is an integrated energy company operating worldwide through Gas & Low Carbon Energy, Oil Production & Operations, and Customers & Products segments. CEO Meg O'Neill (effective April 2026, from Woodside Energy) is BP's fourth CEO since 2020 and first external hire in over a century — she eliminated the 'low carbon energy' business unit, simplified the organizational chart, and reemphasized core oil and gas operations. Q2 2026 delivered underlying replacement-cost profit of $5.7B (vs $2.4B Q2 2025), while Q1 2026 revenue of $52.3B rose 11.5% YoY with $3.8B profit (+453% YoY). Market cap ~$119B.

Investment Thesis
BP at $46.36 with a ~$119B market cap is one of the cheapest integrated oil majors globally at 8.69x forward earnings with a 4.35% dividend yield. H1 2026 demonstrated a powerful earnings inflection — Q1 profit surged 453% YoY to $3.8B on $52.3B revenue (+11.5%), and Q2 underlying RC profit reached $5.7B (more than double Q2 2025) driven by higher liquids and gas realizations and stronger refining margins [1]. New CEO Meg O'Neill has moved decisively — eliminating the low carbon energy unit, restructuring the organization, raising the dividend 4% — bringing operational credibility from Woodside Energy as the first external hire in over a century [2]. D/E improved to 95.1% from 124% as elevated cash flows fund deleveraging, and the 15-year unbroken dividend payment history (post-Deepwater Horizon) anchors income investor appeal.
The bear case centers on governance instability, commodity dependency, and structural financial weakness. Four CEOs in six years — including Looney's personal conduct departure and the board chair's ouster over bullying allegations — represents the worst leadership continuity among major oil companies [2][3]. At the 2026 AGM, shareholders rejected governance proposals and key institutional investors including Legal & General opposed board reappointments over the climate U-turn. Profit margin of just 2.55% and ROE of 8.87% remain the weakest among integrated oil majors — the earnings surge reflects elevated commodity prices, not operational improvement [1]. The trailing payout ratio of 95.25% underscores dividend vulnerability if commodity prices normalize; a return to $70/bbl oil would pressure the dividend, potentially triggering BP's third cut in 15 years. The 5yr dividend growth CAGR of only 3% reflects the 2020 cut's lingering impact. The Inspire score of -69 (MISALIGNED) makes BP inaccessible to faith-based mandates [3].
