Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance Brokers ·NYSE ·United States
Brown & Brown is one of the largest independent insurance intermediaries in the United States, providing risk management solutions, insurance products, and related services to individuals and businesses. The company operates through four segments: Retail, National Programs, Wholesale Brokerage, and Services. Founded in 1939 and led by the Brown family through multiple generations, BRO has grown primarily through acquisitions with over 600 acquisitions completed. Headquartered in Daytona Beach, Florida.

Investment Thesis
Brown & Brown delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $1.9B (+35.4% YoY) with diluted EPS of $1.06 and adjusted EPS of $1.39, driven by the transformative Glencar acquisition and strong contingent commissions of $97M [1]. At a forward P/E of 15.01 and D/E of only 63%, BRO offers reasonable valuation for a company with 33 consecutive years of dividend growth, 94% employee satisfaction, and a proven acquisition playbook that has compounded revenue from $2.6B to $5.9B over five years [5]. The AI deployment across 25%+ of submission workflows positions BRO to capture efficiency gains ahead of peers [3]. The meritocratic, decentralized culture — validated by Great Place to Work recognition — is a durable competitive advantage in a relationship-driven industry.
The primary concern is the quality of growth: Q1 2026 organic revenue was flat and organic revenue with contingents grew only 2.2%, revealing that the 35.4% total growth is almost entirely acquisition-driven rather than organic [1]. This raises questions about whether BRO can sustain premium valuations if core organic growth stalls — property rate declines and a pharmacy consulting model shift weighed on Q1. ROE of 11.23% lags the 15% target due to goodwill accumulation from serial acquisitions, and the PEG ratio of 1.85 suggests the market is pricing in growth that may require continued large-scale M&A to deliver [5]. The dividend yield of only 0.97% provides minimal downside protection, and any slowdown in acquisition pace or integration execution could compress the multiple. Consensus expects only 8.3% revenue growth going forward, well below the 22.8% achieved in 2025 [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI agents automating 25%+ of submission workflows across specialty distribution operations, with potential to drive measurable margin expansion as deployment scales beyond wholesale and program businesses
  2. Continued acquisitive growth leveraging BRO's proven playbook of 600+ completed acquisitions, with Q2 2026 earnings expected to show 5.8% EPS growth on July 27
  3. Organic revenue recovery from flat Q1 2026 as property rate headwinds moderate and the pharmacy consulting model transition completes, potentially re-accelerating core growth
Key Risks
  1. Organic revenue growth was flat in Q1 2026 (2.2% including contingents) versus 35.4% total growth, signaling heavy acquisition dependency that may not be sustainable at current valuation multiples
  2. ROE of 11.23% below the 15% target reflects goodwill drag from serial acquisitions — if acquired businesses underperform, impairment charges could materially impact earnings
  3. Property and casualty rate cycle turning negative would compress organic commissions growth across the entire brokerage industry, particularly impacting BRO's Retail segment

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Insurance brokerage is fundamentally relationship-driven — complex commercial risk assessment, client advisory, claims advocacy, and carrier negotiations require human judgment, trust, and nuanced understanding that AI cannot replicate [3]. Regulatory requirements in insurance (licensing, compliance, fiduciary duties) create additional moats against pure AI disruption. However, some back-office functions (submission processing, policy review, billing) are exposed to AI automation, and AI-powered insurtechs could compress margins on commoditized personal lines. BRO's proactive deployment of AI to automate 25%+ of submission workflows positions it as an adopter rather than a victim of the technology. AI Opportunity (1/1): BRO is actively deploying AI agents across specialty distribution operations for underwriting, policy review, and billing automation — CEO Powell Brown explicitly frames AI as a productivity tool that supports employees rather than replaces them [3]. While not yet quantified as revenue, the operational efficiency gains from automating 25% of submission workflows represent measurable AI-driven value creation.
