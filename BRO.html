Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance Brokers ·NYSE ·United States
Brown & Brown is the world's fifth-largest insurance broker, providing property & casualty, employee benefits, and specialty risk solutions through its Retail, Specialty Distribution (programs and wholesale), and Services segments. FY2025 revenue rose 22.5% to $5.76B, lifting BRO to No. 5 among global brokers after the Accession acquisition [4][5]. Q3 2026 revenue was $1.725B (+7.4% YoY), but organic growth was only 1.2% as softer pricing in catastrophe property weighed on results [1][2]. CEO J. Powell Brown positions AI as a productivity tool, targeting 25% submission automation without job cuts [3].

Investment Thesis
Brown & Brown trades at 14.6x forward earnings with a conservative balance sheet (D/E 64%) for a company that has compounded revenue at 17.2% annually since 2020 and reached $5.76B in FY2025, now the No. 5 global broker [5][6][8]. Q3 2026 revenue rose 7.4% to $1.725B and net income rose 5.7% to $240M, with adjusted EPS up 1.9% to $1.07 [1]. Insurance brokerage is one of the more AI-insulated corners of financial services — Mizuho sees a 'low disruption threat' for middle-market brokers — and BRO is using AI agents to automate up to 25% of submissions without job cuts, which could lift margins over time [3]. The dividend was raised 9% to $0.18 quarterly in October 2026 [9].
The bear case is organic stagnation in a softening pricing cycle. Organic revenue grew only 1.2% in Q3 2026 after a 0.7% decline in Q2, and nine-month organic growth was 0.3% versus 4.6% a year earlier, as catastrophe-property rates softened [1][2]. Adjusted EBITDAC margin fell to 34.6% from 36.6%, and diluted EPS declined 1.5% to $0.67 [1]. ROE has fallen to 9.8% as acquisition goodwill expanded equity, and the PEG of 1.8 implies the market is not cheap relative to growth [8]. If the P&C market softens further, commission revenue will fall regardless of AI productivity gains.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI submission automation: management targets 25% automation of submissions in Specialty Distribution without job cuts, which could restore EBITDAC margins from 34.6% toward the prior-year 36.6% [1][3]
  2. Accession integration and cross-selling: full integration of the business that lifted FY2025 revenue 22.5% to $5.76B should produce cost synergies and help organic growth recover from the 0.3% nine-month pace [2][5]
  3. Dividend growth and capital return: the October 2026 increase to $0.18 per quarter (+9%) continues a 12.3% five-year dividend CAGR with a payout ratio near 21% [8][9]
Key Risks
  1. Soft-market pricing: catastrophe-property rate declines already cut organic growth to 1.2% in Q3 2026; a broader P&C softening would pressure commission revenue across Retail and Specialty Distribution [1][2]
  2. Margin compression: adjusted EBITDAC margin fell 200bps to 34.6% in Q3 2026; continued compression would undermine the 14.6x forward multiple [1]
  3. Acquisition dependence: with organic growth near zero, total growth relies on M&A, and ROE has already fallen to 9.8% as goodwill builds; overpaying for deals would further dilute returns [6][8]
