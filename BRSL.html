Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Gambling ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Brightstar Lottery PLC (formerly International Game Technology PLC) is a pure-play global lottery technology and services company following the July 2024 sale of its Gaming & Digital business to Apollo Global Management. The company designs, operates, and services lottery systems, produces instant ticket games, provides iLottery platforms, and offers AI-driven player engagement and marketing services. Brightstar operates across the U.S., Italy (Lotto Italia concession through 2034), and internationally, generating $2.52B in trailing twelve-month revenue.

Investment Thesis
Brightstar Lottery is a pure-play global lottery technology leader trading at an attractive 16.8x forward P/E with an 8.8% dividend yield — a compelling income proposition as the business stabilizes post-restructuring [5]. Q1 2026 results were strong: adjusted EBITDA grew 15% to $287M with 48.9% margin, and income from continuing operations surged to $63M ($0.20/share) from just $8M a year prior [1]. The Lotto Italia concession through 2034 and dominant U.S. state lottery positions provide highly visible, recurring revenue streams with government-granted monopoly protections. Full-year 2026 revenue and profit guidance was reaffirmed, and net debt was trimmed to $2.75B. The Sao Paulo lottery launch and Italy B2C digital expansion provide incremental growth catalysts through 2028.
The bear case focuses on extreme leverage and stagnant organic growth. D/E of 482% with $2.75B net debt against a $1.91B market cap creates refinancing risk — the company is essentially a leveraged play on lottery concessions [5][6]. The payout ratio of 108% exceeds earnings, meaning the $0.92 annual dividend is being funded partly by cash reserves rather than sustainable earnings. Organic revenue has been flat for multiple years ($2.51B in 2025, $2.52B TTM), and even with Q1 2026 improvement, growth is mid-single-digits at best [1]. The 2024 data breach exposing 103,000+ individuals' information and Texas Lottery contract controversies highlight operational and reputational risk [3]. EPS of $0.81 TTM with -44% YoY decline reflects the continued drag from concession amortization and post-divestiture normalization. At $10.41, the stock is 44% below its 52-week high of $18.57, suggesting the market has repriced the dividend sustainability risk.

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Lottery technology and operations are deeply protected by government-granted monopoly licenses, rigorous security certifications, and long-term concession agreements that AI cannot displace [1]. Brightstar's core business — operating lottery transaction processing systems, producing instant tickets, and managing physical retail terminal networks — involves regulated physical-world infrastructure built over decades. Switching costs are extreme: replacing a state's lottery system is a multi-year, high-stakes process requiring regulatory approval. However, digital lottery (iLottery) faces some competitive pressure from AI-enhanced gaming platforms, and AI could theoretically enable new entrants or enhance competitor offerings in jurisdictions opening new lottery licenses. AI Opportunity (1/1): Brightstar actively deploys AI-driven CRM, predictive analytics, AI-generated game designs for its 250-game iLottery library, and an AI-powered route optimization tool for field operations [3][4].
