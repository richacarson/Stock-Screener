Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Boston Scientific Corporation develops, manufactures, and markets medical devices used in interventional medical specialties worldwide. The company operates through MedSurg and Cardiovascular segments, offering products for cardiac rhythm management, electrophysiology, interventional cardiology, peripheral interventions, endoscopy, urology, and pelvic health. BSX serves over 37 million patients annually and has invested over $1B through Boston Scientific Ventures in 70+ portfolio companies. The company completed a 2-for-1 stock split in March 2025. CEO Mike Mahoney has led the company since 2012, transforming it into a high-growth med-tech leader. Headquartered in Marlborough, Massachusetts.

Investment Thesis
Boston Scientific is a high-growth med-tech compounder trading at 12.5x forward earnings with a 0.6x PEG ratio, offering an exceptional combination of growth and value among large-cap healthcare names. Q1 2026 delivered $5.2B in net sales (+11.6% reported, +9.4% organic), followed by Q2 at $5.4B (+7.5%), with adjusted EPS guided to $3.28-$3.32 (+7-8% YoY) [1][2]. FARAPULSE pulsed field ablation is capturing dominant share in the rapidly growing cardiac EP market, and the $3.7B Axonics acquisition creates an entirely new pelvic health franchise. BSX's D/E of 50.2% and ROE of 15.3% demonstrate financial health, while the $1B+ venture portfolio provides a continuous pipeline of next-generation technologies. CEO Mahoney's 14-year track record of transforming BSX from a troubled company to a $62B high-growth platform demonstrates exceptional leadership execution [3].
The primary risk is revenue growth deceleration — organic growth slowed from 9.4% in Q1 to 7.0% in Q2 2026, prompting management to lower full-year guidance to 5-6% organic growth and $3.28-$3.32 adjusted EPS, citing WATCHMAN and electrophysiology headwinds [1][2]. The stock's decline from $46.88 (50-day SMA) to $42.60 reflects market concern that the high-growth narrative is moderating. BSX pays no dividend, making total return entirely dependent on capital appreciation and continued execution. At 17.3x trailing earnings, a miss on growth expectations could trigger a significant multiple compression. The company's competitive language — 'We want to win' — signals a finite mindset that could lead to overreach through aggressive M&A or market share battles that destroy value. Animal testing involvement flagged by ESG rating agencies may affect certain investor mandates [4]. Integration risk from the $3.7B Axonics acquisition remains as BSX absorbs a large, complex business.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Boston Scientific's core business of designing, manufacturing, and selling medical devices is deeply protected from AI disruption — pacemakers, stents, catheters, and surgical tools require physical manufacturing, FDA clinical trials, physician training, and in-body implantation that AI structurally cannot replace [1][3]. The medical device industry has among the highest regulatory barriers (FDA 510(k), PMA approvals), longest sales cycles, and deepest physician switching costs of any sector. AI enhances BSX's products (smarter pacemakers, AI-guided imaging, predictive diagnostics) but does not threaten the fundamental device-based business model. The WATCHMAN and FARAPULSE platforms require surgeon expertise for implantation. AI Opportunity (1/1): BSX Ventures has deployed over $1B across 70+ portfolio companies including digital health and AI-driven diagnostics, providing a pipeline of AI-enabled product enhancements [3]. AI-enhanced imaging guidance, data analytics platforms for physicians, and smart device monitoring generate revenue as integrated features of BSX's device ecosystem.
