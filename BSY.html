Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Bentley Systems provides infrastructure engineering software — design, simulation, construction and asset-operations applications (MicroStation, OpenRoads, SYNCHRO, the iTwin digital-twin platform and Cesium geospatial 3D) — sold mostly on subscription to civil, structural, utility and industrial engineering firms and owner-operators. FY2025 revenue was $1.50B (+11%) with net income of $278M [3]. Q2 2026 revenue rose 12.8% to $410.7M, ARR reached $1.536B (12% constant-currency growth) and net revenue retention was 109% [1]. Nicholas Cumins became CEO in July 2024, the first non-Bentley-family CEO in the company's 40-year history [4].

Investment Thesis
Bentley is a high-quality compounding software franchise: revenue grew from $801.5M in 2020 to $1.50B in 2025 (13.4% CAGR) and Q2 2026 revenue rose 12.8% to $410.7M with ARR of $1.536B growing 12% in constant currency and 109% net retention [1][3]. ROE of 24.5% and an 18.1% net margin show real operating leverage, free cash flow rose to $63.8M in Q2, and management reiterated 2026 guidance of 10.5–12.5% constant-currency ARR growth and $1.69–1.72B revenue [1][2]. Global infrastructure spending on grids, water and transport — partly driven by AI data-center power needs — is a multi-year tailwind for its engineering and digital-twin tools.
The bear case is valuation and AI seat risk. At 45x trailing and 26.9x forward earnings with a 1.57 PEG, the stock prices in steady double-digit growth, and Q2 operating margin slipped to 21.6% from 23.2% while revenue came in slightly below consensus [1][2]. If generative design automation reduces the number of engineers per project, per-seat economics could compress before consumption pricing offsets it. The leadership transition and layoffs add execution risk, and D/E of 105% is higher than typical for software [2][4]. Inspire Impact Score of 69 (ALIGNED).
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results (early November) — consensus revenue of $420.4M and non-GAAP EPS of $0.32; confirming ARR growth at the upper half of the 10.5–12.5% range would ease AI-disruption fears [1]
  2. Monetization of AI inside existing workflows under new CRO Andy Rahden, converting AI adoption into consumption-based ARR [4]
  3. Grid, water and transport spending tied to data-center power demand and public infrastructure programs expanding the user base for OpenRoads, SYNCHRO and iTwin [1]
Key Risks
  1. Generative design automation reducing engineering headcount at customers, compressing seat-based subscription revenue
  2. Operating margin compression (21.6% vs 23.2% in Q2) if AI investment and go-to-market reorganization costs outpace revenue growth [1]
  3. Cultural disruption after the first non-family CEO transition, with reported layoffs and team dissolutions risking loss of key engineering talent [4]
