Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Bentley Systems provides infrastructure engineering software solutions worldwide, including MicroStation (design/modeling), OpenRoads (civil), OpenBuildings, STAAD/RAM (structural), OpenPlant, iTwin (digital twins), ProjectWise (project collaboration), and AssetWise (asset lifecycle). The company serves transportation, public works, utilities, industrial, mining, and commercial/residential sectors. Under new CEO Nicholas Cumins (first non-family CEO in 40 years), Bentley is accelerating AI integration with six MCP servers released and AI-driven workflows powering customer applications. Q2 2026 revenue reached $410.7M (+12.8% YoY) with ARR of $1,536M.

Investment Thesis
Bentley Systems occupies a defensible niche as the dominant infrastructure engineering software platform, with Q2 2026 revenue of $410.7M (+12.8% YoY), subscription revenue of $378.6M (+13.6%), and ARR reaching $1,536M [1]. ROE of 24.53% reflects efficient capital deployment, the 109% dollar-based net retention rate demonstrates strong customer stickiness, and the 40-year family-controlled governance structure (Bentley brothers maintaining Class B oversight) aligns management with long-term value creation. CEO Cumins' AI strategy — releasing six MCP servers to enable AI-driven infrastructure engineering workflows — positions Bentley at the intersection of the global infrastructure spending cycle and enterprise AI adoption [2]. Revenue grew from $965M (2021) to $1,502M (2025), an 11.7% 5yr CAGR reflecting consistent double-digit growth. The Inspire Impact Score of 69 (ALIGNED) with 19 positive attributions and zero negative attributions makes BSY one of the strongest faith-alignment profiles in the technology sector. Sustainalytics rates Bentley's controversy level as 'None.'
The primary concern is valuation — at 25.91x forward earnings and a PEG of ~2.0, Bentley trades at a premium that demands sustained double-digit growth and successful AI monetization [3]. The stock has declined 35% from its 52-week high of $57.25 to $37.43, suggesting the market has already begun adjusting expectations. Profit margin of 18.12% lags best-in-class software peers (Autodesk at ~30%), and the operating margin narrowed to 21.6% in Q2 2026 from 23.2% prior year, indicating AI investment spending is compressing margins before generating returns [1]. D/E of 101.26% with $1.17B in debt against $105M cash creates moderate balance sheet risk if growth decelerates. The dividend yield of 0.87% ($0.28 annual) provides negligible income support. Competitive risk from Autodesk's expanding infrastructure focus and potential AI-native design tools could erode Bentley's niche positioning. Revenue concentration in infrastructure and public works sectors creates sensitivity to government spending cycles, fiscal austerity, and the political durability of infrastructure investment programs.

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Bentley's infrastructure engineering software is deeply embedded in complex, regulated engineering workflows with high switching costs — transportation agencies, utilities, and engineering firms build decades of project data and institutional knowledge in Bentley platforms [1]. Infrastructure design requires domain-specific expertise, regulatory compliance (building codes, environmental regulations, safety standards), and integration with physical-world constraints that generic AI tools cannot replicate. The 109% net retention rate quantifies these switching costs. However, AI-powered design automation from competitors (Autodesk AI, AI-native entrants) could eventually compress portions of the engineering workflow that Bentley currently monetizes, and some document management and search functions face AI substitution risk. Analysts have tempered growth assumptions given AI disruption uncertainty [2]. AI Opportunity (1/1): Bentley is generating meaningful AI-enabled revenue through its six released MCP servers, AI-driven workflows integrated across the product suite, and the iTwin digital twin platform with AI analytics. CEO Cumins described strong customer demand for AI-instrumented applications, and the company is actively monetizing AI capabilities through its subscription platform [2].
