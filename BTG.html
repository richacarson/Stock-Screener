Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Canada
B2Gold is an international mid-tier gold producer operating four mines: Fekola (Mali), Masbate (Philippines), Otjikoto (Namibia), and the Goose Mine (Nunavut, Canada). The company produced 441,411 ounces in H1 2026 and guides for 820,000-920,000 ounces for the full year. Founder and longtime CEO Clive Johnson retired at the June 2026 AGM, succeeded by former CFO Mike Cinnamond, with Johnson remaining as chair emeritus and Kelvin Dushnisky serving as Executive Chair. TTM revenue of $3.69 billion driven by record gold prices averaging $3,767/oz in Q2 2026. Headquartered in Vancouver, Canada.

Investment Thesis
B2Gold offers compelling value at ~6x forward P/E with TTM revenue of $3.69B (+23% YoY) and a fortress balance sheet (D/E 14%) in a record gold price environment [1][3]. Q2 2026 revenue of $789M was driven by an average realized gold price of $3,767/oz, and the company generated TTM free cash flow of $436M supporting both the $92M share buyback and the $0.08 annual dividend [1]. The newly secured Fekola Regional Permit opens future development options in Mali, and the Goose Mine ramp toward ~330K oz/year by 2027 provides a clear production growth trajectory. Improved AISC guidance of $2,370-$2,550/oz combined with gold prices above $3,700 generates substantial operating margins. CEO Mike Cinnamond inherits a well-positioned company with strong operational momentum and a clean balance sheet [3].
The bear case centers on production headwinds, gold price dependency, and execution risk. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.03 missed analyst estimates of $0.07 significantly, and gold production of 203,648 oz was down 11.2% YoY, reflecting Otjikoto step-down and Fekola stripping requirements [1]. Full-year production guidance was narrowed from 820,000-970,000 to 820,000-920,000 oz, confirming 2026 as a transition year with output below FY2025's ~980,000 oz. Mali geopolitical risk remains the most significant tail risk — the military government could impose new taxes, royalties, or partial nationalization of Fekola, which represents ~40% of production capacity [1]. AISC of $2,370-$2,550/oz is elevated relative to peers, and margins compress rapidly below $3,000/oz gold. The dividend was previously cut 50% (from $0.16 to $0.08 annually), limiting income investor appeal. The CEO transition from founder Clive Johnson to CFO-background Mike Cinnamond introduces uncertainty during a critical Goose Mine ramp period, and the stock has declined from $5.30 to $4.10 (-23%) since Q1 results [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Goose Mine production ramp toward ~250K oz in 2026 and ~330K oz in 2027 restores growth and validates the multi-billion dollar Nunavut investment, diversifying production away from geopolitically risky Mali [1]
  2. Gold prices sustaining above $3,700/oz generate exceptional margins and free cash flow at B2Gold's cost structure — TTM FCF of $436M annualizing supports both capital returns and exploration investment [1][3]
  3. Fekola Regional Permit (secured Q2 2026) opens future development options in Mali, potentially extending mine life and production beyond current reserves, creating long-term value if geopolitical risk stabilizes [1]
Key Risks
  1. Mali geopolitical instability — military government could impose new taxes, royalties, or partial nationalization of the Fekola Mine, which represents approximately 40% of production capacity and is the company's most profitable asset [1]
  2. Gold price decline below $3,000/oz would severely compress margins given AISC guidance of $2,370-$2,550/oz, potentially eliminating free cash flow generation and forcing further dividend reductions [1]
  3. Production transition risk — 2026 guidance of 820K-920K oz is below FY2025's ~980K oz, Q2 EPS missed estimates by 57%, and new CEO Cinnamond must navigate the Goose Mine ramp while managing operational challenges across four mines on three continents [1]
