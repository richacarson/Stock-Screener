Company Profile
Energy ·Thermal Coal ·NYSE ·United States
Peabody Energy is a large U.S.-based coal producer with thermal coal operations in the Powder River Basin and Illinois Basin, seaborne thermal coal from Australia, and seaborne metallurgical coal from Australian mines including the new Centurion longwall mine. FY2025 revenue fell to $3.86B with a net loss of $52.9M as coal prices normalized [3]. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.00B with a loss of $0.74/share and Adjusted EBITDA of $24.0M as Centurion commissioning costs ran high; the 2026 Centurion sales target was cut to 2.0–2.5 Mt [1]. CEO Jim Grech has led since June 2021 and in January 2026 was named chair of the National Coal Council [4].

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Peabody plays a largely finite game: its fortunes move with coal prices, it went through Chapter 11 in 2016–2017, and its strategy is about maximizing cash from a declining-but-durable resource base. Discipline in walking away from the Anglo American coal deal and the Centurion met-coal pivot show some long-term thinking, but there is no Just Cause that would outlive the commodity.

Investment Thesis
Peabody is a cheap, low-leverage coal producer: D/E of 12.9% and a forward P/E of 7.5 reflect consensus forward EPS of $2.80 as Centurion ramps and U.S. thermal demand benefits from data-center power needs [2][4]. The company refinanced by issuing $250M of 2031 convertibles and repurchasing $241.2M of 2028 notes, and keeps a dividend ($0.30/yr) while cash-generating PRB operations fund the met-coal pivot [1]. If Centurion reaches design rates, seaborne met-coal margins should restore earnings after a FY2025 net loss of $52.9M.
The bear case is execution and commodity risk. Q2 2026 produced a $0.74/share loss and only $24.0M of Adjusted EBITDA versus $93.3M a year earlier; Centurion's 2026 sales target was cut from 3.5 Mt to 2.0–2.5 Mt, and seaborne met lost $17.0M of EBITDA in the quarter [1]. Revenue fell from $4.98B in 2022 to $3.86B in 2025 as prices normalized [3]. Coal faces structural decline from decarbonization policy, ESG-driven financing constraints (Sustainalytics rates its ESG risk 'Severe' at 49.7) and a history of bankruptcy [5].
