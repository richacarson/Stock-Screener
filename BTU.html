Company Profile
Energy ·Thermal Coal ·NYSE ·United States
Peabody Energy is the world's largest private-sector coal company, mining and marketing thermal and metallurgical coal for electricity generation and steelmaking. Operations span the United States (Powder River Basin, Illinois Basin, Appalachia) and Australia (Queensland, New South Wales). Emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2017. The company is ramping its flagship Centurion premium met coal mine in Australia, targeting 4.7M tons/year over a 25+ year mine life. Q2 CY2026 revenue was $1.0B (+12.7% YoY), though GAAP EPS was -$0.74 due to Centurion commissioning costs. FY2026 analyst consensus: $4.54B revenue, $2.73 EPS.

Investment Thesis
Peabody at 10.5x forward earnings ($2.73 FY2026 consensus) with D/E of just 9% offers a deep-value proposition in coal — the market prices significant terminal value risk that may be premature given AI-driven electricity demand extending coal plant lifetimes [1][4]. Q2 CY2026 revenue of $1.0B (+12.7% YoY) signals top-line momentum, and Centurion's ramp to 4.7M tons/year of premium met coal at $105/ton costs should drive material EBITDA expansion in H2 2026, transforming the met coal platform from commissioning drag to earnings engine [2]. The 65-100% FCF return framework and post-bankruptcy balance sheet provide capacity for meaningful shareholder returns as the mine ramp normalizes margins.
The bear case is structural and substantial. Q2 2026 GAAP loss of $0.74/share (76% below consensus) with adjusted EBITDA collapsing to $24M demonstrates that Centurion execution risk is real and ongoing — the mine ramp must deliver to justify the stock's recent run from $22 to $29 [1]. ROE of -3.69% and profit margin of -4.55% show a company currently destroying value. Coal is secularly declining as renewables achieve cost parity globally, and revenue has been volatile ($5.0B in 2022 to $3.9B in 2025) with commodity price swings [3][4]. The 1.04% dividend yield is modest with only ~4 years of payment history post-bankruptcy, providing limited evidence of sustainability through a full cycle. CEO Jim Grech's planned 2028 departure creates succession uncertainty with no publicly articulated vision for the company beyond coal extraction [2].

  3. Commodity price volatility with no pricing power — revenue swung from $5.0B (2022) to $3.9B (2025) with coal benchmarks, and Peabody's pure-play coal model offers no diversification buffer against cyclical downturns [4]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Peabody's core business — extracting coal through surface and underground mining — is entirely physical-world and structurally immune to AI substitution [1]. Coal mining requires heavy equipment, geological expertise, safety management, MSHA regulatory compliance, land access, and logistics infrastructure that AI cannot replace. Furthermore, growing electricity demand from AI data centers is increasing demand for baseload power generation, including coal-fired electricity in regions where coal plants remain the marginal provider [2]. Metallurgical coal for steelmaking is equally immune — no AI substitute exists for the coking process in blast furnace steel production. Scores 8 rather than 9 because the broader energy transition (renewables displacing coal-fired generation) is a structural secular threat, though this is energy policy risk rather than AI disruption.
