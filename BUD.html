Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Brewers ·NYSE ·Belgium
Anheuser-Busch InBev SA/NV is the world's largest brewer by volume and revenue, with a portfolio of over 500 beer brands including Budweiser, Bud Light, Corona, Stella Artois, Michelob Ultra, and Beck's. The company operates across 50+ countries with dominant positions in Brazil, Mexico, South Africa, and the United States. AB InBev also operates BEES, a B2B digital commerce platform with $1.1B quarterly GMV. H1 2026 revenue reached $31.9B (+11.5% YoY) with profit of $7.1B (+54.6%). Led by CEO Michel Doukeris since July 2021. Market cap approximately $140B.

The company's massive global scale, century-old brands, and strategic investments in BEES and Beyond Beer demonstrate long-term thinking, but the 3G Capital management DNA — zero-based budgeting, aggressive cost targets, and performance-driven culture — reflects a finite orientation toward quarterly optimization. Investment Thesis
AB InBev at 18x forward earnings offers the world's largest brewer at reasonable valuation with accelerating profitability — H1 2026 profit surged 54.6% to $7.1B on revenue of $31.9B (+11.5%), with normalized EBITDA of $11.4B (+12%) and underlying EPS growth of 22% [1][2]. The premiumization strategy is delivering measurable results: Corona revenue grew 8.2%, gross margins expanded to 56.6%, and the portfolio continues shifting toward higher-margin premium and super-premium brands. The BEES digital platform ($1.1B quarterly GMV, +55% YoY) is transforming retailer relationships and generating AI-driven efficiency gains across 6M+ accounts [1]. Forward P/E of 18x is below consumer staples peer averages, and post-SABMiller deleveraging has improved D/E to 80% with continued trajectory toward investment-grade leverage targets [4].
The bear case centers on organic growth quality, structural leverage, and reputational headwinds. Organic revenue growth of only ~5.7% with volumes near flat reveals that reported 11.5% growth is substantially FX-driven — underlying demand for beer in mature markets is stagnant [2]. ROE of ~9% and profit margin of 11.9% are mediocre for a company of this scale, reflecting high legacy acquisition debt and capital-intensive global operations [4]. The Bud Light controversy permanently damaged the U.S. brand, with Bud Light losing its #1 U.S. beer position, and the company's retreat from ESG messaging ('greenhushing') satisfies neither progressive nor conservative stakeholders [3]. Beer consumption faces long-term secular decline among younger demographics shifting toward cannabis, health-conscious beverages, and non-alcoholic alternatives. The 3G Capital-influenced culture of zero-based budgeting and cost optimization may limit innovation capacity and talent retention. The Inspire Impact Score of -1 (MIXED) driven by Alcohol involvement may limit faith-based investor allocation.
Key Catalysts
  1. BEES platform scaling toward $5B+ annual GMV and expanding beyond ordering into fintech, logistics, and AI-driven analytics for 6M+ retailers globally, creating a defensible tech-enabled distribution moat [1]
  2. Premiumization driving revenue per hectoliter growth as Corona (+8.2%), Stella Artois, and Michelob Ultra gain share in premium tiers, expanding gross margins toward 58% and partially offsetting volume softness [1][2]
  3. H1 2026 profit growth of 54.6% and EPS growth of 22% establishing a rerating trajectory if sustained through H2, with FY2026 EBITDA guidance of 4-8% potentially conservative relative to performance [2]
Key Risks
  1. Organic revenue growth of only ~5.7% with near-flat volumes masks FX-driven reported growth of 11.5% — if USD strengthens, reported growth could decelerate materially and expose the underlying stagnation [2]
  2. Secular decline in beer consumption among younger demographics shifting to cannabis, non-alcoholic alternatives, and health-conscious beverages threatens long-term volume trajectory in developed markets [3]
  3. Bud Light controversy continues to weigh on U.S. market share and brand perception, and the company's 'greenhushing' response — pulling ESG advertising, retreating from public sustainability commitments — creates credibility risk with ESG-focused investors [3]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): AB InBev's core business — brewing, distributing, and selling physical beer products — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. Beer production requires agricultural inputs, fermentation, bottling/canning, cold-chain logistics, and physical retail placement that AI cannot substitute. The 50+ country distribution network, 6M+ retailer relationships via BEES, and brand equity built over centuries (Stella Artois founded 1366) create moats beyond AI's reach. Regulatory requirements for alcohol manufacturing, distribution, and sales add further protection. The primary AI risk vector is that AI-driven marketing tools could reduce the cost advantage of AB InBev's massive advertising spend, and AI-powered micro-brewers could chip away at craft segments. AI Opportunity (1/1): The BEES digital platform generated $1.1B GMV in Q1 2026 (+55% YoY), integrating AI for personalized retailer ordering, dynamic pricing, demand forecasting, and delivery route optimization [1]. AI-driven digital asset production has streamlined marketing workflows across 500+ brands. These represent measurable, AI-embedded revenue and cost savings across the value chain.
