Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Precious Metals & Mining ·NYSE ·Peru
Compañía de Minas Buenaventura is Peru's largest publicly traded precious and base metals mining company, founded in 1953. The company explores for and produces gold, silver, copper, zinc, and lead across multiple Peruvian operations, including the newly commissioned San Gabriel gold mine (2026 guidance: 70,000-80,000 oz gold) and the flagship Uchucchacua silver mine. BVN also holds a significant stake in Cerro Verde, one of Peru's largest copper operations. Q1 2026 revenue doubled to $624.6M with net income of $335.4M.

Investment Thesis
Buenaventura is a Peruvian mining company riding a historic precious metals supercycle with exceptional Q1 2026 results — revenue doubled to $624.6M, net income of $335.4M (53.7% margin), gold production surged 80% to 30,000 ounces, and EBITDA from direct operations more than tripled YoY [1]. San Gabriel production guidance was raised to 70,000-80,000 ounces for 2026 (from initial 48-55K), and the company ended March with $759.9M in cash and a net cash position (leverage -0.05x) [1]. At 10.4x forward P/E with a 45.2% profit margin, 21.4% ROE, and 108% YoY revenue growth, BVN offers compelling value exposure to gold prices and Peru's copper resources, with the $3.4B Trapiche project providing long-term optionality as AI data center buildout drives copper demand [4].
The bear case is dominated by commodity price dependency and Peru country risk. BVN's exceptional financial profile is entirely leveraged to gold and silver prices — if gold retreats from current record levels, the revenue and EBITDA guidance collapses proportionally [1]. Peru's chronic mining social license issues remain an active concern: contract worker protests at Uchucchacua entered their third week in 2026, with the site accounting for over 20% of group silver production, and production costs are rising 15% YoY [3]. The $3.4B Trapiche copper project carries significant execution and permitting risk in Apurimac, an area with both mineral reserves and sensitive ecosystems. BVN has no AI adoption or operational digitization, leaving it potentially less efficient than technologically advanced mining competitors in the long term [2]. The dividend payment history is inconsistent (skipped 2020-2021), limiting income investor confidence despite the current 3.24% yield and 300%+ dividend increase in 2025.
Key Catalysts
  1. San Gabriel gold mine exceeding expectations with 2026 production guidance raised to 70,000-80,000 oz and processing rate on track for 2,000 TPD, adding high-margin ounces at record gold prices [1]

  3. Trapiche copper project ($3.4B) environmental approval advancing toward construction, positioning BVN for long-term copper exposure as AI data center and electrification demand drives copper prices [3]
Key Risks
  1. Gold and silver price dependency — a sustained decline in precious metals prices from current record levels would compress margins and revenue proportionally, as BVN's Q1 2026 53.7% profit margin reflects peak commodity pricing conditions [1]
  2. Peru political and social license risk — contract worker protests at Uchucchacua entering their third week over wages threaten production at a site responsible for 20%+ of group silver output, with broader Peru mining unrest creating ongoing operational uncertainty [3]
  3. Trapiche copper project execution risk — the $3.4B capex requirement, environmental sensitivities in Apurimac, and multi-year construction timeline create significant capital allocation risk in a politically volatile jurisdiction [3]
