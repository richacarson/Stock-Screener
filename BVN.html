Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Peru
Compañía de Minas Buenaventura is Peru's largest publicly traded precious metals mining company, engaged in the exploration, development, and operation of gold and silver mines. Listed on the Lima Stock Exchange since 1971 and NYSE since 1996, the company operates multiple mines across Peru and holds strategic interests in joint ventures. Q2 2026 revenue rose 43% YoY to $529M with net income surging 165% to $261M, driven by record gold prices, increased production at Yumpag, and the ramp-up of the San Gabriel gold project which achieved first commercial sales [1]. Q1 2026 revenue more than doubled to $624.6M with net income of $355M. H1 2026 EBITDA reached $663.4M versus $256.4M in the prior year period. Board appointed Roque Eduardo Benavides Ganoza as chairman and named Shehzad Bharmal as lead independent director in April 2026 [2].

Investment Thesis
Buenaventura is a gold mining turnaround story firing on all cylinders — Q2 2026 revenue of $529M (+43% YoY) with net income surging 165% to $261M, following Q1 revenue that more than doubled to $624.6M with net income of $355M [1]. H1 2026 EBITDA of $663.4M versus $256.4M a year earlier reflects the dual tailwinds of record gold prices and the San Gabriel mine ramp-up, which achieved first commercial sales in Q2. The financial profile is exceptional: profit margin of 47.72%, D/E of just 18%, and forward P/E of 13.14 — deeply discounted to the broader market despite accelerating earnings growth. Raised 2026 silver guidance to 16.1 million ounces demonstrates production momentum. The company represents the purest play on Peru's world-class mineral endowment with the financial strength ($1.68B cash) to fund continued exploration and development. Dividend yield of 3.58% provides current income during the gold bull market.
The bear case centers on gold price cyclicality, Peru country risk, and governance concentration. Buenaventura's exceptional H1 2026 results are heavily driven by gold prices near all-time highs — a mean reversion in gold toward $2,000/oz from current levels of $3,000+ would compress margins, reduce production economics, and potentially impair the San Gabriel project's returns. Peru's political instability remains a persistent risk — mining tax increases, community opposition, and environmental regulatory changes have historically disrupted operations, and the current leftist-leaning political environment maintains that overhang [2]. The Benavides family's multi-generational influence on the board raises governance concentration concerns that may deter institutional investors seeking independent oversight. Consolidated gold production of 30,500 oz in Q2 is modest by global standards, limiting Buenaventura's ability to influence pricing or achieve economies of scale. The stock's innovation (2/10) and inspiration (2/10) scores reflect a company that excels at execution but lacks the transformative vision or mission-driven culture that the IOWN framework values — strong current financials may not translate into long-term competitive durability if gold prices normalize.
Key Catalysts
  1. San Gabriel gold mine ramp-up toward full production capacity — first commercial sales in Q2 2026, with increasing quarterly output expected to drive sustained revenue growth independent of gold price movements [1]
  2. Record gold prices above $3,000/oz creating exceptional margin expansion — profit margin of 47.72% and H1 EBITDA of $663.4M (2.6x YoY) could further expand if gold rallies toward $3,500+ as some analysts forecast
  3. Silver production guidance raised to 16.1 million ounces for 2026 — Yumpag expansion and silver's dual role as precious metal and industrial input (solar, electronics, AI hardware) provide diversification from gold [1]
Key Risks
  1. Gold price cyclicality — current record prices near $3,000/oz drive the exceptional profit margin and earnings growth, but gold is cyclical and a normalization toward $2,000-2,200/oz would compress margins by 30-40%, reduce free cash flow, and potentially impair development project economics
  2. Peru country risk — political instability, mining tax increases, community opposition to mining, and environmental regulatory changes have historically disrupted operations; the Conga mine conflict (a Buenaventura joint venture) resulted in project suspension due to community protests [2]
  3. Governance concentration with multi-generational Benavides family influence on the board may limit independent oversight and deter institutional ESG-focused investors, despite recent board modernization with an independent lead director [2]
