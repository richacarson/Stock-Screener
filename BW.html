Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Babcock & Wilcox Enterprises is an Akron, Ohio-based provider of steam generation, environmental and power generation technologies and services for utility and industrial customers. It is pivoting toward baseload and behind-the-meter power for AI data centers, anchored by a $2.4B power generation project for Base Electron serving Applied Digital AI factory campuses. Q2 2026 revenue rose 130% to $319.7M, including $100.7M from Base Electron, and backlog reached $2.6B. The stock has fallen to about $4.56 from a $22.03 52-week high amid securities class actions over related-party ties to largest shareholder BRC Group Holdings.

Investment Thesis
The bull case is that B&W has real AI-power revenue at a distressed price. Q2 2026 revenue rose 130% to $319.7M with $100.7M from Base Electron, net income swung to $14.3M from a $58.5M loss, and FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was raised to $80-$105M [1]. The balance sheet was rebuilt with $259.8M of new equity: cash and restricted cash total $382.8M against $239.8M of secured debt and bonds, and equity turned positive at $57.4M [1]. At $4.56 (a $679M market cap) the stock trades at 10.2x forward earnings against a $2.6B backlog and a $14.0B+ pipeline [1][3].
The bear case is governance and dilution. Class actions covering November 5, 2025 to March 11, 2026 allege B&W failed to disclose that the $2.4B Base Electron counterparty was tied to largest shareholder BRC Group Holdings, and Applied Digital reportedly holds only 10% of Base Electron [5]. The stock is about 79% below its high and fell 6.85% on October 16 alone [5]. Basic shares rose 44% to 142.3M in a year, and D/E of 561.8% leaves little room for project overruns [1][3]. Q2 bookings of only $151.0M against $319.7M of revenue mean backlog is now being drawn down [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results on November 9, 2026, which will show whether Base Electron revenue conversion and the $80-$105M FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance hold [1][5]
  2. Conversion of the $14.0B+ global pipeline into new baseload and behind-the-meter data center orders that diversify away from the single Base Electron contract [1]
  3. Execution of the $50M share repurchase authorization (only $5.0M used through H1 2026) at depressed prices near $4.56 [1][3]
Key Risks
  1. Securities class actions over undisclosed related-party ties between Base Electron and largest shareholder BRC Group Holdings; renegotiation or cancellation of the $2.4B contract would remove most of the $2.6B backlog [1][5]
  2. Concentration and execution risk: a single customer contributed $100.7M of Q2 revenue, and skilled-labor shortages are raising construction costs on fixed-scope projects [1]
  3. Dilution and leverage: $259.8M of equity issued in H1 2026 (basic shares +44% YoY) and D/E of 561.8% could force further raises if cash flow disappoints [1][3]
