Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
BWX Technologies designs, manufactures, and services nuclear reactors, nuclear fuel, and precision components primarily for the U.S. Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program. The company also provides commercial nuclear power services, medical radioisotopes, TRISO fuel for advanced reactors, and space nuclear propulsion systems (DRACO program with NASA/DARPA). H1 2026 revenues rose 21.8% to $1.76B with record backlog of $8.4B (up 40% YoY). Full-year 2026 guidance calls for ~$3.8B revenue, $4.70-$4.80 non-GAAP EPS. Headquartered in Lynchburg, Virginia, founded 1867.
11,000 Employees ·www.bwxt.com

Investment Thesis
BWXT is the irreplaceable backbone of U.S. nuclear defense and a primary beneficiary of the nuclear energy renaissance. H1 2026 revenue rose 21.8% to $1.76B and record backlog of $8.4B (up 40% YoY, TTM book-to-bill ~1.7x) provides multi-year revenue visibility [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance of ~$3.8B revenue and $4.70-$4.80 EPS represents high-teens growth. Commercial Operations revenue surged 121% YoY in Q1 as utilities and data center operators pursue nuclear power for baseload electricity. As the sole supplier of naval nuclear reactors and fuel, BWXT holds the most strategically protected monopoly in U.S. defense — a position reinforced by Columbia and Virginia-class submarine programs requiring decades of sustained nuclear fuel production [1]. The stock's 30%+ pullback from its April 2026 high of $241.82 has brought the forward P/E to 26.35x, improving risk/reward from earlier premium levels [4].
The bear case centers on valuation and margin constraints. At a forward P/E of 26.35x and trailing P/E of 35.75x, BWXT still commands a premium that requires sustained high-teens revenue growth and margin expansion — creating vulnerability if the nuclear renaissance thesis stalls or if defense sequestration compresses spending [4]. Profit margins of 10.11% reflect the cost-plus nature of government contracts, structurally limiting upside even as revenue scales. D/E of 151.4% is elevated for a company whose growth requires significant capital investment in new facilities (Erwin enrichment, TRISO production). Earnings growth decelerated to 14.1% YoY from 22% in prior periods, suggesting the easy comparisons are fading. Commercial nuclear growth, while promising (121% Q1 2026), remains nascent relative to the dominant naval propulsion business, and advanced reactor deployment timelines are notoriously uncertain. The 0.77% dividend yield provides negligible income cushion during any correction [4].
