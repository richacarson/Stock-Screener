Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Specialty ·NYSE ·United States
Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust Inc. is a newly organized REIT focused on acquiring and owning mission-critical data center assets that power the modern digital economy. The company completed its IPO in May 2026, raising $2B in the largest blind-pool REIT IPO in history with 100.6M shares outstanding. BXDC targets newly constructed, stabilized data centers in Tier 1 U.S. markets, leased to investment-grade hyperscale tenants (AWS, Microsoft, Meta) on long-term contracts. Externally managed by an affiliate of Blackstone, the world's largest alternative asset manager and the largest financial investor in data center and digital infrastructure globally. As of June 30, 2026, no data center assets had been acquired, with IPO proceeds held in cash generating $9.3M in Q2 2026 interest income. CEO is Nick Pell, formerly president of Blackstone's Link Logistics business.

Investment Thesis
BXDC at $19.33 (1.01x book value, $1.95B market cap) is a Blackstone-managed data center REIT positioned to capture the AI-driven infrastructure megatrend through a differentiated stabilized-asset acquisition strategy [1]. The company raised $2B in the largest blind-pool REIT IPO in history, backed by Blackstone — the world's largest alternative asset manager ($1.1T+ AUM) and the largest financial investor in data center infrastructure globally [2]. Target: newly built, stabilized data centers in Tier 1 U.S. markets leased to investment-grade hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft, Meta) on long-term contracts with contractual rent escalations. The AI-driven data center TAM is projected to exceed $1T by 2030, U.S. vacancy is at all-time lows, and rents have doubled since 2021 [1]. AI resilience is exceptional at 9/10 — data centers ARE the physical layer that AI requires to operate. Management expects to deploy IPO capital within three quarters, which should rapidly convert the cash balance into rental income and drive NAV growth.
The bear case centers on execution uncertainty, premium valuation, and governance structure. BXDC has zero operating assets and zero rental revenue — the $2B sits in cash generating interest income while management searches for acquisition targets in an extremely competitive market where data center valuations are at record highs [1][4]. Forward P/E of 40.3x prices in successful deployment at attractive returns that have not yet materialized. Tenant concentration is a key governance concern — if the portfolio exceeds 50% exposure to a single hyperscaler (AWS, Microsoft, or Meta), BXDC becomes concentrated credit risk with real estate optics [3]. Related-party pricing on potential asset transfers from Blackstone affiliates creates conflict-of-interest risk. The external management structure (Blackstone fees) creates a permanent drag on returns and misaligns management incentives (fees tied to assets under management, not total return). Data center opposition from communities over energy consumption and environmental impact could slow permitting and development. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) provides no faith-alignment signal.
Key Catalysts
  1. First data center acquisition announcement — deploying IPO capital into a stabilized asset leased to an investment-grade hyperscaler would validate the thesis, convert cash to rental income, and establish a baseline NAV and FFO run rate for re-rating [1]
  2. AI-driven hyperscale demand acceleration — continued investment by AWS, Microsoft, Meta, and Google in AI infrastructure creates a structural supply-demand imbalance that should support premium rents and high occupancy for BXDC's target assets [2]

Key Risks
  1. Pre-revenue execution risk — BXDC has zero operating assets and must deploy $2B in capital at attractive returns in a market where data center valuations are at record highs, with no guarantee that acquisition pricing will generate acceptable returns [1]
  2. Tenant concentration and related-party conflict — if the portfolio exceeds 50% exposure to a single hyperscaler, the REIT becomes concentrated credit risk; related-party asset transfers from Blackstone affiliates create pricing conflicts that investors cannot independently verify [3]
  3. Data center market cycle risk — if AI investment slows, hyperscaler capex is curtailed, or new data center supply catches up with demand, the favorable pricing environment that underpins BXDC's thesis could reverse, particularly given the premium implied by the 40.3x forward P/E [4]
