Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Packaged Foods ·NASDAQ ·United States
Beyond Meat develops, manufactures, and sells plant-based meat products including burgers, sausages, chicken, and jerky through retail and foodservice channels globally. The company is expanding its Beyond Immerse protein drink line and focusing on Europe and Canada for growth. Q2 2026 revenue of $68.8M declined 8.2% YoY, though the company posted $16.4M net income driven by a one-time debt restructuring gain. The company is executing a 1-for-30 reverse stock split on August 14, 2026 to regain Nasdaq listing compliance. Founded by CEO Ethan Brown. Headquartered in El Segundo, California.
589 Employees ·www.beyondmeat.com

Investment Thesis
Beyond Meat shows early signs of revenue stabilization at deeply depressed levels — Q2 2026 revenue of $68.8M declined only 8.2% YoY versus 15.3% in Q1, and Q3 guidance of $60-65M implies manageable deceleration [1]. The company posted $16.4M net income in Q2 (driven by debt restructuring) and continues reducing operating losses through cost discipline. Beyond Immerse protein drink expansion to Europe and Canada opens a potential new growth category beyond struggling meat analogs. A new COO hire signals commitment to operational improvement. At $0.45/share ($29M market cap pre-reverse split), the stock prices in substantial distress and any positive inflection in revenue trends or a strategic partnership/takeout bid could generate significant upside from the deeply depressed base. The Inspire Impact Score of 44 (ALIGNED) and Climate Solutions Certification validate the environmental mission [4].
The bear case is existential. Beyond Meat has lost 99.8% of its value from the $230 IPO peak, revenue has declined four consecutive years ($419M in FY2022 to $276M in FY2025), and the plant-based meat category has structurally stagnated as mainstream consumers returned to animal protein [1][2]. The company carries $411.6M in debt against $205.8M in cash with deeply negative operating margins (-40%), creating ongoing cash burn with no clear path to operating profitability [2]. The 1-for-30 reverse stock split on August 14, 2026 is a last-resort measure to maintain Nasdaq listing compliance — historically, reverse splits in distressed companies precede further stock declines. Employee morale is critically low with Glassdoor describing leadership as 'detached from reality,' and insider selling of -$2.1M with zero purchases over two years signals limited executive confidence [5]. D/E of 570.5% reflects a balance sheet that is one missed debt payment from insolvency, and the positive equity of $56.8M is entirely an artifact of one-time debt restructuring gains rather than operating improvement. Category-level secular decline in plant-based meat penetration means Beyond Meat's challenges are structural, not cyclical.
Key Catalysts
  1. Revenue decline narrowing from -15.3% (Q1) to -8.2% (Q2) may signal approaching stabilization — Q3 guidance of $60-65M implies the rate of decline is moderating, and any quarter of flat or positive revenue growth would be transformative for the stock [1]
  2. Beyond Immerse protein drink expansion to Europe and Canada could open a new category beyond struggling meat analogs, potentially reaching consumers who are not interested in meat substitutes but seek plant-based protein beverages [1]
  3. Deeply depressed valuation ($29M pre-reverse-split market cap) makes the company a potential acquisition target for larger food companies seeking plant-based brands and R&D capabilities at distressed pricing
Key Risks
  1. Existential going-concern risk — $411.6M debt against $205.8M cash with deeply negative operating margins and four consecutive years of revenue decline, requiring continued debt restructuring or capital raises at severely dilutive terms [2]
  2. Plant-based meat category structural decline — consumer adoption peaked in 2020-2021 and has reversed, with penetration rates declining as mainstream consumers returned to animal protein and 'ultra-processed' food backlash intensified [1]
  3. 1-for-30 reverse stock split on August 14, 2026 historically correlates with continued stock price decline in distressed companies — the split addresses Nasdaq compliance but not the underlying business deterioration [2]

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Beyond Meat's core business — manufacturing and selling physical food products — has meaningful natural protection from AI disruption, as AI cannot replace the physical production, distribution, and consumption of food [3].
