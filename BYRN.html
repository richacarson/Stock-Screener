Company Profile
Industrials ·Security & Protection Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Byrna Technologies Inc. designs, develops, manufactures, and sells less-lethal personal security devices and munitions. The flagship product is the Byrna launcher platform, a CO2-powered handheld device that fires chemical irritant, kinetic, and marking projectiles for civilian self-defense and law enforcement applications. Products are sold through direct-to-consumer e-commerce (byrna.com), authorized dealer networks, and retail chain store channels. Operating under the 'Live Safe' brand, Byrna is pioneering the consumer less-lethal self-defense category. Founded in 2005 as Security Devices International, rebranded in 2019, headquartered in Andover, Massachusetts.
175 Employees ·byrna.com

Investment Thesis
Byrna Technologies delivered record FY2025 results with $118.1M revenue (up 38% YoY), 61% gross margins, and $16.8M adjusted EBITDA, demonstrating the viability of the consumer less-lethal self-defense category [2]. The AI-driven marketing strategy produced viral content (66M+ views) and a 76% surge in web traffic, showcasing efficient customer acquisition [3]. The balance sheet is essentially debt-free ($2.05M total debt, $7.5M cash) with no near-term solvency risk, and recent insider buying of $520K across 14 transactions by five executives signals management conviction at depressed valuations [1][6]. At an $80M market cap and 0.74x trailing price-to-sales, the stock prices in significant near-term distress.
The bull case faces serious near-term headwinds. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew only 11% (decelerating sharply from 38%), net income collapsed to $0.8M from $1.7M, and management warned Q2 is 'developing below expectations' without providing guidance [1]. The stock has declined 85% from its 52-week high of $34.30 to $3.54, reflecting market skepticism about the growth trajectory [5]. The CEO transition from founder Bryan Ganz to Conn Davis (former MasterBrand EVP) introduces execution risk at a critical juncture — Davis must stabilize declining DTC conversion rates, reverse operating expense margin deterioration, and restore profitability while investing in retail channel expansion [4]. Average order values are declining due to product mix shifts, and the company remains heavily dependent on a single product platform (the Byrna launcher) with no diversification pipeline disclosed.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO Conn Davis's strategic plan execution — Davis brings public company growth experience from MasterBrand and could improve operational efficiency and retail partnerships, as evidenced by $520K in insider buying across 14 transactions by five executives in the past 90 days [4][6]
  2. Retail channel expansion into dealer and chain store networks diversifying revenue away from the decelerating DTC channel, which faces declining web-to-sale conversion rates and falling average order values [1][2]
  3. Continued AI advertising innovation driving cost-efficient customer acquisition — the viral AI campaign model that generated 66M+ views and 76% web traffic growth could be replicated to rebuild momentum at lower cost [3]
Key Risks
  1. DTC revenue deceleration accelerating — Q1 FY2026 showed declining average order values and weakening web-to-sale conversion rates, with management providing no quantified guidance for Q2 recovery, risking further earnings misses [1]
  2. Operating expense growth outpacing revenue: FY2025 opex grew 29% ($46.1M to $59.6M) against 38% revenue growth, but Q1 FY2026 opex remains elevated against decelerating revenue, pushing TTM net income to -$3.69M and eroding the profitability demonstrated in FY2025 [1][2][5]
  3. Single-product concentration risk — the Byrna launcher platform generates substantially all revenue, and any product safety issue, regulatory change (ATF reclassification), or consumer preference shift could materially impair the business without a diversified product portfolio [2]
