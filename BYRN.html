Company Profile
Industrials ·Security & Protection Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Byrna Technologies designs, develops, manufactures, and sells less-lethal personal security devices and munitions. The flagship product is the Byrna launcher platform, a CO2-powered handheld device that fires chemical irritant, kinetic, and marking projectiles for civilian self-defense and law enforcement. Products are sold through direct-to-consumer e-commerce (byrna.com), Amazon, authorized dealer networks, and a retail footprint of 1,500+ chain store locations including Academy Sports + Outdoors and Murdoch's. Revenue grew from under $1M in FY2019 to $118.1M in FY2025, but has collapsed in recent quarters with the stock declining 87% from its 52-week high of $30.62. New CEO Conn Davis was appointed in March 2026. Headquartered in Andover, Massachusetts.
159 Employees ·byrna.com

Investment Thesis
Byrna Technologies trades at 0.7x trailing revenue ($88M market cap on $109M TTM revenue) and is the undisputed pioneer of the consumer less-lethal self-defense category, having grown revenue from under $1M to $118M in six years [2]. The balance sheet is essentially debt-free (D/E 3.6%), insider buying of $520K by five executives signals management conviction at depressed prices, and the AI-driven marketing model demonstrated viral content creation (66M+ views, 76% web traffic surge) that could be replicated at lower cost [3][6]. Retail expansion into Academy Sports (targeting 200-250 locations) and Murdoch's provides physical distribution diversification away from the decelerating DTC channel. At $3.78, the stock prices in severe near-term distress with substantial optionality on a turnaround.
The bear case is acute and worsening. Revenue has collapsed -42.5% YoY, reversing the +38% growth trajectory of FY2025, and management has suspended guidance, stating Q2 was 'developing below expectations' with declining website conversion and average order value [1][4]. The stock has plunged 87% from its 52-week high of $30.62 to $3.78, reflecting market skepticism that the demand surge was a one-time spike rather than a sustainable category build. Cash has deteriorated from $15.5M to $9.6M in a single quarter with negative free cash flow (-$4.5M), potentially forcing dilutive capital raises at depressed prices. The CEO transition from founder Ganz to outsider Davis introduces execution risk at the worst possible moment — he must simultaneously stabilize collapsing DTC revenues, reverse margin deterioration, and invest in retail expansion [4]. Glassdoor culture scores (40% recommendation rate, 2.3/5 culture) suggest organizational dysfunction that could impede turnaround execution [5]. The company remains single-product dependent with no diversified pipeline disclosed.
Key Catalysts
  1. Academy Sports retail expansion from ~50 to 200-250 locations by year-end, diversifying revenue away from the collapsing DTC channel and providing physical in-store product demonstration that could improve conversion rates [1]
  2. New CEO Conn Davis's strategic plan execution — brings public company growth experience from MasterBrand Inc. and could improve operational efficiency, evidenced by $520K in insider buying across 14 transactions by five executives [4][6]
  3. AI advertising innovation enabling cost-efficient customer acquisition — the viral AI campaign model that generated 66M+ views could be replicated to rebuild marketing momentum at lower cost than traditional channels [3]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue collapse of -42.5% YoY with suspended guidance and declining website conversion signals the consumer demand thesis may have been a temporary spike rather than durable category creation, with no evidence of stabilization [1][4]
  2. Cash runway dangerously short at $9.6M with -$4.5M quarterly free cash flow, potentially requiring dilutive equity raises within 2-3 quarters that would further compress the $88M market cap [1]
  3. Single-product concentration risk — the Byrna launcher platform generates substantially all revenue, and any product safety issue, ATF reclassification, or consumer preference shift could materially impair the business without a diversified portfolio [2]
