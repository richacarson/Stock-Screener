Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NASDAQ ·China
Kanzhun Limited operates BOSS Zhipin, China's largest online recruitment platform by monthly active users. The platform uses AI-powered recommendation algorithms to connect job seekers with employers through a direct real-time chat model, covering blue-collar, white-collar, and professional positions. The company also develops the Nanbeige AI large language model and has integrated DeepSeek-R1 for enhanced recruitment matching. Founded in 2014 by CEO Peng Zhao, headquartered in Beijing, China.
4,884 Employees ·ir.zhipin.com

Investment Thesis
Kanzhun operates China's dominant recruitment platform (60.9M MAU, 7.1M paid enterprise customers) with exceptional financials — Q1 2026 revenue of RMB2.07B ($300M), net income up 120% YoY, operating income up 41.8%, and 33.1% profit margins on 85% gross margins [1]. Forward P/E of 10.84 and PEG of 0.11 represent deep value for a profitable, growing tech platform with a fortress balance sheet ($2.86B cash, virtually no debt) [5]. The self-developed Nanbeige LLM and DeepSeek-R1 integration power AI-enhanced recruitment features serving the largest user base in China's online hiring market [4]. Management's commitment to return at least 50% of adjusted net income through buybacks and dividends, with $400M repurchase authorization, provides shareholder return visibility [1]. Q2 2026 guidance of 13-15% revenue growth signals accelerating momentum from the 7.6% Q1 growth rate.
The bear case centers on China macro risk, regulatory uncertainty, and revenue deceleration. China's challenging economic environment has pressured employer recruitment spending — Q1 2026 revenue growth of 7.6% represents meaningful deceleration from 12.4% in FY2025 and 14% historically, though Q2 guidance of 13-15% suggests recovery [1]. As a Chinese ADR listed on NASDAQ (also HKEX: 2076), Kanzhun faces VIE structure risk, potential delisting threats under HFCAA, and regulatory unpredictability from PRC authorities — the company was subject to a cybersecurity review shortly after its 2021 IPO [4]. The stock has declined 35% year-to-date to $13.48, well below its 200-day average of $19.17, reflecting broad China tech sentiment weakness [5]. AI is both an enabler and potential disruptor — generalist AI tools could eventually bypass specialized recruitment platforms, and international expansion remains limited. Despite exceptional fundamentals, the China discount may persist indefinitely given U.S.-China geopolitical tensions.
