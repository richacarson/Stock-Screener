Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Packaged Foods ·NYSE ·United States
Conagra Brands manufactures and markets branded consumer packaged foods across the frozen, snacks, and shelf-stable categories, with brands including Birds Eye, Marie Callender's, Banquet, Slim Jim, and Duncan Hines. Founded in 1919 and headquartered in Chicago, the company reported FY2026 revenue of $11.28B, a GAAP net loss of $1.92B, and is undergoing a leadership transition to new CEO John Brase alongside a strategic reset that includes a 50% dividend cut announced in July 2026.

Investment Thesis
Conagra trades at a discounted 10.80x forward P/E with a 51-year unbroken dividend payment history, and new CEO John Brase's decisive 50% dividend cut in July 2026 frees up capital to accelerate deleveraging as part of a comprehensive strategic reset [1][4]. The company retains scale and category positions in frozen (Birds Eye, Marie Callender's) and snacks (Slim Jim, Duncan Hines), and management's willingness to reset the payout in favor of balance sheet health, rather than protecting the dividend at all costs, signals a pragmatic capital allocation shift under new leadership.
The bear case is dominated by a deteriorating fundamental picture. Conagra reported a FY2026 GAAP net loss of $1.92B on revenue of $11.28B (down 2.9% YoY), with ROE of -25.06% and profit margin of -17.02%, and FY2027 guidance calls for further mid-single-digit organic sales declines with operating margin compressing to 10-10.5% versus roughly 16% several years ago [1]. The company was removed from the S&P 500 in the same period as the dividend cut, and 5-year revenue CAGR is negative at -0.67%, indicating structural, not cyclical, category headwinds from private label competition and shifting consumer preferences. Glassdoor reviews citing 'all-time low morale' and D/E of 118% (above the 70% target) further compound the risk picture, and the newly reset dividend growth streak (0 consecutive years of growth, -10.95% 5-year CAGR) removes what was previously Conagra's strongest Infrastructure asset.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO John Brase's strategic reset and accelerated deleveraging from the 50% dividend cut could stabilize the balance sheet and set up a re-rating from the current 10.80x forward P/E if execution proves out over FY2027 [1][4]
  2. Stabilization of organic sales trends following the FY2027 mid-single-digit decline guidance would validate that the worst of the volume erosion has been absorbed into estimates [1]
  3. Portfolio simplification and cost discipline under new leadership could restore operating margin toward historical levels above the current 10-10.5% FY2027 guidance range [1]
Key Risks
  1. FY2026 GAAP net loss of $1.92B and negative ROE of -25.06% reflect a business in active distress, with FY2027 guidance calling for further mid-single-digit organic sales declines rather than stabilization [1]
  2. The 50% dividend cut breaks a 51-year payment streak's growth trajectory and signals to income investors that the balance sheet is more stretched than previously understood, with D/E at 118% against a 70% target [1][4]
  3. Persistently negative 5-year revenue CAGR (-0.67%) alongside recent S&P 500 removal indicates structural category decline from private label and shifting consumer preferences that cost-cutting alone cannot reverse [1]
