Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Packaged Foods ·NYSE ·United States
Conagra Brands manufactures and markets branded consumer packaged foods across frozen, snacks, and shelf-stable categories, with brands including Birds Eye, Marie Callender's, Banquet, Slim Jim, Duncan Hines, Healthy Choice, and Reddi-wip. Founded in 1919 and headquartered in Chicago. FY2026 revenue was $11.3B (-2.9% YoY) with a GAAP net loss of $1.92B driven by ~$3B in impairments. New CEO John Brase (former Smucker COO) took over in June 2026 from Sean Connolly, implementing a strategic reset including a 50% dividend cut to $0.70/year and FY2027 guidance of organic sales decline of 3-1% with adjusted EPS of $1.40-$1.50.
17,400 Employees ·www.conagrabrands.com

Investment Thesis
Conagra at $13.30 trades at just 8.7x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.40-$1.50, one of the cheapest valuations in packaged foods, with a 5.2% dividend yield even after the 50% cut [4]. New CEO John Brase's decisive dividend cut frees ~$350M annually to accelerate deleveraging of the $7.76B debt load, and his strategic reset -- portfolio simplification, supply chain optimization, AI investment through Project Catalyst -- provides a framework for margin recovery from the guided 10.0-10.5% toward historical levels above 14% [1][2]. The company retains scale and category positions in frozen (Birds Eye, Marie Callender's, Banquet) and snacks (Slim Jim, Duncan Hines), and the core business of manufacturing physical food products is structurally immune to AI disruption (AI Resilience 8/10). The Inspire Impact Score of 46 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions supports faith-based consideration.
The bear case dominates the fundamental picture. Conagra reported a FY2026 GAAP net loss of $1.92B on revenue of $11.3B (-2.9% YoY), driven by ~$3B in impairments that reflect value destruction from the 2018 Pinnacle Foods acquisition [1]. FY2027 guidance calls for further organic sales declines of 3-1% and adjusted operating margin of only 10.0-10.5%, with no timeline for returning to growth. The stock was removed from the S&P 500, the dividend was cut 50% (resetting the growth streak to 0 and producing a -11% 5yr dividend CAGR), ROE is -24.9%, and D/E of 121% reflects $7.76B in debt against only $372M cash [4]. The packaged-food category faces structural headwinds from private-label competition, consumer shift to fresher and less-processed alternatives, and GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption reducing snacking volumes. Glassdoor reviews describing 'all-time low morale' raise questions about whether the workforce can execute Brase's turnaround vision [2].
