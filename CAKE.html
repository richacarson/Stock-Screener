Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NASDAQ ·United States
The Cheesecake Factory Incorporated operates and licenses restaurants in the United States and Canada under The Cheesecake Factory, North Italia, Flower Child, and Fox Restaurant Concepts brands. The company also operates bakeries producing proprietary cheesecakes and baked products. Q2 2026 revenue surpassed $1 billion for the first time ($1.03B, +7.7% YoY) with record net income of $68M. Founded in 1972, headquartered in Calabasas, California, with approximately $4.9B market cap and 48,000 employees.

Investment Thesis
The Cheesecake Factory delivered record Q2 2026 results — revenue surpassing $1 billion for the first time ($1.03B, +7.7%), record net income of $68M (+25%), adjusted EPS of $1.44 beating Street estimates of $1.15, and restaurant-level margin of 20%, the highest in a decade [1]. Comparable sales growth of 5.8% at flagship restaurants demonstrates strong consumer demand in a challenging casual dining environment. At 19.6x forward earnings with ROE of 38.8%, up to 26 new openings planned for FY2026, the multi-brand platform (North Italia, Flower Child) providing higher-growth vectors, and 7-8% average annual revenue growth targets, CAKE offers a rare combination of operational excellence and growth in the restaurant sector [4]. The 13-year Fortune Best Companies streak, $12M average unit volumes, and Inspire Insight ALIGNED score of 42 strengthen the quality and values thesis.
The bear case centers on structural restaurant industry challenges and financial leverage. D/E of 401% reflects massive operating lease liabilities across hundreds of locations ($2.1B total debt against $195M cash), creating cash flow vulnerability if consumer spending weakens in a recession [4]. Profit margin of 4.6% leaves minimal cushion against food cost inflation, minimum wage increases, or traffic declines — the casual dining sector faces ongoing secular headwinds from fast-casual competition and shifting consumer preferences toward convenience. Revenue 5yr CAGR of ~5% and PEG of 1.74 suggest the current growth rate may not justify the premium over peers. The company's moderate ESG controversy level on Sustainalytics, including a past $1M California wage theft settlement involving janitorial workers, creates tension with the Fortune Best Companies employer brand [3]. The ambitious 585-restaurant long-term plan requires sustained capital allocation discipline and management bandwidth across multiple brands while maintaining the scratch-made quality that defines the concept.

  2. Razor-thin margins — profit margin of 4.6% provides minimal cushion against food cost inflation, minimum wage increases, or same-store traffic declines in a highly competitive casual dining market where fast-casual alternatives continue gaining share [4]
  3. Revenue 5yr CAGR of ~5% and PEG of 1.74 suggest the current valuation may fully reflect the company's growth trajectory, with limited upside if comparable sales decelerate from the strong 5.8% pace [1][4]
