Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NYSE ·United States
Calix, Inc. provides cloud and software platforms, systems, and services that enable broadband service providers (BSPs) to simplify operations, engage subscribers, and grow their businesses. The AI-native Calix One platform processes 4.3 billion workflows annually and ingests more than a petabyte of data daily, serving Tier 2/3 broadband providers across the United States. The company has transitioned from a hardware networking vendor to a cloud-first, AI-native platform company over 15+ years with more than $2 billion in cumulative investment. Founded in 1999, headquartered in San Jose, California.
1,921 Employees ·www.calix.com

Investment Thesis
Calix is delivering on a 15-year platform investment with record Q2 2026 revenue of $293M (+21% YoY), Q1 revenue of $280M (+27% YoY), and raised full-year guidance of 15-20% revenue growth [1][6]. The AI-native Calix One platform has achieved remarkable internal adoption (98% employee AI usage, 400+ AI agents, $37M estimated productivity gains) and is increasingly differentiated for BSP customers [2]. The debt-free balance sheet (D/E 2%), forward P/E of 20.0, and estimated forward PEG of 0.54 offer attractive valuation for a company with 27.8% TTM revenue growth and accelerating profitability ($17.1M Q2 net income). The exceptional workplace culture (Fortune #85, Glassdoor Top 25 in Tech & AI) creates sustainable competitive advantage through talent retention. The Inspire Impact Score of 71 (ALIGNED) provides faith-based investment compatibility [5].
The primary risk is the stock's 35.5% decline from its 52-week high of $71.22 to $37.98, reflecting market concerns about the sustainability of growth and the impact of memory cost inflation on hardware margins [6]. The 51.85 trailing P/E (despite only $0.73 EPS) means the valuation assumes significant profit margin expansion from the current 4.61% — if margins don't expand, the stock is overvalued [5]. The FY2024 revenue decline of 20% demonstrates the cyclicality risk inherent in BSP spending patterns, and another capital expenditure downturn could repeat this pattern. Calix serves primarily Tier 2/3 BSPs, creating customer concentration risk if rural broadband subsidies decline. Competition from larger players (Adtran, Nokia, Ericsson) with deeper resources could pressure margins, and Q3 2026 guidance disappointed investors, contributing to the recent stock decline [6]. ROE of only 6.91% suggests the company has not yet fully converted its revenue growth into shareholder returns.
Key Catalysts
  1. Profit margin expansion from 4.61% toward double digits as the software and cloud revenue mix increases and operational leverage improves, potentially driving significant EPS growth at the current 36.8% 3-year forecast rate
  2. Continued AI-native platform adoption by BSP customers as agentic AI capabilities differentiate Calix from hardware-only competitors, driving recurring revenue growth and customer stickiness
  3. Federal broadband subsidy programs (BEAD funding) reaching deployment phase could accelerate BSP capital spending and drive demand for Calix platforms and equipment through 2027-2028
Key Risks
  1. Memory cost inflation pressuring appliance gross margins — Calix has introduced surcharges to offset higher memory costs, but these may not fully compensate and could dampen customer demand
  2. BSP capital expenditure cyclicality demonstrated by the 20% revenue decline in FY2024, creating risk of another spending downturn if rural broadband subsidies are delayed or reduced
  3. Trailing P/E of 51.85 on $0.73 EPS assumes aggressive margin expansion from 4.61% profit margins — failure to expand margins would reveal the stock as overvalued despite strong revenue growth
