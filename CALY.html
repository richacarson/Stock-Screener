Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Leisure Products ·NYSE ·United States
Callaway Golf Company (NYSE: CALY), formerly Topgolf Callaway Brands, is a global leader in golf equipment, apparel, and lifestyle brands. The company completed the sale of a 60% majority stake in Topgolf to Leonard Green & Partners in January 2026 for ~$1.1B, returning to pure-play golf. Portfolio includes Callaway golf equipment, Odyssey putters, TravisMathew apparel, and OGIO gear. Q2 2026 reported revenue of $612M (+2% YoY), GAAP net income from continuing operations of $75.8M (+67%), and adjusted EBITDA of $125M (+36%). The company has paid off $1.4B in debt and achieved a net cash position, with $84M in share repurchases completed in H1 2026.

Investment Thesis
Callaway Golf at $15.74 with a PEG of 0.34 is a compelling post-restructuring turnaround — the pure-play golf business is delivering accelerating profitability with Q2 2026 net income from continuing operations surging 67% to $75.8M, adjusted EBITDA up 36% to $125M, and gross margins expanding 460bps [1]. The company has paid off $1.4B in debt to achieve a net cash position, initiated $84M in H1 buybacks, and raised full-year EBITDA guidance by $31M at the midpoint. US golf ball market share hit a record 23%+ (up 250bps YoY), and the Ai-10x technology platform differentiates Callaway's premium equipment. Forward P/E of 24.62x looks reasonable for a business inflecting from Topgolf-distorted losses to double-digit continuing margins.
The bear case centers on trailing metric distortions and growth limitations. Trailing profit margin of -15.03% and ROE of 2.21% reflect the $1B+ Topgolf loss that continues to weigh on reported results — investors must trust that continuing operations margins (~12.4% in Q2) represent the true run rate [4]. Revenue growth of only 2% YoY in Q2 raises questions about organic growth potential for a mature golf equipment business post-separation. The 24.62x forward P/E and $15.74 stock price (+108% in 52 weeks) have already priced in significant recovery, leaving limited upside if margin expansion decelerates. Golf participation could revert toward pre-pandemic norms, and TaylorMade's recent IPO brings a well-capitalized competitor with increased marketing and R&D intensity. Glassdoor culture ratings of 3.0/5 suggest internal organizational challenges that could constrain execution through the transition [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continuing operations margin expansion as Topgolf separation costs roll off — Q2 2026 gross margin expanded 460bps and operating income from continuing ops rose 67%, a trend that should accelerate in H2 2026 and normalize trailing ROE and profit margin [1]
  2. Record US golf ball market share of 23%+ with 15% revenue growth in the ball segment, driven by engineering innovation and brand strength — this share gain trajectory has room to continue [1]
  3. Balance sheet transformation from $1.4B debt repayment to net cash position enables expanded shareholder returns (buybacks, potential dividend reinstatement) and opportunistic M&A in golf lifestyle [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth of only 2% YoY in Q2 2026 suggests the pure-play golf equipment business may face a low organic growth ceiling, limiting upside in a stock that has already rallied 108% in 52 weeks [1]
  2. Trailing financial distortions from Topgolf separation ($15.03% negative profit margin, 2.21% ROE) may mask underlying challenges if margin normalization is slower than expected or one-time costs extend [4]
  3. TaylorMade's recent IPO brings a well-capitalized competitor with increased marketing and R&D spend, intensifying competition for premium golf equipment market share and potentially compressing Callaway's margins [3]
