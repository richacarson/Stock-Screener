Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·China
Cango Inc. is a Bitcoin mining company with operations deployed across 40+ sites in North America, the Middle East, South America, and East Africa. Originally a Chinese automotive transaction service platform, Cango entered the crypto mining business in November 2024 and divested its PRC business for $352M to accelerate the transformation. The company is exploring GPU-based AI compute infrastructure as an adjacent business line. Incorporated in the Cayman Islands, listed on NYSE as an ADR.

Investment Thesis
Cango offers a highly speculative option on Bitcoin mining economics and an emerging AI compute pivot at a deeply distressed valuation. The stock trades at $0.21 (down 92.6% from its 52-week high) with a $77M market cap against $117.8M in cash — technically below cash value if the mining operations are excluded [3]. Mining operations span 40+ global sites with a 9% reduction in average cash cost per Bitcoin in Q1 2026 [1]. The AI compute pivot — appointing CTO Jack Jin and planning modular GPU node deployment for enterprise AI inference — could provide higher-margin revenue if successfully executed [2]. At this distressed valuation, any stabilization of Bitcoin prices, successful AI compute commercialization, or further cost reduction in mining could drive outsized percentage returns.
The bear case is overwhelming. Q1 2026 produced a $261.1M net loss on $102M revenue, with $151.8M in non-cash fair value losses and $49M in mining machine impairment [1]. Revenue declined 43% sequentially as hash rates fell. The 10-for-1 reverse stock split in July 2026 is typically a last-resort measure to maintain exchange listing requirements, and daily dollar volume of ~$422K fails the IOWN liquidity floor, making the stock effectively uninvestable at institutional scale [3]. The company has pivoted its entire business model twice in two years (auto finance to mining, now mining to AI compute), suggesting management chases trends rather than building durable businesses. Leadership instability — three CFO transitions in 18 months — creates governance risk compounded by the Cayman Islands incorporation structure that limits shareholder protections. Net debt of $107M against the $77M market cap implies the equity may have limited recovery value if Bitcoin prices remain range-bound. Wall Street consensus expects a $117M net loss for full-year 2026 [1].
