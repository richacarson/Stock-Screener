Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NASDAQ ·United States
CarGurus is an online automotive platform connecting car shoppers with dealers through a data-driven marketplace anchored by transparency and AI-powered tools. The company operates in the U.S. and internationally, generating revenue from dealer subscriptions, advertising, and financing partnerships. CarGurus reported Q2 2026 revenue of $251M (+13% YoY) with full-year guidance of 10-13% revenue growth, driven by AI product adoption, premium tier migration, and net dealer additions [1][2]. The company celebrated its 20th anniversary in 2026 with a 'next chapter of AI-powered innovation' initiative, having divested the CarOffer wholesale business in 2023 to focus on its core marketplace [3].

Investment Thesis
CarGurus at 10.1x forward earnings and a PEG of 0.69 is a significantly undervalued AI-powered marketplace delivering double-digit revenue growth with expanding profitability. Q2 2026 revenue of $251M (+13% YoY) and non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA of $84.7M demonstrate that AI investments are translating to measurable business results — AI tripled dealer leads and products explicitly drove 13% revenue growth [1][2][4]. The financial profile is exceptional: ROE of 53.5%, D/E of 70.0%, profit margin of 18.2%, and forward PEG of 0.69 place CarGurus among the best-valued growth stocks in internet services [5]. Full-year 2026 guidance of 10-13% revenue growth with stable margins provides visibility, while the 20-year data moat (IMV pricing database) and 91% employee AI tool adoption rate create durable competitive advantages [3]. The post-CarOffer divestiture focus on core marketplace has improved capital efficiency and strategic clarity.
The bear case centers on AI disruption risk, competitive intensity, and cyclical auto market exposure. Despite CarGurus' AI offensive, conversational AI assistants from Google, Apple, or well-funded startups could fundamentally change how consumers discover and purchase vehicles, potentially disintermediating traditional listing platforms and reducing the value of dealer subscriptions. Cars.com and AutoTrader compete directly for dealer budgets, and dealer consolidation could reduce the addressable market. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors. Revenue growth guidance of 10-13% is solid but not accelerating, and the stock has declined from its 52-week high of $41.22 to $30.57 (-26%), suggesting market skepticism about sustaining growth rates. OEM and national advertising revenue trends remain uncertain in a shifting automotive landscape. The company has 1,218 employees — relatively lean, but this also means limited organizational depth for executing an ambitious AI transformation across multiple product lines.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI product monetization acceleration as CarGurus Discover (conversational vehicle search), PriceVantage, and dealer AI tools drive premium tier adoption and higher ARPU per dealer — AI already tripled leads and drove 13% revenue growth [2][4]
  2. Forward PEG of 0.69 suggests significant multiple expansion potential if the market re-rates CarGurus from a legacy auto marketplace to an AI-powered platform, with forward P/E of 10.1x offering valuation support [5]
  3. International expansion and net dealer additions providing incremental revenue growth on top of domestic marketplace monetization improvements, contributing to the 10-13% full-year revenue growth outlook [2]
Key Risks
  1. AI disruption from conversational AI assistants (Google, Apple, ChatGPT) could fundamentally change vehicle discovery, reducing the relevance of traditional listing-based marketplaces and compressing dealer subscription willingness-to-pay
  2. Competitive intensity from AutoTrader and Cars.com for a finite pool of dealer budgets, with dealer consolidation potentially reducing the total addressable market for marketplace subscriptions
  3. Cyclical exposure to auto market conditions — any slowdown in new or used vehicle sales could reduce dealer appetite for marketing spend, compressing CarGurus' revenue growth below the 10-13% guidance range [2]
