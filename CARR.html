Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & HVAC ·NYSE ·United States
Carrier Global is the world's leading provider of intelligent climate and energy solutions, with operations spanning HVAC (heating, ventilation, air conditioning), refrigeration, and building automation. Spun off from United Technologies in April 2020, Carrier has since transformed its portfolio through the $13B acquisition of Viessmann Climate Solutions (2024) and divestiture of Fire & Security businesses to become a pure-play climate solutions company. CEO David Gitlin describes it as a '100-year-old startup,' honoring Willis Carrier's 1902 invention of modern air conditioning. Q2 2026 sales reached $6.4B (+4% YoY), orders surged 40% with data center orders quadrupling, and backlog surpassed $8B. FY2026 guidance was raised to ~$23B in sales and ~$2.90 adjusted EPS [1].

Investment Thesis
Carrier at $60.37 (19.6x forward earnings) offers a compelling entry into the global leader in climate solutions at an inflection point — the pure-play transformation is complete, data center cooling is exploding (orders 4x, revenue forecast raised to ~$2B), and FY2026 guidance was raised to ~$23B sales and ~$2.90 adjusted EPS [1]. The $8B+ backlog with 40% order growth provides strong visibility, and the Viessmann integration unlocks European heat pump leadership as regulatory mandates accelerate the energy transition [2]. Forward P/E of 19.6x is attractive relative to the 20x target and well below the 43.4x trailing PE that reflects depressed GAAP earnings from integration costs. As Viessmann synergies materialize and integration costs normalize, GAAP margins should converge toward the 17.2% adjusted operating margin, potentially driving 30-40% EPS expansion over the next 2 years [1]. Carrier's 120-year heritage as the inventor of modern air conditioning provides brand moat and institutional knowledge that competitors cannot replicate.
The bear case focuses on integration execution, margin compression, and growth sustainability. Adjusted operating margins compressed 190bps to 17.2% in Q2, and GAAP profit margin of only 5.52% reflects the real cost of integrating a $13B acquisition — margin recovery is not guaranteed [1][4]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~2% demonstrates that organic growth has been modest, with acquisitions and divestitures masking an underlying business that grows with construction cycles and replacement demand. D/E of 92% reflects acquisition-driven leverage that could constrain financial flexibility if end markets soften. ROE of only 8.97% — well below the 15% target — reflects the diluted return profile of a company mid-transformation. Residential HVAC demand is cyclical and sensitive to interest rates, and the European heat pump market faces policy uncertainty. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) will limit allocation for faith-based investors despite strong sustainability credentials [3].
