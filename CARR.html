Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NYQ ·United States
Carrier Global is the world's leading provider of intelligent climate and energy solutions, spanning HVAC, heat pumps, refrigeration, and building automation. Spun off from United Technologies in April 2020, Carrier transformed its portfolio through the $13B Viessmann Climate Solutions acquisition (2024) and divestiture of Fire & Security to become a pure-play climate solutions company. CEO David Gitlin describes it as a '100-year-old startup,' honoring Willis Carrier's 1902 invention of modern air conditioning. Q2 2026 sales reached $6.4B with organic sales up 3%, orders surged 40% YoY with data center orders quadrupling, and backlog surpassed $8B. FY2026 guidance raised to approximately $23B in sales and $2.90 adjusted EPS [1].

Investment Thesis
Carrier at $55.03 (16.6x forward earnings, PEG 1.07) offers a compelling entry into the global leader in climate solutions at a strategic inflection point — the pure-play transformation is complete, data center cooling is exploding (orders 4x YoY, revenue forecast raised to approximately $2B), and FY2026 guidance was raised to approximately $23B in sales and $2.90 adjusted EPS [1]. The $8B+ backlog with 40% order growth provides strong revenue visibility, and the Viessmann integration unlocks European heat pump leadership as regulatory mandates accelerate the energy transition [2]. Forward P/E of 16.6x and PEG of 1.07 are attractive relative to the company's positioning at the intersection of two secular tailwinds — data center cooling and decarbonization. As Viessmann synergies materialize and integration costs normalize, GAAP margins should converge toward the 17%+ adjusted operating margin, potentially driving 30-40% EPS expansion over the next two years [1].
The bear case focuses on integration execution, margin compression, and organic growth concerns. GAAP profit margin of only 5.5% reflects the real cost of integrating a $13B acquisition — Viessmann synergies are promised but margin recovery is not guaranteed [1]. Revenue growth YoY of only 3.9% and 5-year CAGR of approximately 4.5% demonstrate that organic growth has been modest, with the Riello divestiture partially offsetting Viessmann's contribution. D/E of 92% reflects acquisition-driven leverage that constrains financial flexibility if end markets soften. ROE of only 8.97% — well below the 15% target — reflects the diluted return profile of a company mid-transformation. EPS declined 11.8% YoY in the most recent quarter due to integration charges. Residential HVAC demand is cyclical and sensitive to interest rates, and the European heat pump market faces policy uncertainty if government subsidies are reduced [1][3].
