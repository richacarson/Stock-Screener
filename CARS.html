Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NYSE ·United States
Cars.com is an audience-driven technology company providing digital marketplace and media solutions for the automotive industry. The company offers dealer websites, reputation management, digital financing tools, trade-in products (AccuTrade), and media solutions (Cars Social, VIN Performance Media) to local dealers, OEMs, and dealer groups. Cars.com reported Q2 2026 revenue of $179.9M (+1% YoY) with full-year guidance of flat to 2% revenue growth, while net income rose 103% to $14.3M on margin expansion [1][2]. New CEO Tobias Hartmann took over in January 2026 amid a period of cultural and strategic uncertainty.

Q2 2026 Marketplace revenue grew 7% — the fastest since 2021 — and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 29.4%, demonstrating that the core dealer subscription business retains pricing power [1]. The $90M share repurchase program at these depressed levels ($10.35 stock price, down from $13.69 high) could meaningfully reduce share count [2]. Net income growth of 103% shows operational leverage is working even with minimal revenue growth. The company has an ALIGNED Inspire Impact Score of 39.
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. Revenue growth has stagnated at 0-2%, with 5yr CAGR of only 3.4% — the company is not growing in a market that demands growth [1][4]. Profit margin of 4.7% is extremely thin for a digital platform, and ROE of 7.4% is well below the cost of equity. The Glassdoor culture reviews are alarming: 'poor management,' 'aversion to innovation,' 'hierarchical and secretive,' 'layoffs hurting morale,' and 'erosion of trust in leadership' suggest deep organizational dysfunction that new CEO Tobias Hartmann (Jan 2026) has not yet addressed [3]. OEM and National revenue declined 18%, offsetting Marketplace gains. AI disruption risk is real — CarGurus has far more aggressively embraced AI (tripling dealer leads with ML tools) while Cars.com employees report leadership 'relying entirely on basic AI tools.' The company risks becoming the Kodak of online auto marketplaces — defending a legacy model while more innovative competitors redefine the category. D/E of 100.8% adds financial risk to operational challenges.
