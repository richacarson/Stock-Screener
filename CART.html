Company Profile

Maplebear Inc., doing business as Instacart, operates a technology platform connecting consumers with grocery retailers for delivery and pickup. The company offers Instacart Marketplace, Instacart Enterprise (SaaS for retailers), Instacart Ads (CPG brand advertising), and Caper Cart AI-powered smart shopping carts. Q1 2026 revenue crossed $1B for the first time at $1.019B (+14% YoY) with $144M GAAP net income (+36%). Q2 2026 revenue reached $1.04B (+14%) with GTV of $10.35B (+14%) and adjusted EBITDA of $313M (+19%) [1]. CEO Chris Rogers succeeded Fidji Simo in August 2025. Market cap approximately $11.8B.

Investment Thesis
Instacart is the dominant U.S. grocery delivery marketplace with near-perfect financial infrastructure — virtually debt-free (D/E 1.35%), 16% ROE, and strong cash generation from a $4.0B TTM revenue base growing 14% [1][4]. Q2 2026 extended momentum with $1.04B revenue (+14%) and $313M adjusted EBITDA (+19%), while management guided Q3 GTV of $10.3-10.55B. At 10.2x forward earnings, the stock has repriced significantly lower from 20x in prior screen, creating potential value for a dominant platform with $850M cash, advertising revenue growing 16% from 9,000+ brands, and Enterprise AI Solutions extending the technology moat. The Caper Cart, ChatGPT shopping integration, and Instaleap international expansion provide growth vectors beyond core delivery [2].
The bear case centers on AI disintermediation risk, gig worker liability, and growth deceleration. Meta's Muse agent directly threatens Instacart's advertising engine, and former CEO Simo's move to OpenAI to build grocery AI agents validates the platform displacement risk [2][3]. The FTC's $60M prior settlement and new AI pricing probe signal escalating regulatory scrutiny around Instacart's core algorithms. The gig model faces structural legal risk from the $46.5M California misclassification settlement and algorithmic pricing legislation proposed in 12+ states. Revenue growth has decelerated from 39% (2022) to 14% (2026), and Q2 GAAP EPS of $0.45 missed the $0.54 consensus due to $142M stock-based compensation [1]. The PEG ratio expansion from 0.82 to 2.01 suggests the market has repriced growth expectations lower. The Inspire Impact Score of -75 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors [4].

  3. Revenue growth deceleration from 39% (2022) to 14% (2026) with PEG expanding to 2.01 — Q2 GAAP EPS missed estimates by $0.09, and slowing order growth (+9%) suggests market saturation risk in core U.S. grocery delivery [1][4]
