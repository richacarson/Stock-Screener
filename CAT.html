Company Profile
Industrials ·Farm & Heavy Construction Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Caterpillar is the world's largest manufacturer of construction and mining equipment, off-highway diesel and natural gas engines, industrial gas turbines, and diesel-electric locomotives. The company operates through three primary segments: Construction Industries, Resource Industries, and Energy & Transportation. Under CEO Joe Creed (Chairman since April 2026), CAT is experiencing a historic growth cycle driven by AI data center power generation demand, U.S. infrastructure investment, and global reindustrialization. Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $20.5B (+24% YoY) with backlog soaring 92% to $72.1B.

Investment Thesis
Caterpillar at $850 is the dominant global franchise in construction and mining equipment riding the most powerful capex super-cycle in decades — record Q2 2026 revenue of $20.5B (+24% YoY), record backlog of $72.1B (+92% YoY), and a guidance raise to mid-to-high-teens growth reflect demand driven by AI data center construction, U.S. infrastructure investment, and global reindustrialization [1]. The Energy & Transportation segment's power generation revenue surged 48% as hyperscalers deploy CAT turbines and gensets at scale, with orders extending into 2030. ROE of 56.97%, a 27% payout ratio supporting a 33-year dividend growth streak at 7.95% CAGR, and $74.7B in TTM revenue demonstrate a capital-efficient franchise returning cash while investing in autonomous and AI-powered equipment platforms [4][6].
The primary risk is cyclicality meeting premium valuation — at 29.3x forward earnings, CAT prices in sustained mid-teens growth, but the construction equipment cycle has historically peaked after 2-3 years of strong demand, and a global slowdown or tariff escalation could compress both volumes and margins simultaneously [4]. The 233% debt-to-equity ratio, driven by Cat Financial's $45.1B lending portfolio, introduces leverage risk if credit conditions tighten or equipment residual values decline. Insider selling of $87M+ in the last 90 days across multiple C-suite executives suggests leadership may view the stock as fully valued near all-time highs [5]. The 0.78% dividend yield offers minimal income protection in a correction, and Glassdoor-reported cultural challenges under new leadership could affect operational execution during the most demanding growth period in CAT's history.
