Company Profile
Industrials ·Farm & Heavy Construction Machinery ·NYQ ·United States
Caterpillar is the world's largest manufacturer of construction and mining equipment, off-highway diesel and natural gas engines, industrial gas turbines, and diesel-electric locomotives. The company operates through Construction Industries, Resource Industries, and Energy & Transportation segments. Under CEO Joe Creed (Chairman since April 2026), CAT is experiencing a historic growth cycle driven by AI data center power generation demand, U.S. infrastructure investment, and global reindustrialization. Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $20.5B (+24% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $8.17 (+73%), and backlog soared 92% to $72.1B [1].

Investment Thesis
Caterpillar at $725.24 (down ~15% from its ~$850 peak) offers improved value at 22.3x forward earnings for the world's dominant heavy equipment franchise riding the strongest capex super-cycle in decades — record Q2 2026 revenue of $20.5B (+24% YoY), adjusted EPS of $8.17 (+73%), and record backlog of $72.1B (+92% YoY) reflect demand driven by AI data center construction, U.S. infrastructure investment, and global reindustrialization [1]. The Energy & Transportation segment's power generation revenue surged 48% as hyperscalers deploy CAT turbines and gensets at scale, with orders extending into 2030. Management raised FY2026 guidance to mid-to-high-teens revenue growth. ROE of 57%, a 26% payout ratio supporting a 33-year dividend growth streak at 7.2% CAGR, and the NVIDIA Cat AI Assistant partnership position CAT as both an infrastructure beneficiary and AI participant [1][2].
The bear case centers on cyclicality meeting a still-premium valuation — at 22.3x forward earnings (improved from 29.3x at peak), CAT prices in sustained growth, but the construction equipment cycle has historically peaked after 2-3 years of strong demand, and backlog conversion could decelerate if global macro conditions deteriorate [1]. The 232.8% debt-to-equity ratio, driven by Cat Financial's ~$45B lending portfolio, introduces leverage risk if credit conditions tighten or equipment residual values decline. Insider selling of $87M+ by C-suite executives in Q2 2026 signals potential valuation concern even at lower prices [3]. The ESG controversy around Caterpillar equipment use in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict has resulted in exclusion from socially responsible indexes and TIAA-CREF divestment, carrying ongoing reputational risk [3]. Profit margin of 14.5% is below the 20% target, and the 0.9% dividend yield offers minimal income protection in a correction despite the strong growth streak.
