Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NYSE ·United States
CAVA Group, Inc. owns and operates a chain of fast-casual Mediterranean restaurants under the CAVA brand in the United States. The company serves customizable bowls, pitas, and salads made with chef-crafted dips and spreads, and sells a line of dips and spreads through grocery retail channels. Founded in 2006, IPO'd in June 2023 at $22/share, headquartered in Washington, D.C. CAVA operates over 367 restaurants with an expansion target of 1,000 locations by 2032. Q2 FY2026 delivered revenue of $365.4M (+31.3% YoY), same-restaurant sales growth of 9.0% with traffic up 5.3%, restaurant-level profit margin of 25.7%, and net income of $23M (+25.3%). TTM revenue reached $1.29B with $61.6M in profits.

Investment Thesis
CAVA continues to execute at elite levels, with Q2 FY2026 delivering $365.4M revenue (+31.3% YoY), same-restaurant sales growth of 9.0% with traffic up 5.3%, restaurant-level profit margin of 25.7%, and net income up 25.3% to $23M [1]. TTM revenue has reached $1.29B with the company guiding 75-77 net new openings for FY2026 and SSSG of 4.5-6.5%. With a clear path to 1,000 locations by 2032 and the Mediterranean fast-casual category still in early innings of national penetration, CAVA has significant runway for sustained 20%+ revenue growth. The company's commitment to 75th-95th percentile wages, Connected Kitchen AI technology, and mission-driven culture creates a differentiated employer brand in a tight labor market, with D/E of just 62% reflecting a conservative balance sheet [5].
The primary concern is extreme valuation: at 125x trailing earnings, 107x forward earnings, and a PEG ratio of 4.01, CAVA is priced for perfection with minimal margin of safety [5]. Net profit margin of 4.79% and ROE of 8.02% are thin for a stock commanding this premium -- any compression from rising food costs, wage inflation, or new market entry challenges could trigger severe multiple contraction. The stock has declined 32% from its 52-week high of $98.79 to $66.88, reflecting valuation normalization despite continued operational excellence. The aggressive expansion plan to 1,000 locations by 2032 assumes consistently successful new market entries, but CAVA has yet to prove it can maintain unit economics as it expands beyond core East Coast markets into less familiar geographies. Restaurant-level margin of 25.7% in Q2 was down 60bps YoY, suggesting early signs of margin pressure from wage and food cost inflation [1].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): CAVA's core business — operating physical restaurants, preparing fresh Mediterranean food, and delivering in-person hospitality — is structurally protected from AI disruption [2]. Restaurants require physical labor for food preparation, customer service, and facility management that AI cannot substitute. The fast-casual dining experience depends on human interaction, ingredient freshness, and physical ambiance beyond AI's reach. However, CAVA faces some indirect AI exposure: AI-powered ordering platforms and food delivery aggregators could reduce the differentiation of the in-store experience, and AI-driven competitive intelligence could accelerate rival expansion strategies. AI Opportunity (0/1): While CAVA has deployed AI operationally through camera vision for food prep monitoring, labor forecasting, and Connected Kitchen predictive scheduling, these are internal efficiency tools generating cost savings rather than AI products or services generating external revenue [2]. No quantifiable AI-specific revenue has been reported.
