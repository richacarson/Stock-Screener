Company Profile
Industrials ·Consulting Services ·NYSE ·United States
CBIZ, Inc. is the largest full-service professional services advisor of its kind in the United States, providing financial, benefits, and insurance services to organizations of all sizes. The company serves middle-market clients with tax, accounting, advisory, benefits administration, insurance brokerage, and consulting services. CBIZ completed its transformative acquisition of Marcum LLP in November 2024, creating a combined platform with approximately $2.8 billion in annualized revenue. In July 2026, Grant Thornton announced a definitive agreement to acquire CBIZ for approximately $5 billion in an all-cash transaction expected to close in Q4 2026. Headquartered in Cleveland, Ohio.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 3 /10
CBIZ demonstrates finite game characteristics as viewed through Sinek's lens. The company's strategic arc — growing through acquisitions, optimizing operations, and ultimately selling to Grant Thornton for $5B — is the textbook finite game: playing to win this round rather than playing to keep playing indefinitely. CEO Grisko's strategic priorities focus on scale, market share, and shareholder value rather than a cause that transcends any single transaction. The Marcum acquisition was bold, but the subsequent decision to sell to Grant Thornton within two years suggests the strategy was always about building enterprise value for an exit, not advancing a Just Cause over decades.

Investment Thesis
CBIZ offers a compelling near-term value proposition as an acquisition target: Grant Thornton's announced $5B all-cash acquisition provides a definitive price floor and clear path to value realization expected in Q4 2026 [3]. The company generated $2.77B in TTM revenue and $154.3M in profits, with H1 2026 GAAP EPS of $2.83 (up 9.7% YoY) and adjusted diluted EPS of $3.44 [1]. The forward P/E of 9.26 and PEG of 0.76 reflect an attractive valuation relative to growth, partially driven by deal arbitrage. The Marcum acquisition integration is largely complete, and the combined platform serves as the largest US middle-market professional services advisor, a position validated by Grant Thornton's strategic interest [2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 22.86% demonstrates a proven acquisition-driven growth engine.
The bear case centers on deal risk and fundamental weakness: the Grant Thornton acquisition requires CBIZ shareholder approval and regulatory clearance, and any deal failure would leave CBIZ with $1.99B in debt, 0.6% organic revenue growth, and 5.57% profit margins — a challenging standalone profile [1][3]. Activist investor Reference Equity has publicly criticized CBIZ's capital allocation, creating governance noise that could complicate the deal process [3]. The professional services sector faces structural AI disruption risk that would compress margins and reduce headcount needs over the medium term. H1 2026 performance was mixed: while GAAP EPS grew 9.7%, Q2 net income fell 55.6% sequentially as the seasonal tax season ended, and adjusted EBITDA declined 3.8% [1]. If the Grant Thornton deal fails, the stock would need to be re-evaluated as a standalone entity with meaningful challenges.

AI Risk Protection (4/9): CBIZ's core services — tax preparation, audit, benefits administration, insurance brokerage, and financial consulting — face material AI substitution risk. AI tools (TurboTax-style automation, AI-powered audit analytics, automated compliance checking) are already partially replacing routine professional services work. CBIZ is adapting by integrating AI into its own service delivery, but the outcome is uncertain. The benefits administration and insurance brokerage segments have somewhat more defensible moats due to regulatory complexity and relationship-driven selling, but the accounting and tax core faces real headwinds. Analysts have broadly flagged professional services as an AI disruption zone.
