Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·United States
The Chemours Company is a global specialty chemicals company spun off from DuPont in 2015, operating across three segments: Titanium Technologies (TiO2 pigments), Thermal & Specialized Solutions (Opteon refrigerants, liquid cooling for data centers), and Advanced Performance Materials (fluoropolymers, battery binder technology). The company generated $5.82 billion in trailing twelve-month revenue and is led by CEO Denise Dignam, a 26-year DuPont/Chemours veteran appointed in 2024.

Investment Thesis
Chemours offers a deep value turnaround opportunity trading at 11.6x forward earnings, with Q1 2026 EPS of $0.05 beating the -$0.04 estimate and full-year 2026 EPS forecast of $1.36 representing a swing from -$2.80 in FY2025 [1]. The Thermal & Specialized Solutions segment delivered 22% YoY sales growth driven by Opteon refrigerant demand from the global HFC phasedown mandate and emerging AI data center liquid cooling solutions [1][2]. The appointment of Google's VP of Data Centers to the board validates the AI cooling opportunity. FY2026 guidance of 3-5% revenue growth and $800-900M adjusted EBITDA suggests a meaningful recovery, and the June 2026 EPA PFAS settlement ($22.5M penalty + $90M mitigation) represents progress in resolving legacy environmental liabilities [5].
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. Chemours carries $4.39B in debt against approximately $216M equity (D/E of 2034%), creating existential refinancing risk if credit markets tighten [6]. The PFAS litigation liability remains massive and partially unquantified — the $1.185B water systems settlement, $875M New Jersey settlement, and June 2026 EPA settlement are milestones but not endpoints, with ongoing federal and state claims and UN human rights experts condemning the company's conduct [4][5]. The 2024 CEO/CFO suspension for financial reporting irregularities destroyed governance credibility and may have ongoing legal consequences [3]. TTM profit margin of -6.65%, ROE of -103%, and 65% dividend cut paint the financial profile of a deeply distressed company [6]. TiO2 faces secular pressure from Chinese oversupply, and even the Opteon growth story depends on regulatory enforcement that could change under future administrations. At $20.14 with a $3.27B market cap, the stock prices in some turnaround but the accumulation of financial, legal, and governance risks makes this speculative.
