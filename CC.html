Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·United States
The Chemours Company is a global specialty chemicals company spun off from DuPont in 2015. Its segments are Titanium Technologies (Ti-Pure TiO2 pigment), Thermal & Specialized Solutions (Opteon low-GWP refrigerants and a Liquid Cooling Venture for AI data centers), Advanced Performance Materials (fluoropolymers), and Performance Solutions. Led by CEO Denise Dignam. Q2 2026 net sales were ~$1.59B (roughly flat YoY, missing the $1.67B estimate), with a GAAP net loss of $274M (-$1.81/share), adjusted EPS of $0.42, and adjusted EBITDA of $247M. FY2026 revenue guidance is $5.98-6.10B. Net leverage is 4.4x, targeted at ~3.8x by year-end. In June 2026 Chemours agreed to a $450M federal PFAS settlement covering Washington Works, Fayetteville Works and Chambers Works, alongside a separate North Carolina settlement. Stockholders' equity turned negative (-$49M) at June 30, 2026.

Investment Thesis
The bull case is a deep-value turnaround. At $13.53 the stock trades at 7.3x forward EPS of ~$1.86, with a 2.6% yield [5]. Q2 2026 free cash flow rose 128% to $114M, net leverage fell to 4.4x with a ~3.8x year-end target, and a global TiO2 price increase lifted segment pricing ~5% year to date [1]. The June 2026 federal ($450M) and North Carolina settlements and the July 2026 Fayetteville GenX settlement remove major litigation overhangs [3]. Performance Solutions grew 8% on data-center and semiconductor demand, and the Opteon two-phase immersion cooling fluid begins production in 2026 [1][2].
The bear case is the balance sheet and liabilities. Stockholders' equity is now negative (-$49M) against $4.12B of debt, ROE is -319%, and the company lost $386M in FY2025 and $274M in Q2 2026 alone [4][5]. The dividend was cut from $1.00 a year to a $0.35 rate, the 5-year dividend CAGR is -12.5%, and individual resident PFAS claims remain unresolved [3]. North Carolina officials are urging the court to reject the federal deal. Opteon sales fell 10% in Q2 after AIM Act channel fill, and TiO2 remains a cyclical, oversupplied commodity [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Deleveraging toward ~3.8x net leverage by year-end 2026 after EUR230M of term-loan paydown and 128% higher Q2 free cash flow [1]
  2. Court approval of the $450M federal PFAS settlement (payable over years, with a $22.5M civil penalty in three installments), capping a major liability [3]
  3. Commercial production of the Opteon two-phase immersion cooling fluid with Navin Fluorine in 2026 and the DataVolt AI data-center partnership [2]
Key Risks
  1. Negative stockholders' equity (-$49M) and $4.12B of total debt leave little cushion if TiO2 or refrigerant markets weaken further [4]
  2. Unresolved individual PFAS claims and state opposition to the federal settlement could create liabilities beyond current reserves [3]
  3. Further dividend reduction: the payout is not covered by trailing GAAP earnings (EPS -$2.02) [5]
