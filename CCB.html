Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NASDAQ ·United States
Coastal Financial Corporation operates as the bank holding company for Coastal Community Bank, providing traditional community banking services and a banking-as-a-service (CCBX) platform that enables fintech partners including T-Mobile, Robinhood, Greenwood, Aven, and LendingPoint to offer FDIC-insured deposit, lending, and card products. Headquartered in Everett, Washington, with $5.66B in total assets. Q2 2026 revenue of $178.1M (up 49% YoY) was overshadowed by a $68.8M credit expense from a single CCBX partner, triggering securities fraud investigations.

Investment Thesis
Coastal Financial's CCBX platform has driven a remarkable revenue transformation — 5-year CAGR of 37.5% with Q2 2026 revenue of $178.1M (up 49.1% YoY) and record net interest income of $89.4M (up 16.4% YoY), powering 20+ active fintech partners including T-Mobile and Robinhood [1][2]. At forward P/E of 6.88 with a conservatively managed balance sheet (D/E ~11.4%, $1.01B cash on hand), the stock prices in excessive credit risk from the isolated Q2 partner event. CEO Sprink's $444,500 insider purchase at $44.45 signals management conviction that the $68.8M credit expense was truly a one-off rather than systemic, and consensus forward EPS projects earnings normalization [5].
The Q2 2026 loss exposed critical concentration risk: a single CCBX partner relationship generated a $68.8M credit expense ($22.8M provision plus $46.0M valuation adjustment on credit enhancement assets), wiping out an entire quarter of profitability and triggering securities fraud investigations from BFA Law, Holzer & Holzer, and Hagens Berman [1][3]. The stock has declined 67% from its January 2026 high of $120.05 to $39.94. Nonperforming loans rose to 1.50%+ for CCBX operations (exceeding the 0.50% preferred threshold for community bank loans) with net charge-offs annualizing at 5.18% [1][2]. The BaaS model inherently creates exposure to fintech partners' credit quality, and Coastal's risk management framework proved inadequate at preventing large single-counterparty losses. The CFO transition during crisis period raises governance questions, and only 35% of Glassdoor reviewers have a positive business outlook [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Earnings normalization in H2 2026 as the isolated $68.8M credit expense rolls off, with forward P/E of 6.88 implying significant upside if credit quality stabilizes and quarterly earnings return to the $12M+ Q1 2026 run rate [1]
  2. CCBX platform revenue momentum continuing at 40%+ YoY as existing partners scale and new fintech relationships onboard, with net interest income at a record $89.4M in Q2 [1]
  3. CEO Sprink's insider purchase of $444,500 at $44.45 per share, plus strengthened executive team (new CCBX President, CPO, CCrO), signals management conviction in the recovery thesis [3][5]
Key Risks
  1. Securities fraud investigations by multiple law firms could result in costly litigation, management distraction, and damages if material misrepresentation is proven regarding BaaS partner risk disclosures — the stock trades 67% below its January high reflecting this overhang [3]
  2. BaaS concentration risk remains structurally unresolved — the Q2 loss demonstrated that a single partner failure can eliminate quarterly profitability, and the model's counterparty exposure is difficult to diversify quickly [1]
  3. Net charge-offs annualizing at 5.18% and nonperforming CCBX loans at 1.50%+ suggest credit quality deterioration that could extend beyond the single partner, particularly if fintech lending standards prove systemically weak across the CCBX portfolio [1][2]
