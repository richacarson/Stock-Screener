Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NASDAQ ·United States
Coastal Financial Corporation operates as the bank holding company for Coastal Community Bank, providing traditional community banking services and a banking-as-a-service (CCBX) platform that enables fintech partners to offer FDIC-insured deposit, lending, and card products. Headquartered in Everett, Washington, with $5.5 billion in total assets and 15.3 million shares outstanding.

Investment Thesis
Coastal Financial's CCBX banking-as-a-service platform has driven a remarkable revenue transformation — from $97.6M in FY2021 to $349M in FY2025 and $178M in Q2 2026 alone (49% YoY growth) — positioning the company as a significant fintech infrastructure provider with a conservative balance sheet (D/E of 11.4%, $1.01B cash on hand) [1][2][4]. Forward P/E of 9.41 reflects deep value pricing as analysts project earnings normalization with consensus EPS of $4.40 for next year, implying the market is pricing in too much credit risk from the isolated Q2 partner event. With $5.5B in assets and a capital-light platform model, the BaaS franchise has significant operating leverage as new partners scale on existing infrastructure.
The Q2 2026 loss exposed critical concentration risk: a single CCBX partner relationship generated a $68.8M credit expense ($22.8M provision plus $46.0M valuation adjustment on credit enhancement assets), wiping out an entire quarter of profitability and triggering securities fraud investigations from law firms Holzer & Holzer and Kirby McInerney [1][3]. The stock has declined 52% from its January 2026 all-time high of $118.97. Nonperforming loans rose to 1.79% of total loans with net charge-offs annualizing at 5.18% — both elevated for a bank of this size [1][2]. The BaaS model inherently creates exposure to fintech partners' credit quality, and Coastal's risk management framework has proven inadequate at preventing large single-counterparty losses. Additionally, the CFO transition announced in July 2026 during a crisis period raises governance questions about leadership stability [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Earnings normalization in H2 2026 as the isolated $68.8M credit expense rolls off, with forward P/E of 9.41 implying significant upside if credit quality stabilizes
  2. CCBX platform revenue growth momentum continuing at 40%+ YoY as existing partners scale and new fintech relationships are onboarded
  3. CEO insider buying of $444,500 at $44.45 per share signals management conviction that the Q2 loss was truly isolated and not systemic
Key Risks
  1. Securities fraud investigation by multiple law firms could result in costly litigation, management distraction, and potential damages if material misrepresentation is proven regarding BaaS partner risk disclosures
  2. BaaS concentration risk remains unresolved — the Q2 2026 loss demonstrated that a single partner failure can eliminate quarterly profitability, and the model's counterparty exposure is structurally difficult to diversify quickly
  3. Net charge-offs annualizing at 5.18% and nonperforming loans at 1.79% suggest credit quality deterioration that could extend beyond the single isolated partner, particularly if fintech lending standards prove systemically weak across the CCBX portfolio

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Coastal's core banking infrastructure — the FDIC-insured banking charter, regulatory compliance framework, and partner settlement systems — creates structural moats that AI cannot substitute. Banking-as-a-service requires a chartered bank's regulatory status, capital reserves, and compliance oversight, none of which AI can replicate [1][2]. However, the BaaS model faces indirect AI risk: AI-native fintechs could reduce their dependency on bank sponsors by pursuing their own charters, and AI-powered credit decisioning could commoditize the underwriting capabilities Coastal provides to partners. The Q2 2026 credit loss from a single partner highlights concentration risk inherent in the BaaS model [1]. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of AI-generated revenue.
