Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Non-Alcoholic ·NASDAQ ·United Kingdom
Coca-Cola Europacific Partners is the world's largest Coca-Cola bottler by revenue, producing, distributing, and selling Coca-Cola branded and other non-alcoholic beverages across Western Europe, Australia, the Pacific, and Indonesia. Serves approximately 600 million consumers across 31 countries with brands including Coca-Cola, Fanta, Sprite, Monster Energy, and Costa Coffee. Formed through the 2021 merger with Coca-Cola Amatil. H1 2026 delivered revenue of EUR 10.7B (+6.1% FX-neutral) with diluted EPS up 10.6% to EUR 2.20 and comparable operating profit of EUR 1.48B (+8.1% FX-neutral).

Investment Thesis
Coca-Cola Europacific Partners is the world's largest Coca-Cola bottler, offering defensive consumer staples exposure with improving profitability. H1 2026 delivered EUR 10.7B revenue (+6.1% FX-neutral) with comparable operating profit up 8.1% FX-neutral and diluted EPS rising 10.6% to EUR 2.20 [1]. FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at 3-4% revenue growth, ~7% operating profit growth, comparable FCF of at least EUR 1.7B, and a EUR 1B share buyback. ROE of 23.5% demonstrates efficient capital allocation, and exclusive bottling agreements across 31 countries serving 600M consumers create a durable competitive moat [4]. MSCI AA ESG rating and CDP A List for nine consecutive years attract ESG-focused capital [3]. At 18.4x forward earnings with a ~2.3% dividend yield growing at 18% CAGR over five years, CCEP offers an attractive total return profile for a defensive compounder.
The bear case centers on low organic growth and structural margin constraints. FY2026 revenue guidance of 3-4% falls below the company's own midterm 7% target, suggesting consumer volume headwinds in mature European markets from health-conscious consumption trends, sugar taxes, and GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption potentially reducing caloric beverage demand [1]. D/E of 139% reflects Amatil acquisition leverage constraining financial flexibility and increasing interest expense sensitivity. Profit margin of 9.35% is structurally limited by the bottling model's high raw material costs (sugar, PET plastic, aluminum) and energy-intensive manufacturing [4]. The PEG ratio of 2.79 suggests the stock may be fully valued relative to its modest growth trajectory. Currency risk is significant with operations across 31 countries and EUR-reported earnings but USD-priced shares. KnowTheChain scored CCEP 10/100 for forced labor supply chain practices, highlighting ethical supply chain risks despite strong headline ESG ratings [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Operating profit growth of ~7% outpacing 3-4% revenue growth in FY2026, demonstrating continued margin expansion from Amatil integration synergies and AI-driven operational efficiency improvements [1]
  2. EUR 1B share buyback program reducing share count and boosting per-share metrics on top of the ~2.3% dividend yield with 18% five-year dividend growth CAGR, providing compelling total shareholder return [1]
  3. Asia-Pacific volume growth (5.4% FX-neutral in H1 2026) in Indonesia and Australia providing a higher-growth offset to mature European markets as CCEP captures rising per capita Coca-Cola consumption in emerging economies [1]
Key Risks
  1. Secular health-conscious consumer trends, sugar taxes expanding across European markets, and GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption potentially reducing caloric beverage demand, with FY2026 revenue guidance of only 3-4% already below the midterm 7% target [1]
  2. Raw material and energy cost inflation — sugar, PET plastic, aluminum, and energy prices directly compress the structurally thin 9.35% profit margin with limited pass-through ability to large retailers [4]
  3. Currency translation risk across 31 countries with EUR-reported earnings and USD-priced stock creating earnings volatility, compounded by D/E of 139% increasing sensitivity to rising interest rates [4]

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Beverage bottling and distribution is a physical-world manufacturing and logistics operation that AI structurally cannot displace — producing, bottling, warehousing, and delivering billions of servings across 31 countries requires massive physical infrastructure including bottling plants, cold-chain logistics, and distribution fleets [1]. Exclusive bottling agreements with The Coca-Cola Company create contractual moats AI cannot circumvent. Consumer beverage demand is non-discretionary and AI-immune. Food safety regulatory requirements across 31 jurisdictions create additional barriers. The only theoretical AI risk is potential for AI-driven alternative distribution models, but physical infrastructure requirements make this implausible at CCEP's scale.
