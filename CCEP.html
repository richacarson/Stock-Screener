Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Non-Alcoholic ·NASDAQ ·United Kingdom
Coca-Cola Europacific Partners is the world's largest Coca-Cola bottler by revenue, producing, distributing, and selling Coca-Cola branded and other non-alcoholic beverages across Western Europe, Australia, the Pacific, and Indonesia. Serves approximately 600 million consumers across 31 countries with brands including Coca-Cola, Fanta, Sprite, Monster Energy, and Costa Coffee. Formed through the 2021 merger with Coca-Cola Amatil. Headquartered in Uxbridge, United Kingdom.

Investment Thesis
Coca-Cola Europacific Partners is the world's largest Coca-Cola bottler, offering defensive consumer staples exposure with improving profitability and growing shareholder returns. Q1 2026 delivered EUR 5.13B revenue (up 6.7% reported, 9.4% FX-neutral) with volume growth of 1.6%, driven by pricing, mix, and market share gains [1]. FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at 3-4% revenue growth, ~7% operating profit growth, comparable free cash flow of at least EUR 1.7B, and a EUR 1B share buyback. ROE of 22.89% demonstrates efficient capital allocation, and the exclusive bottling agreements across 31 countries serving 600 million consumers create a durable competitive moat with predictable cash flows. The MSCI AA ESG rating and CDP A List status for nine consecutive years attract ESG-focused capital (25%+ of float) [5]. At a forward P/E of approximately 19.5 with a 2.41% dividend yield growing at 18.4% CAGR over five years, CCEP offers an attractive total return profile for a defensive compounder.
The bear case centers on low organic growth and structural margin constraints. FY2026 revenue guidance of 3-4% falls below the company's own midterm 7% target, suggesting consumer volume headwinds in mature European markets from health-conscious consumption trends, sugar taxes, and GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption potentially reducing caloric beverage demand [1]. D/E of 141% reflects Amatil acquisition leverage that constrains financial flexibility and increases interest expense sensitivity. Profit margin of 9.29% is structurally limited by the bottling model's high raw material costs (sugar, PET plastic, aluminum) and energy-intensive manufacturing — input cost inflation directly compresses margins. The PEG ratio of 2.79 suggests the stock may be overvalued relative to its modest growth trajectory. Currency risk is significant with operations across 31 countries and EUR-reported earnings but USD-priced shares, with BEUC greenwashing complaints about recyclability claims creating additional ESG-related reputational risk [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Operating profit growth of ~7% outpacing 3-4% revenue growth in FY2026, demonstrating continued margin expansion from Amatil integration synergies and AI-driven operational efficiency gains [1]
  2. EUR 1B share buyback program reducing share count and boosting per-share metrics on top of the 2.41% dividend yield with 18.4% five-year dividend growth CAGR [1]
  3. Asia-Pacific volume growth in Indonesia and Australia providing a higher-growth offset to mature European markets as CCEP captures rising per capita Coca-Cola consumption in emerging economies
Key Risks
  1. Secular health-conscious consumer trends, sugar taxes, and GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption potentially reducing caloric beverage demand in mature European markets, with 2026 revenue guidance of only 3-4% already below the midterm 7% target
  2. Raw material and energy cost inflation — sugar, PET plastic, aluminum, and energy prices directly compress the structurally thin 9.29% profit margin with limited pass-through ability to large retailers
  3. Currency translation risk across 31 countries with EUR-reported earnings and USD-priced stock creating earnings volatility, compounded by D/E of 141% increasing sensitivity to rising interest rates

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Beverage bottling and distribution is a physical-world manufacturing and logistics operation that AI structurally cannot displace — producing, bottling, warehousing, and delivering billions of servings across 31 countries requires massive physical infrastructure including bottling plants, cold-chain logistics, and distribution fleets [1]. Exclusive bottling agreements with The Coca-Cola Company create contractual moats that AI cannot circumvent. Consumer beverage demand is non-discretionary and AI-immune. Increasing urbanization and warm weather trends may actually increase beverage consumption. The only AI risk is potential for AI-driven DTC or alternative distribution, but the physical infrastructure requirements make this implausible at scale. AI Opportunity (0/1): CCEP deploys AI internally for procurement analytics, demand forecasting, and talent management, generating operational efficiency gains, but no quantifiable AI-driven revenue products have been reported [3].
