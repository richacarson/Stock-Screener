Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Specialty ·NYSE ·United States
Crown Castle is a U.S. communications infrastructure REIT that leases space on its portfolio of cell towers to wireless carriers. On May 1, 2026 it completed the $8.5B sale of its Fiber Solutions and Small Cell businesses, becoming a pure-play U.S. tower company, and used the proceeds to repay more than $7B of debt and repurchase $1B of stock [2][3]. Full-year 2026 AFFO guidance is $1.98B, or $4.60 per share [1]. President and CEO Chris Hillabrant leads the company after the board terminated former CEO Steven Moskowitz in March 2025 [3][4]. The quarterly dividend was reset to $1.0625 in 2025, a 32% per-share cut [3][6].

Investment Thesis
The bull case for Crown Castle is a simpler, de-levered tower REIT. The fiber and small cell sale closed May 1, 2026 for $8.5B, funding more than $7B of debt repayment (about $160M of annual interest savings) and a $1B buyback [2]. Management raised 2026 AFFO guidance to $1.98B, or $4.60 per share, after Q3 [1], so the $4.25 annualized dividend is covered at ~92% of AFFO. The current yield is 4.8% [8]. Towers are physical infrastructure that AI cannot displace, and rising mobile data traffic supports long-term leasing demand [4]. Management says it expects to raise the dividend as cash flows grow [3]. Faith alignment is strong (Inspire score +53).
The bear case is shrinking revenue and a broken dividend record. Revenue fell 7.2% YoY, and management expects 2026 site rental revenue to decline about $200M, driven by carrier churn including the terminated DISH Wireless contract [2][5][8]. The dividend was cut 32% per share to $1.0625 per quarter in 2025, ending CCI's growth record, and the GAAP payout ratio remains 173% [3][8][9]. At 30.3x forward earnings, the stock is not cheap for a business with negative revenue growth [8]. Leadership turnover (a CEO terminated within a year) and activist-driven strategy raise execution risk as the company moves to a pure-play tower model [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Deleveraging after the fiber sale: repaying more than $7B of debt cuts annual interest expense by about $160M, supporting AFFO per share growth from the raised $4.60 2026 midpoint [1][2]
  2. Dividend growth resumption: management states it expects to increase the dividend per share as cash flows grow, from the $4.25 annualized base set after the 2025 reset [3][9]
  3. 5G densification and AI-driven traffic: carrier network investment for rising data traffic could lift tower amendment and colocation activity after churn from the DISH contract termination ends [4][5]
Key Risks
  1. Carrier churn and consolidation: the terminated DISH Wireless contract and an expected ~$200M decline in 2026 site rental revenue show how concentrated CCI's tenant base is among a handful of carriers [2][5]
  2. Interest-rate sensitivity: at 30.3x forward earnings and a 4.8% yield, CCI's valuation is exposed to higher-for-longer rates despite lower post-sale debt [8]
  3. Leadership and strategy instability: the board terminated CEO Moskowitz in March 2025 amid activist pressure, and BMO flagged added uncertainty; further turnover could disrupt the pure-play tower transition [4]
