Company Profile
Energy ·Uranium ·NYSE ·Canada
Cameco is one of the world's largest uranium producers, providing uranium fuel for nuclear power reactors globally. The company operates through three segments — Uranium, Fuel Services, and Westinghouse Electric (40% stake) — with key mining assets at McArthur River/Key Lake and Cigar Lake in Saskatchewan. Listed on both NYSE and TSX, Cameco generated TTM revenue of approximately $2.6B with a 10.2% profit margin, benefiting from the global nuclear energy renaissance driven by AI data center power demand and net-zero commitments.

Investment Thesis
Cameco at $80.51 is the premier pure-play on the nuclear energy renaissance, uniquely positioned as the only vertically integrated uranium miner and fuel fabricator through its Westinghouse stake. AI data center power requirements are driving nuclear as the baseload solution of choice, with Microsoft, Google, and Amazon all signing nuclear PPAs. Conservative D/E of 17% and 29-year dividend history provide downside protection while uranium contract prices of $60-80/lb drive earnings growth [1][2].
The bear case centers on valuation and execution risk. At 43x forward earnings, Cameco prices in a sustained nuclear renaissance requiring significant new reactor builds to materialize — regulatory delays or a nuclear safety event could derail the thesis. Revenue declined 7.2% YoY due to delivery timing, highlighting lumpy contract fulfillment. ROE of 5.1% suggests the Westinghouse acquisition hasn't yet delivered on earnings potential. The uranium spot market remains volatile and subject to geopolitical disruption from Kazakhstan production decisions [1][2].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center nuclear demand accelerating — Microsoft TMI restart, Amazon Talen PPA, and Google Kairos SMR deal validate nuclear as AI infrastructure, driving long-term uranium contract pricing above $60/lb [1]
  2. Westinghouse revenue synergies materializing — AP1000 new builds in Poland and elsewhere plus fuel assembly demand across half the world's reactor fleet create recurring revenue beyond commodity pricing [1]
  3. McArthur River production ramp to full 18M lb/year capacity in a supply-constrained market where secondary supply sources are depleting [1]
Key Risks
  1. Premium valuation at 43x forward earnings prices in a nuclear renaissance that requires multi-year new reactor build commitments — regulatory delays or nuclear safety incident could deflate the thesis [1][2]
  2. Westinghouse integration risk — $7.9B joint venture hasn't yet demonstrated ROE improvement, adding operational complexity to a mining company [1]
  3. Revenue delivery timing creates quarterly volatility — 7.2% YoY decline shows how contract schedules obscure underlying demand trends [1][2]
