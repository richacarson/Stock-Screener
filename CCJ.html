Company Profile
Energy ·Uranium ·NYSE ·Canada
Cameco Corporation is one of the world's largest uranium producers and a leading provider of nuclear fuel cycle services, operating through Uranium, Fuel Services, and Westinghouse segments. The company mines, mills, refines, converts, and fabricates uranium for nuclear power generation, and through its 49% stake in Westinghouse Electric Company (acquired 2023), provides nuclear reactor technology, maintenance, and engineering services globally. With TTM revenue of approximately $3.5B and a 10.2% profit margin, Cameco is headquartered in Saskatoon, Canada. Q1 2026 adjusted net earnings more than doubled YoY to C$203M ($0.47/share), driven by 15% uranium revenue growth to C$712M on higher volumes and prices [1].

Courage to Lead

Courage to Lead (2/2): Cameco demonstrated extraordinary courage by placing McArthur River — one of the world's premier uranium mines — on care-and-maintenance from 2018 to 2022, forgoing billions in potential revenue rather than selling uranium below prices that would sustain the long-term health of the industry [1][3]. This decision cost Cameco significant near-term earnings during a period when competitors continued producing. The company then doubled down with the $4.5B Westinghouse acquisition in 2023, betting heavily on the nuclear renaissance before the current wave of tech-company nuclear contracts validated the thesis. Investment Thesis
Cameco at $80.51 with a $35B market cap is uniquely positioned at the intersection of the nuclear renaissance and AI-driven electricity demand. The company is the world's second-largest uranium producer with long-term contracts covering 28M+ lbs U3O8 annually, and its 49% Westinghouse stake provides exposure to nuclear reactor technology serving approximately half the world's operating fleet [1][2]. Q1 2026 adjusted net earnings more than doubled to C$203M ($0.47/share), with uranium revenue up 15% to C$712M. The structural demand thesis is compelling: AI data centers require enormous baseload electricity, tech companies are signing nuclear power purchase agreements, and 30+ countries pledged to triple nuclear capacity by 2050 at COP28. With a conservative D/E of 17%, $1.1B in cash, and 2026 production guidance of 19.5-21.5M lbs, Cameco offers leveraged exposure to the secular uranium bull market [1].
The bear case centers on valuation, earnings volatility, and execution risk. At 43x forward earnings and 136x trailing earnings, Cameco prices in substantial uranium price appreciation and Westinghouse earnings growth that have not yet materialized [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue fell 7% YoY with EPS declining 92% due to lower planned deliveries and reduced Westinghouse equity earnings, demonstrating the lumpiness of uranium sales and the early-stage nature of Westinghouse integration. ROE of just 5.1% on the current asset base is well below industrial targets, and profit margin of 10.2% reflects the capital intensity of mining operations. The uranium market faces potential supply increases from Kazakhstan's Kazatomprom and new African projects, and any delay in nuclear new-build timelines would undercut the long-duration demand thesis. The Westinghouse leadership transition (CEO resignation in early 2025) adds integration uncertainty, and Cameco's stock price at $80.51 sits near its 52-week low of $77.70, suggesting the market is already repricing the growth outlook [2][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-driven nuclear power demand — tech companies (Microsoft, Google, Amazon) are signing multi-billion-dollar nuclear power purchase agreements for data center baseload electricity, directly driving uranium demand and long-term contracting at prices favorable to Cameco's production portfolio [1]

  2. Earnings lumpiness and Q2 2026 weakness — uranium deliveries are concentrated in specific quarters under long-term contracts, creating volatile quarterly results that can mislead on underlying trends, as Q2 2026's 92% EPS decline demonstrated [2]
  3. Westinghouse integration and leadership risk — the subsidiary's CEO resigned in early 2025, and the Westinghouse equity earnings already declined in H1 2026, raising questions about whether the $4.5B investment will generate the returns needed to justify Cameco's premium valuation [2][3]
