Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold & Silver Mining ·NYSE ·United States
Coeur Mining is a diversified precious metals producer operating gold, silver, and copper mines across North America. Following its transformative acquisition of New Gold (Rainy River, New Afton), Coeur has approximately doubled its production and revenue base, reporting record Q2 2026 revenue of $1.09B (+126% YoY) and Q1 2026 revenue of $856M (+138% YoY). The company operates mines at Rochester (Nevada), Kensington (Alaska), Wharf (South Dakota), and the newly integrated Rainy River and New Afton operations in Canada. At forecast gold prices of $4,000/oz, Coeur expects 2026 EBITDA of approximately $2.3B and free cash flow of $1.5B. CEO Mitchell Krebs has led the company since 2011.

Investment Thesis
Coeur Mining at 9.37x forward earnings offers compelling value exposure to elevated precious metals prices, with the transformative New Gold acquisition creating a diversified production platform generating record revenue of $1.09B in Q2 2026 (+126% YoY) and $856M in Q1 2026 (+138% YoY) [1][2]. At forecast gold of $4,000/oz, silver of $60/oz, and copper of $6/lb, management guides to $2.3B EBITDA and $1.5B free cash flow for 2026 — a free cash flow yield exceeding 10% on the current market cap. The balance sheet is conservatively structured with D/E of just 8.8%, profit margins of 26.82%, and the company has initiated a $750M buyback program and its first dividend in 30 years, signaling confidence in sustained cash generation [3]. CEO Mitchell Krebs has executed a multi-year transformation from a subscale silver miner into a top-10 North American precious metals producer.
The bear case centers on commodity price dependency, acquisition integration risk, and environmental liabilities. Coeur's revenue and cash flow projections are highly sensitive to precious metals prices — the $2.3B EBITDA guidance assumes gold at $4,000/oz, and a meaningful correction to 2024 levels (~$2,300/oz) would compress EBITDA by 50%+ and eliminate the free cash flow supporting buybacks and the inaugural dividend [2]. The massive revenue growth (96% YoY in 2025, 126% Q2 2026) is entirely acquisition-driven, not organic, raising execution risk across geographically dispersed mine sites. The EPA settlement over Kensington mine pollution (200+ wastewater violations, unauthorized acid rock drainage) exposes ongoing environmental compliance risks inherent to mining [5]. ROE of 12.85% remains below the 15% target, suggesting acquired assets have not yet achieved full capital efficiency. The Inspire Impact Score of 57 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors, but mining's inherent cyclicality and commodity price dependency make this a precious metals bet rather than a structural growth story.
