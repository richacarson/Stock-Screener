Cardio Diagnostics Holdings develops and commercializes AI-driven epigenetic-genetic clinical tests for cardiovascular disease, including Epi+Gen CHD (3-year CHD risk assessment), PrecisionCHD (coronary heart disease detection), and HeartRisk (SaaS cardiovascular risk intelligence platform). The company completed a 1-for-30 reverse stock split in May 2026 and secured its first commercial insurance coverage with AHP. Pre-revenue with approximately $14,825 in TTM revenue, Q2 2026 revenue of $5.36K, and a $5.89M market cap. Headquartered in Chicago, Illinois.

Investment Thesis
Cardio Diagnostics offers a speculative option on AI-driven cardiovascular diagnostics with a potentially transformative technology platform. The Integrated Genetic-Epigenetic Engine has secured CMS reimbursement at $854 per test, won industry recognition (Nature/Merck, Fast Company, Biotech Breakthrough), secured a Japanese patent, and obtained its first commercial insurance coverage with AHP [3][4]. The go-to-market strategy shift toward non-reimbursement channels (telemedicine, concierge practices, employers, benefit brokers) could accelerate commercialization outside the slow-moving insurance reimbursement pathway. The cardiovascular diagnostics market opportunity is enormous — heart disease is the #1 global killer — and epigenetic testing represents a paradigm shift from reactive to predictive medicine. The balance sheet is clean with D/E of just 10% and $5.11M cash against $695K debt [2].
The bear case is overwhelming. After 9 years, Cardio Diagnostics has generated only $14,825 in TTM revenue — effectively zero commercial traction [1]. Q2 2026 revenue was just $5.36K with a $1.5M net loss and ($0.51) diluted EPS. The $5.89M market cap, 17 employees, and $2.00 stock price (down 87.88% over 52 weeks) make this a micro-cap with significant going-concern risk [2]. Cash declined to $5.11M from $7.08M, and at ~$6-7M annual cash burn, runway is approximately 9-10 months without dilutive capital raises. The 1-for-30 reverse stock split in May 2026 cratered daily trading volume to 75,750 shares ($151K daily dollar volume), failing the IOWN liquidity floor by a wide margin and making position entry/exit nearly impossible for any meaningful investor [2]. Competition from Abbott, Roche, Siemens, and tech giants deploying AI diagnostics with vastly greater resources creates existential risk for a company that cannot demonstrate product-market fit after nearly a decade.
Key Catalysts
  1. First commercial insurance coverage with AHP and CMS reimbursement at $854/test could serve as proof points for additional payer adoption, potentially unlocking revenue scaling from the current negligible base [4]
  2. Go-to-market strategy shift to non-reimbursement channels (telemedicine, concierge/functional practices, employers, unions, benefit brokers) could bypass slow insurance adoption and generate near-term revenue
  3. International expansion leveraging the Japanese patent and partnerships in markets with high cardiovascular disease burden and underdeveloped diagnostic infrastructure
Key Risks
  1. Going-concern risk — cash declined to $5.11M from $7.08M against ~$6-7M annual burn rate, providing approximately 9-10 months runway; dilutive capital raises are near-certain and the 1-for-30 reverse split signals NASDAQ listing vulnerability [1][2]
  2. Zero commercial traction after 9 years — $14,825 TTM revenue ($5.36K in Q2 2026) suggests fundamental product-market fit challenges that awards, patents, and CMS reimbursement have not resolved [1]
  3. Post-reverse-split illiquidity — daily dollar volume of $151K fails the IOWN liquidity floor ($1M minimum) by 85% and makes position entry/exit nearly impossible for any institutional or meaningful retail investor [2]
