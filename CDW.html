Investment Thesis
CDW is a recovering IT cycle play accelerating into AI-driven growth, posting record Q2 2026 revenue of $6.57B (+10% YoY) with TTM revenue of $23.5B (+7.4%) driven by enterprise AI infrastructure demand and international momentum (23% growth) [1]. At 12.6x forward earnings with 44% ROE and strong free cash flow generation, CDW is attractively valued for a company positioned at the center of the multi-year enterprise AI hardware buildout. The 42-year evolution from mail-order computers to AI advisory and managed services, combined with CEO Leahy's early AI investment, an 11-year consecutive dividend growth streak, and MSCI ESG A rating with ALIGNED Inspire Impact score (43), make CDW a compelling franchise adapting to the next technology cycle [2][3].
The bear case centers on leverage, thin margins, and secular disintermediation risk. D/E of 263% with significant total debt (LBO legacy) limits balance sheet flexibility and creates interest rate sensitivity [4]. Profit margin of 4.6% leaves minimal buffer for revenue shortfalls, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of ~4.5% (including a sharp 2023-2024 contraction from $23.75B to $21.0B) shows the business is cyclically exposed to IT spending fluctuations. CDW's reseller model faces structural risk as AI procurement tools automate product selection, cloud migration reduces on-premise hardware demand, and OEMs increasingly sell direct — the company's 10-K explicitly acknowledges this disintermediation threat [2]. Employee sentiment is declining with concerns about layoffs, outsourcing, and organizational uncertainty from workforce restructuring. PEG remains elevated relative to the modest growth profile, suggesting limited margin of safety if the AI infrastructure spending cycle decelerates.
Key Catalysts
  1. Enterprise AI infrastructure spending cycle — Q2 2026 record revenue up 10% driven by GPU server, networking, and AI advisory demand, with CDW positioned as the largest pure-play IT solutions provider capturing the multi-year AI hardware buildout [1]
  2. International expansion momentum — 23% growth in Q2 2026 with record results in Canada and continued U.K. momentum demonstrates CDW's geographic diversification strategy is delivering incremental revenue [1]
  3. Services mix shift — evolution from commodity hardware toward higher-margin AI advisory, managed services, and solutions integration driving gross profit improvement, with Q2 gross profit up 6.3% to $1.32B [1]
Key Risks
  1. AI-driven disintermediation — procurement AI tools, vendor-direct sales, and cloud-native purchasing platforms could structurally reduce enterprise demand for IT resellers, threatening CDW's core intermediary business model over the medium term [2]
  2. High leverage vulnerability — D/E of 263% (LBO legacy) creates interest rate risk and limits strategic flexibility, with thin 4.6% profit margin leaving no buffer for revenue shortfalls during IT spending downturns [4]
  3. Cyclical exposure — revenue contracted from $23.75B (2022) to $21.0B (2024) during the IT downturn, demonstrating vulnerability to enterprise budget cycles that could reverse the current AI-driven recovery if macro conditions deteriorate [1]
