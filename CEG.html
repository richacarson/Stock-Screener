Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Independent Power Producers ·NASDAQ ·United States
Constellation Energy is the largest producer of carbon-free energy and the nation's largest electricity producer, operating approximately 60 GW of generating capacity following the $16.4B Calpine acquisition completed January 2026. The fleet consists of 21 nuclear reactors across 12 facilities plus natural gas, wind, solar, and geothermal assets from Calpine. The company serves 2.5 million customers and is restarting the Crane Clean Energy Center (Three Mile Island Unit 1) under a 20-year Microsoft PPA to power AI data centers.

Investment Thesis
Constellation Energy is the defining AI-era energy infrastructure play — the nation's largest electricity producer with 60 GW of capacity and the largest nuclear fleet serving the accelerating electricity demand of hyperscaler data centers [1][2]. Q1 2026 delivered $11.12B revenue (23.6% beat) and GAAP EPS of $4.49 (1,082% YoY increase) driven by the Calpine acquisition and strong nuclear operations [1]. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $11.00-$12.00 is reaffirmed, with free cash flow projected at $8.4B in 2026-2027 rising to $11.5-$13.0B by 2028-2029. The Crane Clean Energy Center restart received both a $1B DOE loan guarantee and FERC waiver approval in June 2026, removing key regulatory hurdles [3]. At approximately 21x forward P/E with a PEG of 1.19, the valuation is reasonable for a utility with growth-stock characteristics. D/E of 66% is conservative, ROE of 16.1% exceeds the target, and the dividend has grown 14.7% annually [5].
The bear case centers on execution risk, insider selling, and the 42% decline from the $412.70 high to $239.25. The Calpine integration is enormous — absorbing a $16.4B acquisition while simultaneously managing the Crane restart creates operational complexity and management bandwidth constraints [1]. CEO Dominguez sold $25.3M in shares, EVP Hanson sold $7.2M, and CFO Eggers sold $5.2M — nearly $38M in combined insider selling by the top three executives in February 2026, raising questions about management conviction at elevated prices [4]. The NRC environmental assessment for Crane is projecting a final finding in September 2026, and any delays to the 2028 restart target would push back earnings contributions. The Calpine natural gas fleet introduces carbon-intensity that creates ESG headwinds and potential regulatory risk. With $22.47B in total debt, interest rate sensitivity is significant for a capital-intensive utility. Profit margin of 12.69% remains below the utility peer average for a stock commanding growth-stock multiples [5].
