Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Aluminum ·NASDAQ ·United States
Century Aluminum is the largest producer of primary aluminum in the United States, operating smelters in Ravenswood, West Virginia and Grundartangi, Iceland. In Q1 2026, the company sold its Hawesville, Kentucky smelter for a $287.9M gain, retaining 6.8% equity in an AI data center redevelopment of the site. Century is developing a major new greenfield smelter in partnership with Emirates Global Aluminium, backed by a $500M DOE grant. Founded in 1995, headquartered in Chicago, Illinois.

Investment Thesis
Century Aluminum is the largest US primary aluminum producer at the center of an aluminum renaissance driven by tariff protections on imported aluminum and surging LME prices. Q1 2026 delivered $649.2M net sales with adjusted net income of $170.7M (adjusted EPS $1.63, beating consensus $1.56) and adjusted EBITDA of $231.4M [1][6]. Q2 2026 EBITDA guidance of $315-335M implies massive sequential improvement driven by higher realized LME prices and regional premiums [1]. The financial profile is exceptional: forward P/E of 6.13, PEG of 0.42, ROE of 32.26%, and clean balance sheet with D/E of 47% [3]. The Oklahoma greenfield JV with Emirates Global Aluminium — first new US smelter since 1980, backed by $500M DOE grant — positions Century to significantly expand domestic capacity over the next decade [1].
The bear case centers on the cyclical, policy-dependent nature of Century's profitability. The stock has risen 296% in 52 weeks, pricing in peak tariff-driven earnings that may not prove durable if trade policy shifts [3]. Q1 2026 GAAP net income of $337.5M includes $287.9M in one-time Hawesville gains and $33M in insurance proceeds, significantly inflating reported profitability [1]. The company's severely poor organizational culture (Glassdoor 2.7/5.0 with 'toxic' reviews and discrimination allegations) poses operational and legal risk [4]. Net insider selling of $2.4M suggests management views current prices as elevated. Energy costs (30-40% of smelting costs) remain a structural risk, and the Oklahoma smelter faces multi-year execution and capital risk. Without tariff protections, Century's competitive position against lower-cost global producers would deteriorate rapidly.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $315-335M represents 36-45% sequential improvement, driven by higher realized LME aluminum prices and strengthening US regional premiums
  2. Oklahoma greenfield smelter JV with Emirates Global Aluminium backed by $500M DOE grant — first new US primary aluminum smelter since 1980, positioning Century for long-term domestic capacity expansion

  3. Glassdoor rating of 2.7/5.0 with toxic culture allegations and discrimination complaints indicates organizational dysfunction that creates operational risk, employee retention challenges, and potential legal exposure
