Company Profile
Technology ·Security Technology & Industrial Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Cemtrex is a Hauppauge, NY-based holding company. Its Security segment, operating through Vicon Industries, sells AI-enabled video surveillance, analytics and access-control systems to government, education, healthcare and corporate customers. Its Industrial Services segment (Advanced Industrial Services) provides rigging, millwrighting, machinery installation and in-plant maintenance. In fiscal 2026 the company added an Aerospace & Defense segment through acquisition. Fiscal Q3 2026 (June quarter) revenue was $18.4M, up 9% YoY. The company has a long history of net losses, going-concern warnings and repeated reverse splits, and the stock trades near $1.78, against a 52-week high of $97 (split-adjusted).

Investment Thesis
The bull case is a deep-value turnaround. Cemtrex has about $71M of TTM revenue and $32.0M of book equity but a market cap of only about $3M, roughly 0.1x book [1][6]. Revenue has compounded at 11.9% a year from FY2020 to FY2025, and fiscal Q3 2026 revenue rose 9% YoY to $18.4M, helped by the new Aerospace & Defense segment and 6% Industrial Services growth [1][2]. Vicon's AI-powered video analytics line and the physical, AI-immune Industrial Services business give the company two real operating franchises. Fiscal Q2 2026 even showed a small net profit of $0.86M [1].
The bear case dominates. TTM net loss is $27.2M on $71.2M of revenue, and going-concern doubt has appeared repeatedly in filings [1][5]. The equity has been diluted almost to nothing through repeated financings and reverse splits; the stock fell from a split-adjusted $97 to $1.78 within a year, and Q1 FY2026 alone carried $12.1M of interest and financing expense [1][6]. Governance risk is acute: the company and CEO Saagar Govil settled SEC fraud-provision charges in 2022 over misleading customer claims, and founder Aron Govil settled charges of misappropriating $7.3M [5]. Liquidity sits right at IOWN's $1M floor and could fall below it under FMP's lower volume figure [6].
