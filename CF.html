Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Agricultural Inputs ·NYSE ·United States
CF Industries is the world's largest producer of ammonia and a leading manufacturer of hydrogen and nitrogen products for fertilizer, clean energy, emissions abatement, and industrial applications. The company operates the largest ammonia production network globally, including the Donaldsonville complex (world's largest nitrogen fertilizer plant). CF is pioneering low-carbon ammonia through the $4B Blue Point Joint Venture with JERA and Mitsui, and operates carbon sequestration in partnership with ExxonMobil. Headquartered in Deerfield, Illinois.

Investment Thesis
CF Industries is a best-in-class commodity producer delivering elite profitability on a tight global nitrogen market. Q1 2026 net earnings of $615M and adjusted EBITDA of $983M on $1.99B revenue (19% growth) reflect the structural benefit of cheap US natural gas and rising global fertilizer prices [1]. At 6.21x forward P/E with 27.3% ROE, 23.73% profit margin, and $1.62B free cash flow, CF is strikingly undervalued relative to its quality and growth profile [2]. The clean energy pivot — the $4B Blue Point JV producing 1.4M metric tons of low-carbon ammonia, Section 45Q credits generating ~$170M annually, and first commercial ammonia shipments to non-agricultural markets — provides a multi-decade secular growth layer atop the cyclical fertilizer base [2]. The company's 44% D/E ratio and 21 consecutive years of dividend payments demonstrate financial discipline.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, regulatory risk, and environmental liability. The DOJ launched an antitrust investigation into major fertilizer producers following a 40% urea price spike — a material legal overhang that could result in fines or pricing restrictions [4]. Chinese urea overcapacity of 4-6M metric tons could flood global markets, collapsing nitrogen prices and reversing CF's supply-driven margin expansion. Rising natural gas costs (EIA projects $4/MMBtu in 2026) threaten input margins. The Donaldsonville complex is Louisiana's largest GHG emitter with 333 reported accidents and 185 accidental chemical releases since 2006, creating significant environmental liability and reputational risk in an environmental justice context [4]. Revenue growth 5-year CAGR of 11.5% masks extreme volatility — the 2022 spike to $11.19B collapsed to $5.94B by 2024 — and forward consensus projects -1.8% revenue growth over 3 years, suggesting the current cyclical peak may be near [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Section 45Q carbon sequestration tax credits generating ~$170M annually ($85/ton on ~2M tons CO2) as a high-margin, fertilizer-price-independent revenue stream [1][2]
  2. Blue Point Joint Venture ($4B with JERA/Mitsui) producing 1.4M metric tons of low-carbon ammonia annually, opening the non-agricultural ammonia market projected to grow 20-30% by 2030 [2]
  3. Forward P/E of 6.21x on $1.62B free cash flow — if the DOJ antitrust investigation resolves favorably and nitrogen supply remains tight, multiple expansion could drive significant upside [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. DOJ antitrust investigation into major fertilizer producers following 40% urea price spike — potential for fines, pricing restrictions, or consent decrees that limit pricing power [4]
  2. Chinese urea overcapacity of 4-6M metric tons could flood global markets in late 2026-2027, collapsing nitrogen prices from current elevated levels and reversing the supply-driven rally [4]
  3. Environmental liability at Donaldsonville — 185 accidental chemical releases, 10M tons annual GHG emissions, and environmental justice scrutiny in 'Cancer Alley' create litigation, regulatory, and reputational risk [4]
