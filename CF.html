Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Agricultural Inputs ·NYSE ·United States
CF Industries is the world's largest producer of ammonia and a leading manufacturer of nitrogen fertilizer products including urea, UAN, and ammonium nitrate. The company operates nine manufacturing complexes in the U.S., Canada, and the UK, with North America's largest nitrogen production network. Under new CEO Chris Bohn (January 2026), CF is pivoting toward clean energy through the Blue Point One joint venture with JERA and Mitsui — a $3.7B investment to build the world's largest low-carbon ammonia plant in Louisiana (1.4M tpy, 98% CO2 capture, operational 2029). Q1 2026 revenue reached $1.99B (+19% YoY) with $615M net earnings and $983M adjusted EBITDA, driven by tight global nitrogen supply. Market cap approximately $19.9B.

Investment Thesis
CF Industries at 7.10x forward earnings is one of the cheapest large-cap nitrogen producers globally, benefiting from a structural tightening in global nitrogen supply. Q1 2026 delivered $1.99B revenue (+19% YoY) and $615M net earnings, beating analyst estimates by 59% on EPS, driven by higher average selling prices across all segments amid global supply disruptions [1]. D/E of 44%, ROE of 27.30%, and profit margin of 23.73% demonstrate a financially fortress-like business with conservative leverage and strong capital efficiency [4]. The Blue Point One JV ($3.7B with JERA and Mitsui) positions CF as a first-mover in clean ammonia production with 1.4M tpy capacity and 98% carbon capture — opening hydrogen/energy as a new addressable market beyond agriculture [3]. North American natural gas cost advantage provides a structural margin moat versus European and Asian producers facing higher energy costs.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, commodity risk, and growth limitations. CF Industries' revenue has swung from $6.54B (2021) to $11.19B (2022) to $5.94B (2024), demonstrating extreme sensitivity to global nitrogen pricing that makes earnings inherently unpredictable [2]. The current 20.85% revenue growth is driven by tight supply conditions that could reverse if geopolitical tensions ease, European plants restart, or Chinese export restrictions loosen — any of which would compress nitrogen prices and margins. The 5yr revenue CAGR of approximately 2.5% reflects this cyclicality and suggests the current pricing environment may be peak-cycle rather than a new structural baseline. Blue Point One's $3.7B investment introduces execution risk on an unprecedented scale, with clean ammonia pricing, carbon credit markets, and hydrogen demand all still uncertain [3]. The 1.62% dividend yield underperforms the Growth sleeve criteria, and the company's strategy of returning capital through buybacks rather than dividends may not appeal to income-oriented investors. The Inspire Impact Score of 36 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions is favorable for faith-based investors, but commodity cyclicality creates portfolio volatility that may be uncomfortable for conservative allocations.
