Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Agricultural Inputs ·NYSE ·United States
CF Industries is a global producer and distributor of hydrogen and nitrogen products: anhydrous ammonia, granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate and diesel exhaust fluid. Its customers include agricultural cooperatives, distributors and industrial users, and its manufacturing complexes are in North America and the UK. Christopher Bohn, a 17-year CF veteran and former CFO, became CEO in January 2026, succeeding Tony Will. The company is building Blue Point One, a low-carbon ammonia joint venture with JERA and Mitsui (CF owns 40%), at Modeste, Louisiana, with production expected in 2029. Q2 2026 net sales were $2.22B, up 17.6% YoY, on tight global nitrogen markets.

Investment Thesis
CF is earning near peak-cycle economics. Q2 2026 net sales rose 17.6% to $2.22B and diluted EPS doubled to $4.73 (from $2.37), as Iran-related supply disruptions tightened an already tight global nitrogen market [1][3]. TTM net income of $2.10B on $7.74B of revenue (27% margin), 40% ROE and only $1.13B of net debt support aggressive capital return; weighted shares fell from 168.6M to 153.6M in five quarters [2]. At 8.7x trailing and 10.1x forward earnings, roughly 4.6x EV/EBITDA, and a 10.7% FCF yield, the stock prices in substantial normalization [2][7]. Blue Point One, with permits secured, construction started in August 2026 and offtake contracted with JERA, gives CF a first-mover position in low-carbon ammonia from 2029 [4].
The bear case is cyclicality. Consensus expects EPS to fall from $15.07 in FY2026 to $11.66 in FY2027 and about $8.80 by 2030, which means today's earnings are a peak rather than a new baseline [7]. Revenue history shows how quickly that can unwind: $6.54B (2021), $11.19B (2022), $5.94B (2024) [2]. Q2 2026 sales volumes fell 15% and revenue missed the roughly $2.45B consensus, so pricing alone is carrying growth [3]. Political risk is rising, with Sen. Hawley's March 2026 price-gouging accusations and reports of a DOJ fertilizer-pricing probe [3]. Blue Point One also concentrates execution risk in a $3.7B to $4B project whose economics depend on unproven low-carbon ammonia demand and carbon-credit policy [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 earnings in early November 2026, where consensus of about $2.99 EPS compares with $2.19 a year ago, testing whether Iran-related nitrogen tightness is still lifting realized prices [3]
  2. Blue Point One construction progress (groundbreaking August 2026, 1.4M tons a year, start-up 2029) with contracted JERA offtake of about 490K tons a year for power-sector ammonia co-firing [4]

Key Risks
  1. Nitrogen price normalization: consensus EPS falls 23% from FY2026 to FY2027 and about 42% by 2030, and history shows revenue can halve within two years ($11.19B in 2022 to $5.94B in 2024) [2][7]
  2. Political and antitrust exposure from Sen. Hawley's March 2026 fertilizer price-gouging accusations and reported DOJ scrutiny of U.S. fertilizer pricing [3]
  3. Blue Point One execution and demand risk on a $3.7B to $4B JV whose returns depend on low-carbon ammonia premiums, 45Q carbon-capture credits and Japanese co-firing demand that are not yet proven at scale [4]
