Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail ·NASDAQ ·Hong Kong
Creative Global Technology Holdings Limited is an investment holding company that sources and resells recycled consumer electronic devices including smartphones, tablets, and laptops, primarily pre-owned Apple products which account for approximately 90% of revenue. Founded in 2016 and headquartered in Kwun Tong, Hong Kong, the company IPO'd on NASDAQ in November 2024. Market cap of approximately $12M.

Investment Thesis
CGTL operates in the growing global refurbished electronics market projected to reach $150B+ by 2030, driven by sustainability trends and demand for affordable technology in developing markets [1]. The zero-debt balance sheet eliminates leverage risk, and the company's gross margin improved to 17.8% in FY2024 (from 9.9%), suggesting some success in shifting toward higher-margin orders. The heavily traded stock (avg daily volume ~15M shares) provides liquidity for speculative positions despite the micro-cap status [4].
The bear case is overwhelming. Revenue collapsed 40.4% in H1 FY2025 to $12.2M with a net loss of $15.3M — the loss exceeding revenue [1]. The stock has declined 86% from its IPO market cap of $85M to $12M, with shares at $0.70 well below the $4.00 IPO price. Management team average tenure of 1.9 years, new CEO (March 2025), and 90% revenue concentration in Apple products create execution, governance, and concentration risks [3]. The 12-person workforce lacks the scale, brand recognition, and operational infrastructure to compete against well-funded platforms. No institutional investor backing and no analyst coverage leave the stock without price support or visibility. Nasdaq delisting risk persists if the bid price remains below $1.00 [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Potential revenue stabilization in H2 FY2025 as the new leadership team under CEO Angel Siu executes on operational improvements and higher-margin product mix
  2. Growing global demand for affordable refurbished electronics in developing markets driven by sustainability mandates and rising smartphone adoption
  3. Nasdaq compliance regain by maintaining $1.00+ bid price, which would remove the overhang of delisting risk and stabilize the investor base
Key Risks
  1. Revenue declined 40.4% in H1 FY2025 with net loss exceeding revenue ($15.3M loss on $12.2M revenue), raising going-concern risk for a company with only $18M in equity
  2. 90% revenue concentration in pre-owned Apple products creates existential dependence on Apple's product cycle and direct competition with Apple's own trade-in and refurbishment programs
  3. Nasdaq delisting risk is immediate — failure to maintain $1.00 bid price compliance would remove access to U.S. capital markets and likely accelerate the stock's decline
