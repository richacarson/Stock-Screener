Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Community Healthcare Trust Incorporated is a self-managed, fully-integrated healthcare real estate investment trust that acquires, owns, and manages healthcare properties leased to hospitals, doctors, and healthcare service providers primarily in non-urban and secondary markets. Founded in 2014 and headquartered in Franklin, Tennessee, the company holds approximately $1.2 billion in gross real estate investments across 198 properties totaling 4.52 million square feet in 36 states. The portfolio includes medical office buildings, physician clinics, outpatient treatment facilities, behavioral health properties, and inpatient rehabilitation facilities. CEO David Dupuy assumed leadership after founder Timothy Wallace's passing in March 2023. In Q2 2026, CHCT cut its quarterly dividend 31% from $0.48 to $0.33 to fund a $99M acquisition pipeline and portfolio reinvestment, resetting the AFFO payout ratio from 87% to approximately 60%.

Investment Thesis
CHCT at $16.86 offers a deep-value entry into a healthcare REIT trading at ~70% below its 2022 highs, with a 7.83% forward dividend yield and a strategic capital reallocation that should drive FFO growth. The post-cut AFFO payout ratio of 60% (vs. 87% prior) retains $25-30M over 24 months to fund a $99M acquisition pipeline at 9-10% yields with ~2.5% annual escalators, plus $70M+ of capital recycling dispositions and occupancy improvement from 89.8% toward the 92% target — an estimated $6M in incremental annualized NOI [1][4]. Q1 2026 showed early momentum with revenue up 4.8% YoY to $31.5M, FFO up 5.8% to $0.50/share, and net income up 60% [2]. CEO Dupuy's $151K open-market purchase at $15.13 in August 2026 signals insider conviction in the turnaround thesis, and 4 analysts maintain a consensus Buy rating with an $18.75 average price target representing ~11% upside [3][4].
The bear case centers on weak GAAP profitability, elevated leverage, and broken dividend growth momentum. ROE of 1.13% and profit margin of 4.21% — even accounting for REIT depreciation distortions — suggest the portfolio generates returns barely above cost of capital, and the trailing P/E of 153x reflects minimal GAAP earnings power [3]. Debt-to-equity of 140% with $564M in total debt against a $421M market cap creates financial fragility if interest rates remain elevated or healthcare property values decline. Revenue growth of 4.8% YoY and 3.9% 5-year CAGR significantly trail the healthcare REIT peer group growing at ~13% per annum, exposing CHCT as a laggard in its sector [2]. The 31% dividend cut breaks an 11-year streak of quarterly increases, potentially triggering forced selling by income-focused funds and damaging investor trust that may take years to rebuild. Occupancy at 89.8% leaves over 10% of the 4.52M sq ft portfolio vacant, and re-leasing healthcare facilities in secondary and non-urban markets is structurally more difficult than in urban centers [1]. The $99M acquisition pipeline, while promising, has not yet closed or contributed to earnings, and execution risk is elevated for a small REIT team navigating a higher interest rate environment.
