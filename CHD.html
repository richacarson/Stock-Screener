Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Household & Personal Products ·NYQ ·United States
Church & Dwight develops, manufactures, and markets household, personal care, and specialty products through a portfolio of category-leading niche brands including Arm & Hammer, OxiClean, Trojan, Waterpik, TheraBreath, Batiste, Hero (Mighty Patch), and First Response. The company generates TTM revenue of $6.2B with a 12.0% profit margin across three segments: Consumer Domestic, Consumer International, and Specialty Products. Q2 2026 organic sales grew 5.8% (above 1% outlook), driven by 4.3% volume growth and 1.5% positive price/mix, though reported revenue growth was only 1.6% due to 2025 divestitures [1].

Investment Thesis
Church & Dwight at $105.96 delivers steady compounding through a portfolio of category-leading niche brands anchored by Arm & Hammer, OxiClean, Trojan, and high-growth additions like Hero Cosmetics and TheraBreath [1]. Q2 2026 organic sales grew 5.8% (nearly 6x the company's 1% outlook), driven by strong volume growth of 4.3%, prompting raised full-year guidance to $6.20-$6.27B revenue and $3.74-$3.81 adjusted EPS [1]. The balance sheet is conservatively managed at 55.9% D/E with $939M in free cash flow funding both the 28-year dividend growth streak and the acquisition pipeline. Infrastructure scores well on payout ratio (38.6%), dividend consistency, and capital efficiency (17% ROE), while the defensive product portfolio provides recession resistance.
The bear case centers on premium valuation and modest growth. At 26.3x forward earnings and a PEG of 2.94, CHD is priced as a premium compounder but delivers only low-single-digit reported revenue growth (1.6% YoY), with the organic strength masked by divestitures [1]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~5% is acquisition-dependent, and the company's growth model requires continued successful deal-making to supplement modest organic growth in mature consumer categories. Profit margin of 12.0% is below the 20% target for the framework and significantly trails more profitable CPG peers like Procter & Gamble or Colgate. The 1.16% dividend yield offers minimal income for a Dividend sleeve stock, and the 4.0% five-year dividend growth CAGR barely exceeds inflation [1]. Private-label competition in commodity categories (laundry, cleaning) is an ongoing margin headwind, and a shareholder proposal on plastic packaging signals growing scrutiny of the company's environmental footprint [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Organic sales acceleration — Q2 2026 organic growth of 5.8% (triple the 2% full-year guidance at the start of the year) driven by Hero Cosmetics, TheraBreath, and Waterpik volume growth, with raised full-year organic growth guidance of 4-5% [1]
  2. Acquisition-driven growth pipeline — CHD's track record of buying niche brands at reasonable valuations (Hero, TheraBreath, Waterpik, Touchland) and scaling them through its distribution network provides a repeatable growth algorithm [1]
  3. Conservative balance sheet (55.9% D/E, $939M FCF) provides dry powder for additional acquisitions while sustaining the 28-year dividend growth streak and low 38.6% payout ratio [1]
Key Risks
  1. Premium valuation (26.3x forward P/E, PEG 2.94) provides limited margin of safety — any acquisition misstep, organic growth disappointment, or earnings miss could trigger a re-rating given the stock already trades at a significant premium to the consumer staples sector [1]
  2. Acquisition dependency for growth — the company's growth model relies on continued deal-making in a competitive M&A environment where valuations for consumer brands have compressed, and integration risk rises with each new addition [1]
  3. Private-label competition in commodity categories (laundry detergent, cleaning products, baking soda) pressures margins and volume in Arm & Hammer and XTRA brands as retailers expand store-brand offerings [1]
