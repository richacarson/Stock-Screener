Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Household & Personal Products ·NYSE ·United States
Church & Dwight manufactures and markets a diversified portfolio of consumer household and personal care products across three segments: Consumer Domestic, Consumer International, and Specialty Products. Core brands include Arm & Hammer (baking soda, laundry), OxiClean (stain removal), Trojan (condoms), First Response (pregnancy tests), Vitafusion (gummy vitamins), Batiste (dry shampoo), TheraBreath (oral care), HERO Cosmetics (skin care), and TOUCHLAND (hand sanitizer). Q2 2026 revenue reached $1.53B with 5.8% organic growth, and management raised FY2026 guidance for organic sales, EPS (5-8% growth to $3.74-$3.81), and cash from operations (~$1.15B).

Investment Thesis
Church & Dwight at 26.3x forward earnings is a premium consumer staples compounder delivering accelerating organic growth — Q2 2026 organic sales growth of 5.8% outpaced management's own plan, driven by 4.3% volume growth demonstrating genuine consumer demand rather than price-led growth [1]. Gross margin expanded 240bps to 45.4%, and management raised FY2026 guidance for organic sales, EPS growth (5-8% to $3.74-$3.81), and cash from operations (~$1.15B). The portfolio transformation through TheraBreath, HERO Cosmetics, and TOUCHLAND has shifted the mix toward faster-growing personal care categories. ROE of 17.62%, D/E of 60%, payout ratio of 36.55%, and 28 consecutive years of dividend increases reflect balance sheet and capital return discipline that supports continued compounding [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of 56 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes CHD attractive for faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on valuation stretch and structural growth limitations. Forward P/E of 26.3x and PEG of 3.66 price the stock at a meaningful premium to consumer staples peers (~18-20x forward), yet reported revenue growth was only 1.6% in Q2 and the 5-year revenue CAGR is just 4.85% — the gap between organic and reported growth reflects divestitures and FX that reduce actual top-line dollars [1][3]. Profit margin of 11.88% is below many consumer staples peers (Procter & Gamble ~19%, Colgate ~13%), limiting earnings leverage. Private label competition in commodity-adjacent categories like laundry and cleaning — potentially accelerated by AI-powered formulation tools — could compress Arm & Hammer's brand premium [4]. The CEO transition to Rick Dierker, while well-planned, has yet to be tested through a downturn or major strategic decision. The dividend yield of 1.24% is below the 2% threshold many income investors target, making the Dividend sleeve assignment a portfolio legacy rather than a current income story.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued TOUCHLAND momentum and new product innovation contributing roughly half of organic growth in 2026, with gross margin expansion from productivity and favorable mix shift toward higher-margin personal care brands [1]
  2. Portfolio transformation toward faster-growing categories (oral care, skin care, hand care) through successful integration of TheraBreath, HERO Cosmetics, and TOUCHLAND acquisitions, driving organic growth above the legacy 3-4% rate [2]
  3. Rick Dierker's first full year as CEO could establish a 'new chapter' narrative if he accelerates innovation pace or makes a strategic acquisition that signals portfolio evolution beyond traditional CPG categories [2]
Key Risks
  1. Valuation compression risk if organic growth reverts to the historical 3-4% range after the Q2 2026 acceleration — Forward P/E of 26.3x and PEG of 3.66 leave no margin of safety for a consumer staples company growing EPS at 5-8% [3]
  2. Private label competition in commodity cleaning categories (Arm & Hammer laundry, OxiClean) intensifying as retailers deploy AI-powered formulation and shelf optimization tools to improve store-brand quality and placement [4]
  3. Reported revenue growth of only 1.6% vs 5.8% organic reveals meaningful FX and divestiture headwinds that reduce actual cash-generating top-line growth available to support the $1.15B CFO target [1]

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Church & Dwight's core business is manufacturing and distributing physical consumer products — baking soda, laundry detergent, condoms, vitamins, skin care — that AI structurally cannot replace [1]. Brand equity, shelf placement, distribution networks, and regulatory approvals (FDA-regulated products like Trojan, First Response, Vitafusion) create meaningful moats against AI disruption. However, analysts are not flagging AI as a material risk to CHD's core business model, and physical product moats remain intact.
