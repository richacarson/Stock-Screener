Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Education & Training Services ·NYSE ·United States
Chegg, Inc. operates a learning platform that historically provided subscription-based homework help, writing tools, and math solvers. The company is now pivoting from its decimated B2C student homework help business — which lost $14B in market cap to ChatGPT disruption over three years — toward B2B enterprise skilling in AI, coding, data analytics, and professional development. After cutting 45% of staff, CEO Dan Rosensweig returned to lead the restructuring. Founded in 2005, headquartered in Santa Clara, California.

The company optimized for quarterly subscriber growth during the pandemic boom, failed to invest in defensive moats against AI disruption despite early warning signs, and has responded with reactive layoffs and CEO shuffles rather than principled strategic pivots. CEO Rosensweig admitted he initially dismissed AI's threat. The pivot to B2B skilling is driven by survival necessity rather than an enduring cause, and the organizational trauma of cutting two-thirds of the workforce leaves no foundation for trust or long-term thinking.

Investment Thesis
Chegg's Q1 2026 results show the restructuring is producing some stabilization: the company reported its first positive GAAP net income in two years ($0.2M), adjusted EBITDA of $15.5M at a 24% margin, and generated $3.1M in free cash flow despite $12.9M in severance payments [1]. Chegg Skilling grew 9% YoY to $17.6M with management targeting double-digit full-year growth through enterprise distribution partnerships [2]. At $1.15 per share and approximately $120M market cap with $34M in net cash, the stock prices in near-complete destruction — offering asymmetric upside if the B2B skilling pivot scales to replace even a fraction of the lost homework help revenue.
The bear case is overwhelming and existential. Revenue declined 48% YoY in Q1 2026 to $63.3M, and Q2 guidance of $49-50M implies continued sharp sequential deterioration — annualizing to approximately $200-220M against a $773M peak just five years ago [1][3]. Two rounds of layoffs eliminated nearly two-thirds of the workforce, and the failed CheggMate AI product demonstrated that competing with free AI tools through paid subscriptions is a dead end. The $70M annualized Chegg Skilling revenue cannot offset the hundreds of millions lost from homework help, and the B2B skilling market is fiercely competitive with established players like Coursera, LinkedIn Learning, and Pluralsight. The FTC action for data breaches exposing 40M users and ongoing academic dishonesty lawsuits create additional legal risk [4]. The convertible notes ($33.8M) create refinancing risk at current share prices, and daily dollar volume near the $1M threshold limits institutional participation.
Key Catalysts
  1. Chegg Skilling revenue accelerating to double-digit growth as enterprise partnerships (DHL, GI Group) scale and new distribution channels materialize in H2 2026, potentially proving the viability of the B2B pivot [1][2]
  2. Cost restructuring achieving sustained adjusted EBITDA profitability on the smaller revenue base, demonstrating a path to long-term viability — Q1 2026 showed 24% adjusted EBITDA margin on $63.3M revenue [1]
  3. Legacy homework help subscriber declines stabilizing as the easy AI substitution cycle matures and remaining subscribers prove sticky for structured learning and credentialing content
Key Risks
  1. Revenue continues accelerating decline — Q2 guidance of $49-50M implies ~$200M annualized vs $773M peak, and there is no evidence the decline is decelerating, with academic services revenue still falling far faster than skilling grows [1]
  2. Competition in B2B enterprise skilling from Coursera, LinkedIn Learning, Pluralsight, and Udemy — all better-capitalized, more established, and with stronger brand recognition in the enterprise market
  3. FTC enforcement action for data breaches exposing 40M users and shareholder lawsuits alleging investor fraud and insider trading create ongoing legal liability that could consume remaining cash reserves [4]
