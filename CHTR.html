Company Profile
Communication Services ·Cable & Satellite ·NASDAQ ·United States
Charter Communications, Inc. operates under the Spectrum brand as the second-largest U.S. cable operator, providing broadband internet, mobile, video, and voice services to residential and business customers across 41 states reaching nearly 59 million homes and businesses passed. Charter also owns regional Spectrum News channels and provides enterprise connectivity solutions. Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut.

Investment Thesis
Charter trades at a distressed-looking 2.97x forward P/E and a calculated ~0.16x forward PEG despite a still-solid 27.5% ROE and 9.0% profit margin, after the stock fell roughly 66% over the trailing year on fears of accelerating broadband share loss to fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless [1][7]. The reported SpaceX talks for a consumer mobile partnership — which sent shares up over 24% on the news — would let Charter monetize its terrestrial backhaul and ground infrastructure to co-opt satellite competition rather than simply lose share to it, a potentially significant re-rating catalyst if a deal materializes [5]. Insiders have been buying: CEO Chris Winfrey and multiple independent directors made open-market purchases in April-May 2026 totaling a net $3.76M over 90 days, signaling management sees the sell-off as overdone [6]. The company continues investing at scale — the largest network expansion since the 1980s and $7B+ in rural gigabit build-out — positioning Charter to defend its broadband franchise once the current upgrade cycle completes [2].
The bear case is that Charter's core business is genuinely shrinking, not just cheaply valued: Q1 2026 revenue fell 1% YoY and the trailing 5-year revenue CAGR is just 1.5%, with the company having missed consensus EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters heading into the July 24, 2026 print [1]. D/E of 456% (well above the 300% distress threshold used in this framework) reflects a highly leveraged, buyback-funded balance sheet that leaves little room for error if broadband subscriber losses to AT&T Fiber, T-Mobile/Verizon fixed wireless, and Starlink accelerate [7]. Customer-service quality is a persistent liability — Yelp reviews average just 1.4/5 with recurring complaints about billing errors and improperly continued charges after cancellation, a pattern that could accelerate churn even where competitive alternatives are limited [3]. The SpaceX partnership remains only in the 'talks' stage as of July 2026 — if it fails to materialize or delivers less economic benefit than the market has priced in, the extreme valuation discount could persist or widen [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. A signed SpaceX consumer mobile partnership converting Charter's terrestrial backhaul into a monetizable asset rather than a competitive casualty, building on the >24% stock pop when talks were first reported [5]
  2. Stabilization of broadband subscriber trends as the DOCSIS/fiber network upgrade program (largest since the 1980s-90s) reaches completion, potentially validating the stock's extreme valuation discount [1][2]
  3. Continued Spectrum Mobile bundling growth reducing broadband churn and diversifying revenue away from a shrinking core video/broadband base
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 456% on a still-declining revenue base (Q1 2026 -1% YoY, 5yr CAGR 1.5%) leaves limited flexibility if fiber overbuilder and fixed-wireless competition intensifies further [1][7]
  2. Persistently poor customer service (1.4/5 average Yelp rating, recurring billing/cancellation complaints) creates ongoing churn and reputational risk independent of network quality [3]
  3. The SpaceX partnership remains unsigned as of July 2026; failure to finalize a deal on favorable terms would remove the primary catalyst behind the stock's recent rally and leave Charter fully exposed to satellite/fixed-wireless substitution [5]
