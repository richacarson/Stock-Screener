Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Internet Retail / Pet Care ·NYSE ·United States
Chewy is the largest online retailer of pet food, products, and services in the United States, serving 21.5 million active customers through a digital-first platform that includes e-commerce, Autoship subscription delivery, veterinary telehealth (Connect with a Vet), Chewy Pharmacy, pet insurance, and a growing Chewy Vet Care clinic network. Under CEO Sumit Singh since 2018, Chewy has grown from a $2B company to over $12.8B in TTM revenue while achieving record profitability. Q1 FY2026 delivered 7.7% net sales growth, record adjusted EBITDA margin of 7.5%, and nearly 200,000 net new active customers [1].

Investment Thesis
Chewy at $24.02 (15.0x forward earnings, PEG 0.52) is a rare combination of growth, improving profitability, and value — trading at a significant discount to the broader market despite dominant market position in the $150B+ US pet industry. Q1 FY2026 delivered 7.7% revenue growth to $3.6B, record adjusted EBITDA margin of 7.5% (+130bps YoY), and nearly 200,000 net new active customers, demonstrating that the revenue growth and margin expansion story are executing simultaneously [1]. The 21.5 million active customer base with high Autoship penetration creates a recurring revenue foundation that is unusual for e-commerce. The healthcare platform expansion (vet clinics, pharmacy, insurance, telehealth) adds higher-margin, stickier revenue streams that could re-rate the stock from an e-commerce multiple to a pet care platform multiple [2]. Free cash flow of $584.5M TTM against a $9.8B market cap provides a 6% FCF yield. The Inspire Impact Score of 43 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions makes Chewy suitable for faith-based portfolios.
The bear case centers on growth deceleration, margin sustainability, and competitive pressure. Revenue growth has slowed from double-digits to 7.7% with FY2026 guidance of 6.3-7.5%, reflecting a 'more challenging consumer environment impacting discretionary attachment and premiumization behavior' per management [1]. The trailing P/E of 40.12x looks expensive relative to the 7-8% revenue growth trajectory and thin 1.99% profit margin — the attractive forward P/E and PEG rely on continued margin expansion that is not guaranteed. Amazon continues to invest aggressively in its pet category, and Walmart's expanding pet offerings create persistent competitive pressure on pricing and delivery speed. D/E of 123% is elevated, and the high ROE (63.82%) is an artifact of the thin equity base from accumulated historical losses rather than genuine capital efficiency. Autoship consumer complaints and class action lawsuits represent reputational and legal risk that could undermine the 'empathy at scale' brand promise [3]. The vet clinic expansion requires significant capital investment with uncertain returns in a fragmented veterinary market.
Key Catalysts
  1. Pet healthcare platform expansion (vet clinics, pharmacy, insurance) could drive higher-margin revenue and re-rate the stock from an e-commerce multiple (~1x revenue) to a pet care platform multiple (3-5x), as healthcare services carry structurally higher margins than product e-commerce [2]
  2. Continued adjusted EBITDA margin expansion from 7.5% record toward 10%+ would demonstrate operating leverage in the business model and drive significant EPS growth from the current $0.60 base, supporting the attractive 0.52x PEG ratio [1]
  3. Active customer growth (+200K net in Q1, 21.5M total) and increasing spend per customer through cross-selling pharmacy, insurance, and wellness services could accelerate revenue growth back toward double-digits [1]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration from double-digits to 6-8% guidance reflects consumer spending pressure on discretionary pet products — further slowdown could compress the 40x trailing P/E if the market prices Chewy as a mature e-commerce company rather than a growth platform [1]
  2. Amazon's expanding pet category and Walmart's investment in pet e-commerce create persistent competitive pressure on pricing, selection, and delivery speed that could erode Chewy's market share advantage over time [2]
  3. Autoship consumer complaints, class action lawsuits, and lack of formal ESG disclosure create reputational risk that conflicts with the 'empathy at scale' brand promise and could undermine customer trust if not addressed [3]

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Chewy's core business — selling physical pet food, medicine, and supplies — involves tangible products that AI cannot digitize or replace. Pets still need food, treats, medication, and toys delivered to their owners' homes [1]. The Autoship subscription model (recurring delivery) creates meaningful switching costs that protect against AI-driven competitor entry. However, Chewy operates in e-commerce retail where AI-enhanced competition from Amazon, Walmart, and other platforms could erode market share through superior recommendation engines, logistics optimization, and pricing algorithms. Chewy's customer service moat (personalized, empathetic human interactions) could be partially replicated by increasingly capable AI agents, potentially commoditizing a key differentiator. The vet telehealth and pharmacy businesses face moderate AI disruption risk from AI diagnostic tools and telehealth platforms. AI Opportunity (1/1): Chewy actively deploys AI in customer-facing applications — personalized product recommendations, predictive pet care suggestions, and customer service automation as part of Singh's 'empathy at scale' initiative [2].
