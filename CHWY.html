Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Internet Retail ·NYQ ·United States
Chewy is the largest online retailer of pet food, products, and services in the United States, serving 21.5 million active customers through e-commerce, Autoship subscription delivery (84.4% of net sales), Chewy Pharmacy, pet insurance, and a growing Chewy Vet Care clinic network. Q2 FY2026 delivered net sales of $3.33B (+7.3% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA margin of 6.8% (+90bps), and full-year guidance was raised to 6.3-7.5% net sales growth [1].

Investment Thesis
Chewy at $19.71 (10.5x forward earnings, PEG 0.34) offers a rare combination of growth, improving profitability, and deep value — trading at a significant discount to the broader market despite dominant market position in the $150B+ US pet industry [1]. Q2 FY2026 delivered 7.3% net sales growth to $3.33B with adjusted EBITDA margin of 6.8% (+90bps YoY), Autoship customer sales grew over 10% representing 84.4% of total net sales, and full-year guidance was raised [1]. The 21.5 million active customer base with industry-leading Autoship penetration creates a recurring revenue foundation unusual for e-commerce. The healthcare platform expansion (Modern Animal, pharmacy, insurance, Chewy Vet Care clinics) adds higher-margin, stickier revenue that could re-rate the stock from an e-commerce multiple to a pet care platform multiple. Free cash flow of approximately $449M TTM against an $7.9B market cap provides a 5.7% FCF yield. The Inspire Impact Score of 43 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions makes Chewy suitable for faith-based portfolios.
The bear case centers on growth deceleration, margin sustainability, and competitive pressure. Revenue growth has slowed from double-digits to 7.3% with management citing a 'more challenging consumer environment impacting discretionary attachment and premiumization behavior,' and full-year guidance of 6.3-7.5% growth reflects continued deceleration [1]. D/E of 313% is severely elevated (largely from lease obligations and thin equity from accumulated historical losses), and the high ROE (72%) is an artifact of this thin equity base rather than genuine capital efficiency. Profit margin of only 2.1% means Chewy is still a razor-thin-margin business that could see profitability evaporate if competition intensifies. Amazon continues to invest aggressively in its pet category, and Walmart's expanding pet offerings create persistent pricing pressure. The trailing P/E of 30.3x looks expensive relative to the 7-8% revenue growth trajectory, and the attractive forward P/E relies on continued margin expansion that is not guaranteed. The Chewy Vet Care clinic expansion requires significant capital investment with uncertain returns in a fragmented veterinary market.
Key Catalysts
  1. Pet healthcare platform expansion (Modern Animal clinics contributing approximately $290M in FY2026 revenue, pharmacy, insurance) could drive higher-margin revenue and re-rate the stock from an e-commerce multiple to a pet care platform multiple [1]
  2. Continued adjusted EBITDA margin expansion toward 8-10% would demonstrate operating leverage and drive significant EPS growth from the current base, supporting the compelling 0.34x PEG ratio and 10.5x forward P/E [1]
  3. Autoship customer sales growing over 10% YoY with 84.4% penetration and 21.5M active customers (+3.6% YoY) demonstrate the stickiness of the platform and cross-selling opportunity into pharmacy, insurance, and wellness services [1]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration to 6-8% range amid consumer spending pressure on discretionary pet products — further slowdown could compress the 30x trailing P/E if the market prices Chewy as a mature e-commerce company rather than a growth platform [1]
  2. Amazon's expanding pet category and Walmart's investment in pet e-commerce create persistent competitive pressure on pricing, selection, and delivery speed that could erode Chewy's market share advantage over time [2]
  3. D/E of 313% and profit margin of only 2.1% reflect a thin equity base and razor-thin profitability that provide minimal buffer against macroeconomic headwinds or competitive intensification [1]

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Chewy's core business — selling physical pet food, medicine, and supplies — involves tangible products that AI cannot digitize or replace; pets still need food, treats, medication, and toys delivered to their owners' homes [1]. The Autoship subscription model (84.4% of net sales) creates meaningful switching costs that protect against AI-driven competitor entry. The Chewy Vet Care clinics and pharmacy provide physical-world moats that are more defensible.
