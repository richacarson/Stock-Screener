The Cigna Group is a global health services company serving approximately 180 million customer relationships worldwide through two segments: Evernorth Health Services (pharmacy benefit management through Express Scripts, specialty pharmacy, clinical programs, care delivery) and Cigna Healthcare (employer medical plans, individual/family plans, behavioral health, dental, global health). Investment Thesis
The Cigna Group is a deeply valued managed care leader trading at just 8.7x forward earnings with approximately $280B in annual revenue, PEG of 0.94, and 16% adjusted EPS growth [4]. Q2 2026 delivered $71.7B revenue (+7% YoY) with profit of $1.7B (+8%), prompting a second consecutive guidance raise to at least $30.45 adjusted EPS for FY2026 [1]. Evernorth Health Services is the growth engine — Q1 2026 Evernorth earnings grew 20% — with specialty pharmacy distribution and clinical programs driving above-market expansion while diversifying beyond traditional insurance underwriting. The company generates substantial free cash flow supporting a ~2.1% dividend yield (growing 9% annually over 5 years with only 21% payout ratio) and significant share buybacks [4]. The orderly CEO succession to Brian Evanko and ALIGNED faith considerations for positive ESG attributes provide strategic continuity.
The bear case centers on medical cost pressure, regulatory risk, and cultural weakness. Revenue growth decelerated to 7% in Q2 2026 from double-digit rates in FY2025, potentially signaling headwinds from the ACA marketplace exit and pharmacy volume normalization [1]. Profit margin of 2.27% is structurally thin for managed care, leaving minimal buffer if medical loss ratios deteriorate or utilization spikes. PBM regulatory reform is a bipartisan priority — Congressional proposals targeting drug pricing transparency, rebate practices, and pharmacy benefit pass-through models could structurally compress Evernorth margins. CEO transition introduces execution risk during industry upheaval — Evanko must prove he can sustain Cordani's transformative track record. Glassdoor at 3.4/5 declining 2% with constant reorgs signals organizational instability [2]. Competition from UnitedHealth/Optum's superior AI-driven vertical integration and emerging tech-native health platforms could erode Cigna's position. AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Cigna's core businesses — managing health insurance risk pools, negotiating provider networks, processing medical claims, and operating the Express Scripts PBM — involve deeply regulated processes, massive capital requirements, complex payer-provider relationships, and physical specialty pharmacy infrastructure that AI structurally cannot displace [1]. State-by-state insurance licensing, HIPAA compliance, formulary management, and drug distribution create high switching costs and regulatory barriers. However, AI-powered health platforms are increasingly automating utilization management and prior authorization workflows, and tech-forward competitors (UnitedHealth/Optum) are investing heavily in AI-driven care models that could erode Cigna's competitive position if it falls behind in AI adoption [2]. PBM transparency legislation could further compress structural advantages. AI Opportunity (0/1): Cigna deploys AI internally through Chief AI Officer Andresen's team for claims adjudication, clinical decision support, and population health analytics, contributing to operational efficiency [2]. However, no AI-specific revenue product or quantified savings have been separately reported — AI remains an internal efficiency tool rather than a customer-facing revenue driver.
