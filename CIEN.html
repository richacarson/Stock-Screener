Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NYSE ·United States
Ciena is a global leader in optical networking, supplying coherent optical systems (WaveLogic), routing and switching, and network software to cloud providers, telecom carriers, and governments. AI data-center demand has driven a surge: fiscal Q3 2026 revenue was a record $1.67B (+37% YoY), backlog reached $8.5B, and FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to $6.42B (~35% growth), with FY2027 guided to at least $8.3B [1][2]. Under CEO Gary Smith (roughly 25 years as CEO), Ciena is shifting R&D from residential broadband toward coherent optics and in-data-center interconnect, including pluggable optical engines from its Nubis acquisition [3][4].

Investment Thesis
Ciena is one of the clearest beneficiaries of AI network build-outs. Fiscal Q3 2026 revenue hit a record $1.67B (+37% YoY), adjusted EPS jumped to $2.11 from $0.67, and gross margin reached 45.4% [1][2]. Backlog grew $800M sequentially to $8.5B with book-to-bill well above 1, and management expects backlog above $10B by year-end, guiding FY2026 revenue to $6.42B (~35% growth) and FY2027 to at least $8.3–8.4B [1][2]. Trading at 27x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.32 and ROE of 22.4%, the valuation is reasonable for that growth [7]. Faith alignment is strong (Inspire score +49).
The risks are concentration, supply, and cyclicality. Two customers account for 41.7% of revenue, so a capex pause by one hyperscaler would hit results hard [1]. Supply constraints limit how fast the $8.5B backlog converts to revenue, and the stock fell about 10% after Q3 because Q4 guidance only matched consensus and gross margin is guided down from 45.4% [1][2]. Profit margin is only 10.9% and D/E is 107.5% [7]. Executives sold about $8.87M of stock over 90 days in 2026 with no insider buying [6]. Ciena's history includes the 2001 telecom bust, a reminder of how quickly optical demand can reverse [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Backlog conversion: backlog of $8.5B (up $800M sequentially) is expected to exceed $10B by fiscal year-end, underpinning FY2027 revenue guidance of at least $8.3–8.4B (+30%) [1][2]
  2. In-data-center expansion: pluggable optical engines from the Nubis acquisition, which cut AI interconnect power by up to 70%, open a new market beyond Ciena's traditional WAN business [4]
  3. Supply-chain relief: easing component constraints would let Ciena convert backlog faster and protect gross margins near the Q3 level of 45.4% [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration: two customers represented 41.7% of fiscal Q3 2026 revenue; a slowdown in either's AI capex would hit growth materially [1]
  2. Supply constraints and margin step-down: component shortages limit backlog conversion, and Q4 gross margin guidance steps down from 45.4%, which triggered a ~10% post-earnings drop [1][2]
  3. Cyclical optical demand and valuation: at $320 the stock trades at 72x trailing earnings; any pause in AI infrastructure spending could compress multiples, as in prior optical cycles [3][7]
