Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·Brazil
Companhia Energetica de Minas Gerais (CEMIG) is a Brazilian integrated electric utility operating across generation, transmission, distribution, and trading of electricity, with a focus on regulated distribution in the state of Minas Gerais. The company is controlled by the State of Minas Gerais (51% voting capital, 17% total capital) and also distributes natural gas. CEMIG is executing a 'Focus on Minas and Sell' strategy with a R$44 billion investment plan through 2028, extending key concessions, and expanding in Brazil's free power market. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.15B (beating the $1.88B estimate), with distribution EBITDA up 26.6% YoY. New CEO Alexandre Ramos Peixoto was appointed May 2026. Market cap approximately $6.7B.

Investment Thesis
CEMIG at 7.53x trailing earnings and 11.43x forward earnings offers deep value with a 10.85% dividend yield — among the highest yields in the global utility sector [5]. Q2 2026 revenue of $2.15B beat estimates by 14.4% with distribution EBITDA up 26.6% YoY, demonstrating strong operational momentum [1]. ROE of 15.94% exceeds the 15% target, leverage remains healthy at 2.45x, and the R$44 billion investment plan through 2028 provides multi-year growth visibility through grid modernization and concession extensions. CEMIG achieved a 30% expense reduction through IBM's agentic AI deployment, providing a structural efficiency tailwind [2]. The 'Focus on Minas and Sell' strategy has improved financial discipline, and FY2025 produced record investments (R$6.6B), recurring EBITDA of R$7.3B, and recurring net profit of R$4.2B. The potential corporatization (migration to Novo Mercado) could unlock significant governance premium if the state reduces its controlling stake [3].
The bear case centers on Brazilian political risk, currency exposure, and governance uncertainty. As a state-controlled enterprise (51% voting capital held by Minas Gerais), CEMIG is subject to political interference in leadership appointments (CEO replaced May 2026), capital allocation, and tariff negotiations [2][3]. The inclusion of CEMIG among state assets potentially transferred to the federal government to settle Minas Gerais' public debt creates existential governance uncertainty — the company's ownership structure could change dramatically for non-economic reasons. The ADR shares expose investors to BRL/USD currency risk; dividend growth YoY of -3.69% partly reflects BRL depreciation against the dollar, and the 3Y revenue growth forecast of -4.13% (per StockAnalysis) suggests analysts expect USD-denominated revenue to decline [5]. Profit margin of 10.51% reflects regulated utility economics with limited pricing power. The 68.26% payout ratio leaves less room for reinvestment or dividend increases, and only 1 consecutive year of dividend growth in USD terms raises income stability concerns despite the headline 10.85% yield. The stock's $1.95 per ADR price and 0.06 beta suggest limited institutional interest in the U.S.-listed shares.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Electric utilities are structurally immune to AI displacement. CEMIG's core business — generating, transmitting, and distributing electricity through physical infrastructure (power plants, transmission lines, substations, distribution networks) to 8.8 million customers in Minas Gerais — involves physical-world operations that AI cannot substitute [1]. The business is protected by regulated concessions granted by ANEEL (Brazil's electricity regulator), exclusive distribution territories, multi-decade concession agreements, and tariff frameworks set by government agencies. AI cannot replace power lines, transformers, or the physical delivery of electricity. If anything, AI increases electricity demand through data center proliferation and computing workloads. CEMIG is proactively using AI (IBM's agentic AI achieving 30% expense reduction) as an operational enabler rather than facing AI as a competitive threat [2]. The one-point deduction reflects long-term uncertainty about distributed energy and smart-grid technology potentially reducing centralized utility dependency. AI Opportunity (0/1): CEMIG does not generate revenue from AI products sold to external customers. AI is deployed internally through IBM's agentic AI solutions for customer service and operational efficiency, achieving a 30% expense reduction, but this is cost optimization rather than AI revenue generation [2].
