Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·Brazil
CEMIG is a Brazilian state-controlled electric utility operating in generation, transmission, distribution, and sale of energy in the state of Minas Gerais and beyond. The company operates 32 hydro plants (4,434 MW), 2 wind farms (71 MW), and 12 photovoltaic stations (169 MW), along with 365,577 miles of distribution lines [1]. Q2 2026 net operating revenue rose 3.4% YoY to R$11.16B with recurring EBITDA up 9.3% to R$2.47B, though recurring net income fell 15.6% to R$1.12B due to higher financial expenses [1]. Founded in 1952, headquartered in Belo Horizonte.
5,320 Employees ·www.cemig.com.br

Investment Thesis
CEMIG at $2.32 per ADR offers a high-yield value proposition in Brazilian regulated utilities, trading at 7.5x trailing earnings with a 6.3% dividend yield — attractive for income-focused investors willing to accept emerging market and currency risk [5]. The Q2 2026 EBITDA margin improved 1.0pp YoY to 20.1% with recurring EBITDA up 9.3% to R$2.47B, and a 6.5% tariff adjustment effective May 2026 supports near-term revenue growth [1]. The R$70B strategic investment plan through 2030 targets infrastructure modernization, and 25 consecutive years in the Dow Jones Sustainability Index reflect institutional commitment to ESG standards unusual for a state-controlled Brazilian utility [4]. The distribution segment is the primary growth driver, with Q1 2026 EBITDA up 26.6% in that business [1].
The bear case is centered on governance risk and earnings volatility. Recurring net income fell 15.6% YoY in Q2 2026 due to weaker trading results, higher financial expenses, and non-recurring items, demonstrating earnings sensitivity to Brazil's volatile interest rate environment [1]. The 79% payout ratio is elevated and leaves little buffer for reinvestment or dividend safety if earnings deteriorate further. CEO turnover driven by political interference — the governor replaced the CEO three months after appointment for political realignment reasons — creates institutional instability that undermines strategic continuity and long-term capital allocation discipline [3]. Currency risk is significant: the ADR dividend declined from $0.27 in 2024 to $0.19 in 2025 despite stable BRL dividends, reflecting BRL depreciation against USD. The stock's extremely low beta (0.055) and modest $6.6B market cap limit institutional investor interest [5].
