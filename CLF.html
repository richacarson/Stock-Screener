Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NYSE ·United States
Cleveland-Cliffs is the largest flat-rolled steel producer in North America, operating a vertically integrated portfolio spanning iron ore mining, direct reduced iron (DRI), steelmaking via blast furnaces and electric arc furnaces, and downstream finishing operations. Transformed from a pure-play iron ore miner into a vertically integrated steelmaker through acquisitions of AK Steel (2020), ArcelorMittal USA (2020), and Stelco (2024). CEO Lourenco Goncalves has led since August 2014. Q2 2026 revenue reached $5.23B (+6% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $286M, and management guided Q3 EBITDA to ~$575M (more than doubling Q2), with H2 2026 expected to be the strongest since 2021.

Investment Thesis
Cleveland-Cliffs at $11.32 and a forward P/E of 15.5 offers optionality on a steel cycle recovery now underway. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $286M tripled from Q1, and management guided Q3 EBITDA to ~$575M — more than doubling again sequentially — with H2 2026 expected to be the strongest since 2021 [1]. The PEG ratio of 0.43 signals that the earnings recovery is significantly undervalued relative to expected growth. CLF's vertically integrated model (iron ore to DRI to steel to finishing) provides cost advantages when scrap prices are elevated, and the #1 North American flat-rolled steel position gives pricing power with automotive and industrial customers. The Palantir AI partnership could drive operational efficiencies across 27 facilities, and trade policy (Section 232 tariffs) provides a structural floor for domestic steel prices [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 31 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions enables full faith-based allocation.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability and cultural risk. CLF has posted multiple consecutive quarters of net losses despite being North America's largest flat-rolled steel producer, with negative ROE of -13.9% and profit margin of -4.6% indicating the acquisition-driven growth strategy has not yet consistently delivered [4]. D/E of 132.7% reflects the leveraged balance sheet built through three debt-funded acquisitions, and interest expense consumes significant operating cash flow. CEO Goncalves' confrontational leadership creates governance risk — Glassdoor reviews describe a 'fear based culture' and 'tyrannical CEO,' and the key-man risk is elevated given his dominant role in strategy, external relations, and labor negotiations [2]. CFO insider selling of $2.9M in shares undermines confidence. Steel remains a deeply cyclical commodity business, and the revenue trajectory shows decline from the $23B 2022 peak to $18.6B in 2025. Any recession or sustained decline in automotive production would erode the recovery thesis.
