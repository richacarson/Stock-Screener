Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NYSE ·United States
Cleveland-Cliffs is the largest flat-rolled steel producer in North America, operating an integrated portfolio spanning iron ore mining, direct reduced iron (DRI), steelmaking via blast furnaces and electric arc furnaces, and downstream finishing operations. Transformed from a pure-play iron ore miner into a vertically integrated steelmaker through acquisitions of AK Steel (2020), ArcelorMittal USA (2020), and Stelco (2024). Serves automotive, infrastructure, appliance, and construction end markets. Q2 2026 revenue reached $5.23B (+6% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $286M, though net loss was $(145)M. LTM revenue approximately $19.2B.

Investment Thesis
Cleveland-Cliffs at $11.10 offers optionality on a steel cycle recovery and structural improvement in North American steel pricing. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $286M improved significantly from depressed 2025 levels, and management guides Q3 EBITDA to approximately $575M — more than doubling sequentially — as HRC prices recovered above $800/ton [1]. The Stelco acquisition adds low-cost Canadian capacity and iron ore self-sufficiency, while tariff protection under current trade policy provides a floor for domestic steel prices. CLF's vertically integrated model (iron ore to DRI to steel to finishing) gives it cost advantages that pure-play EAF competitors lack when scrap prices are elevated. The Palantir AI partnership could drive operational efficiencies across 27 facilities, and the stock trades at a compelling 0.3x LTM sales [2].
The bear case is significant: CLF has posted net losses for six consecutive quarters despite being the largest flat-rolled steel producer in North America, with negative ROE of -21.58% and profit margin of -4.56% indicating the acquisition-driven growth strategy has not yet delivered scale economies [4]. D/E of 126% reflects the leveraged balance sheet built through three major acquisitions, and interest expense consumes a material portion of operating cash flow. Forward P/E of 29.67x on negative current earnings requires a profitability recovery that remains unproven. CEO Goncalves' confrontational leadership style creates governance risk — the 'fear based culture' described by employees limits organizational adaptability, and key-man risk is elevated given his dominant role in strategy and external relations [2]. Steel remains a deeply cyclical commodity business with limited pricing power, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of -1% demonstrates that scale has not translated to growth. CFO insider selling of shares further undermines confidence in near-term outlook.
