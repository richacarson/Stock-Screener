Company Profile
Financial Services ·Closed-End Fund - Equity ·NYSE ·United States
Cornerstone Strategic Investment Fund is a closed-end equity mutual fund managed by Cornerstone Advisors, LLC, investing globally across diversified sectors in value and growth stocks. The fund pays monthly distributions with a stated distribution rate of ~21% annualized, though distributions are largely return of capital rather than investment income. As of June 2026, net assets were ~$1.99B with shares trading at a significant premium to NAV (~24% above NAV). The fund has been managed by Ralph W. Bradshaw since 2002, with Joshua G. Bradshaw taking over as CEO of Cornerstone Advisors in January 2025. Market cap ~$1.68B.

Investment Thesis
CLM offers a 21.66% stated distribution yield for income-focused investors who prioritize current cash flow over total return, with 38+ years of continuous monthly distributions providing a long payment history [1]. The fund is managed by the Bradshaw family with 20+ years of portfolio management continuity, providing institutional stability. The diversified equity portfolio provides broad market exposure across global equities. Net assets of ~$1.99B and daily dollar volume of ~$6.2M provide adequate liquidity. The closed-end fund structure allows the fund to remain fully invested without redemption risk, a structural advantage over open-end funds during market dislocations.
The bear case is compelling and fundamental. The 21% distribution rate is largely funded by return of capital — shareholders are receiving their own money back, not investment income — which erodes NAV over time and represents a wealth-destructive distribution policy [1]. The fund trades at a ~24% premium to NAV as of June 2026, meaning investors pay $1.24 for every $1.00 of underlying assets, creating an immediate 19% potential loss if the premium normalizes. The 1.16% expense ratio on a fund that essentially holds diversified equities provides questionable value relative to index ETFs charging 0.03-0.10%. The distribution has been declining over time (0 consecutive years of growth, negative 5-year CAGR), and rights offerings have been used to raise additional capital — diluting existing shareholders. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects the fund's lack of any specific ESG characteristics, positive or negative. For investors seeking equity income, dividend-growth ETFs or individual dividend aristocrats provide superior total return with lower fees and no NAV premium risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. Equity market rally could increase NAV and support the distribution rate, though any gains would be diluted by the 24% premium investors pay above underlying asset value [1]
  2. If the fund reduces its distribution rate to a sustainable level, the NAV erosion would slow and the fund could transition toward a more durable income model — though this would likely compress the premium
  3. New CEO Joshua Bradshaw (January 2025) could introduce strategic changes to the fund's approach, including potential adoption of covered call strategies or other income-enhancing techniques [1]
Key Risks
  1. Distribution is largely return of capital, not investment income — shareholders receive their own money back while NAV erodes, creating a wealth-destructive illusion of high yield [1]
  2. 24% premium to NAV means investors pay $1.24 for every $1.00 of underlying assets — any premium compression (market correction, distribution cut, rights offering) could produce losses of 19%+ independent of market performance [1]
  3. 1.16% expense ratio provides questionable value versus index ETFs at 0.03-0.10%, especially given the fund's diversified equity strategy that does not demonstrate alpha generation or unique portfolio construction [1]
