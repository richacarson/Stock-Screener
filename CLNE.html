Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NASDAQ ·United States
Clean Energy Fuels Corp. is the largest provider of renewable natural gas (RNG) fuel for the transportation market in North America. The company operates 600+ fueling stations, supplies RNG, CNG, and LNG for medium and heavy-duty vehicle fleets, and invests in dairy farm RNG production through partnerships with TotalEnergies and BP. Co-founded by T. Boone Pickens in 1997, headquartered in Newport Beach, California.

Investment Thesis
Clean Energy Fuels is the dominant RNG provider for North American transportation with 600+ fueling stations and growing production capacity through dairy farm partnerships with TotalEnergies and BP. Q1 2026 delivered $117.6M revenue (up 13.3% YoY), beating analyst estimates with EPS of -$0.01 vs. expected -$0.03 and adjusted EBITDA of $16.6M (14.1% margin) [1]. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance was raised to $456M, and losses are narrowing dramatically — Q1 net loss improved from $(135)M to $(12.4)M year-over-year. The RNG value proposition benefits from LCFS credits, EPA's Renewable Fuel Standard, and fleet operators' need to meet emissions mandates without the capital cost of full EV fleet conversion.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability and existential technology risk. At -22.7% profit margin and -17.2% ROE, Clean Energy has never achieved sustained GAAP profitability despite $425M+ in annual revenue [2]. The Altman Z-Score of -0.52 signals elevated bankruptcy risk. The rapid adoption of battery-electric trucks by major fleet operators (Amazon, FedEx, UPS) threatens to strand CLNE's fueling infrastructure as a transitional technology. The stock trades at $1.86 (down 40% from its 52-week high of $3.11), reflecting market skepticism that RNG can compete long-term against electrification [2]. Revenue was essentially flat at ~$420M for 2022-2025 before Q1 2026's acceleration, and the company's dependence on government credits (LCFS, RINs) exposes revenue to regulatory policy risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to $456M with narrowing net loss guidance of $(66)M-$(71)M, representing an accelerating path toward profitability with Q1 EBITDA already at $16.6M [1]
  2. Upstream RNG production capacity expansion through dairy farm partnerships with TotalEnergies and BP, shifting revenue mix toward higher-margin owned production versus third-party sourced fuel
  3. Q1 2026 revenue growth accelerating to 13.3% YoY after four years of ~2% growth, with upcoming Q2 earnings on August 6 potentially confirming the inflection [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent GAAP unprofitability with Altman Z-Score of -0.52 and -22.7% profit margin — the company has never achieved sustained profitability despite 27 years of operations [2]
  2. Battery-electric truck adoption by major fleet operators could strand CLNE's 600+ fueling station network as a transitional technology, particularly as BEV total cost of ownership declines for long-haul applications
  3. Heavy reliance on regulatory credits (LCFS, RINs) exposes revenue to policy risk — any reduction in credit values or program scope under changing political administrations could materially impact economics [1]
