Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYSE ·Canada
Celestica is a Toronto-headquartered design, manufacturing, hardware platform and supply chain solutions provider. It operates through two segments: Connectivity & Cloud Solutions (CCS), which designs and manufactures custom AI servers, networking switches, and storage hardware for hyperscalers, and Advanced Technology Solutions (ATS), serving aerospace & defense, industrial, capital equipment, and HealthTech end markets. Market cap $41.5B.

Investment Thesis
Celestica is one of the cleanest pure-play hyperscaler AI infrastructure beneficiaries. Q1 2026 delivered $4.05B revenue (53% YoY growth) with GAAP EPS of $1.83 (up 147%) and adjusted EPS of $2.16 (up 80%), beating analyst expectations [1][2]. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $19.0B revenue (+53%) and $10.15 adjusted EPS (+68%), confirming accelerating momentum from the AI data center buildout. CCS segment revenue surged 76% YoY as hyperscalers expanded AI/ML compute infrastructure. ROE of 52.45% and D/E of just 45% demonstrate strong financial execution alongside rapid growth. At Forward PEG of 0.79, the stock offers reasonable value relative to its growth rate despite the 123% 52-week price gain to $359.85 [5]. CEO Rob Mionis's 11-year tenure and the smooth CCS leadership transition to Steven Dorwart position the company for sustained execution.
The primary risk is AI capex cyclicality and the sustainability of 53% revenue growth rates. EMS/ODMs are leveraged plays on hyperscaler capex, and any pause in AI infrastructure spending — whether from a model-efficiency breakthrough reducing compute requirements, a Big Tech capex reset, or macroeconomic deceleration — would disproportionately impact Celestica's growth trajectory [1]. Customer concentration on a small number of hyperscalers (Meta, Google, Microsoft, Amazon) creates meaningful single-customer-loss risk. The stock has more than doubled from $175 in April to $360, pushing the trailing PE to 43.6x and Forward PE to 31.8x — premium multiples that leave limited margin of safety [5]. Net insider selling of $63.2M in three months, while common for rapidly appreciating stocks, signals that management is not deploying personal capital at current prices [2]. Profit margins of 6.95% remain structurally lower than pure-play OEMs, constraining earnings leverage relative to revenue growth.
