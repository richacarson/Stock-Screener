Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYQ ·United Kingdom
Clarivate is an information, analytics, and workflow solutions company serving academic, government, and corporate customers through proprietary databases including Web of Science (academic citation indexing), Derwent Innovation (patent intelligence), CompuMark (trademark search), and ProQuest (academic content). Under CEO Matti Shem Tov (appointed August 2024), the company is executing a Value Creation Plan focused on margin expansion and organic growth. Q1 2026 revenue was $585.5M with organic revenue up 0.6%; Q2 2026 revenue was $587.3M with organic revenue declining 1.5% and a $221.7M goodwill impairment. Full-year guidance: $2.30-2.42B revenue with recurring organic growth of 0.75-2.25% [1].

Investment Thesis
Clarivate at $1.60 (2.06x forward earnings, PEG 0.18) is a deep-value turnaround candidate with irreplaceable data assets but severe execution risk. The crown jewels — Web of Science, Derwent, and CompuMark — represent proprietary databases with multi-decade moats that AI cannot replicate, generating approximately 80% recurring revenue [1]. CEO Shem Tov's Value Creation Plan is delivering margin improvement with adjusted EBITDA margins above 41% in Q1, and the company is using excess cash flow to pay down debt ($4.26B total). At a market cap of approximately $1.0B against $2.4B in annual revenue, the stock prices in significant distress — any stabilization of organic growth (currently oscillating around flat to slightly negative) toward the guided 0.75-2.25% recurring organic growth could unlock substantial re-rating [1]. The Inspire Impact Score of 62 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions is the highest faith alignment score in this batch.
The bear case is formidable: three CEO changes in five years signal chronic strategic instability, and the 75%+ stock decline from 2021 highs reflects the market's verdict on $14B+ in acquisitions that destroyed shareholder value [1]. The Q2 2026 goodwill impairment of $221.7M pushed quarterly net loss to $268.6M, demonstrating that acquisition integration has failed to create lasting value. Organic revenue is declining (-1.5% in Q2, -0.4% in H1), and the company's AI narrative ('AI is an amplifier, not a disruption') is contradicted by the revenue trajectory and market reality — LLM-powered research tools from Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, and Elicit are actively eroding the value proposition of paid citation databases [2]. GAAP profitability remains deeply negative (-13.8% net margin) despite strong adjusted EBITDA, suggesting heavy non-cash charges from acquisition intangibles and restructuring. ROE of -7.0% with $4.26B in total debt creates a levered balance sheet vulnerable to further deterioration. The fundamental question is whether these data assets can ever grow organically under any management team, or whether AI disruption has structurally impaired the business model.
