Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Household & Personal Products ·NYSE ·United States
The Clorox Company manufactures and markets consumer and professional products globally through four segments: Health and Wellness, Household, Lifestyle, and International. Iconic brands include Clorox, Pine-Sol, Glad, Brita, Burt's Bees, Kingsford, Hidden Valley, and Fresh Step. CEO Linda Rendle is driving the IGNITE strategy to transform Clorox into a faster-growing, digitally enabled company with $3B in targeted cost savings. FY2026 (ending June 2026) revenue was $6.72B (-5.4% YoY) with earnings of $587M (-27.5%), as the company continues recovering from the August 2023 cyberattack while navigating inflation and private-label competition. The company is a Dividend Aristocrat with 50 consecutive years of dividend increases.

Investment Thesis
Clorox at 13.9x forward earnings and 5.57% dividend yield is a deep-value Dividend Aristocrat with 50 consecutive years of increases and dominant #1/#2 brand positions across bleach, trash bags, water filtration, and charcoal [4]. The IGNITE strategy's $3B cost savings target by FY2030 should rebuild margins from the current 11.18% profit margin, and Q3 FY2026 EPS of $1.54 (+3% YoY) demonstrates the early stages of earnings recovery [1]. The forward P/E of 13.9x is deeply discounted relative to the consumer staples sector average of ~18-20x, suggesting meaningful multiple re-rating potential if growth returns. The 113-year corporate history and category-leading brands provide defensible market positions that should persist through economic cycles.
The bear case centers on structural growth challenges and unsustainable dividend coverage. FY2026 revenue of $6.72B declined 5.4% with organic sales down 1%, and FY2027 guidance of -1.5% to +0.5% organic growth offers no near-term inflection point [2]. The payout ratio of approximately 106% means dividends exceed current earnings — mathematically unsustainable without significant earnings recovery, and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of just 2.05% barely exceeds inflation. D/E of 1958.7% ($2.7B debt on $138M equity) creates vulnerability to rising interest rates, though the ROE of 546.1% is artificially inflated by the tiny equity base from buybacks [4]. Private-label competition continues to erode branded product premiums in a price-sensitive consumer environment. The August 2023 cyberattack aftermath exposed operational fragility. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Clorox's core business — manufacturing and selling physical consumer products (bleach, trash bags, water filters, charcoal) — is fundamentally protected from AI disruption. These products require physical manufacturing, distribution infrastructure, and brand trust that AI cannot replicate [2]. Retail distribution relationships and shelf space positions create structural advantages. However, AI could enable more effective competitors in three ways: (1) DTC brands using AI-driven marketing and personalization to erode traditional brand premiums, (2) private-label manufacturers using AI to optimize formulations and reduce the quality gap with branded products, and (3) retailers deploying AI to promote own-brand alternatives more aggressively. Clorox's IGNITE digital transformation and AI-powered consumer personas are defensive investments against these threats. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI deployment for digital consumer personas, supply chain optimization, marketing targeting, and product development acceleration generates measurable efficiency gains that should support margin expansion and competitive positioning [2]. The company's scale and data advantages in consumer insights create meaningful AI upside if executed well.
