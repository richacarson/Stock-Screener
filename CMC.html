Company Profile
Industrials ·Metal Fabrication ·NYSE ·United States
Commercial Metals Company manufactures, recycles, and fabricates steel and metal products through its North America Steel Group, Europe Steel Group, and Emerging Businesses Group segments. CMC operates electric arc furnace steel mills (including next-generation micro mills with Q-One renewable energy technology), scrap metal recycling operations, and rebar fabrication facilities in the U.S., Poland, and China. The company reported Q3 FY2026 (ended May 2026) revenue of $2.48B (+22.9% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $1.73 and core EBITDA of $353.6M (+78.6% YoY), the highest in three years. Founded in 1915 and headquartered in Irving, Texas.

Investment Thesis
CMC at 8.79x forward earnings offers deep cyclical value in a steel company executing a successful earnings recovery — Q3 FY2026 revenue surged 22.9% YoY to $2.48B with core EBITDA up 78.6% to $353.6M, the highest in three years, driven by stronger metal margins, TAG efficiency gains, and precast concrete acquisition contributions [1]. The 40-year consecutive dividend track record demonstrates capital return commitment (1.24% yield with 14% payout ratio), while the Q-One micro mill technology provides a structural cost advantage through direct renewable energy integration [4]. CMC's position as a key supplier of rebar and structural steel benefits from the U.S. infrastructure spending cycle (IIJA, CHIPS Act) and data center construction boom. Analysts forecast 16.5% earnings growth and 8% revenue growth per annum, suggesting the current cyclical trough is behind the company.
The bear case centers on steel cyclicality, thin margins, and limited growth quality. CMC's 6.72% profit margin is inherently thin for a commodity steel manufacturer, and the 22.9% revenue growth in Q3 FY2026 reflects a cyclical recovery from the FY2024-2025 trough rather than structural organic growth — the estimated 5-year revenue CAGR of ~6% reflects the boom-bust pattern of steel markets [5]. The $110M litigation verdict in the Pacific Steel Group case (alleging exclusionary contract practices) creates legal and reputational overhang [4]. ROE of 13.79% falls below the 15% target, and the anomalous PEG ratio of 13.13 (likely distorted by the downcycle) suggests the market questions sustainable growth rates. The 1.24% dividend yield is below the 2% Dividend sleeve threshold, and the cyclical nature of earnings makes dividend growth unreliable. New CEO Peter Matt's finance background may optimize capital allocation but lacks the operational depth of predecessor Barbara R. Smith.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q-One micro mill technology at the Mesa, Arizona facility enabling direct renewable energy integration for structural cost advantages and sustainability credentials — providing both margin expansion and customer differentiation as construction buyers prioritize low-carbon steel [4]
  2. U.S. infrastructure spending cycle (IIJA, CHIPS Act, IRA) driving sustained demand for rebar and structural steel, with data center construction creating incremental demand for steel fabrication at premium margins [1]
  3. TAG transformation program delivering ongoing efficiency gains — core EBITDA margins expanded as the program reduces costs across CMC's manufacturing footprint while precast concrete acquisitions diversify the revenue base [1]
Key Risks
  1. Steel price cyclicality — the 22.9% revenue growth reflects cyclical recovery, not structural acceleration, and a downturn in construction activity or steel prices could compress margins from the current 6.72% toward breakeven, as seen in FY2024-2025 [1][5]
  2. $110M litigation verdict in Pacific Steel Group v. Commercial Metals Company (alleging exclusionary contract practices) creates legal expense, potential damages, and reputational risk that could affect customer relationships [4]
  3. Thin profit margins of 6.72% leave minimal buffer for input cost increases (scrap metal, energy, labor) — in a commodity business without pricing power, even small margin compression disproportionately impacts earnings [5]
