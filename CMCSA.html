Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Comcast is a global media and technology company operating through broadband/connectivity, media, studios, theme parks, and streaming (Peacock). The company completed the Versant Media Group spin-off in January 2026 and is advancing the NBCUniversal and Sky separation. Q2 2026 revenue was $29.94B (+4.7% pro forma) with Peacock reaching its first-ever quarterly profit ($189M) and 48M paid subscribers. The wireless business reached 9M total lines. CEO Brian Roberts has led the company since 2002 as part of the founding Roberts family's multi-generational stewardship. Market cap ~$85B with TTM revenue of $125.3B.

Investment Thesis
Comcast at 6.76x forward earnings is one of the most deeply undervalued large-cap dividend payers in the market, generating $19.2B in annual FCF with a 5.56% dividend yield covered at just 25% payout — leaving enormous room for continued buybacks and dividend growth (18 consecutive years of increases at 8% CAGR) [3]. Q2 2026 demonstrated the convergence thesis working: Peacock reached its first-ever quarterly profit ($189M) with 48M paid subscribers, wireless added 1.5M lines in 2025 reaching 9M total, and broadband losses are improving as simplified pricing takes hold [1][2]. The advancing NBCU/Sky separation will unlock value by allowing the market to properly value the connectivity business (broadband + wireless) separately from media/entertainment. ROE of 20.92% and $125.3B TTM revenue demonstrate the earning power of the infrastructure-heavy business model.
The bear case centers on structural broadband subscriber erosion, post-spin-off complexity, and media industry fragmentation. Broadband lost 587,000 subscribers in 2025 as 5G fixed wireless (T-Mobile, Verizon) and fiber competitors expand coverage into Comcast's footprint — a secular trend that simplified pricing may slow but likely cannot reverse [1]. Revenue growth of ~5% YoY reflects a mature business with limited organic growth levers. D/E of 107% is manageable but above target, limiting acquisition flexibility. The NBCU/Sky separation adds complexity and transition costs while removing diversification. Content investment in Peacock, NBA rights, and Epic Universe totals billions with uncertain returns in an increasingly fragmented media landscape. The Inspire Impact Score of -83 (MISALIGNED) reflects content-related controversies (Abortion Activism, LGBT Activism, Sexually Explicit attributions from NBCUniversal properties) that may limit allocation for faith-based investors. The 5-year revenue CAGR of just 4% and declining EPS from the Versant spin-off create a challenging growth narrative despite the deep value proposition.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Comcast's core broadband infrastructure — physical last-mile connections serving 32M+ subscribers — has meaningful structural moats against AI disruption through switching costs, embedded customer relationships, and physical network assets that AI cannot replicate or displace [1]. Theme parks (Universal/Epic Universe) are physical-world experiences immune to AI substitution. However, the media and studios operations (NBCUniversal, Peacock) face real AI exposure: AI-generated content could reduce production differentiation, AI-powered cord-cutting tools accelerate subscriber losses, and AI-targeted digital advertising competes with traditional media ad revenue. The advancing NBCUniversal/Sky separation will de-risk the remaining connectivity-focused entity. Analysts view Comcast's broadband and theme park infrastructure as defensible but flagged media operations as exposed to AI disruption. AI Opportunity (0/1): Comcast uses AI internally for network optimization, content personalization, and customer service, but does not generate quantifiable revenue from AI-specific products or services.
