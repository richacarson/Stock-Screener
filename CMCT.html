Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NMS ·United States
Creative Media & Community Trust is a small-cap REIT that owns, operates, and develops multifamily residential and creative office properties primarily in select U.S. markets. Led by CEO David Thompson, the company is transitioning its portfolio from office-heavy to multifamily-focused, with same-store multifamily occupancy reaching 95.3% and NOI growing 238% YoY in Q2 2026. Office occupancy is 72.3% and hotel RevPAR reached $180.47. Q2 2026 total revenue was essentially flat at $29.7M with a net loss of $11.0M, narrowing from $14.3M loss a year earlier. Market cap is approximately $14.4M after reverse stock splits and $243M preferred stock redemption into common.

Investment Thesis
CMCT at $4.48 with a micro-cap market capitalization of just $14.4M is a deep value turnaround bet on a REIT successfully pivoting from distressed office to performing multifamily. Q2 2026 same-store multifamily NOI surged 238% YoY with 95.3% occupancy, hotel RevPAR improved 8.2% to $180.47, and the net loss narrowed to $11.0M from $14.3M [1]. The $243M preferred stock redemption into common should improve FFO going forward, and CEO Thompson cited a 69% increase in leasing activity through the first 9 months of 2025 [2]. If the multifamily conversion succeeds and office occupancy (currently 72.3%) stabilizes, the deeply depressed valuation offers asymmetric upside.
The bear case is existential for a $14.4M market cap REIT burning cash. Profit margin is -39.3%, ROE is -15.6%, and D/E stands at 202.8%, reflecting an overleveraged balance sheet funding money-losing operations [1]. Revenue declined 9.5% YoY with flat Q2 results despite operational improvements, and analyst consensus expects Q3 revenue to drop sharply to $21.2M [1]. Office occupancy of 72.3% remains well below breakeven in a secular office decline, and the company has limited financial flexibility to fund the multifamily conversion given its micro-cap size and recurring losses. Reverse stock splits and preferred-to-common conversions have destroyed existing shareholder value through dilution. Daily trading volume of ~$2.5M creates significant liquidity risk for institutional investors, and the company's micro-cap status limits analyst coverage and institutional interest [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Multifamily portfolio reaching critical mass — 95.3% same-store occupancy and 238% NOI growth demonstrate the conversion strategy is working, and further office-to-multifamily conversions could accelerate the transformation [1]
  2. FFO improvement from preferred stock elimination — the $243M preferred stock redemption into common removes preferred dividend obligations, potentially pushing the company toward FFO breakeven [1]
  3. Potential asset sales or strategic alternatives — management is evaluating asset sales that could unlock value, reduce debt, and refocus the portfolio on performing multifamily assets [1]
Key Risks
  1. Existential financial risk — $14.4M market cap with -39.3% profit margin, -15.6% ROE, and 202.8% D/E ratio creates genuine going-concern risk if the turnaround does not materialize within the funding runway [1]
  2. Office secular decline — 72.3% office occupancy in a market where remote/hybrid work continues to reduce demand for traditional office space, with limited capital to reposition or sell impaired properties [1]
  3. Liquidity and dilution risk — micro-cap status with ~$2.5M daily dollar volume limits investor base, and the preferred-to-common conversion has already diluted existing shareholders, with further capital needs likely requiring additional dilutive transactions [1]
