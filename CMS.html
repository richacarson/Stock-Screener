Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
CMS Energy is a Michigan utility holding company whose principal subsidiary, Consumers Energy, provides regulated electric and natural gas service across Michigan; NorthStar Clean Energy holds its non-utility generation business. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS was $1.44 (vs $1.23 consensus) while GAAP EPS fell to $0.39 on NorthStar impairment and restructuring charges; 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $3.83–3.90 (targeting the high end), 2027 guidance of $4.08–4.17 and 6–8% long-term growth were reaffirmed [1]. Consumers has a signed 1 GW hyperscale data-center agreement within a ~9 GW pipeline [4]. CEO Garrick Rochow has led since 2020.

Investment Thesis
CMS is a premium regulated utility with visible growth: Q3 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.44 beat the $1.23 consensus, 2026 guidance of $3.83–3.90 (targeting the high end) and 2027 guidance of $4.08–4.17 were reaffirmed, and long-term 6–8% EPS growth is supported by rate-base investment [1]. A 1 GW hyperscale data-center contract and ~9 GW pipeline offer upside not yet in the capital plan [1][4]. The 3.22% yield has grown for 19 straight years, at a 5.9% 5-year CAGR, and the stock trades at 17.1x forward earnings [2]. The gas rate case was approved with a 9.8% ROE [1].
The bear case is regulatory and balance-sheet risk. The gas rate order granted only ~60% of the request ($139M), and the $443M electric case (10.25% ROE requested) is not decided until April 2027 [1]. D/E of 196% makes earnings sensitive to rates, and GAAP ROE of 5.3% and margin of 9.7% were hit by NorthStar impairments, lifting the GAAP payout ratio to 80% [2]. Data-center load is delayed by local zoning moratoriums, and a Yahoo PEG of 2.53 leaves limited valuation upside [1][2]. Inspire Impact Score of 53 (ALIGNED).
Key Catalysts
  1. Signing and siting the 1 GW hyperscale data center and adding data-center load from the ~9 GW pipeline to the capital plan after the midterm elections [1][4]
  2. Michigan PSC electric rate order (requested $443M at a 10.25% ROE) expected by early April 2027 [1]
  3. Delivering 2026 adjusted EPS at the high end of $3.83–3.90 and resetting NorthStar after the Q3 impairments [1]
Key Risks
  1. Adverse Michigan PSC rate outcomes — the gas case awarded ~60% of the request — limiting recovery of a large capital plan [1]
  2. Data-center projects stalled by local zoning moratoriums and political opposition, delaying load growth [1][4]
  3. High leverage (D/E 196%) and interest-rate sensitivity, with further NorthStar write-downs possible [1][2]
