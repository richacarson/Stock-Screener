Company Profile
Healthcare ·Healthcare Plans ·NYSE ·United States
Centene is the largest Medicaid managed care organization in the United States, providing healthcare plans to government-sponsored and commercially insured individuals. The company serves 25.9 million members across Medicaid (12.1M), Medicare PDP (8.8M), and ACA Marketplace (3.5M) segments. Q2 2026 revenue reached $53.6B (+10% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $2.51, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $193.5B-$197.5B revenue with adjusted EPS above $4.80. Led by CEO Sarah London since March 2022, the company recently appointed former Fannie Mae CIO Bradley Bolivar to lead its AI and digital transformation.

Investment Thesis
Centene at $70.51 with a $34.8B market cap is the nation's largest Medicaid managed care organization executing a strong earnings recovery — Q2 2026 revenue reached $53.6B (+10% YoY) with $1.1B profit and adjusted EPS of $2.51, management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $193.5B-$197.5B revenue with adjusted EPS above $4.80, and the Medicaid medical loss ratio improved to 93.9% [1][3]. At 13.18x forward earnings, the stock trades at a significant discount to healthcare plan peers, reflecting concerns about Medicaid membership declines (expected -8% to -9% in 2026) that may be overly punitive given the company's diversification into Medicare PDP (8.8M members) and ACA Marketplace. The appointment of an AI-focused CIO from Fannie Mae signals commitment to operational modernization [2].
The bear case centers on structural membership headwinds and governance concerns. Medicaid membership is declining faster than expected (-8% to -9% vs prior -6% guidance), and ACA Marketplace enrollment fell to 3.5M from 5.9M a year earlier, meaning total membership dropped 7.6% YoY to 25.9M [3]. The trailing GAAP profit margin of -2.8% and ROE of -20.4% indicate significant charges that distort reported profitability. More fundamentally, the $939M+ in Medicaid pharmacy overbilling settlements across 17+ states — the largest being $215M with California — raises serious governance questions about whether Centene's billing practices reflect systemic issues rather than isolated errors [5]. Regulatory scrutiny of managed care organizations continues to intensify, and the company's heavy dependence on government-sponsored programs (Medicaid, Medicare, ACA) exposes it to policy changes, rate adequacy decisions, and political risk from both parties [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Earnings recovery and guidance raises — adjusted EPS guidance above $4.80 (raised from $3.40) implies significant margin improvement as medical loss ratios normalize and membership stabilizes [1][3]
  2. AI and digital transformation under new CIO Bradley Bolivar — AI-driven claims automation and care management optimization could drive material cost savings across the company's 25.9M-member base [2]
  3. Medicare PDP membership growth to 8.8M members diversifies revenue beyond declining Medicaid, with Medicare Advantage expansion offering additional growth runway [3]
Key Risks
  1. Accelerating Medicaid membership decline — full-year 2026 expected -8% to -9% (vs prior -6%), driven by state redeterminations and eligibility verifications that could continue removing members from rolls [3]
  2. Governance and litigation overhang — $939M+ in Medicaid pharmacy overbilling settlements across 17+ states, with additional state investigations potentially pending, creates ongoing legal liability and reputational risk [5]
  3. Government program dependence — virtually all revenue derives from Medicaid, Medicare, and ACA programs subject to political risk, rate adequacy decisions, and regulatory changes that Centene cannot control [1][4]
