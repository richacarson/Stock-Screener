Company Profile
Healthcare ·Healthcare Plans ·NYSE ·United States
Centene Corporation is the largest Medicaid managed-care organization in the United States, also serving members through Medicare and ACA Marketplace plans. Under CEO Sarah London, Centene has refocused on its three core lines of business (Medicaid, Medicare, Marketplace), exited non-core operations, and driven a 'One CenTeam' cultural unification across roughly 60,000 employees. Q2 2026 results showed a sharp turnaround with adjusted EPS of $2.51 (vs. $1.10 consensus), revenue of $53.58B (+9.9% YoY), and medical loss ratio improving to 89.6% from 93% a year earlier [1]. Full-year 2026 EPS guidance was raised to greater than $4.80.

Investment Thesis
Centene delivered a decisive Q2 2026 turnaround, with adjusted EPS of $2.51 crushing the $1.10 consensus, revenue up 9.9% YoY to $53.58B, and medical loss ratio improving to 89.6% from 93% a year earlier as 2025's Medicaid repricing pain works through the model [1]. Full-year 2026 EPS guidance was raised to greater than $4.80 (from $3.40+), and the stock trades at a cheap 13.91x forward earnings and 0.36 PEG, suggesting the market has not fully priced in the margin recovery. As the largest Medicaid managed-care insurer with ~60,000 employees, Centene retains structural scale advantages and AI-enabled risk stratification (HALO) that could sustain the improved cost trend.
The bear case centers on trust and governance overhang. Centene has settled Medicaid pharmacy-benefit overbilling allegations with at least 20 states, set aside a $1.25B reserve, and continues litigating related disputes as of April 2026 — a pattern that materially undercuts any 'transforming community health' Just Cause narrative and creates ongoing regulatory/political risk given Centene's total dependence on government contracts [3]. TTM ROE of -20.4% and profit margin of -2.83% reflect a company still digesting losses, Medicaid funding and eligibility redetermination policy remains a persistent macro risk, and a Glassdoor review warning about senior leadership points to unresolved internal culture issues. This is a WATCH: the turnaround is real and the valuation is cheap, but governance and mission-execution risk keep it below a BUY.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained medical loss ratio improvement toward the 90.5%-91.3% full-year 2026 guide would validate the margin-recovery thesis and support the raised EPS guidance of >$4.80 [1]
  2. Successful resolution of remaining state Medicaid pharmacy-benefit settlement negotiations would remove a persistent governance overhang on the stock [3]
  3. Continued scaling of AI-enabled risk stratification (HALO) and fraud/payment-integrity tools could structurally lower the cost trend beyond the current guidance range [2]
Key Risks
  1. Medicaid eligibility redetermination and state-level funding pressure remain a structural risk given Centene's near-total dependence on government-sponsored health programs [1]
  2. Ongoing Medicaid pharmacy-benefit litigation and unresolved state settlements (20+ states involved since 2021) create continued regulatory, reputational, and financial overhang [3]
  3. Negative internal culture signals (Glassdoor senior-leadership warnings) alongside a still-negative TTM ROE of -20.4% suggest the turnaround is not yet fully de-risked operationally or culturally [2]
