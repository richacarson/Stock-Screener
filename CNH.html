Company Profile
Industrials ·Farm & Heavy Construction Machinery ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
CNH Industrial N.V. designs, manufactures, and distributes agricultural and construction equipment under iconic brands including Case IH, New Holland Agriculture, and CASE Construction Equipment. Created as a pure-play ag/construction company after spinning off Iveco Group in January 2022, CNH is executing its 'Breaking New Ground' strategy combining AI, autonomy, and precision technology with legacy heavy equipment. CEO Gerrit Marx (appointed July 2024, previously CEO of Iveco Group) has designated agriculture as the 'primary focus' and restructured the leadership team. Q2 2026 revenue was $4.8B (+2% YoY), beating estimates and triggering a 16% stock jump. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance narrowed to $0.41-$0.46.

Investment Thesis
CNH Industrial at 19.7x forward earnings and a PEG of 0.37 offers a high-quality agricultural equipment franchise at a cyclical trough with visible stabilization. Q2 2026 revenue of $4.8B (+2% YoY) beat estimates and triggered a 16% stock surge, with management narrowing full-year EPS guidance to $0.41-$0.46 (higher end of prior range) [1]. Construction segment sales grew 12% in Q2, signaling diversified demand recovery. CNH's precision agriculture technology stack — AI-powered autonomous equipment shipping in production, AI Tech Assistant across dealer networks, Raven Industries and Hemisphere GNSS capabilities, and a 2030 target to nearly double Precision Tech revenue — positions it to capture greater value per unit as the ag cycle turns [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 64 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions and 17 positive attributions makes CNH particularly attractive for faith-aligned portfolios seeking industrial exposure at cyclical trough valuations.
The bear case centers on the depth and duration of the agricultural downturn and elevated leverage. Trailing EPS of $0.25 produces a 53.9x P/E reflecting deeply depressed earnings, with profit margin of only 1.71% and ROE of 3.9% demonstrating near-zero operating leverage [4]. Revenue has declined from $24.7B (2023 peak) to $18.1B (FY2025), and full-year guidance implies ~$19B — still well below peak. D/E of 335% exceeds IOWN's 300% scoring floor, though this includes the captive financial services arm (industrial-only leverage is much lower). Competitor John Deere invests more aggressively in autonomous and electric equipment, AI-powered agronomic platforms, and precision agriculture software monetization, potentially widening the technology gap during the downturn [2]. The dividend has been cut significantly (from $0.47 in 2024 to an estimated $0.20 in 2026), signaling management's own caution about near-term cash generation. CEO Marx has been in the role since only July 2024 and has not yet proven the restructured organization can execute through a full cycle. Agricultural demand recovery assumed by consensus for 2027 is not guaranteed if commodity prices remain depressed.
