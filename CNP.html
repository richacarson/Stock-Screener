Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
CenterPoint Energy operates as a regulated utility holding company providing electric transmission and distribution, natural gas distribution, and electric generation services across Houston, Indiana, Minnesota, Ohio, and Texas. The company serves approximately 2.86 million metered customers and operates 355 substations with 81,692 MVA transformer capacity [4]. Q2 2026 revenue rose 10.7% YoY to approximately $2.15B with EPS of $0.40, beating consensus of $0.37 [1]. Founded in 1866, headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Investment Thesis
CenterPoint Energy at $40.55 offers a defensive regulated utility play with a 2.37% dividend yield, 64+ consecutive years of payments, and exposure to the AI-driven electricity demand supercycle through Houston-area data center load growth [4][5]. The $66.7B 10-year capital investment plan provides long-term earnings visibility through regulated rate base growth, and Q2 2026 revenue grew 10.7% YoY with EPS of $0.40 beating consensus by $0.03 [1]. Management's 2026 EPS guidance midpoint of $1.90 implies 8% growth over 2025 delivered results, with the forward P/E of 19.4x reasonable for a regulated utility with data center demand tailwinds. The low beta of 0.46 provides portfolio stability, and 4 consecutive years of dividend growth (2022-2025) signal recovery from the 2020-2021 cuts [5].
The bear case centers on regulatory risk, governance concerns, and capital structure leverage. The Texas AG investigation into CenterPoint's conduct during Hurricane Beryl remains unresolved, and ongoing legislative proposals could impose costly grid hardening mandates or penalties that reduce earned returns on equity [3]. The D/E ratio of 210% and $24.6B total debt against only $453M cash create a capital structure that is vulnerable to rising interest rates and limits financial flexibility for the $66.7B capex plan, which requires continuous capital market access. The 5-year revenue CAGR of just 0.1% reveals the utility's fundamentally low-growth nature — the 10.7% Q2 2026 YoY growth is driven by tariff adjustments and weather rather than structural demand acceleration. The 53% payout ratio leaves modest room for dividend growth, and the 3.5% 5-year dividend CAGR barely exceeds inflation after the 2020-2021 cuts reduced the base [5]. Negative free cash flow of -$5.2B reflects the massive capex program consuming all operating cash flow and then some.
