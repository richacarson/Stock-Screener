Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
CenterPoint Energy is a regulated utility holding company operating electric transmission and distribution, natural gas distribution, and energy services across Texas (Houston metro), Indiana, Ohio, Minnesota, Mississippi, Louisiana, and Oklahoma. The company serves approximately 2.9 million metered customers and is executing a decade-long $65.5 billion grid modernization plan focused on undergrounding lines, self-healing automation, and AI-driven grid management in partnership with Palantir and NVIDIA. Founded through predecessor companies dating to 1866, CenterPoint reported TTM revenue of $9.62B and guided FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $1.89-$1.91 (+8% YoY).

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 6 /10
CenterPoint Energy demonstrates infinite game qualities inherent to regulated utilities — a 150+ year operating history, genuine commitment to providing essential community infrastructure, and the willingness to commit $65.5 billion over a decade to grid modernization that will not generate returns for years [1][2]. However, recent history reveals finite-game pressures: the widely criticized Hurricane Beryl response in July 2024 that left 2.6+ million customers without power for days drew regulatory investigations and the CEO himself called the response 'inexcusable' [4]. CEO turnover (Jason Wells is the third CEO since 2019), the 2020 dividend cut, and Glassdoor reviews citing negative cultural impact from leadership changes suggest organizational instability inconsistent with infinite game principles.

Investment Thesis
CenterPoint Energy at 19.47x forward earnings offers a regulated utility with visible earnings growth — Q2 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.40 met estimates, full-year guidance of $1.89-$1.91 represents 8% growth over 2025, and management targets 7-9% annual EPS growth through 2035 [1]. The $65.5 billion decade-long grid modernization plan provides a multi-year rate base growth engine funded by regulated cost recovery, while Houston's position as a hub for energy, petrochemical, and emerging AI data center development drives organic electric load growth [2]. The dividend yield of 2.35% with 4 consecutive years of growth (CAGR ~7%) provides income recovery after the 2020 cut, and the payout ratio of 54.77% leaves room for continued increases. CenterPoint's AI partnership with Palantir and NVIDIA through Chain Reaction positions it as a technology leader among regulated utilities, potentially improving operational efficiency and storm response [2]. The stock has pulled back 13% from its June highs, creating a more attractive entry at Forward P/E below the 20x target.
The bear case centers on execution risk, leverage, and Hurricane Beryl's lingering impact. D/E of 210% and the capital-intensive $65.5 billion grid plan will require continued debt issuance and potential equity dilution, creating financing risk if interest rates remain elevated or regulators deny full rate recovery [5]. The Hurricane Beryl disaster in July 2024 — 2.6+ million customers without power, CEO calling the response 'inexcusable,' Texas Senate investigation, AG Ken Paxton inquiry — damaged regulatory and public trust in CenterPoint's primary service territory and could complicate future rate case approvals [4]. Revenue growth of 7.10% YoY and 2.9% 5yr CAGR are modest, and the PEG ratio of 2.22 suggests limited margin of safety for execution misses. CEO turnover (three CEOs since 2019) and Glassdoor reviews citing negative cultural changes under current leadership indicate organizational instability. The 70% emission reduction target by 2035 adds regulatory compliance costs. The Inspire Impact Score of 42 (ALIGNED) is positive for faith-based investors.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): CenterPoint Energy's regulated utility business is structurally immune to AI disruption — electric transmission and distribution, natural gas delivery, and physical grid infrastructure cannot be replaced by software or AI [1]. The business involves physical-world operations (power lines, substations, gas pipelines, meters) protected by regulatory monopoly franchises, rate-of-return regulation, and long-lived physical assets. AI is broadly expected to INCREASE demand for CenterPoint's core product (electricity) as data centers, AI training clusters, and hyperscale computing expand in Houston and other service territories [2]. Analysts see zero substitution risk and view CenterPoint as a net beneficiary of AI infrastructure buildout. The only modest risk is that AI-enabled distributed energy resources (rooftop solar, batteries, microgrids) could reduce grid dependency at the margin over the long term. AI Opportunity (0/1): While CenterPoint deploys AI operationally through the Palantir Chain Reaction partnership, Neara storm recovery AI, and Technosylva weather forecasting platform, these are internal efficiency tools, not revenue-generating AI products [2]. The company does not sell AI services externally.
