Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United Kingdom
Centessa Pharmaceuticals was a clinical-stage biotechnology company pioneering orexin-based neuroscience therapeutics. Its lead product candidate, cleminorexton (ORX750), is a novel oral OX2R agonist in late-stage development for narcolepsy type 1, narcolepsy type 2, and idiopathic hypersomnia. The pipeline also included ORX142 and ORX489 for broader neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric indications. Eli Lilly acquired Centessa in June 2026 for $38.00 per share in cash plus a contingent value right of up to $9.00 per share, totaling up to $7.8B in equity value. CNTA has been delisted from NASDAQ and is now a wholly-owned subsidiary of Lilly. Dr. Mario Alberto Accardi served as CEO from January 2026 until the acquisition closed.

Investment Thesis
Centessa Pharmaceuticals was acquired by Eli Lilly in June 2026 for $38.00/share in cash plus a contingent value right of up to $9.00/share (total equity value up to $7.8B), validating the cleminorexton pipeline as one of the most significant acquisitions in sleep medicine [1]. The deal demonstrated that Centessa's orexin receptor 2 agonist platform — cleminorexton for narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia, plus follow-up candidates ORX142 and ORX489 for neurodegenerative indications — was scientifically compelling enough to attract a top-5 pharma acquirer at a substantial premium. Under Lilly's ownership, cleminorexton benefits from commercial infrastructure, regulatory expertise, and balance sheet resources that Centessa could not have matched independently.
The stock is no longer tradeable. CNTA has been delisted from NASDAQ following the completed Eli Lilly acquisition, and the company is now a wholly-owned Lilly subsidiary [2]. Shareholders received $38.00/share in cash, and the CVR provides potential additional upside of up to $9.00/share tied to clinical and regulatory milestones. The IOWN framework scores presented here reflect Centessa's pre-acquisition standalone status — a clinical-stage biotech with strong pipeline innovation but no commercial infrastructure, no revenue, and no profitability. The acquisition rendered the investment thesis moot: the scientific value was recognized and acquired, but Centessa ceased to exist as an independent company. The SELL recommendation reflects the untradeable status rather than a fundamental assessment of the now-Lilly-owned pipeline.
