Company Profile
Healthcare ·Specialty Pharmaceuticals ·NASDAQ ·United States
Corcept Therapeutics Incorporated is a commercial-stage pharmaceutical company focused on discovering and developing drugs that modulate the effects of the stress hormone cortisol. The company markets Korlym (mifepristone), the only FDA-approved treatment for hyperglycemia secondary to Cushing's syndrome, and Lifyorli (relacorilant), approved by the FDA in March 2026 for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. Led by co-founder CEO Joseph Belanoff, M.D. (27-year tenure), the company has generated over $761M in annual revenue from its Cushing's franchise and holds $515M in cash. Founded in 1998, headquartered in Menlo Park, California.

Investment Thesis
Corcept Therapeutics stands at an inflection point with the March 2026 FDA approval of Lifyorli (relacorilant) for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, opening a market opportunity orders of magnitude larger than the Cushing's syndrome franchise that generates $761M in annual revenue [1]. The company has raised 2026 revenue guidance to $950M-$1,050M, implying 25-38% growth, and holds $515M in cash with virtually zero debt [1]. Co-founder CEO Dr. Belanoff's 27-year tenure pursuing cortisol modulation provides unusual strategic clarity and founder alignment [2]. The Korlym monopoly in Cushing's syndrome provides a durable revenue base while Lifyorli ramps. Revenue 5-year CAGR of 16.6% demonstrates consistent growth acceleration.
The bear case centers on valuation, legal risk, and clinical uncertainty. At 112x forward earnings and PEG of 6.39, CORT is priced for near-perfect Lifyorli execution with zero margin of safety — any commercial disappointment could trigger severe multiple compression [3]. The Q1 2026 net loss of $31.8M (vs. $20.5M profit a year earlier) reflects heavy Lifyorli launch spending that may persist through 2026, and the TTM profit margin of 6.2% is thin [3]. The company faces multiple legal headwinds: a qui tam whistleblower lawsuit alleging off-label promotion and kickback practices (DOJ declined to intervene but the case continues), securities class actions following the relacorilant CRL rejection, and the Teva patent challenge to Korlym exclusivity [6]. Heavy insider selling by the CFO ($3.25M), CDO ($1.86M), and director trust undermines the founder narrative. The Inspire Impact score of -62 (MISALIGNED) due to abortifacient classification of mifepristone (Korlym's active ingredient) presents values-alignment risk for faith-based investors.
