Company Profile
Technology ·Capital Markets ·NMS ·United States
Core Scientific is one of North America's largest digital infrastructure and AI data center operators, transitioning from bitcoin mining to high-density colocation for AI and high-performance computing workloads. The company emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2024 and has rapidly repositioned as a leading AI infrastructure provider. Q2 2026 revenue was $164.2M (up 109% YoY), with colocation revenue surging to $136.7M from just $10.6M a year earlier, while self-mining revenue declined to $21.5M from $62.4M. The company has 1.1GW of total contracted capacity with $24B in base contracted revenue, anchored by a 15-year, $14B+ partnership with AMD for 530MW. Led by CEO Adam Sullivan since August 2023.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)

Core Scientific demonstrates a mixed but predominantly finite orientation. The company's dramatic pivot from bankrupt bitcoin miner to AI infrastructure leader shows exceptional existential flexibility — exactly the kind of transformation Sinek celebrates. The addition of Equinix's former CEO and Penguin Solutions' former CEO to the board suggests a learning-from-rivals mindset. However, the transformation was driven by financial necessity rather than a Just Cause, the post-bankruptcy culture is still stabilizing, and the company's current leadership model prioritizes aggressive growth and contracted revenue accumulation over purpose-driven organizational building.

Investment Thesis
Core Scientific at $15.36 with a $4.9B market cap represents one of the purest plays on AI infrastructure buildout. The company's transformation from bankrupt bitcoin miner to leading AI data center operator is backed by $24B in base contracted revenue across 1.1GW of capacity, anchored by a 15-year, $14B+ partnership with AMD for 530MW [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue of $164.2M doubled YoY as colocation revenue surged to $136.7M from $10.6M, and adjusted EBITDA improved to $41.1M from $8.9M in Q1, demonstrating the operating leverage as capacity comes online [1]. The board additions of Equinix's former CEO and Penguin Solutions' former CEO bring world-class data center and AI infrastructure expertise. With contracted capacity stretching into the early 2040s and a perfect AI Resilience score of 10, Core Scientific is positioned as critical infrastructure for the AI revolution.
The bear case is severe on financial fundamentals. The company reported a $1.15B GAAP net loss in Q2 2026 (primarily from warrant revaluation), negative shareholders' equity of approximately -$2.4B, debt of $4.3B, deeply negative free cash flow (-$583.9M in Q2), and capital expenditures of $797.5M in a single quarter [1]. The infrastructure score of 3 reflects a balance sheet under extreme stress — the company is essentially betting its financial viability on the AI buildout delivering returns before the capital structure becomes untenable. Customer concentration is severe: the AMD contract represents the majority of contracted capacity, creating binary risk if AMD's AI strategy shifts or if the partnership is renegotiated. CEO compensation of $41.94M while the company is unprofitable raises governance concerns [3]. The stock has fallen from $18.24 (50-day SMA) to $15.36, reflecting investor uncertainty about whether the capital-intensive transition can generate the returns needed to service $4.3B in debt and justify a $4.9B market cap. The company's cryptocurrency mining legacy and recent bankruptcy create reputational overhang with institutional investors.
