Core Scientific, Inc. provides high-density colocation services and digital asset mining infrastructure in the United States. The company is executing a dramatic pivot from Bitcoin mining to AI data center infrastructure, with $24B+ in total contracted revenue through partnerships with CoreWeave and AMD. In Q2 2026, revenue surged 109% YoY to $164M, and the company announced a landmark $14B+ AMD partnership for up to 2.5 GW of AI-ready data center capacity. Core Scientific shareholders rejected a $9B CoreWeave acquisition in late 2025, choosing to remain independent.

Investment Thesis
Core Scientific reported Q2 2026 revenue of $164M (up 109% YoY) with colocation revenue reaching $137M as its AI infrastructure pivot accelerates [1]. The company secured a transformative $14B+ AMD partnership for up to 2.5 GW of AI-ready data center capacity over 15 years with 2.5% annual escalators, following the shareholder rejection of a $9B CoreWeave acquisition — total contracted revenue now exceeds $24B across AMD and CoreWeave [2]. Adjusted EBITDA improved to $41.1M from $8.9M sequentially, demonstrating operational leverage as capacity scales. The combination of 1.1 GW contracted capacity, long-term take-or-pay contracts, and the structural undersupply of AI data center capacity positions Core Scientific as one of the most valuable independent AI infrastructure operators in North America. The Inspire Impact Score of 46 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based investor allocation.
The bear case centers on massive financial losses and leveraged balance sheet. Core Scientific posted a $1.15B net loss in Q2 2026 (primarily $1.1B+ non-cash warrant revaluation) and carries $4.41B in debt against $1.77B cash [1][4]. Forward P/E of 166.67x reflects extreme pre-profitability valuation — GF Value of $11.17 vs. $19.78 stock price implies 77% overvaluation [4]. The Chapter 11 bankruptcy in December 2022 is a governance red flag. Revenue growth 5yr CAGR of -20.6% captures the volatile mining downturn. The company's heavy concentration with CoreWeave and AMD as dominant customers creates counterparty risk, and the broader AI infrastructure buildout faces potential risks from energy cost inflation, regulatory constraints on data center expansion, and the possibility that AI capex spending proves cyclical rather than secular [3]. The warrant structure from the AMD deal introduces additional shareholder dilution risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. AMD partnership ramping from initial 500+ MW deployment starting 2027 toward 2.5 GW total capacity, with each MW of AI colocation generating significantly higher revenue per MW than Bitcoin mining — $14B+ base contracted revenue over 15 years [2]
  2. Adjusted EBITDA inflection from $8.9M to $41.1M sequentially in Q2 2026, with continued margin expansion as contracted capacity comes online and colocation revenue scales toward $500M+ annually [1]
  3. Continued structural undersupply of AI data center capacity globally, with Core Scientific's permitted and energized sites providing a speed-to-market advantage that hyperscalers and new entrants cannot easily replicate [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Net loss of $1.15B in Q2 2026 and $4.41B debt load against $1.77B cash demonstrate extreme financial leverage — any delay in capacity deployment or customer drawdown could strain debt covenants and liquidity [1][4]
  2. Chapter 11 bankruptcy in December 2022 is a governance red flag, and the AMD warrant structure introduces potential shareholder dilution if AMD exercises options at market prices [2]
  3. Heavy revenue concentration with CoreWeave and AMD as the two dominant customers creates dual-counterparty risk — any financial distress, strategic pivot, or contract renegotiation by either partner would severely impact the revenue outlook [1][2]
