Company Profile
Consumer Staples ·Personal Products ·NYSE ·United States
Coty is a global beauty company with prestige and consumer beauty brands spanning fragrance, color cosmetics, and skin care. Key brands include Burberry, Calvin Klein, Hugo Boss, and Marc Jacobs (Prestige, ~66% of revenue), plus CoverGirl, Sally Hansen, and Rimmel (Consumer Beauty). FY2026 (ending June 2026) revenue was $5.81B, declining 2% reported and 5% like-for-like, as the company navigated a CEO transition — Sue Nabi departed and Markus Strobel (former P&G executive) became Interim CEO and Executive Chairman effective January 2026 [1]. The company is executing its 'Coty.Curated' turnaround strategy focused on fewer, bigger, better initiatives. Multiple securities class action lawsuits have been filed alleging misstatements about segment trends and profitability [2].

Investment Thesis
Coty at $2.50 and 5.96x forward earnings is a deeply distressed turnaround play in the global beauty market, with the investment case resting on whether Interim CEO Strobel's Coty.Curated strategy can stabilize revenue and restore profitability. The prestige fragrance portfolio (Burberry, Calvin Klein, Hugo Boss, Marc Jacobs) at 66% of revenue provides brand equity that is difficult to replicate, and operating cash flow of $538M / free cash flow of $348M demonstrate the business generates meaningful cash despite GAAP losses [1]. The Sustainalytics ESG rating upgrade to 18.1 (leading global beauty) and MSCI upgrade to A reflect genuine sustainability progress [3]. At a market cap of ~$2.2B against $5.8B in revenue, the stock is priced for significant distress — any stabilization of Consumer Beauty or margin improvement could drive meaningful re-rating.
The bear case dominates: FY2026 revenue declined 5% like-for-like, the company reported a $545.8M net loss, and shares fell 17% after Q4 results as management described FY2027 as a 'transition year' with no full-year guidance [1]. The CEO departure, securities class action lawsuits, and appointment of an interim CEO signal governance dysfunction that may take years to resolve. Coty has cycled through multiple turnaround narratives without sustained execution — the Coty.Curated strategy may be another chapter in this pattern. D/E of 98% with $3.43B in debt creates financial fragility during a revenue decline, and the $257M cash position provides limited cushion [4]. Consumer Beauty continues losing market share with no clear path to stabilization. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) restricts allocation for faith-based investors. FY2027 guidance is limited to Q1 and first-half free cash flow only, suggesting management lacks visibility into the recovery trajectory.

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Coty's core business — manufacturing and marketing physical beauty products (fragrances, cosmetics, skincare) — is structurally protected from AI disruption. AI cannot replace the sensory experience of fragrance, the physical application of cosmetics, or the emotional brand connections that drive beauty purchases [1]. The beauty industry's moats are brand equity, distribution relationships, and formulation expertise — none of which AI can easily replicate. However, AI does create competitive pressure in digital marketing (AI-generated content reducing Coty's marketing efficiency advantage), virtual try-on technology (enabling DTC brands to compete without physical retail), and consumer insights (leveling the data advantage large beauty companies held). The company acknowledges this by incorporating AI into its Coty.Curated strategy for efficiencies and consumer engagement [2]. AI Opportunity (0/1): No quantifiable AI revenue. The company discusses AI-driven operational efficiencies and data-driven consumer engagement, but these are cost-saving measures, not revenue-generating AI products [2].
