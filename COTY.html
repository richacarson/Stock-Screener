Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Household & Personal Products ·NYSE ·United States
Coty Inc. manufactures and markets beauty products worldwide across Prestige and Consumer Beauty segments, with brands including Burberry, Calvin Klein, Hugo Boss, CoverGirl, Max Factor, and Rimmel. The company is the world's largest fragrance company by revenue. FY2026 (ended June 2026) revenue was $5.81B, down 2% YoY on a reported basis and 5% like-for-like, with a net loss reflecting negative 10.4% profit margin. Headquartered in New York, Coty is conducting a strategic review of its Consumer Beauty division and faces the exit of its Gucci fragrance license in FY2028.

Three CEO changes in two years, a strategic review that signals confusion about which businesses to keep, the loss of the Gucci fragrance license, and a declining LFL revenue trajectory paint a picture of a company without a clear vision of its future. There is no evidence of a Just Cause that has been validated through consistent execution, no pattern of courageous leadership decisions, and organizational instability has eroded trust. The strategic review of Consumer Beauty could represent existential flexibility, but it reads more as defensive restructuring than purpose-driven reinvention.

Investment Thesis
Coty at $2.92 with a $2.57B market cap trades at just 12.0x forward earnings, pricing in significant pessimism about the company's turnaround prospects. FY2026 generated $5.81B in revenue with $538M operating cash flow and $348M free cash flow despite the top-line decline, suggesting the business still generates cash even in a downturn [1]. New Interim CEO Markus Strobel brings 33 years of P&G experience in global skin and personal care, and the strategic review of Consumer Beauty could unlock value through divestiture or refocused investment [3][4]. The Prestige segment (Burberry, Calvin Klein, Hugo Boss fragrances) remains the company's strength, and a leaner, prestige-focused Coty could command a higher multiple.
The bear case is severe and multifaceted. Coty is unprofitable on a GAAP basis with a -10.4% profit margin and -16.1% ROE, meaning the company is destroying equity value [1]. Three CEO changes in two years signals deep organizational dysfunction, and Interim CEO Strobel's permanent appointment is uncertain [3]. FY2026 LFL revenue declined 5%, with the core business shrinking even before the Gucci license exit, which will remove a material revenue stream starting FY2028 [1][4]. D/E of 97.5% adds financial risk to an already challenged operating profile. Management guided FY2027 Q1 revenue to decline by a low-to-mid single-digit percentage, with FY2027 described as a 'transition year' — code for continued challenges [4]. Competitors L'Oreal and Estee Lauder are investing heavily in AI-driven beauty technology and digital experiences while Coty has no visible AI strategy, risking further share loss in a digitally evolving market.
Key Catalysts
  1. Strategic review of Consumer Beauty division (~40% of revenue) could result in a value-unlocking divestiture, allowing Coty to become a leaner, pure-play prestige fragrance company with higher margins [4]
  2. Markus Strobel's 33-year P&G leadership background brings operational discipline and stability to a company that desperately needs both — if appointed permanently, he could arrest the leadership instability [3]
  3. Forward P/E of 12.0x on $348M FCF suggests significant pessimism already priced in — any stabilization in LFL trends or successful Consumer Beauty transaction could drive meaningful rerating [1]
Key Risks
  1. Gucci fragrance license exit in FY2028 removes a material prestige revenue stream with no announced replacement, shrinking the company's most valuable segment at a time when it can least afford it [4]
  2. Three CEO changes in two years and GAAP losses suggest a company in structural decline — -10.4% profit margin and -16.1% ROE mean the turnaround has not yet begun despite years of restructuring efforts [1][3]
  3. Competitive gap widening as L'Oreal and Estee Lauder invest in AI-powered beauty tech, digital personalization, and DTC channels while Coty has no visible technology or innovation strategy to maintain relevance [3]
