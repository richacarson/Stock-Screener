Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Education & Training Services ·NYSE ·United States
Coursera operates the world's largest online learning platform following its May 2026 acquisition of Udemy, offering guided projects, courses, specializations, professional certificates, and bachelor's/master's degrees through partnerships with 300+ universities and industry partners. Q2 2026 revenue reached $299M (+60% YoY including Udemy), with raised FY2026 guidance of $1.22B-$1.245B. Headquartered in Mountain View, California, the company operates as a certified B Corp and public benefit corporation, serving individuals, enterprises, governments, and campus customers globally through its Coursera and Helix AI-native platforms.

Investment Thesis
Coursera at $4.94 with a $1.41B market cap is the cheapest stock in the screen on a forward P/E basis at 6.0x, offering a call option on the post-Udemy integration creating the dominant global online learning platform with $1.2B+ in guided revenue [1][2]. The fortress balance sheet ($982M cash, $9.7M debt) eliminates near-term financial risk, Q2 2026 non-GAAP gross margin of 62% (up 620bps) demonstrates improving unit economics, and the Helix AI-native platform positions Coursera to benefit from rather than be disrupted by AI through credentials and enterprise learning that AI tools cannot replicate. The secular shift toward continuous upskilling driven by AI-driven job displacement creates massive TAM expansion — workers need credentials that employers trust, not just free AI tutoring [3].
The bear case centers on existential AI disruption risk and persistent unprofitability. Coursera has never achieved GAAP profitability despite $885M TTM revenue, posting a Q2 2026 GAAP net loss of $80.4M, and the stock's 80%+ decline from its 2021 IPO peak reflects the market's severe doubt about the business model's durability [1][2]. Free AI tutoring tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) can teach most skills that individual Coursera courses cover at zero cost, directly threatening the consumer segment. The 59.6% headline revenue growth is almost entirely acquisition-driven — organic Q1 growth was just 9% — and Udemy integration risk is substantial given overlapping product lines and customer bases [1]. CEO Greg Hart has been in role for only 20 months with no track record of profitable execution at Coursera, and the stock at $4.94 signals deep market skepticism about the path to sustainable profitability [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Udemy integration synergies — combined platform creates the dominant global online learning provider with $1.22B-$1.245B FY2026 revenue guidance, and cost synergies from eliminating duplicate infrastructure could accelerate the path to GAAP profitability [2][5]
  2. Helix AI-native platform and AI micro-credentials positioning Coursera as the go-to destination for AI upskilling credentials that employers trust, converting AI disruption anxiety into enrollment demand [3]
  3. Enterprise segment growth — businesses need structured, trackable learning paths with verified outcomes for workforce development, a segment where credentials and compliance requirements create switching costs AI tutoring cannot match [2]
Key Risks
  1. Free AI tutoring tools directly threaten the consumer segment by offering personalized instruction at zero cost — if individual learners stop paying for courses they can learn from ChatGPT, Coursera's largest revenue segment erodes [3]
  2. Persistent GAAP unprofitability with Q2 2026 net loss of $80.4M despite $299M revenue raises fundamental questions about whether the business model can generate positive earnings at scale, particularly post-acquisition [2]
  3. Udemy integration execution risk — overlapping product lines, customer bases, and technology stacks require significant management bandwidth, and the $1.3B acquisition price paid primarily in equity diluted existing shareholders [5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
5
AI Risk Protection (4/9): Coursera faces genuine AI substitution risk — free AI tutoring tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) can teach many skills that Coursera courses cover, and the marginal cost of AI-generated instruction is approaching zero [3]. The company's defensive moats are credentials (university-branded degrees and professional certificates that AI cannot replicate), enterprise contracts (businesses need verified learning paths and compliance tracking), and its 300+ university partnerships. However, the consumer segment — individual learners paying for courses — is directly threatened by AI tools that can provide personalized instruction without a paywall. One investment analysis notes Coursera is integrating AI micro-credentials, treating AI as opportunity rather than threat, but timing and execution remain uncertain [3]. AI Opportunity (1/1): Coursera Coach (AI tutor) and the Helix AI-native platform represent revenue-generating AI products that ship to customers, and the company's AI-related professional certificates (Google, IBM, Meta) generate enrollment revenue from workers seeking AI credentials [2][3].
