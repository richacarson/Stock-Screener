Company Profile
Technology ·Education & Training Services ·NYSE ·United States
Coursera is one of the world's largest online learning platforms, offering courses, certificates, and degree programs from 325+ university and industry partners. Following the May 2026 merger with Udemy ($2.5B), the combined company serves 270+ million registered learners globally with projected FY2026 revenue of $1.22B-$1.25B. Coursera is a Public Benefit Corporation, co-founded by Stanford professors Andrew Ng and Daphne Koller in 2012. CEO Greg Hart (appointed February 2025) leads a workforce of 1,307 after post-merger restructuring. The company announced a $100M strategic investment in LearnVector, Andrew Ng's AI-native learning company, to accelerate AI strategy.

Investment Thesis
Coursera is executing aggressively on the $2.5B Udemy merger, delivering Q2 2026 revenue of $299M (+60% YoY) with enterprise revenue surging 118% and consumer growing 10% for the fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit gains [2]. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to $1.22B-$1.25B, with annual net synergies now expected to reach at least $85M run-rate by year-end — accelerating the original 24-month timeline [2]. At $6.02/share with Forward P/E of 7.88x and PEG of 0.16, the market is pricing in extreme skepticism about profitability and AI disruption risk, creating potential deep-value optionality if the merged entity achieves positive earnings [6]. The balance sheet is essentially debt-free (D/E 0.01%), and the $100M LearnVector investment positions Coursera at the intersection of AI and education through founder Andrew Ng's continued innovation [2]. The 270M+ registered learner base, 325+ university partners, and combined enterprise platform (Coursera for Business + Udemy Business) create scale advantages that individual AI tools cannot replicate. The Inspire Impact Score of 55 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions and the Public Benefit Corporation structure are attractive for faith-based investors.
The bear case is dominated by AI disruption risk to the core education business model. Free AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) provide personalized tutoring, concept explanation, and practice generation that directly competes with paid course content — Chegg's 2023 AI impact warning sent the entire EdTech sector lower and the structural threat has intensified since [7]. The stock has fallen 47% in the past 52 weeks and 85%+ from post-IPO highs above $40, reflecting profound market skepticism. Profit margin of -15.39% and ROE of -15.20% confirm the company is not yet profitable, and the Udemy integration is consuming management bandwidth and resources ($8-11M severance, 150 layoffs) [5][6]. The workforce reduction from 2,011 to 1,307 and Glassdoor reviews questioning mission authenticity suggest cultural erosion during restructuring [3]. Revenue growth had decelerated to 9% organically in 2025 before the Udemy acquisition artificially boosted the YoY comparison. A class action lawsuit over subscription renewals and data privacy litigation for sharing viewing history with Meta add legal risk. The company's ability to monetize AI education demand while defending against AI substitution of its core content remains the central unresolved question.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience HIGH RISK
4
AI Risk Protection (3/9): Coursera's core business — delivering online courses and educational content — faces real and recognized AI substitution risk. Free AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) can answer questions, explain concepts, generate practice problems, and provide personalized tutoring, directly competing with paid course content [7]. In 2023, peer company Chegg warned that ChatGPT was significantly impacting new customer growth, foreshadowing structural risk across education technology. AI coding assistants may reduce demand for programming courses, one of Coursera's largest content categories. The $100M LearnVector investment acknowledges that AI-native learning could disrupt Coursera's own platform model [2]. However, defensive moats exist: university-branded credentials and degrees carry signaling value AI cannot replicate, enterprise customers need structured learning paths with compliance tracking, and the 325+ institutional partner network creates content difficult to commoditize. The Udemy merger increases exposure to the individual learner segment where AI substitution risk is highest. AI Opportunity (1/1): Coursera generates meaningful revenue from AI-related content — AI course enrollment surged 1,060% with generative AI courses, over 40% of AI learners hold director-level or higher roles, and AI specializations from Stanford and DeepLearning.AI are among the platform's fastest-growing categories [3]. The LearnVector partnership positions Coursera to be an AI education provider rather than just an AI disruption victim.
