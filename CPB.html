Company Profile
Consumer Staples ·Packaged Foods ·NASDAQ ·United States
The Campbell's Company (formerly Campbell Soup) is a 155-year-old packaged food and snacking company operating in two segments: Meals & Beverages (Campbell's Soup, Rao's, Pace, Swanson, Pacific Foods, Prego) and Snacks (Goldfish, Pepperidge Farm, Snyder's-Lance, Cape Cod, Kettle Brand). The $2.7B acquisition of Sovos Brands (Rao's) closed in March 2024, shifting the portfolio toward premium. CEO Mick Beekhuizen succeeded Mark Clouse effective February 2025 [2]. FY2026 guidance calls for organic net sales of -2% to -1% and adjusted EPS of $2.15-$2.25, with Q3 FY2026 revenue declining 4.4% YoY to $2.37B as tariff-related cost inflation pressured margins [1]. Market cap approximately $6.6B.
13,700 Employees ·www.thecampbellscompany.com

Investment Thesis
Campbell's at $22.60 with a 7.10% dividend yield and 11.49x forward earnings is a defensive income play offering substantial yield support during a transitional period. The 155-year brand heritage, dominant shelf-stable soup position, and premium Rao's franchise provide durable competitive advantages in packaged food — a sector structurally immune to AI disruption [1][3]. ROE of 15.39% demonstrates the business generates adequate returns despite current margin pressure. Operating cash flow remains healthy, and the 50-year dividend payment streak provides income reliability. The stock's 31.5% decline over the past year prices in considerable negativity — any stabilization of organic sales (guided -2% to -1%) or successful integration of the Rao's brand at scale could support re-rating from the current distressed multiples [1].
The bear case centers on execution risk under financial pressure. Adjusted EPS guidance of $2.15-$2.25 represents a 30% decline from FY2025 as tariff-related cost inflation (240bps of gross margin compression in Q3) overwhelms pricing actions and cost savings [1]. D/E of 182% with $7.34B in debt and only $402M cash creates meaningful financial leverage risk — the company must service acquisition debt while volumes decline (-5% in Q3) and margins contract [4]. The 77.3% payout ratio is elevated for a company with declining earnings, raising questions about dividend sustainability if the margin headwinds persist. Organic revenue has been declining for multiple quarters, and the Consumer Beauty shift in the snack portfolio faces intense competition from both private label and premium competitors. New CEO Beekhuizen inherits a challenging operating environment with limited financial flexibility. The Inspire Impact Score of 51 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based allocation, and the yield provides income while waiting for the turnaround — but patience may be required as management described the environment as uncertain with cost headwinds persisting into FY2027.
