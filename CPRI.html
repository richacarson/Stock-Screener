Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Luxury Goods ·NYSE ·United States
Capri Holdings is a global fashion luxury group operating two iconic brands: Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo. The company sold Versace to Prada Group during FY2026 after the failed $8.5B Tapestry merger was blocked by the FTC. CEO John D. Idol has led the company since 2003. FY2026 (ended March 2026) revenue was approximately $3.46B, down sharply from $4.44B in FY2025 due to the Versace divestiture. The company reported trailing twelve-month net income of $79M, returning to profitability after a $1.2B loss in FY2025 that included merger-related charges. Debt-to-equity of 245% reflects heavy leverage against a reduced equity base.

Investment Thesis
Capri Holdings at 7.10x forward earnings is a deep value play on a post-restructuring turnaround. The company returned to profitability in FY2026 ($79M net income vs $1.2B loss in FY2025) and guides to low single-digit revenue growth and ~40% EPS growth in FY2027 [1]. The Versace divestiture to Prada simplifies the portfolio and allows management to focus exclusively on Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo. Forward PEG of 0.39 is deeply discounted versus the industry average of 0.99, suggesting the market prices in little to no growth recovery [3]. If Michael Kors can stabilize revenue and expand margins from the current 4.44% toward historical mid-teens levels, significant earnings leverage exists against the current depressed base.
The bear case is substantial. Capri Holdings is a declining luxury fashion business with structural headwinds. Revenue has fallen from $5.65B (FY2022 peak) to $3.46B (FY2026) — a 39% decline in four years, and the 5yr CAGR of -1.75% understates the recent trajectory [1]. Michael Kors, the core remaining brand, has been losing share in the accessible luxury segment for years as consumers trade up to true luxury or down to fast fashion. Debt-to-equity of 245% ($2.88B debt vs $120M cash) creates fragility — any revenue shortfall or margin compression could trigger covenant concerns [3]. The failed Tapestry merger and subsequent Versace sale reflect strategic exhaustion rather than confident reinvention. CEO Idol has led since 2003, but under his tenure the company peaked in 2022 and has been in decline since. Profit margin of 4.44% leaves no cushion for execution missteps. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) due to abortion activism will limit allocation from faith-based investors. Employee culture concerns and the departure of the Chief Legal/Sustainability Officer in June 2026 signal internal instability [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Michael Kors brand stabilization and margin recovery — FY2027 guidance projects low single-digit revenue growth and ~40% EPS growth from the depressed FY2026 base, suggesting early turnaround momentum if executed [1]
  2. Potential debt reduction from Versace sale proceeds, which could de-lever the 245% D/E ratio and reduce interest expense, improving the currently thin 4.44% profit margin

Key Risks

  3. CEO strategic credibility risk — John Idol's track record over the last 5 years (failed Tapestry merger, forced Versace sale, 39% revenue decline from peak, market share losses) raises questions about whether current management can execute a turnaround [1]
