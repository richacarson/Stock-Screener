Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NMS ·United States
Copart, Inc. operates the world's largest online vehicle auction platform, connecting sellers (primarily insurance companies, fleet operators, and dealers) with a global buyer base for salvage, total-loss, and used vehicles. The company manages the entire remarketing process — towing, storage, titling, and auctioning — across over 200 locations in 11 countries. FY2026 (ended July 31, 2026) revenue was approximately $4.7B (up 0.4% YoY), with net income of $1.48B and a 33.4% net profit margin. Copart announced the all-cash acquisition of ACV Auctions to expand into the used-vehicle market. Founded in 1982 by Willis Johnson, the company is led by returning CEO A. Jayson Adair, who succeeded Jeffrey Liaw effective August 2026.

Investment Thesis
Copart at $27.48 with a $25.5B market cap is the world's dominant online vehicle auction platform with a financial profile that few specialty services companies can match — virtually zero debt (D/E ~1%), 33.4% net profit margin, 17.5% ROE, and forward P/E of 17.83 [1][5]. The company's unmatched physical infrastructure moat of 200+ locations across 11 countries, combined with deep insurance carrier relationships and network effects in buyer liquidity, creates exceptional competitive positioning that has resisted all challenger attempts. The planned all-cash ACV Auctions acquisition expands the addressable market from salvage into used vehicles, potentially reigniting growth after a soft FY2026 (0.4% revenue growth) [3]. International revenue grew 14.1% in Q3 FY2026, demonstrating continued expansion outside the mature U.S. market [1]. Average selling prices rose 4.6% despite declining unit volumes, demonstrating pricing power even in a challenging environment.
The bear case centers on growth stagnation and premium valuation. FY2026 revenue grew just 0.4% to $4.7B with net income declining 4.4% and EPS falling 2.5% to $1.55 — a sharp deceleration from the 14% five-year revenue CAGR [1]. The PEG ratio of 3.61 (5yr expected) signals that the stock is priced for growth significantly above what the market expects, and Q4 FY2026 EPS of $0.35 missed consensus estimates of $0.39 as gross margin compressed and operating costs rose [1]. The CEO transition — Jayson Adair returning as CEO after Jeffrey Liaw's departure following less than two years — raises succession planning concerns and suggests potential strategic disagreement at the top [5]. Employee sentiment is a persistent weakness: Glassdoor 3.2/5 with only 42% recommending and Comparably culture score of 2.7/5 (grade D) suggest an organization where purpose and trust are not deeply embedded [5]. Long-term, improving vehicle safety technologies (ADAS, collision avoidance) could structurally reduce accident frequency and shrink the salvage vehicle supply pool that is Copart's core business, while AI-powered inspection and pricing tools could reduce the value of Copart's platform intermediation.
Key Catalysts
  1. ACV Auctions acquisition expands total addressable market from salvage/total-loss into the broader used-vehicle remarketing segment, potentially reigniting growth after FY2026's 0.4% revenue increase [3]
  2. International expansion — revenue up 14.1% YoY in Q3 FY2026 with operations across 11 countries, driven by increasing adoption of online auction platforms in markets where physical auctions still dominate [1]
  3. Founder-CEO Jayson Adair's return signals renewed strategic focus and hands-on execution leadership from the architect of Copart's digital transformation and global expansion [5]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth stagnation — FY2026 revenue grew only 0.4% with declining unit volumes and EPS contraction of 2.5%, and the PEG ratio of 3.61 prices in growth that recent performance cannot support [1][5]
  2. Structural decline in salvage supply — improving vehicle safety technologies (ADAS, autonomous driving features, collision avoidance systems) could reduce accident frequency over time, shrinking the pool of total-loss and salvage vehicles that is Copart's core supply source [5]
  3. Weak organizational culture — Glassdoor 3.2/5, 42% recommendation rate, Comparably culture score 2.7/5 (grade D), and recurring employee complaints about favoritism, micromanagement, and lack of career growth create long-term talent retention and execution risk [5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Copart's core business has meaningful defensive moats against AI disruption. The physical infrastructure — over 200 locations across 11 countries for towing, storing, and managing salvage vehicles — represents assets that AI cannot replicate or replace [1][5]. Regulatory requirements (title processing, environmental compliance, vehicle disposal regulations), deep switching costs for insurance carrier customers, and the proprietary land bank create structural barriers. The online auction platform benefits from network effects that would be difficult for an AI-powered alternative to replicate quickly. However, two AI-related risks exist at the margin: (1) improving vehicle safety technologies (ADAS, autonomous driving) could reduce accident frequency over time, shrinking the pool of salvage vehicles that is Copart's core supply [5]; (2) AI-powered competitor platforms could eventually challenge Copart's digital marketplace efficiency. Analysts have not flagged AI as a near-term disruption risk to Copart's business model, and the physical infrastructure moat provides strong protection. AI Opportunity (0/1): Copart is developing AI-enabled image recognition and Title Express for operational improvement, but these are internal efficiency tools rather than products generating AI-specific revenue. The company does not sell AI products or services to customers.
