Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Residential ·NYSE ·United States
Camden Property Trust is an S&P 500 multifamily REIT that owns, manages, develops, and operates apartment communities across the United States. As of mid-2026, Camden holds interests in approximately 177 properties with nearly 60,000 apartment homes, concentrated in Sun Belt markets including Houston, Dallas, Phoenix, Nashville, Tampa, and Atlanta. In 2026, Camden executed a transformative $1.625B exit from California to double down on Sun Belt growth, while repurchasing $694M of its own shares at an average price of $105.17. New CEO Alex Jessett succeeded co-founder Ric Campo in 2025.

Investment Thesis
Camden Property Trust offers a high-quality, Sun Belt-focused multifamily portfolio backed by one of the best corporate cultures in America (Fortune 100 Best Companies, Glassdoor 4.5/5) and 33 consecutive years of dividend payments at a 3.76% yield [2][4]. The $1.625B California exit — selling 11 properties and redeploying into younger Sun Belt assets — is a masterclass in capital allocation, reducing regulatory drag by approximately 80 basis points of annual NOI [1]. The concurrent $694M share buyback at an average $105.17/share (below current price of $113.70) has already generated value. Exposure to Sun Belt markets positioned near AI infrastructure build-out (Dallas, Houston, Phoenix) provides secular tailwind from technology-driven population migration. The orderly CEO succession from Campo to Jessett ensures cultural and strategic continuity.
The bear case centers on supply-driven rent pressure and stagnant growth. Q2 2026 blended lease rate growth was negative 0.2% (new leases -3.3%, renewals +2.8%), and same-store revenue growth guidance of 0.5% excluding California indicates a near-zero growth environment [1]. FY2026 EPS guidance of $6.68-$6.82 reflects earnings compression, and the GAAP payout ratio of 119% exceeds earnings (though covered by FFO). Elevated apartment supply deliveries in Austin and Denver continue to pressure rents and occupancy in key markets. D/E of 104% creates interest rate sensitivity, and higher-for-longer rates compress the spread between Camden's 3.76% yield and risk-free alternatives. The $53M RealPage antitrust settlement raises governance questions with potential for further legal exposure from ongoing industry-wide litigation. Richard Campo's net insider selling of $6.3M undermines the conviction narrative.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sun Belt apartment supply wave expected to peak in 2026 and decline sharply in 2027-2028, which should improve pricing power and restore positive blended lease rate growth across Camden's core markets [1]
  2. AI infrastructure build-out driving technology employment growth in Camden's Sun Belt markets (Dallas, Houston, Phoenix, Nashville), accelerating population migration and apartment demand near major tech campuses
  3. California exit completion saves approximately 80 basis points of annual NOI in regulatory costs while redeploying $1.625B into younger, higher-growth Sun Belt assets with better long-term return profiles [1]
Key Risks
  1. Blended lease rate growth of -0.2% in Q2 2026 and guided same-store revenue growth of only 0.5% (ex-California) indicate a near-zero growth environment driven by elevated apartment supply in Austin, Denver, and other Sun Belt markets [1]
  2. The $53M RealPage antitrust settlement over alleged algorithmic rent coordination creates reputational risk, and ongoing industry-wide litigation could result in additional legal exposure or regulatory scrutiny
  3. D/E of 104% and GAAP payout ratio of 119% create sensitivity to higher-for-longer interest rates, which increase financing costs on approximately $4.3B of debt and reduce the attractiveness of the 3.76% yield versus risk-free alternatives
