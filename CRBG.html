Company Profile
Financial Services ·Life Insurance & Retirement Solutions ·NYSE ·United States
Corebridge Financial, formerly AIG's Life & Retirement business (IPO September 2022), is a Houston-based provider of retirement and insurance products in four segments. Individual Retirement sells fixed, fixed-indexed and variable annuities. Group Retirement provides defined contribution recordkeeping and advice. Life Insurance writes U.S. term and universal life policies and also operates in the UK and Ireland. Institutional Markets offers pension risk transfer, stable value wraps and GICs. Marc Costantini, formerly of Manulife, became CEO on December 1, 2025. Corebridge has agreed to an all-stock merger with Equitable Holdings into a new holding company (Mountain Holding / 'New Equitable'), under which each Corebridge share converts into 1.000 New Equitable share; definitive proxy materials were filed in 2026. Market cap is about $15.7B.

Investment Thesis
Corebridge trades at 6.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.73 and about 1.5x book, with a 2.8% dividend yield. Consensus expects EPS to grow from $4.57 in FY2026 to $7.60 by FY2029 [2][9]. Underlying earnings are healthier than headline results suggest: excluding variable investment income, Q2 2026 operating EPS rose 14% YoY, and run-rate operating EPS of $1.35 was up 16% [3]. Buybacks are aggressive, with weighted shares down from 646M in FY2022 to 454M [2]. The pending all-stock merger with Equitable (1.000 New Equitable share per Corebridge share) would create a retirement company with more than $30B of pro forma GAAP book value and more than $25B of statutory capital, giving Corebridge holders scale and diversification [6].
The bear case starts with earnings quality and volatility. GAAP results swing widely on derivative and market-risk-benefit marks: a $664M loss in Q1 2025, $814M of profit in Q4 2025 and a $16M loss to common in Q2 2026 [2]. Q2 2026 operating EPS fell to $1.12 from $1.22 and adjusted pre-tax operating income fell to $664M from $842M on weaker variable investment income [3]. Leverage of about 102% debt-to-equity and roughly 39x assets-to-equity leaves the company sensitive to credit losses in a $265B investment portfolio [2]. The Equitable merger brings integration risk, leadership changes and loss of Corebridge's independent identity. With a fixed exchange ratio, CRBG's price will increasingly trade with Equitable's stock rather than on Corebridge's own fundamentals [6].

Key Risks
  1. Merger execution and identity risk: integration with Equitable, executive turnover (new CEO in December 2025, interim CFO) and a fixed exchange ratio that ties CRBG's value to Equitable's stock [4][6]
  2. Credit and spread risk in a roughly $265B investment portfolio with about 39x financial leverage; private-credit or commercial real estate losses would directly hit statutory capital and buyback capacity [2]
  3. Weaker variable investment income and lower rates compressing spreads; adjusted pre-tax operating income fell from $842M to $664M YoY in Q2 2026 [3]

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Corebridge's economics depend on spread income from a roughly $265B investment portfolio, regulated statutory capital and long-duration annuity and pension liabilities [2].
