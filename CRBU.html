Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Caribou Biosciences is a clinical-stage CRISPR genome-editing biopharmaceutical company developing off-the-shelf, allogeneic CAR-T and NK cell therapies using its proprietary chRDNA technology platform. Lead candidate vispa-cel (anti-CD19) received FDA alignment on the pivotal ANTLER-3 trial design in 2026, while CB-011 (anti-BCMA) earned RMAT designation on a 92% overall response rate in multiple myeloma. Founded by CRISPR co-inventor Jennifer Doudna and headquartered in Berkeley, CA, the company had $118.6M in cash as of Q1 2026 with runway into H2 2027.

Investment Thesis
Caribou Biosciences offers high-risk exposure to the next frontier of cancer immunotherapy through its proprietary chRDNA CRISPR platform, which enables off-the-shelf allogeneic cell therapies that could dramatically reduce cost and expand access relative to autologous approaches. Two significant clinical milestones in 2026 — FDA alignment on the pivotal ANTLER-3 trial for vispa-cel and RMAT designation for CB-011 based on a 92% overall response rate in multiple myeloma — validate the scientific platform [1]. Clear Street projects CB-011 peak sales of $734M by 2040, implying massive upside from the current $156M market cap [2]. With $118.6M in cash and runway into H2 2027, the company has time to execute key data readouts that could catalyze re-rating or partnership interest.
The bear case is substantial and binary. CRBU is a pre-revenue, deeply unprofitable clinical-stage biotech burning approximately $133M annually with only $118.6M in remaining cash, implying a capital raise is likely necessary to fund the vispa-cel pivotal trial [1][4]. The stock has declined to $1.57 from a 52-week high of $3.54, and analyst consensus still expects significant losses in 2026. Binary clinical trial risk is extreme — a single negative data readout in ANTLER-3 or CB-011 expansion could wipe out 50%+ of market value. No allogeneic CAR-T therapy has achieved FDA approval to date, raising questions about whether the entire modality will deliver on its promise. Glassdoor reviews flagging cultural dysfunction and leadership disconnection at a 97-person company represent organizational risk that could impact execution during a critical pivotal trial phase [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. ANTLER-3 pivotal trial initiation and enrollment for vispa-cel in relapsed/refractory B-cell NHL, with FDA-aligned trial design potentially enabling an accelerated approval pathway [1]
  2. CB-011 dose expansion data readouts in multiple myeloma later in 2026 — with 92% ORR and RMAT designation, strong data could support $734M peak sales projections and trigger partnership or acquisition interest [1][2]
  3. Strategic partnership or licensing deal for ex-U.S. rights could provide non-dilutive funding for the pivotal trial and validate the chRDNA platform's commercial potential at a premium to the current $156M market cap
Key Risks
  1. Cash runway of approximately 15 months ($118.6M cash at ~$133M annual burn) necessitates a dilutive capital raise or partnership before the pivotal trial generates data, likely pressuring the share price at sub-$2 levels [1][4]
  2. Binary clinical risk — any safety signal, manufacturing failure, or lack of durable response in vispa-cel or CB-011 could be catastrophic for a single-platform company with no diversified revenue streams
  3. No allogeneic CAR-T therapy has achieved FDA approval to date; regulatory pathway uncertainty for the entire modality persists regardless of individual clinical efficacy data, and the competitive landscape includes better-funded peers like Allogene and CRISPR Therapeutics
