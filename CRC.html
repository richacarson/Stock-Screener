Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
California Resources Corporation is the largest oil and gas producer in California, operating primarily in the San Joaquin Basin at the Elk Hills field. Following its 2024 merger with Berry Corporation, CRC produces approximately 154 MBoe/d (81% oil). The company is also developing Carbon TerraVault, California's first operational carbon capture and storage project, positioning itself as an energy and carbon management company.

Investment Thesis
CRC offers a compelling value proposition at 8.21x forward PE with Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDAX of $304M and raised full-year guidance to $1,400-$1,500M (up 42%), demonstrating strong cash generation from its position as California's largest oil producer at 154 MBoe/d (81% oil) [1]. The Berry merger has been accretive, with synergy targets raised 12% to $90-$100M annually. Carbon TerraVault provides optionality on federal CCS tax credits (45Q), with California's first operational CO2 injection underway at Elk Hills [2]. The 3.08% dividend yield with $46M returned to shareholders in Q1 alone provides income while awaiting commodity recovery. Total liquidity of $1,276M provides substantial financial flexibility.
GAAP earnings are severely distorted by $792M in non-cash commodity derivative losses in Q1 2026 alone, producing a reported net loss of $711M and making traditional profitability metrics (ROE -14.39%, profit margin -13.37%) misleading [1][4]. Analysts expect 12% revenue decline in 2026 as oil prices soften, exposing the commodity dependence that defines this business. Carbon TerraVault faces active litigation from environmental groups challenging the project's environmental analysis, and CCS economics remain dependent on federal 45Q tax credits that could face political risk [2]. CRC's post-bankruptcy history (2020), CEO turnover, and Berry integration complexity introduce execution uncertainty. California's regulatory environment — among the most restrictive for oil production in the U.S. — creates ongoing permitting and compliance risk that peers in Texas and North Dakota do not face [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Raised EBITDAX guidance of $1,400-$1,500M and year-end production exit rate targeting 175 MBoe/d as drilling acceleration delivers volume growth through 2026
  2. Carbon TerraVault CCS expansion beyond the initial 100,000 ton/year pilot, with potential for substantial 45Q federal tax credits at $85/ton for permanent geological storage
  3. Berry merger synergies reaching the $90-$100M annual target, validating the consolidation thesis and improving per-unit operating costs across the combined California asset base
Key Risks
  1. Commodity price exposure: 81% oil production mix means a $10/bbl decline in oil prices would compress adjusted EBITDAX by approximately $300-400M annually, eroding the 3.08% dividend and free cash flow
  2. Carbon TerraVault faces active environmental litigation and community opposition, with groups arguing CCS prolongs fossil fuel production; regulatory delays could strand CRC's CCS investment and 45Q tax credit strategy
  3. California regulatory risk: increasingly restrictive state-level permitting for new wells and production, combined with setback requirements and climate legislation, could constrain CRC's ability to maintain 154 MBoe/d production over the medium term
