Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
California Resources Corporation is the largest oil and gas producer in California, operating primarily in the San Joaquin Basin. Following the 2024 Aera Energy acquisition, CRC produces approximately 150–155 MBoe/d (80% oil). The company also operates Carbon TerraVault, California's first operational carbon capture and storage (CCS) project at Elk Hills, positioning itself as an energy and carbon management company aligned with California's 2045 carbon neutrality goals. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.297B with net income of $514M.

The company emerged from bankruptcy in 2020, acquired Aera Energy in 2024, and launched California's first CCS project in 2026 — but these moves read as strategic repositioning for regulatory survival rather than pursuit of a deeply held Just Cause. The carbon management pivot is becoming more real (CTV I is operational), but the core business remains oil extraction in an increasingly hostile regulatory environment, and the organizational disruption from bankruptcy and acquisition limits the trust foundation Sinek demands.

Investment Thesis
CRC offers deep value at 12.2x forward PE with the PEG ratio of 0.43 suggesting the market underprices growth, supported by Q2 2026 revenue of $1.297B and net income of $514M demonstrating strong cash generation from California's largest oil producer at 150–155 MBoe/d [1]. The Aera Energy acquisition expanded both production scale and CCS capacity, with Carbon TerraVault I now operational — California's first CCS facility capable of storing 1.6M metric tons CO2 annually, providing optionality on federal 45Q tax credits at $85/ton [2]. D/E of 39.6% and payout ratio of 37.8% provide balance sheet discipline, while the 3.19% dividend yield with 24% 5-year CAGR growth rate offers income with above-inflation growth. The Inspire ALIGNED score of 47 reflects strong ESG alignment across air quality, energy management, and environmental risk mitigation [4].
GAAP profitability metrics are severely distorted by $792M in non-cash commodity derivative losses in Q1 2026, producing negative TTM ROE (-3.55%) and profit margin (-3.24%) that mask underlying operational strength — Q2 2026 alone generated $514M net income [1][5]. The stock trades 29% below its 52-week high of $71.98, reflecting market skepticism about oil price sustainability and California regulatory risk. CRC and subsidiaries bear cleanup obligations for 11,000+ idle wells with estimated costs exceeding $1B against only $114.7M in bonds, creating a significant unfunded environmental liability [4]. Carbon TerraVault faces active litigation from environmental groups challenging the project's environmental analysis, and CCS economics remain dependent on federal 45Q tax credits vulnerable to political risk. California's increasingly restrictive permitting for new wells and production, combined with setback requirements, could constrain CRC's ability to maintain production levels over the medium term [4][5].
