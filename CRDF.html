Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Cardiff Oncology, Inc. is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing onvansertib, an oral PLK1 inhibitor, for RAS-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer and other solid tumors and hematologic malignancies. In the Phase 2 CRDF-004 trial (data cutoff January 22, 2026), the selected 30mg onvansertib + FOLFIRI/bevacizumab regimen achieved a confirmed objective response rate of 72.2% versus 43.2% for standard-of-care arms, meeting the trial's primary goal of selecting the dose and regimen for a Phase 3 registrational program. The company also amended its 2017 license agreement with Nerviano Medical Sciences in 2026, resolving a prior dispute over global onvansertib rights. Mani Mohindru, PhD transitioned from interim to permanent CEO in 2026, with Ajay Aggarwal joining as COO in April 2026. As of June 30, 2026, Cardiff held approximately $34.5M in cash plus a subsequent $10M registered direct offering, with runway projected into Q3 2027. Headquartered in San Diego, California.

Investment Thesis
Cardiff Oncology's case rests on materially strengthened Phase 2 data for onvansertib: the January 22, 2026 cutoff of the CRDF-004 trial showed a confirmed objective response rate of 72.2% for the selected 30mg regimen versus 43.2% for combined standard-of-care arms in first-line RAS-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer, meeting the trial's primary goal of selecting the dose and regimen for a Phase 3 registrational program [1]. The 2026 resolution of the Nerviano Medical Sciences licensing dispute removed a significant legal overhang on the company's sole asset, and CEO Mani Mohindru's approximately $79,900 in open-market share purchases across two 2026 transactions, with zero insider sales over the past year, signals strong leadership conviction [2]. The stock's average daily dollar volume of roughly $1.49M now clears the IOWN liquidity floor, an improvement from prior periods when the stock was effectively untradeable.
The bear case remains substantial for a single-asset clinical-stage biotech. Cardiff's cash position was approximately $34.5M as of June 30, 2026 (before a subsequent $10M registered direct offering), with net cash used in operations of $24.1M for H1 2026 alone; even with the new capital, a Phase 3 registrational trial will likely require hundreds of millions of dollars in additional funding, implying further dilution for existing shareholders [1]. ROE of -94.1% and a negative forward P/E reflect a company still years from profitability, and the recent leadership transition (new permanent CEO, new COO, new CMO within roughly a year) adds execution uncertainty at a critical inflection point. Single-asset clinical-stage biotechs historically fail Phase 3 or regulatory review at high rates, and any adverse trial, manufacturing, or regulatory development would be existential for the company.
Key Catalysts
  1. Initiation of the Phase 3 registrational trial for onvansertib in first-line RAS-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer, using the FDA-informed dose and regimen selected from the January 2026 CRDF-004 data cutoff [1]
  2. Potential strategic partnership or licensing agreement with a larger pharmaceutical company to fund Phase 3 development, which would validate the 72.2% ORR data and provide non-dilutive capital [1]
  3. Continued positive readouts from investigator-initiated trials in additional indications (pancreatic cancer, small cell lung cancer, triple-negative breast cancer, CMML) that could expand onvansertib's addressable opportunity beyond colorectal cancer [1]
Key Risks
  1. Cash runway into Q3 2027 will likely require a substantially larger, dilutive capital raise to fund a Phase 3 registrational trial, which typically costs hundreds of millions of dollars [1]
  2. Single-asset dependency — Cardiff's entire value is tied to onvansertib's continued clinical and regulatory success; historical Phase 3 failure rates for oncology assets are high, and any adverse safety, efficacy, or manufacturing finding would be existential [1]
  3. Recent leadership turnover (new permanent CEO, COO, and CMO within about a year) creates execution risk at the critical Phase 2-to-Phase 3 transition point [1][2]
