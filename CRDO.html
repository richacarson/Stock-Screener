Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·Cayman Islands
Credo Technology provides high-speed connectivity solutions for AI, cloud, and hyperscale data center networks. The company designs active electrical cables (AECs), optical PAM4 DSPs, SerDes chiplets, line card PHYs, and SerDes IP licensing. In FY2026 (ended April 2026), revenue more than tripled to $1.34B from $437M, with non-GAAP net income of $662M, driven by surging AI data center demand for high-speed interconnects. Credo pioneered AEC technology that has become critical infrastructure for AI cluster buildouts.

Investment Thesis
Credo Technology is the premier pure-play on AI data center connectivity, having pioneered the active electrical cable category that has become essential infrastructure for GPU cluster buildouts. FY2026 revenue tripled to $1.34B from $437M in FY2025, with non-GAAP net income increasing fivefold to $662M and operating margins reaching 49.6% [1]. Gross margins of 68.0% demonstrate exceptional pricing power in specialized connectivity silicon, while D/E of approximately 1% reflects a virtually debt-free balance sheet with $1.4B in cash [1]. FY2027 guidance projects >80% revenue growth with optical products contributing >$600M, providing a clear multi-year demand runway as the industry transitions from 800G to 1.6T networking [1][3]. At a PEG ratio of approximately 0.49x given the >80% growth outlook, CRDO offers rare hypergrowth at a reasonable growth-adjusted valuation.
The bear case centers on valuation, customer concentration, and cyclical risk. At 109x trailing P/E and 39x forward P/E, the stock is priced for perfection — any execution miss or deceleration in AI capex spending could trigger severe multiple compression from the current $253 price level, which is up 90% YTD [5]. CEO Brennan's persistent insider selling ($20M+ in cumulative sales, 123 dispositions with zero purchases over 3 months) raises alignment questions despite the 10b5-1 plan structure [3]. Revenue is heavily concentrated among a small number of hyperscaler customers, making the company vulnerable to a single design-out or supplier shift. Broadcom, Marvell, and other larger semiconductor companies are aggressively entering the AEC and high-speed connectivity market with greater R&D resources. A slowdown in hyperscaler AI capex — whether from ROI reassessment, macroeconomic headwinds, or export restrictions — would directly impact Credo's order book given near-total AI exposure. The Cayman Islands domicile creates governance complexity.
Key Catalysts
  1. 1.6T networking transition beginning in 2026-2027, requiring next-generation AECs and optical DSPs that represent Credo's core product roadmap, with optical products guided to exceed $600M in FY2027 [1][3]
  2. Continued hyperscaler AI infrastructure buildout with Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Amazon collectively spending $200B+ annually on data centers, driving sustained demand for high-speed connectivity at the physical layer [1]
  3. Operating leverage as revenue scales — non-GAAP operating margins of 49.6% in Q4 FY2026 demonstrate significant incremental profitability that should compound with FY2027's >80% growth guidance [1]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme valuation — 109x trailing P/E and 39x forward P/E at $253 per share (up 90% YTD) embed aggressive growth expectations, leaving minimal margin of safety for any execution miss or AI capex slowdown [5]
  2. Customer concentration risk — a small number of hyperscaler accounts generate the majority of revenue, and a single design-out or supplier shift to Broadcom or Marvell could cause a significant revenue decline [1]
  3. CEO insider selling — 123 share dispositions totaling $20M+ with zero purchases over 3 months raises alignment questions, even within a 10b5-1 trading plan framework [3]
