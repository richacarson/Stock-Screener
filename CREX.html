Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Creative Realities is a digital signage and retail media technology company that designs, deploys, and manages digital display networks and owns its software stack (ReflectView CMS and AdLogic ad-serving, from the 2022 Reflect Systems merger) [3][4]. The acquisition of Cineplex Digital Media (CDM) drove Q2 2026 revenue to $21.5M from $13.0M, with CDM contributing $7.4M; annualized recurring revenue was ~$20.5M [1]. The company is small (~$29M market cap), carries $46.6M of debt, and has flagged risk around meeting upcoming debt obligations [1][5]. CEO Rick Mills has led the company through its pivot toward SaaS recurring revenue [3].

Investment Thesis
The bull case for Creative Realities is a transformed, larger company. The Cineplex Digital Media acquisition lifted Q2 2026 revenue to $21.5M (+65%) and adjusted EBITDA to $2.0M from $1.1M, and management expects Q3 to be the largest revenue quarter in company history, with Q4 higher still [1]. Gross margin should improve in the second half as SaaS and service revenue grows. The AdLogic and ReflectView stack gives CREX ownership of its retail-media technology [3][4]. At a ~$29M market cap with a forward P/E of 10.1 and PEG of 0.48, any execution success could re-rate the stock, and CEO and CFO insider buying in June 2026 signals management conviction [7].
The bear case is balance-sheet stress and illiquidity. The company reported a $4.2M net loss in Q2 2026 and an $11.4M net loss for the first half; operating loss widened to $2.7M [1][5]. Debt is $46.6M against ~$12.8M of revolver availability, and an 8-K flagged risk to 'our ability to satisfy our upcoming debt obligations' [1][5]. ROE is -55% and profit margin -29% [7]. Average daily dollar volume of ~$65K is far below IOWN's $1M liquidity floor, making the stock untradeable at portfolio scale [7].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record H2 2026 revenue: management guides Q3 to be the largest revenue quarter in company history and Q4 to exceed Q3, as CDM contributes a full run-rate [1]
  2. Margin mix shift: rising SaaS and service revenue (ARR ~$20.5M) should lift gross margin in H2 2026 and move adjusted EBITDA (Q2: $2.0M) toward positive net income [1]
  3. Debt refinancing: refinancing the $46.6M debt load would remove the going-concern-style overhang flagged in company filings [1][5]
Key Risks
  1. Debt obligations: $46.6M of debt with only ~$12.8M of revolver capacity and an explicit filing warning about meeting upcoming obligations could force dilutive equity raises [1][5]
  2. Persistent losses: H1 2026 net loss of $11.4M and Q2 operating loss of $2.7M show integration costs outpacing revenue gains [1][5]
  3. Illiquidity and micro-cap volatility: ~30,700 shares (~$65K) trade daily, far below IOWN's $1M floor, and the stock fell 12.1% in the quarter before insider purchases [7]
