Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Crescent Energy Company is an independent oil and natural gas exploration and production company focused on the Permian Basin, Eagle Ford, and Uinta Basin. Backed by KKR, the company has grown rapidly through acquisitions including SilverBow Resources and Vital Energy, transforming from a small E&P into a major operator. Q2 2026 delivered record results: revenue of $1.39B, Adjusted EBITDAX of $798M, operating cash flow of $707M, and levered free cash flow of $418M. Headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Investment Thesis
Crescent Energy at $12.94 with a $4.3B market cap trades at just 5.5x forward earnings, offering deep value in a rapidly scaling E&P operator delivering record financial results — Q2 2026 produced record Adjusted EBITDAX of $798M, record operating cash flow of $707M, and record levered free cash flow of $418M on $1.39B in revenue [2]. The company's acquisition-driven growth strategy has been transformative: revenue surged 55% YoY with volumes up 32% from the Vital Energy merger, and KKR-backed management has demonstrated disciplined capital allocation across multiple accretive deals [1]. The structural AI datacenter demand tailwind for natural gas adds a secular growth dimension to the commodity thesis, and the 3.7% dividend yield provides income while investors wait for valuation re-rating.
The bear case centers on commodity exposure and financial engineering risk. Crescent's 1.3% GAAP profit margin and 1.5% ROE mean the company is barely profitable after acquisition-related charges, and the trailing PE of 118x reveals how far GAAP earnings lag the operating reality — a disconnect that may persist as integration costs continue [1][4]. D/E of 100% from serial acquisitions constrains financial flexibility if commodity prices decline, and as a KKR-backed vehicle, the investment horizon and governance dynamics differ from founder-led companies — KKR's eventual exit could create significant selling pressure on a $4.3B market cap [3][4]. The company has existed for only four years with no track record through a sustained commodity downturn, and integration challenges from SilverBow and Vital Energy (Glassdoor reviews cite 'disorganization' and communication issues) could undermine operational synergies [3]. Oil price volatility remains the dominant risk — a sustained decline below $60/bbl WTI would stress the balance sheet and potentially force dividend cuts and asset sales.
