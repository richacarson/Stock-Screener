Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Exploration & Production ·NYSE ·United States
Crescent Energy is a differentiated U.S. oil and gas exploration and production company focused on the Eagle Ford, Permian, and Uinta Basins, pursuing a disciplined 'growth through acquisition' strategy. The transformative $2.1B acquisition of SilverBow Resources (closed July 2024) nearly doubled the company's scale and created a premier Eagle Ford position. Q2 2026 delivered strong results with revenue of $1.39B (+55.3% YoY), record operating cash flow of $707M, average daily production of 335 MBoe/d (42% oil), and operating margin of 41.6% [1]. CEO David Rockecharlie leads a lean team of ~1,067 employees. Market cap approximately $2.5B.

Investment Thesis
Crescent Energy at $11 and 5.02x forward earnings is a disciplined E&P acquisition compounder delivering record cash flows in a sector structurally immune to AI disruption. Q2 2026 demonstrated the SilverBow integration thesis — $707M record operating cash flow, 41.6% operating margin, 335 MBoe/d production (42% oil), and $120M in Permian synergies captured ahead of plan [1]. The returns-driven acquisition model under CEO Rockecharlie has transformed Crescent from a small single-basin operator to a premier Eagle Ford/Permian producer in just five years. Forward P/E of 5.02 and an approximately 4.4% dividend yield provide substantial value and income support. The lowered 2026 cost guidance signals continued operational improvement, and the lean 1,067-person organization generates industry-leading cash flow per employee [1].
The bear case centers on commodity price risk, leverage, and the inherent cyclicality of E&P businesses. D/E of 100.4% with $5.24B in total debt against only $9.78M in cash creates meaningful financial risk if oil prices decline — the company's profitability is directly tied to WTI and Henry Hub pricing with limited hedging protection [4]. GAAP profitability remains distorted by commodity derivative mark-to-market effects, with TTM profit margin at just 1.7% and ROE at 1.14% despite record operating cash flow — the gap between GAAP and cash flow metrics requires investors to look through reported earnings [4]. The 55.3% Q2 revenue growth is almost entirely acquisition-driven rather than organic, and organic production growth rates are much lower. The acquisition strategy carries integration and capital allocation risk — each deal increases leverage and complexity. The Inspire Impact Score of 38 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based allocation, but oil and gas exposure carries inherent ESG scrutiny regardless of individual company practices. The stock's near-term performance is ultimately a bet on commodity prices that no operational excellence can fully hedge.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued SilverBow synergy capture — $120M in Permian synergies already realized ahead of plan, with the original $65-$100M annual target likely to be exceeded, supporting margin expansion and free cash flow generation [1][2]
  2. AI-driven electricity demand growth creating a structural tailwind for natural gas — data center power requirements are projected to grow significantly over the next decade, directly increasing demand for Crescent's natural gas production [1]

Key Risks
  1. Commodity price exposure — Crescent's revenue and profitability are directly tied to oil and natural gas prices, with limited hedging protection; a sustained decline in WTI below $60/bbl would pressure cash flows and debt serviceability [1]
  2. Leverage risk with $5.24B in debt and only $9.78M in cash — the acquisition-driven growth strategy has loaded the balance sheet, and any integration misstep or commodity price downturn could stress the capital structure [4]
  3. GAAP profitability disconnect — TTM net margin of 1.7% and ROE of 1.14% despite record operating cash flow reflects commodity derivative accounting effects and non-cash charges, creating confusion about true earnings power and limiting institutional investor interest [4]
