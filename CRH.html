Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Building Materials ·NYSE ·Ireland
CRH is the largest building materials company in North America and among the largest globally, operating through Americas Materials Solutions, Americas Building Solutions, and International Solutions segments producing aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete, asphalt, and building products. Q2 2026 revenue reached $10.8B (+6% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $2.63B (+7%), and FY2026 guidance targets net income of $3.9B-$4.1B. Founded in 1936 and headquartered in Dublin, Ireland, CRH listed on the NYSE in 2023 and employs 83,000 people across operations in the Americas, Europe, and internationally.

Investment Thesis
CRH at $83.63 with a $55.6B market cap is the dominant North American building materials franchise trading at an attractive 13.0x forward earnings, delivering record Q2 2026 results with revenue of $10.8B (+6% YoY), net income of $1.5B (+13%), and adjusted EBITDA of $2.63B (+7%) [1]. The company's infrastructure-levered positioning benefits from the $1.2T U.S. infrastructure bill, reindustrialization, and AI data center construction driving demand for aggregates, cement, and concrete. CEO Jim Mintern's five-year plan targets $40B of capital capacity with 7-9% average annual revenue growth and 22-24% EBITDA margins by 2030, and the $8.5B Arcosa acquisition would further consolidate CRH's aggregates leadership [3][4]. FY2026 guidance of $3.9B-$4.1B net income and $5.60-$6.05 diluted EPS provides strong earnings visibility.
The bear case centers on cyclical exposure and modest organic growth. CRH's 5.6% YoY revenue growth and 4.6% 3-year CAGR are below the 10% target, and the Americas Building Solutions segment declined 2% in Q2 2026 as U.S. residential new build activity remained subdued [1]. Profit margin of 9.9% is structurally constrained by the capital-intensive materials business, and D/E of 77.6% with $19.8B debt could become burdensome if acquisition-driven growth disappoints. The pending $8.5B Arcosa deal carries integration and execution risk. ESG critics have challenged CRH's climate accounting assumptions and decarbonization credibility — cement production is inherently carbon-intensive, and investors (Sarasin & Partners, LAPFF) have pressed for science-based 2030 targets tied to executive compensation [5]. CRH's reliance on M&A for growth means organic revenue expansion is modest, and overpaying for acquisitions in a competitive market is an ongoing risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. U.S. infrastructure spending from the $1.2T Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act drives multi-year demand for aggregates, cement, and concrete, with CRH's Americas Materials Solutions segment growing 10% in Q2 2026 as infrastructure and reindustrialization projects ramp [1]
  2. Pending $8.5B Arcosa acquisition (expected Q1 2027) would further consolidate CRH's aggregates-led portfolio, with the company targeting 7-9% average annual revenue growth and 22-24% EBITDA margins by 2030 through $40B of capital capacity [3][4]
  3. AI data center construction boom creating unprecedented demand for building materials — hyperscale data centers require massive quantities of concrete, aggregates, and specialized building products that CRH supplies [1]
Key Risks
  1. Cyclical exposure to U.S. residential construction — Americas Building Solutions declined 2% in Q2 2026, and a sustained housing downturn would pressure the segment that represents ~20% of revenue [1]
  2. ESG and decarbonization credibility risk — cement production is inherently carbon-intensive, and investors (Sarasin & Partners, LAPFF) have challenged CRH's climate accounting assumptions and pressed for science-based targets tied to executive pay, creating potential divestment or regulatory pressure [5]
  3. Acquisition integration risk from the $8.5B Arcosa deal and 11 other acquisitions in Q2 2026 alone ($1.1B), with overpaying or integration failures posing material downside given the company's M&A-dependent growth model [4]
