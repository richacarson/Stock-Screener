Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Apparel Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Carter's, Inc. is the largest branded marketer of young children's apparel in the United States. The company designs, sources, and markets baby and children's clothing under the Carter's, OshKosh B'Gosh, Skip Hop, and Little Planet brands through company-operated stores, e-commerce, and wholesale channels including Target, Walmart, and Amazon. Founded in 1865, headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia.

Investment Thesis
Carter's is the dominant children's apparel brand in the US, and H1 2026 signals a genuine revenue inflection after three years of decline — Q1 delivered 8.1% growth with 10.5% comparable store sales, and Q2 posted 5% growth driven by double-digit e-commerce comps [1]. At a trailing P/E of 7.44 and 20% FCF yield ($293M FCF on $1.46B market cap), CRI is deeply undervalued if the revenue recovery sustains. New CEO Doug Palladini (former Vans Global Brand President who doubled revenue to $4.2B) brings proven direct-to-consumer expertise [3]. The $132M tariff recovery in Q2 2026 and 2.51% dividend yield provide additional near-term shareholder returns. The 161-year-old brand has survived every retail disruption and serves a needs-based market that every parent enters.
The bear case centers on structural revenue decline and competitive pressure. Revenue has fallen from $3.49B (FY2021) to $2.90B (FY2025), a 17% contraction over four years driven by US birth rate declines, intensifying competition from Shein, Temu, and Target/Walmart private labels, and post-COVID normalization of children's spending [2]. The Q2 2026 EPS of $2.87 was inflated by a one-time $132M tariff recovery from the US Government — underlying operating profitability remains thin at 2.9% adjusted operating margin [1]. Short interest of 10.15% signals significant bearish institutional conviction. The dividend was suspended during COVID and reinstated at less than half the pre-COVID level ($1.00 vs. $2.40), and the PEG ratio of 4.25 reflects minimal expected EPS growth. New CEO Palladini's turnaround from Vans has not yet been replicated at Carter's.
Key Catalysts
