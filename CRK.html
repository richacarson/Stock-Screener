Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Comstock Resources is a Frisco, Texas-based natural gas exploration and production company with a dominant position in the Haynesville Shale of Northwest Louisiana and the developing Western Haynesville play in East Texas. Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones is the largest shareholder (~71%) with over $1.1B invested. The company's assets sit adjacent to the Gulf Coast LNG corridor and the Dallas-Houston AI data center buildout. Q2 2026 revenue of $353.3M with production of 113,069 MMcf (+16% sequentially), though net income fell sharply to $8.77M due to lower gas prices. H1 2026 revenue of $940.6M. Market cap ~$4.3B.

Investment Thesis
Comstock Resources is a uniquely positioned natural gas play at the intersection of AI data center power demand and Gulf Coast LNG exports. Q1 2026 delivered $587.4M revenue (+14.5% YoY) with strong profitability, though Q2 dropped sharply to $353.3M with net income of just $8.77M as gas prices weakened [1]. Q2 production of 113,069 MMcf (+16% sequentially) demonstrates operational momentum even as pricing compresses. CEO Jay Allison, the longest-tenured public E&P CEO (38 years) and a man of faith, has built the company around 'the last great play in America' — the Western Haynesville, which sits between the Dallas-Houston AI data center cluster and Gulf Coast LNG terminals [2]. Jerry Jones's $1.1B+ cumulative investment (~71% ownership) with an additional $100M recent purchase represents extraordinary insider conviction [4]. The Western Haynesville's new gas treating plant and potential NextEra power partnership for direct data center supply could transform Comstock from a volatile commodity producer into a contracted energy supplier. Faith alignment score of 39 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions is distinctive in the energy sector.
The bear case centers on natural gas price volatility and the sustainability of profitability. Q2 2026 exposed the earnings vulnerability: net income collapsed from $124.84M to $8.77M YoY as gas prices weakened, compressing ROE from 24%+ to under 16% and profit margin from 31% to 18% [1][5]. H1 2026 revenue of $940.6M is running below H1 2025's $983.1M (-4.3%) despite production recovery. D/E increased to 123% ($3.7B debt) as the capital-intensive Western Haynesville development continues, and the Forward PEG of ~4.6 signals the market expects significant earnings normalization from any gas price strength [5]. The suspended dividend means investors are entirely dependent on gas price recovery and production growth for returns. Concentrated ownership by Jerry Jones (~71%) limits float and creates governance dynamics where minority shareholders ride alongside Jones's strategic preferences, with no clear succession plan for the 38-year-tenure CEO. ESG access-to-capital pressure on natural gas E&P remains a structural headwind despite the MiQ methane certification, and any regulatory tightening on fracking or carbon capture could increase compliance costs for the Haynesville operations [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Western Haynesville production ramp with new gas treating plant coming online, validating the 'last great play' thesis and converting exploration acreage into proved reserves and sustained production growth [2]
  2. Direct-to-data-center power offtake or NextEra partnership signing, converting volatile commodity gas revenue into contracted, higher-multiple recurring revenue at the intersection of AI power demand [2]
  3. Sustained natural gas price recovery driven by LNG export expansion (Plaquemines, Golden Pass, CP2 terminals) and AI data center load growth, leveraging Comstock's strategic Gulf Coast and Dallas-Houston positioning
Key Risks
  1. Natural gas price volatility — Q2 2026 demonstrated the earnings fragility as net income collapsed 93% YoY to $8.77M, and any sustained gas price weakness below $3.00/Mcf would strain the capital-intensive Western Haynesville development program [1]
  2. Elevated leverage at 123% D/E ($3.7B debt) with no dividend and suspended distributions, creating financial stress risk if gas prices remain weak during the peak capex phase of Western Haynesville development [5]
  3. CEO succession risk — Jay Allison's 38-year tenure is both a strength and a vulnerability, with no named successor and no plan disclosed for leadership transition of a company whose culture and strategy are deeply tied to one individual [2]
