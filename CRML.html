Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NASDAQ ·United States
Critical Metals Corp. is a mining exploration and development company focused on rare earth elements and lithium in Southern Greenland and Austria. In April 2026, the Government of Greenland approved transfer of the remaining 50.5% interest in Tanbreez Mining Greenland A/S, bringing the company's ownership to 92.5% of one of the world's largest heavy rare earth deposits. The company also holds the Wolfsberg Lithium Project in Austria, the first fully permitted lithium mine in Europe, and has secured a U.S. Export-Import Bank letter of interest for up to $120 million and offtake commitments covering 25% of near-term Tanbreez production.

Investment Thesis
Critical Metals now controls 92.5% of Tanbreez, one of the world's largest heavy rare earth deposits, following April 2026 Greenland government approval, and has converted geopolitical relevance into concrete commercial and financing wins: a U.S. EXIM Bank letter of interest for up to $120 million (the first overseas mining investment of its kind under the current administration), and offtake commitments covering 25% of near-term production split between DoD-backed Ucore Rare Metals and magnet maker REalloys [2][3]. A 10,000-meter drilling campaign and new environmental approvals from Greenland's EAMRA regulator continue to de-risk the resource, and analyst coverage (albeit thin, one 'Strong Buy') carries a 12-month target of $12 against the current $6.66 share price [6][7].
The bear case is severe. CRML remains pre-revenue ($768,573 TTM) with a $153.3 million annual net loss, an Altman Z-Score of 0.39 signaling elevated bankruptcy risk, and ROE of -140.98% [6]. First customer supply is not targeted until 2029, meaning the company must fund years of additional development — the April 2026 $60 million private placement at $10/share (now underwater at $6.66) illustrates the dilution risk inherent in getting there, and a director sold roughly $402,750 of stock the same month with no offsetting insider buying [5]. Mining development projects routinely face cost overruns and geological or permitting surprises, Greenland's political status remains a wildcard given ongoing U.S. interest in the territory, and short interest of 14.45% signals continued market skepticism about execution risk at the current ~$978 million market cap [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Conversion of the EXIM Bank letter of interest into a definitive $120 million financing commitment, materially de-risking the capital plan to reach 2029 first production
  2. Positive results from the 2026 10,000-meter drilling campaign confirming heavy rare earth grades sufficient to support commercial-scale mine planning
  3. Expansion of offtake commitments beyond the current 25% of near-term production, or additional U.S./EU government support given Tanbreez's designation as a strategically significant non-China supply source
Key Risks
  1. Continued dilutive capital raises (following the April 2026 $60 million placement) as the company funds multi-year development toward a 2029 production target, eroding per-share value before any revenue materializes
  2. Geological, permitting, or Greenland political risk delaying first production beyond 2029, given the territory's evolving status amid ongoing U.S. strategic interest
  3. Rare earth and lithium price volatility undermining project economics before Tanbreez or Wolfsberg reach commercial production, compounded by an Altman Z-Score of 0.39 indicating elevated financial distress risk
