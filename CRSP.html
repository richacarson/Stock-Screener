Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Switzerland
CRISPR Therapeutics AG is a gene-editing biotechnology company developing CRISPR/Cas9-based medicines for serious diseases. Its lead product, CASGEVY (developed with partner Vertex Pharmaceuticals), is the world's first approved CRISPR-based therapy, treating sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia; the FDA expanded its label to children as young as 2 in 2026, opening access to roughly 5,500 additional patients [1]. CASGEVY revenue reached $76M in Q2 2026 (+151% YoY, +78% QoQ) with over 500 patients initiated and reimbursement secured in Germany, plus regulatory submissions advancing in Saudi Arabia and the UK [1]. The pipeline spans oncology (allogeneic CAR-T), cardiovascular disease (in vivo liver-editing candidates CTX310, CTX340, CTX460), autoimmune conditions, and regenerative medicine (CTX213 for Type 1 diabetes). Founded in 2013, headquartered in Zug, Switzerland, with primary R&D operations in Boston, Massachusetts, under CEO and Chairman Dr. Samarth Kulkarni.

Investment Thesis
CRISPR Therapeutics continues to validate its position as the world's first commercial CRISPR gene-editing company, with CASGEVY revenue reaching $76M in Q2 2026 (+151% YoY, +78% QoQ), over 500 patients initiated, and a landmark FDA approval extending the therapy to children as young as 2 — reviewed in just 53 days and opening access to roughly 5,500 additional patients — alongside German reimbursement and advancing Saudi/UK submissions [1]. The company holds $2.364B in cash after raising $585.4M and $600M via convertible notes in 2026, funding a genuinely diversified next-generation pipeline: in vivo liver-editing programs CTX340 (hypertension) and CTX460 (alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency) both entering Phase 1, CTX310 (ANGPTL3) Phase 1b data expected by year-end, and CTX213 for Type 1 diabetes advancing toward the clinic [1]. CEO Samarth Kulkarni's explicit philosophy of building psychological safety around the 80% failure rate inherent to breakthrough R&D, combined with a validated, service-oriented Just Cause (curing serious genetic disease), positions the company well on both innovation and culture dimensions relative to biotech peers.
The bear case centers on financial mechanics: CRISPR Therapeutics posted a GAAP net loss of $451.14M on just $13.39M of TTM revenue (Casgevy's much larger product revenue flows primarily through the Vertex collaboration rather than CRISPR's own income statement), producing a -26.09% ROE and forcing continued reliance on capital markets ($1.2B+ raised via convertible notes in 2026 alone) to fund the broad pipeline [1][4]. D/E of 45% and ample liquidity are genuine strengths, but with no forward P/E or PEG computable given negative earnings, the investment case rests entirely on binary clinical and commercial execution across multiple unproven in vivo programs rather than current financial durability. No AI-related drug discovery initiatives were disclosed, meaning CRISPR does not yet benefit from the AI-driven R&D productivity gains some biotech peers are beginning to tout, and no evidence of community investment or employee retention data limits full confidence in the Inspiration dimension despite a strong mission.
