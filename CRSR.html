Company Profile
Technology ·Computer Hardware ·NASDAQ ·United States
Corsair Gaming designs, manufactures, and markets high-performance gear for gamers, content creators, and PC enthusiasts worldwide. Products span gaming peripherals (keyboards, mice, headsets), PC components (memory, cases, power supplies, cooling), streaming equipment (Elgato capture cards, Stream Deck, lighting), and sim racing hardware (Fanatec wheels, pedals, cockpits). CEO Thi La assumed the role in July 2025 after 14 years at the company, including serving as President and COO. Q2 FY2026 marked a return to profitability with net income of $8.84M, record gross margin of 33.2%, and adjusted EBITDA up 279% YoY. The company raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $1.40-$1.47B. Founded in 1994, headquartered in Milpitas, California.

Investment Thesis
Corsair is executing a successful margin transformation, delivering Q2 FY2026 record gross margin of 33.2% (up 635bps YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $30.8M (up 279% YoY), marking a return to GAAP profitability with net income of $8.84M [3]. The company raised its FY2026 revenue guidance to $1.40-$1.47B, increasing the EBITDA guidance midpoint by $19M, signaling confidence in sustained profitability improvement [3]. At a forward P/E of 13.11x and market cap of roughly $940M, the stock is modestly valued for a company in the early stages of a margin recovery. The integrated ecosystem strategy — iCUE software linking Corsair peripherals, Elgato streaming gear, and Fanatec sim racing hardware — creates switching costs that commodity competitors cannot replicate [1]. The Stream Deck MCP integration positions Corsair at the intersection of physical hardware and AI-powered workflows, potentially broadening the addressable market from gaming into professional productivity [2].
The primary concern is that profitability remains fragile — profit margin of 2.54% and ROE of 1.51% signal that equity capital is barely generating returns despite the Q2 improvement [4]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately -5% reflects the painful post-COVID demand crash from $1.9B peak in 2021, and FY2026 guided revenue of ~$1.44B remains well below pre-pandemic levels [1][3]. Corsair operates in a fiercely competitive market against Logitech (5x revenue, far stronger margins) and Razer, with limited pricing power outside premium niches. The Fanatec acquisition added operational complexity into a small addressable market. A $5.5M class action settlement over misrepresented RAM speed specifications raises product quality governance concerns [6]. Without a step-change improvement in profitability beyond the current ~3% margins, the stock risks remaining a sub-$1B company with limited institutional interest. AI Risk Protection (6/9): Corsair's core business is manufacturing and selling physical gaming hardware — keyboards, mice, headsets, PC components, capture cards, and sim racing equipment — which AI structurally cannot substitute. A gamer needs a physical mouse and keyboard; a sim racer needs physical wheels and pedals; a streamer needs physical capture cards [1]. The iCUE software ecosystem and Elgato Stream Deck have some AI exposure — competing AI-powered workflow tools could emerge — but the hardware moat remains robust. The primary indirect risk is that if AI automates content production, demand for streaming hardware could soften, though early evidence suggests AI is expanding the creator market by lowering barriers to entry [2]. AI Opportunity (1/1): Corsair is demonstrating concrete AI product integration through Stream Deck's Model Context Protocol (MCP) support, enabling the hardware to interface with AI agents and software workflows using natural language and voice commands [2]. This positions Stream Deck as a physical control surface for AI-powered workflows, expanding the addressable market from gaming/streaming into professional productivity and AI development.
