Company Profile
Technology ·Cloud Computing / AI Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
CoreWeave is the fastest-growing AI-native cloud platform, providing GPU compute infrastructure to AI developers, hyperscalers, and enterprises. The company achieved $5.1B in 2025 revenue (+170% YoY) and reported Q2 2026 revenue of $2.58B (doubled YoY) with raised FY2026 guidance of $12.4-13.2B. CoreWeave operates 43+ data centers with 45,000+ GPUs, 850+ MW of active power, and 3.1 GW of contracted capacity. The company holds a $104B AI infrastructure backlog as of Q2 2026, serving hyperscalers including Microsoft (historically ~60%+ of revenue), Meta, and OpenAI. CEO Michael Intrator co-founded the company after pivoting from cryptocurrency mining. IPO in March 2025 at $40/share; current market cap ~$49B. Total debt of $51.6B against $5.5B cash reflects the capital-intensive AI infrastructure buildout, with $31-35B in 2026 capex guidance.

Investment Thesis
CoreWeave at $87/share ($49B market cap) is the purest AI infrastructure play in public markets — $104B in committed backlog, FY2026 guidance raised to $12.4-13.2B (up from $5.1B in 2025), and Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $1.51B demonstrating the unit economics work at scale [2][3]. Revenue doubled YoY in Q2 2026 to $2.58B, and 98% take-or-pay contracts provide revenue certainty. The company's 45,000+ GPUs, 43+ data centers, and partnerships with NVIDIA, Microsoft, Meta, and OpenAI position it at the center of the AI infrastructure buildout — a structural demand cycle that hyperscaler capex plans indicate will accelerate. The Weights & Biases acquisition creates an end-to-end AI platform differentiating CoreWeave from commodity compute. Adjusted EBITDA margins of 56%+ (Q1 2026) demonstrate that profitability is achievable once depreciation and interest costs are covered by revenue scale. The Inspire Impact Score of 36 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors.
The bear case is existential leverage risk. D/E of 1027% with $51.6B in total debt against $5.5B cash makes CoreWeave among the most leveraged technology companies ever created — NYU's Aswath Damodaran characterizes the debt situation as 'danger territory' [5][6]. GAAP net loss of ~$626M in Q2 2026 persists despite record revenue, as depreciation on massive GPU infrastructure and interest on $51.6B debt consume operating income. Microsoft historically accounts for ~60%+ of revenue — loss or material reduction of this relationship would be catastrophic. The $31-35B capex plan for 2026 alone requires continued capital market access at favorable terms; any credit market disruption or AI spending slowdown could strand infrastructure investments. The stock has declined 30%+ from 52-week highs as the market reprices the leverage risk. GPU technology evolves rapidly — today's 45,000+ GPUs could depreciate faster than revenue ramps if next-generation architectures shift demand. Competition from hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) with vastly superior balance sheets creates long-term margin pressure as AI compute commoditizes. Glassdoor cultural concerns and rapid organizational scaling add execution risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2026 revenue execution toward $12.4-13.2B guidance, demonstrating that the $104B backlog converts to recognized revenue at projected rates and that the AI infrastructure demand cycle is accelerating rather than moderating [3]
  2. Path to GAAP profitability as data centers reach operational maturity and utilization rises — Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of 56% demonstrates the underlying economics; the gap to GAAP profitability is primarily depreciation and interest on the $51.6B debt load [2]
  3. Customer diversification beyond Microsoft — if Q2/Q3 2026 earnings show Meta, OpenAI, and enterprise customers growing as a percentage of revenue, the single-customer risk narrative would de-risk significantly [1][3]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme leverage — $51.6B total debt with 1027% D/E ratio means even moderate interest rate increases or credit market disruption could threaten solvency; NYU's Damodaran describes CoreWeave as in 'danger territory' for debt [5][6]
  2. Microsoft customer concentration historically at 60%+ of revenue — contract modification, volume reduction, or loss of this relationship would be existential for a company with $51.6B in debt and negative GAAP earnings [1]
  3. AI infrastructure spending cycle risk — if hyperscaler capex moderates, CoreWeave's take-or-pay contracts protect committed revenue but the $31-35B annual capex plan requires continued demand growth to justify the capital intensity [3]
