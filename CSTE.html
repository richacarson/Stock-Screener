Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NASDAQ ·Israel
Caesarstone Ltd. designs, manufactures, and markets premium quartz surfaces for kitchen countertops, bathroom vanities, and other interior applications. The company operates manufacturing facilities in Israel, the United States, and India, selling through a network of fabricators, distributors, and retailers globally. Approximately 44% of revenue comes from the U.S. market. Founded in 1987 and headquartered in Sdot Yam, Israel.

Investment Thesis
Caesarstone's Q2 2026 results showed some operational improvement — gross margins expanded to 24.0% from 19.6% YoY and adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $1.0M from $6.4M — suggesting the cost restructuring program under CEO Yos Shiran is gaining traction [1]. The company maintains a clean balance sheet with $51.8M cash and no financial debt, providing a runway for the turnaround attempt. However, revenue continues to decline (-4.5% reported, -7.7% constant currency in Q2) and the company remains deeply unprofitable with TTM net loss of $142.8M [1][2].
The bear case is overwhelming. The August 15, 2026 U.S. tariff-rate quota imposes 25-50% additional tariffs on quartz surface imports, which paradoxically hurts Caesarstone's Israeli-manufactured product as much as the Chinese competition it was designed to target — the company is reassessing its timeline to positive EBITDA as a result [1]. The $51.2M silicosis litigation provision signals potential ongoing legal liability. With a market cap of approximately $79M, daily dollar volume of only $243K (severely illiquid for institutional investors), an Altman Z-Score of 1.08 indicating elevated bankruptcy risk, and Glassdoor scores reflecting a demoralized workforce, Caesarstone faces existential questions about its long-term viability [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Cost restructuring program achieving positive adjusted EBITDA in Q3 2026, validating the turnaround path and potentially attracting value investors
  2. U.S. tariff-rate quota on quartz imports could reduce Chinese competition if Caesarstone can shift production to its U.S. facility in Richmond Hill, Georgia to avoid the tariff
  3. Potential recovery in the U.S. housing renovation market, which drives approximately 44% of revenue, if interest rates decline and housing activity rebounds
Key Risks
  1. U.S. tariff-rate quota effective August 15, 2026 imposing 25-50% tariffs on quartz imports may increase Caesarstone's costs on Israeli-manufactured product, forcing the company to reassess its EBITDA timeline
  2. Silicosis litigation exposure beyond the $51.2M provision already recorded — quartz surface manufacturing faces growing regulatory and legal scrutiny over silica dust health risks in fabrication shops
  3. Daily dollar trading volume of approximately $243K makes the stock uninvestable for institutional capital, limiting the shareholder base to retail and creating extreme price volatility risk
