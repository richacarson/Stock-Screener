Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Cintas provides uniform rental and facility services, first aid and safety products, fire protection, and document management to businesses across North America under the 'We help businesses get Ready for the Workday' positioning. FY2026 (ended May 31, 2026) revenue reached $11.26B (+8.9% YoY, 8.3% organic) with diluted EPS of $4.91 (+11.6%) and record gross margin of 51.0% [1]. On March 10, 2026, Cintas agreed to acquire rival UniFirst for $5.5B in cash-and-stock — its third attempt after failed 2022 and early-2025 bids — expecting ~$375M in synergies; the deal cleared a UniFirst shareholder vote but faces an FTC Second Request antitrust review [3].

Investment Thesis
Cintas remains a high-quality compounder with FY2026 revenue of $11.26B (+8.9%, 8.3% organic), record 51.0% gross margin, ROE of 40.71%, and 45 consecutive years of dividend growth — hallmarks of a durable, route-density-driven service moat that AI cannot easily disrupt [1][2]. The pending $5.5B UniFirst acquisition, if it clears the FTC's Second Request, would meaningfully consolidate the North American uniform and facility services market, adding scale and an estimated $375M in run-rate synergies within four years [3]. Low leverage (53% D/E) and consistent double-digit EPS growth (+11.6% in FY2026) support continued capital returns even as the company integrates a major acquisition.
The bear case is valuation and strategic character: Forward P/E of 36.13x and a Forward PEG of 3.11 (worse than the 3.0 IOWN floor) price in flawless execution for a business growing revenue at under 9% organically, leaving little room for integration missteps on the UniFirst deal [2]. The FTC's Second Request signals real antitrust risk that could delay, restructure, or block the transaction outright, and three attempts (2022, early 2025, 2026) to acquire the same target suggest management sees limited organic growth optionality without consolidation [3]. From an Infinite Game lens, Cintas's own Principal Objective is explicitly shareholder-value-maximization language, and the UniFirst pursuit reads as competitor elimination rather than a Just Cause-driven strategy — a governance signal, not a near-term earnings risk, but one that matters for long-duration conviction. Mixed Glassdoor sentiment on executive-field disconnect adds a modest culture-risk flag.
Key Catalysts
  1. UniFirst acquisition closing in 2H 2026 following the shareholder vote, unlocking an estimated $375M in run-rate cost synergies within four years and meaningfully expanding route density and customer breadth [3]

Key Risks

  2. Premium valuation (36.13x forward earnings, 3.11 PEG) leaves little margin for error if organic growth decelerates below the current ~8-9% pace or integration costs exceed expectations [2]
  3. Mixed employee sentiment on executive-field disconnect (Glassdoor) and a shareholder-value-first stated Principal Objective indicate limited mission-driven differentiation versus peers, a soft long-term culture risk [6]
