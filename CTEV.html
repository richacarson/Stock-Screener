Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NYSE ·United States
Claritev Corporation (formerly MultiPlan Corporation) is a healthcare technology, data, and insights company focused on delivering affordability, transparency, and quality across the U.S. healthcare system. The company provides analytics-based services, data and decision science, network-based services, payment and revenue integrity, and end-to-end surprise billing solutions. Founded in 1980, headquartered in McLean, Virginia. Processes approximately $177.6B in medical charges annually, identifying $24.7B in potential savings. Rebranded from MultiPlan and began trading as CTEV on NYSE in February 2025.
2,700 Employees ·www.claritev.com

Investment Thesis
Claritev offers a potential turnaround story in healthcare technology at a heavily discounted valuation. At $25.93 per share with ~$979M TTM revenue, the stock trades at just 0.5x revenue despite generating 61-62% adjusted EBITDA margins ($605-615M guided for 2026) [1][2]. The Vision 2030 plan targeting $1.3B revenue and $800M EBITDA represents 33% revenue growth and significant scale leverage over 4 years. Q1 2026 revenue grew 5.8% YoY to $244.7M, and management raised revenue guidance to $985M-$1B [1]. The appointment of a Chief AI Officer and AI platform integration could drive productivity gains and product differentiation. If Claritev can successfully execute its digital transformation and delever toward 5.0x net leverage, the equity value could appreciate significantly from its current heavily debt-burdened levels.
The bear case is severe. Claritev's $4.74B debt load against negative shareholders' equity of -$167M creates an unsustainable capital structure — annual interest expense exceeds $400M, consuming over 40% of revenue and producing GAAP net losses of $286.5M ($17.32 loss per share) despite strong operating cash flow [1][2]. The company cannot meaningfully delever without either dramatic revenue growth or equity issuance, and the 30%+ decline in market cap over the past year reflects market skepticism about the path forward. The federal antitrust litigation (APTA et al.) alleges systematic underpayment of healthcare providers, creating existential legal risk to the business model [4]. Revenue growth of only 3.9% is insufficient to grow out of the debt burden, and payer in-sourcing of analytics capabilities threatens long-term revenue retention. The IOWN framework's emphasis on financial durability (infrastructure) makes SELL the appropriate recommendation for a company with negative equity and chronic GAAP losses [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful deleveraging toward Vision 2030 target of 5.0x net leverage, potentially through a combination of organic free cash flow generation and non-core asset monetization
  2. Q2-Q3 2026 earnings demonstrating accelerating revenue growth toward the $985M-$1B guidance range, with expanding adjusted EBITDA margins validating the AI and digital transformation investment
  3. Resolution or favorable settlement of the federal antitrust litigation (APTA et al.) that would remove the overhang on the company's business model and valuation
Key Risks
  1. Unsustainable capital structure with $4.74B debt against negative equity of -$167M generates $400M+ annual interest expense, producing chronic GAAP net losses with no clear deleveraging path at current revenue growth rates
  2. Federal antitrust litigation (APTA and others) alleges systematic conspiracy to underpay healthcare providers — an adverse ruling could force fundamental changes to the repricing business model that generates the majority of revenue
  3. Payer in-sourcing risk as large health insurers (UnitedHealth, Cigna, Aetna) invest in AI-powered internal claims processing and payment integrity capabilities, reducing dependence on third-party intermediaries like Claritev

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Claritev's core business — healthcare payment analytics, network optimization, and claims repricing — faces meaningful AI disruption risk from multiple angles [3]. Large health insurers (UnitedHealth, Cigna, Aetna) are investing billions in AI-powered claims processing and payment integrity tools that could reduce reliance on third-party intermediaries like Claritev. AI-powered healthcare pricing transparency (required by federal regulation) could make Claritev's repricing analytics less differentiated. Startups using LLMs for automated claims review could compress margins. However, Claritev's proprietary dataset (processing $177.6B in medical charges, 40+ years of claims data) and established payer relationships provide meaningful switching costs. The company is adapting by appointing a Chief AI Officer and deploying AI across its platform, but the outcome of this race between in-house AI and third-party analytics is uncertain. AI Opportunity (1/1): Claritev generates AI-augmented revenue through its analytics platform that uses machine learning for cost savings identification, network optimization, and pricing transparency. The appointment of a dedicated Chief AI Officer (Fernando Schwartz) and description of the platform as 'world-class technology and AI' suggests meaningful integration [3].
