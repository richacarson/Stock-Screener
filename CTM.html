Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·AMEX ·United States
Castellum, Inc. is a cybersecurity, electronic warfare, and software engineering services company focused on the U.S. Department of Defense and federal agencies. Operating through subsidiaries including Specialty Systems Inc. (SSI), Corvus Consulting, and Mainnerve, the company provides AI-enabled cyber, electronic warfare, C5ISR, data analytics, and mission-critical IT services. Under CEO Glen Ives (since July 2024), the company completed a turnaround from near-bankruptcy to a debt-free balance sheet with record H1 2026 revenue of $28.2M (+10% YoY). Headquartered in Vienna, Virginia.
244 Employees ·www.castellumus.com

Investment Thesis
Castellum's investment case rests on its transformation from near-bankruptcy to an organized defense contractor with a record backlog and growing revenue base. H1 2026 achieved record revenue of $28.2M (+10% YoY), with Q1 accelerating to 23% growth, and management increased the full-year 2026 revenue forecast to $63M [1]. The balance sheet is clean ($16.9M cash, D/E 2.67%), gross margins of 34.7% are strong for a defense services firm, and the AI-enabled cyber and electronic warfare focus aligns with accelerating DoD modernization spending [1][3]. At a $58M market cap trading at barely 1x revenue, the stock offers significant upside if the company achieves sustained profitability on its growing revenue base.
However, Castellum remains a micro-cap stock trading at $0.61 with daily dollar volume of ~$717K that fails the IOWN liquidity floor, making institutional position sizing effectively impossible. The company is still unprofitable after six years of operation (-4.13% net margin, -6.75% ROE) with a H1 2026 net loss of $1.43M, and the Forward P/E of 60.93x implies minimal near-term earnings [4]. Revenue growth was inconsistent — Q2 2026 was flat YoY at $13.9M after Q1's 23% surge, and TTM revenue growth measured by yfinance is -1.1% [4]. The backlog is concentrated in a small number of prime contracts, creating customer and execution risk for a ~244-person company. CEO Ives has been in role just two years, and the company's turnaround has not yet been tested through a downturn in defense spending.
