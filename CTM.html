Castellum, Inc. is a cybersecurity, electronic warfare, and software engineering services company focused on the U.S. Department of Defense and other federal agencies. The company operates through subsidiaries including Specialty Systems Inc. (SSI), Corvus Consulting, and Mainnerve, providing mission-critical IT services, electronic warfare solutions, and cybersecurity capabilities. Founded in 2020 and headquartered in Rockville, Maryland.

Investment Thesis
Castellum's investment case rests on its transition from startup to operational defense contractor with a record $273.3M backlog anchored by three prime contracts totaling ~$220M with five-year periods of performance [2]. FY2025 revenue grew 15.2% to $52.9M, operating loss improved $4.4M YoY to -$2.8M, and adjusted EBITDA reached $1.0M [1]. Q1 2026 accelerated to 23% revenue growth ($14.3M) with $938M qualified pipeline suggesting continued momentum. Clean balance sheet ($15.8M cash, D/E 3%) provides flexibility, and the $59M market cap trades at barely 1x revenue against ~$44M in backlog expected to convert in the next 12 months.
However, Castellum remains a micro-cap stock trading at $0.63 with borderline daily dollar volume (~$1.3M), making institutional position sizing extremely difficult. The company is still unprofitable after five years (-2.85% net margin, -5.08% ROE) with no clear path to sustained GAAP profitability [4]. The $273M backlog is concentrated in three prime contracts, creating significant customer and execution risk for a ~300-person company. CEO Ives has been in role only one year, and the acquisition-driven growth model requires continued successful integration. Former GC Wright's sale of 297K shares at $1.04 — 65% above current price — raises questions about insider conviction. At the current run rate, only $44M of backlog converts in 12 months, and the 19.5-quarter backlog-to-revenue ratio signals potential capacity constraints.
Key Catalysts
  1. Ramp-up of three prime contracts totaling ~$220M with 16% of $273M backlog expected to convert within 12 months and 49% within 24 months
  2. Path to sustained profitability as operating loss narrows from -$7.2M (2024) to -$2.8M (2025) with Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $0.4M suggesting potential breakeven by late 2026
  3. Conversion from $938M qualified pipeline into funded backlog would validate Castellum's growing prime contractor credibility with DoD customers
Key Risks
  1. Micro-cap stock ($59M market cap) with borderline liquidity at ~$1.3M daily dollar volume creates significant entry/exit execution risk and institutional disqualification
  2. Persistent unprofitability after 5 years of operation with -2.85% net margin and -5.08% ROE — the leap from $1M adjusted EBITDA to meaningful GAAP profitability requires operating leverage that remains unproven
  3. Backlog concentration risk: three prime contracts represent ~80% of the $273M backlog, and the 19.5-quarter backlog-to-revenue ratio signals a company that may lack the capacity to execute on its commitments
