Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Coterra Energy Inc. is an independent oil and gas company engaged in the development, exploration, and production of oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids. Operations span the Permian Basin (Texas/New Mexico), Marcellus Shale (Pennsylvania), and Anadarko Basin (Oklahoma). Formed from the October 2021 merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy, Coterra completed a transformative merger with Devon Energy in May 2026 to create one of the largest U.S. independent E&P companies.

Investment Thesis
Coterra delivered Q1 2026 results with $1.95B revenue, $1.65B operating cash flow, and production growth led by a 16% surge in oil volumes to 14.7 MMBbl and 32% NGL growth [1]. The balance sheet is pristine at 28% D/E with standalone FCF guidance of $2.35B at strip pricing, supporting a 3.21% dividend yield with 40.6% payout ratio and ample room for continued growth [5]. The completed Devon merger (May 2026) creates a combined entity exceeding 1 million BOE/d across Permian, Delaware, Williston, Marcellus, and Anadarko basins, targeting $1B/year in pre-tax synergies by year-end 2027 [2]. Forward P/E of 14.2 and PEG of 0.61 reflect attractive valuation relative to growth prospects, and growing AI data center power demand provides a structural tailwind for natural gas consumption.
EPS declined 10% YoY in Q1 2026 ($0.61 vs $0.68) as derivative losses and higher operating costs offset volume gains, underscoring the inherent earnings volatility of commodity-driven businesses [1]. The Devon merger introduces significant integration risk — combining two large organizations with distinct cultures while executing multi-basin development programs is operationally complex, and E&P mega-mergers have a mixed track record [2]. Revenue and profitability remain directly correlated with oil and natural gas prices, with the multi-year revenue trajectory flat when adjusted for commodity cycles. The Dimock, PA water contamination case (criminal no-contest plea, $16M settlement) and a 74% shareholder vote in favor of methane disclosure create ongoing regulatory, reputational, and litigation risk [4]. ROE of 11.9% below the 15% target reflects capital-intensive E&P economics even with conservative leverage.
Key Catalysts
  1. Devon merger synergies targeting $1B/year in pre-tax savings by year-end 2027, with combined production exceeding 1 million BOE/d and enhanced multi-basin diversification across Permian, Delaware, Williston, Marcellus, and Anadarko
  2. Standalone FCF guidance of $2.35B at strip pricing with a 50% reinvestment rate, enabling continued dividend growth from the current 3.21% yield and potential special dividends or accelerated buybacks
  3. Structural increase in natural gas demand from AI data center electricity generation and LNG export capacity additions, benefiting Coterra's leading low-cost Marcellus Shale position
Key Risks
  1. Commodity price volatility remains the dominant risk — a sustained decline in natural gas or oil prices would compress margins and FCF despite low-cost operations, with revenue entirely driven by hydrocarbon prices
  2. Devon merger integration complexity including cultural alignment, basin optimization priorities, and achieving $1B/year in targeted synergies; the activist challenge in November 2025 signals governance concerns
  3. Environmental and legal liability from the Dimock, PA water contamination case and regulatory tightening on methane emissions, with a 74% shareholder methane disclosure vote signaling investor concern that actual emissions may be 50-100% higher than reported [4]
