Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Coterra Energy was an independent oil and gas producer with Marcellus Shale natural gas, Permian Basin and Anadarko Basin assets, formed by the October 2021 merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy. FY2025 revenue was $7.65B with $1.72B of net income and $1.63B of free cash flow. Coterra NO LONGER TRADES: its $58B all-stock merger with Devon Energy closed May 7-8, 2026, each CTRA share converted into 0.70 DVN shares, and the stock was delisted from the NYSE (last quote $32.56). This report scores pre-merger metrics for reference only; the investable successor is DVN.

Investment Thesis
CTRA is no longer investable on its own: the Devon merger closed May 7-8, 2026, converting each share into 0.70 DVN shares, and Coterra holders now own about 46% of the combined company [1]. On its final full year, Coterra was financially strong, with FY2025 revenue of $7.65B (+40%), net income of $1.72B, free cash flow of $1.63B, D/E of 23% and a 40% payout ratio [2][3][5]. The combined company targets $1B of annual pre-tax synergies by year-end 2027 and has authorized an $8B buyback through June 2029, which is the value path for former CTRA holders [1].
The bear case was always commodity exposure and a weak purpose profile. Dividends per share fell from ~$2.50 in 2022 to ~$0.84 in 2024 as variable payouts tracked prices, and FY2025 ended with only $119M of cash after $3.24B of acquisitions [5]. The Dimock contamination case, which produced a 2022 no-contest criminal plea and a $16.29M water-system obligation, is a lasting reputational stain [6]. Any decision should now be made on DVN, which carries the integration risk of a $58B merger and a new CEO.
