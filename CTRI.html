Company Profile
Industrials ·Utility Infrastructure Services ·NYSE ·United States
Centuri Holdings, Inc. is a strategic utility infrastructure services company partnering with regulated utilities to build, maintain, and modernize gas and electric energy networks across the United States and Canada. Operating through four segments — U.S. Gas, U.S. Electric, Canadian Gas, and Canadian Electric — the company provides installation, maintenance, replacement, and repair services. Under CEO Christian Brown (since December 2024), the company achieved record Q1 2026 revenue of $723M (+31% YoY) with 76% gross profit growth and a record $6.5B backlog. Founded 1909, IPO'd from Southwest Gas Holdings in April 2024, headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona.

Investment Thesis
Centuri is riding the most significant utility infrastructure spending cycle in decades, driven by grid modernization, renewable energy integration, and AI-fueled data center power demand [1][4]. H1 2026 revenue of $1.69B (+32% YoY) and Q1 gross profit surging 76% demonstrate accelerating execution [1]. The $6.5B record backlog provides approximately 8 quarters of revenue visibility with a $13B+ bidding pipeline. The stock's 30% decline from $27.50 to $19.35 creates a more attractive entry point — Forward P/E of 20.49x and PEG of 1.01 are reasonable for 32.9% revenue growth [5]. Management's 'Vision One Centuri' plan targets 10-15% revenue CAGR through 2029 with gross margins expanding to 8.7-9.7%, and 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $280-310M represents meaningful profit expansion. The Inspire Impact Score of 42 (ALIGNED) supports allocation for faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on profitability, leverage, and governance risk. Centuri's 0.85% net profit margin and 4.03% ROE are among the lowest in the industrial sector — even at $3.4B+ TTM revenue, the company barely earns money [5]. D/E of 108.19% with $947M total debt creates financial risk if the utility spending cycle decelerates. The trailing P/E of 58.64x reflects depressed current earnings, and earnings growth has actually declined 33.3% YoY. Securities fraud investigations initiated in August 2024 remain a governance overhang. CEO Brown has been in role less than a year, limiting confidence in execution continuity. The 30% stock decline may reflect market concern that the profitability inflection is taking longer than expected — the 'Vision One Centuri' margin targets of 8.7-9.7% gross margin remain aspirational when current gross margins are 7.8%. The company does not pay a dividend, offering no income return while investors wait for margin expansion.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record $6.5B backlog and $13B+ bidding pipeline converting to revenue at accelerating rates, with 2026 guidance of $3.24-3.54B representing 8-18% growth from the $3.0B 2025 base [1]
  2. AI-driven data center expansion requiring massive utility infrastructure investment — Centuri is positioned as a direct beneficiary of increasing load demand driven by AI, creating a secular multi-year tailwind [4]
  3. Gross margin expansion toward the 8.7-9.7% Vision One Centuri target from the current ~7.8% would transform bottom-line profitability and potentially rerate the stock toward industrial comps [1]
Key Risks
  1. Sub-1% net profit margin and 4.03% ROE mean the company barely earns money despite $3.4B+ TTM revenue — any project cost overrun, weather disruption, or pricing pressure could push quarterly earnings negative [5]
  2. D/E of 108.19% with $947M total debt creates financial vulnerability if the utility infrastructure spending cycle decelerates or interest rates rise further, squeezing both margins and the balance sheet [5]
  3. Securities fraud investigations (August 2024) and CEO Brown's limited tenure (since December 2024) create governance and execution uncertainty during a critical growth and margin expansion phase [1]
