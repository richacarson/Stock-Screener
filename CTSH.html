Investment Thesis
Cognizant trades at 10.6x forward earnings with near-zero debt (D/E 7%), a 2.2% dividend yield growing at 7.3% CAGR, and improving operational momentum under CEO Ravi Kumar S [1][4]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 4.5% to $5.5B with 12% Financial Services growth, and management raised full-year guidance to $22.04-22.35B with adjusted EPS of $5.70-5.82 (+8-10%). Trailing 12-month bookings of $29.6B (book-to-bill 1.4x) with 11% growth provide forward revenue visibility. The AI Builder strategy positions Cognizant to capture enterprise AI implementation demand, and the stock's 48% recovery from June lows of $41 to $61 signals improving sentiment. The payout ratio of 27.8% provides significant dividend growth runway [4].
The bear case centers on structural AI disruption risk and modest growth. Cognizant's core business model — selling human labor hours for IT services — faces direct substitution pressure from AI coding tools that could reduce demand for traditional outsourcing by 20-30% over time [2]. Cognizant's own research acknowledges AI disruption is 'more extensive and swift than anticipated.' Revenue growth of 4.5% YoY and ~4% 5-year CAGR signal structural growth limitations for a company in a rapidly evolving industry. Mass layoffs affecting 10-20% of US workforce, rebranded as 'performance management,' damage employee trust and risk institutional knowledge loss during a critical transformation period [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors. While the stock has recovered significantly, the forward PE of 10.6x remains below historical averages, suggesting the market continues to price in structural AI disruption risk to the IT services model.
