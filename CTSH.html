Investment Thesis
Cognizant at $62.47 with a $28.1B market cap trades at a deep value multiple of 9.86x forward earnings (PEG 0.89), reflecting investor skepticism toward IT services amid AI disruption fears. Q2 2026 revenue of $5.5B grew 4.5% YoY with Financial Services (+12%) leading recovery, while adjusted EPS guidance of $5.70-$5.82 represents 8-10% growth [1][4]. The balance sheet is exceptionally clean at 14.5% D/E with $2.2B in free cash flow, supporting a 2.15% dividend yield at a conservative 27.5% payout ratio with significant growth runway. CEO Ravi Kumar S is positioning the company as an 'AI builder' with 4,000+ AI engagements and outcome-based pricing models, while trailing 12-month bookings of $29.6B (+11% YoY, book-to-bill 1.4x) provide forward revenue visibility [2][5].
The bear case centers on structural AI disruption risk to the labor-arbitrage business model. Cognizant generates the bulk of its revenue from headcount-based IT services — application development, testing, maintenance, and BPO — where AI coding assistants and process automation tools threaten 20-30% headcount compression over the next 3-5 years [2]. Revenue growth has decelerated from 5.8% in Q1 to 4.5% in Q2 2026, and management cut the top end of full-year guidance to $22.04-$22.35B, while the 5-year revenue CAGR of 3.2% reflects chronically low organic growth with Belcan masking 2-3 points of inorganic contribution [4]. Profit margin of 10.3% remains structurally below technology sector averages, limiting pricing power in an increasingly AI-commoditized market. Mass layoffs affecting 10-20% of the US workforce, rebranded as performance management, damage employee trust and institutional knowledge during a critical transformation period. The 'AI builder' pivot requires Cognizant to cannibalize its own labor-intensive delivery model, a transition that Accenture and Infosys are pursuing with greater scale and resources.
