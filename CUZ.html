Investment Thesis
Cousins Properties raised FY2026 FFO guidance to $2.92-$2.98/share (midpoint $2.95, +3.9% vs 2025) after Q2 2026 beat expectations with revenue of $268.5M, FFO of $0.75/share, and same-property cash NOI growth of 5.9% [1][2]. Record H1 2026 leasing of 1.9M SF, the 49th consecutive quarter of positive cash rent growth (9.2% Q2 roll-ups), and occupancy climbing to 89.4% (targeting 90% by year-end) demonstrate that Sun Belt trophy-office fundamentals remain robust despite broader office sector pessimism [2]. The 4.0% dividend yield with a 43.4% FFO-based payout ratio appears sustainable, and the forward P/FFO of ~10.8x represents an attractive entry point for a REIT with third straight year of FFO expansion (~4% CAGR since 2023) [3]. The new $1.2B unsecured credit facility at improved spreads provides acquisition capacity for opportunistic growth. The Inspire Impact Score of 48 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions makes CUZ compatible with faith-based mandates.
The bear case centers on stalled dividend growth and GAAP-metric structural weakness. The dividend has been flat at $0.32/quarter with 0% five-year growth CAGR since the 2009 crisis-era cut, a significant drag on Infrastructure scoring and unattractive for income-growth investors [3]. Both ROE (-0.11%) and profit margin (-0.53%) sit at the Infrastructure floor, reflecting REIT depreciation accounting rather than cash-flow problems, but this structural GAAP issue cannot be fully dismissed — the company generates strong FFO but reports negligible GAAP earnings. The February 2026 AI scare trade that hit office REITs broadly signals emerging investor concern that AI-driven corporate productivity could reduce long-term office-space demand, a secular risk that Cousins has not directly addressed [1]. Innovation (2/10) and Just Cause (1/3) scores are inherently low for a REIT business model — Cousins is a competently run asset manager rather than a differentiated, purpose-driven enterprise, limiting multiple-expansion catalysts beyond continued leasing execution and potential dividend resumption of growth.
Key Catalysts
  1. Occupancy trajectory toward 90% year-end target from 89.4%, supported by record H1 2026 leasing volume of 1.9M SF and 49 consecutive quarters of positive cash rent growth with 9.2% Q2 roll-ups [2]
  2. Sun Belt population and corporate-relocation tailwinds continuing to differentiate CUZ's trophy-office portfolio from struggling gateway-market office REITs, with same-property cash NOI growth of 5.9% outpacing the broader office sector [1]
  3. Resumption of dividend growth from the flat $0.32/quarter level, enabled by rising FFO ($2.95 guidance midpoint vs $1.28 annual dividend = 43.4% payout) — any increase would materially improve Infrastructure scoring and attract income-growth investors [3]
Key Risks
  1. Dividend growth has been flat for years (0% five-year CAGR) following the 2009 crisis-era cut, and both GAAP ROE (-0.11%) and profit margin (-0.53%) sit at the Infrastructure floor, structurally limiting the income-quality case for a Dividend-sleeve holding [3]
  2. AI-driven corporate productivity concerns flagged by the February 2026 office REIT selloff could reduce long-term aggregate office-space demand, a secular risk that no amount of Sun Belt positioning fully mitigates [1]
  3. D/E increased to 83.63% from 73.5% alongside the new $1.2B credit facility, and with only 351 employees managing a 21.3M SF portfolio, operational scaling risk exists if leasing velocity sustains at record H1 levels [2][3]
