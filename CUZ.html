Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Cousins Properties is a fully integrated, self-administered and self-managed REIT founded in 1958, focused on developing, acquiring, leasing and managing 'trophy lifestyle office' Class A buildings in high-growth Sun Belt markets including Atlanta, Austin, Charlotte, Dallas, Houston, Nashville, Tampa and Phoenix. Investment Thesis
Cousins Properties raised its full-year 2026 FFO guidance midpoint to $2.94/share (3.5% growth over 2025) after a Q1 2026 beat on FFO ($0.73/share) with same-property cash NOI up 5.5% YoY, driven by record first-quarter leasing volume of 932,000 square feet and 48 consecutive quarters of positive cash rent growth (15.2% roll-up) [1]. The Sun Belt trophy-office concentration strategy continues to outperform broader office fundamentals, and the 4.0% dividend yield with a 45.45% payout ratio appears sustainable off current FFO [1][4]. Glassdoor sentiment (3.8/5, 78% recommend) suggests a stable, well-regarded operating culture supporting execution.
The bear case centers on stalled dividend growth and negative GAAP profitability: the dividend has been flat at $0.32/quarter with 0% five-year growth CAGR since a 2009 crisis-era cut, and both ROE (-0.10%) and profit margin (-0.53%) sit at the Infrastructure floor, even if this partly reflects REIT depreciation accounting rather than cash weakness [3][4]. The February 2026 'AI scare trade' that hit office and real-estate-services stocks broadly signals a real, if early-stage, market concern that AI-driven corporate productivity gains could structurally reduce office-space demand over the medium term — a risk Cousins has not directly addressed [1]. Innovation and differentiated purpose both score low: this is a well-run but largely finite-game operator competing on asset quality and market selection rather than a distinctive cause.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued above-decade-average leasing velocity (932,000 sq ft in Q1 2026) sustaining the 48-consecutive-quarter positive cash rent growth streak and supporting the raised FY2026 FFO guidance of $2.94/share [1]
  2. Sun Belt population and corporate-relocation tailwinds continuing to differentiate CUZ's trophy-office portfolio from struggling gateway-market office REITs
  3. A resumption of dividend growth (currently flat since the 2009 cut) if FFO growth and the 45.45% payout ratio provide room, which would materially improve the Infrastructure dividend sub-score [4]
Key Risks
  1. Dividend growth has been flat for years (0% five-year CAGR) following a 2009 crisis-era cut from $0.25 to $0.15/quarter, and both ROE and profit margin sit at the Infrastructure floor, limiting the income-quality case for a Dividend-sleeve holding [3][4]
  2. The February 2026 AI-disruption selloff that hit CUZ alongside JLL, CBRE and SL Green signals emerging investor concern that AI-driven corporate productivity could reduce long-term office-space demand [1]
  3. Innovation (2/10) and Just Cause (1/3) scores are low — Cousins is a competently run asset manager rather than a differentiated, purpose-driven business, leaving limited multiple-expansion catalysts beyond continued leasing execution
