Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto & Truck Dealerships ·NYSE ·United States
Carvana operates the largest e-commerce platform for buying and selling used cars in the United States, combining online purchase and financing with in-house reconditioning, logistics, home delivery, car vending machines and the ADESA wholesale auction network. Q3 2026 revenue rose ~42% YoY to about $8.0B on ~217,000 retail units (+39%), following a record Q2 of $7.38B revenue and 197,325 units. The stock split 5-for-1 in May 2026, and the Garcia family controls more than 80% of the voting power. Headquartered in Tempe, Arizona.

Investment Thesis
Carvana is compounding at scale: Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $7.38B (+52%), 197,325 retail units (+38%), $513M net income and a record $769M adjusted EBITDA at a 10.4% margin, and Q3 revenue grew ~42% to about $8.0B on ~217,000 units (+39%) [1][2]. Management guides 2026 adjusted EBITDA to $2.7-3.0B versus $2.24B in 2025, and the integrated reconditioning, logistics and ADESA footprint gives it a cost and selection advantage that is hard to replicate [1]. ROE of 54.7%, a forward P/E of 30 and a forward PEG of 0.41 point to growth that is still cheap relative to its pace [2][4].
The bear case centers on governance and earnings quality. Gotham City Research's January 2026 report alleged that Carvana moved more than $1B of expenses through undisclosed related-party dealings with DriveTime, controlled by the CEO's father, sending the stock down 14%; Carvana called it 'inaccurate and intentionally misleading' [6]. The Garcia family controls more than 80% of votes, the board need not be majority independent, and the audit committee chair is a long-time DriveTime banker [6]. Reported 2025 earnings included a ~$2.8B tax benefit, the 6.2% net margin leaves little cushion in a used-car downturn, and debt totals $5.70B [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Delivery of full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA at the high end of the $2.7-3.0B guide with sequential retail-unit growth into Q4 2026 [1][2]
  2. Continued share gains, with retail units growing ~39% YoY against a flat used-car market, as ADESA sites add reconditioning capacity [2]
  3. Revenue growth aligning with unit growth from Q3 2026 as gross-revenue accounting effects fade, improving quality and comparability of reported growth [1]
Key Risks
  1. Regulatory or audit follow-up on Gotham City Research's allegation of more than $1B in related-party expense shifting to DriveTime [6]
  2. Controlled-company governance: Garcia family >80% voting control, non-majority-independent board, and persistent insider selling [6][8]
  3. Used-car credit and pricing cycle: a 6.2% net margin and $5.70B of debt leave limited cushion if subprime auto credit or used-vehicle prices deteriorate [2]
