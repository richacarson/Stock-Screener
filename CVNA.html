Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Auto & Truck Dealerships ·NYSE ·United States
Carvana operates the largest e-commerce platform for buying and selling used cars in the United States, offering a fully online purchasing experience with home delivery or vending machine pickup. The company also operates ADESA wholesale auction sites and provides financing, insurance, and vehicle service contracts. Q2 2026 delivered all-time record revenue of $7.376B (+52% YoY) with 197,325 retail units (+38% YoY), record net income of $513M (7.0% margin), and record adjusted EBITDA of $769M (10.4% margin). Founded in 2012, headquartered in Tempe, Arizona. Executed a 5-for-1 stock split effective May 7, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Carvana has completed one of the most dramatic corporate turnarounds in history, delivering consecutive all-time records through Q2 2026: $7.376B revenue (+52% YoY), $513M net income (7.0% margin), $769M adjusted EBITDA (10.4% margin), and 197,325 retail units (+38% YoY) [1]. Both Q1 and Q2 2026 set all-time records across every key metric, demonstrating the turnaround has real operating leverage, not just top-line recovery. At a forward PEG of approximately 0.80, the stock offers classic GARP characteristics for a company growing revenue 52% with expanding profitability. The online used car market remains massively underpenetrated — Carvana's annualized ~790K units represent approximately 2% of the ~40M annual U.S. used car transactions. The ADESA wholesale infrastructure provides capacity to scale significantly further, and management projects continued significant growth for full-year 2026. ROE has expanded to 60.2%, reflecting the operating leverage in the model [4].
The bear case centers on governance risk, valuation, and cyclicality. Hindenburg Research published a report alleging Carvana's turnaround is a 'mirage,' claiming $800M in loan sales to a suspected undisclosed related party and accounting manipulation — while insiders have sold $414M net over the past 12 months (191 insider sells vs. 1 buy) [3]. An SEC investigation was disclosed via FOIA, and multiple securities law firms are investigating potential violations. Even setting aside the short-seller allegations, $5.62B in total debt against $2.90B cash creates meaningful leverage risk in a cyclical used car market. At 41x trailing PE, the valuation requires sustained 40%+ revenue growth, and used car prices are cyclical and sensitive to economic conditions — any recession-driven decline in vehicle values would compress gross profit per unit and create inventory markdown risk across the reconditioning pipeline.
Key Catalysts
  1. Management projects significant growth in both retail units and adjusted EBITDA for full-year 2026, with Q2 delivering record 197,325 units and $769M EBITDA confirming the growth trajectory [1]
  2. Operating leverage from the ADESA wholesale infrastructure as reconditioning capacity utilization increases — EBITDA margin held at 10.4% in Q2 even as units scaled to 197K, with room for further margin expansion [1]
  3. Accelerating free cash flow from $513M quarterly net income positions Carvana to deleverage the $5.62B debt balance, with potential credit upgrades unlocking lower financing costs and higher equity value
Key Risks
  1. Governance and accounting risk — Hindenburg Research alleges undisclosed related party transactions, an SEC investigation is pending, and insiders have sold $414M net over 12 months (191 sells vs. 1 buy) while the Garcia family retains outsized control [3]
  2. Cyclical exposure — at 41x trailing PE, the valuation prices in sustained hypergrowth in a cyclical used car market where declining vehicle prices would compress gross profit per unit and create inventory markdown risk across the reconditioning pipeline
  3. Competitive response from CarMax (investing in online capabilities), AutoNation, and franchised dealer groups narrowing Carvana's first-mover advantage, while $5.62B debt and 121% D/E create financial vulnerability in any downturn [4]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Carvana's core business — buying, inspecting, reconditioning, storing, financing, and delivering physical used cars — requires massive physical infrastructure including ADESA wholesale auction sites, inspection and reconditioning centers, car hauler fleet, and the iconic vending machines that AI cannot replicate [1]. The used car transaction involves physical logistics, title transfer, state regulatory compliance, financing underwriting, and last-mile delivery that are fundamentally physical-world processes. AI-powered pricing and customer acquisition tools could emerge at competitors, but Carvana's vertically integrated physical operations create a deep defensive moat. The company's data advantage from processing hundreds of thousands of transactions annually further strengthens its AI-powered pricing capabilities. AI Opportunity (1/1): Carvana actively uses AI/ML through its Propel AI acquisition for vehicle pricing optimization, demand forecasting, logistics routing, and customer chatbots, generating measurable efficiency improvements in a business processing $22.5B+ in annual vehicle transactions [2].
