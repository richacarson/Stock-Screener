Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail - Recreational Vehicles ·NYSE ·United States
Camping World Holdings is the largest RV dealer network in the United States, operating over 200 retail locations across 42 states through the Camping World and Good Sam brands. The company sells new and used recreational vehicles, provides RV service and parts, and operates the Good Sam Club — a membership-based community of over 2 million outdoor enthusiasts offering roadside assistance, insurance, campground discounts, and financing. CWH completed a CEO transition in January 2026, with founder Marcus Lemonis retiring and longtime executive Matthew Wagner assuming the CEO role. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.9B (-2.1% YoY) with the company guiding to $230M-$270M adjusted EBITDA for FY2026, reduced from prior guidance of $275M-$325M.

Investment Thesis
CWH at $7.10 trades at 11.5x forward earnings with a 7% dividend yield, reflecting a deeply discounted valuation for the largest RV dealer network in the United States with 200+ locations [1]. Q2 2026 showed sequential improvement with $43.7M net income after a $26.7M Q1 loss, and record market share suggests the company is gaining ground even in a declining revenue environment (-2.1% YoY). The Good Sam Club with 2M+ members provides recurring revenue visibility, and new CEO Matthew Wagner's operational focus on SGA reduction and market share growth could stabilize profitability if the RV cycle inflects. The PEG ratio of 0.92 suggests the market is pricing in a return to earnings growth.
The bear case is substantial: CWH carries $1.416B in long-term debt with a 5.6x net leverage ratio, D/E of 414%, and negative TTM ROE of -33.39% — a balance sheet that leaves no margin for error if the RV downturn extends [2][4]. The FY2026 EBITDA guidance cut to $230M-$270M (from $275M-$325M) signals management has limited visibility and is playing defense [1]. The 7% dividend yield on $0.50/share annual payments consumes 80.6% of estimated forward EPS of $0.62, making the dividend vulnerable to any earnings shortfall — the 5yr dividend CAGR of -4.3% confirms the payout is already deteriorating. The CEO transition from founder Marcus Lemonis is untested, and Comparably's 'D' executive team rating suggests cultural challenges that could impair execution [3]. A securities fraud investigation adds legal overhang [5]. The RV industry faces secular headwinds from consumer affordability pressures and elevated financing costs that suppress big-ticket discretionary purchases.
Key Catalysts
  1. RV industry cyclical recovery could drive significant earnings leverage — CWH's record market share positions it to capture outsized gains when unit volumes inflect, with consensus FY2026 EPS of $0.62 representing a return to profitability [1]
  2. SGA reduction and operational efficiency initiatives under new CEO Matthew Wagner could expand margins — the company identified SGA cuts as a top-three priority alongside market share and Good Sam growth [2]
  3. Good Sam Club acceleration with 2M+ members provides a platform for higher-margin recurring revenue from memberships, insurance, financing, and campground reservations that reduces dependence on volatile new vehicle sales [1]
Key Risks
  1. Unsustainable dividend: $0.50/share annual dividend consumes 80.6% of estimated FY2026 EPS of $0.62, and any earnings miss could force a second dividend cut, devastating the stock at its yield-floor valuation [4]
  2. Balance sheet fragility with $1.416B long-term debt, 414% D/E, and 5.6x net leverage ratio leaves CWH vulnerable to rising interest costs and limits financial flexibility — a prolonged RV downturn could trigger covenant pressure [2]
  3. Securities fraud investigation regarding corporate reporting transparency adds legal risk and potential for material financial penalties, compounding governance concerns during a critical leadership transition [5]
