Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Camping World Holdings, Inc. is the largest retailer of recreational vehicles (RVs) and related products and services in the United States. The company operates a network of RV dealerships, offers F&I (finance and insurance) products, provides RV service and maintenance, and runs the Good Sam membership program with millions of active members. Founded by Marcus Lemonis, the company completed a CEO transition to Matthew Wagner effective January 1, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Camping World is the largest RV dealer in the United States with $6.37B in annual revenue, a dominant dealership network, and the Good Sam membership platform serving millions of outdoor enthusiasts [1]. New CEO Matthew Wagner's focus on technology innovation and customer experience could drive operational improvement, and adjusted EBITDA grew 35%+ in 2025 showing early signs of recovery. Net debt leverage improved from 8.1x to 5.6x, and the forward P/E of 8.84 implies significant upside if the company can return to profitability [4]. At $6.46/share (down 65% over 52 weeks), the stock prices in a worst-case scenario that may already be discounted.
The bear case is severe: D/E of 1,170% with $1.47B in long-term debt against minimal equity, negative net income of -$94M, and -1.49% profit margin make CWH one of the most financially distressed companies in its peer set [1][4]. The securities fraud investigation adds legal risk, the executive team is rated in the Bottom 20% of peers by Comparably, and the founder-CEO's retirement during maximum financial distress raises governance concerns [3][5]. Revenue growth has stagnated at 3-4% annually, and the RV industry is deeply cyclical — elevated interest rates suppress financing affordability, and consumer discretionary spending remains pressured. The dividend has been cut (5-year growth rate of -4.3%), signaling the company is in cash preservation mode rather than shareholder return mode.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO Matthew Wagner's technology and customer experience focus could drive margin improvement and operational efficiency across the dealership network
  2. Net debt leverage ratio improved from 8.1x to 5.6x, and continued deleveraging from adjusted EBITDA guidance of $275-$325M could unlock equity value
  3. RV industry cyclical recovery as interest rates decline would drive unit sales acceleration across Camping World's dominant dealer network
Key Risks
  1. D/E ratio of 1,170% and -$94M net income indicate severe financial distress that could trigger covenant violations or force highly dilutive capital raises
  2. Securities fraud investigation and Comparably executive team rating of 'D' (Bottom 20%) create compounding governance and cultural risks during a critical leadership transition
  3. RV demand is highly cyclical and interest-rate sensitive — sustained high financing costs would continue to suppress unit sales and customer affordability, prolonging the margin recovery timeline
