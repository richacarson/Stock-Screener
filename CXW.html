Company Profile
Industrials ·Security & Protection Services ·NYSE ·United States
CoreCivic, Inc. owns and operates correctional, detention, and residential reentry facilities for government partners across CoreCivic Safety, CoreCivic Community, and CoreCivic Properties segments, providing rehabilitation, education, faith-based, and health care services alongside core security operations. Its largest federal customer, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), drove revenue from $133.2M in Q1 2025 to $261.3M in Q1 2026 (+96.2%) as detention capacity expanded. Total revenue grew 25.8% YoY in Q1 2026 to approximately $615M, with 2026 management guidance of $1.49-$1.59 EPS and $437-$445M EBITDA. Founded in 1983 and headquartered in Brentwood, Tennessee.
13,651 Employees ·www.corecivic.com

Investment Thesis
CoreCivic is delivering exceptional near-term financial performance on the back of expanded federal immigration detention demand: ICE revenue grew 96.2% YoY to $261.3M in Q1 2026, total Q1 2026 revenue rose 25.8% to roughly $615M, and management's 2026 guidance calls for $1.49-$1.59 EPS and $437-$445M EBITDA based on contracts already signed by year-end 2025 [1]. At a Forward P/E of 13.56 and Forward PEG of 1.06, the stock is reasonably valued against 27.3% YoY revenue growth and an 8.77% ROE that is still improving as reactivated facilities scale. Daily dollar volume of roughly $52M provides institutional-grade liquidity, and the physical, government-contracted nature of the business makes it essentially immune to AI disruption, scoring 9/10 on AI Resilience [1].
The bear case is dominated by policy dependency and organizational quality concerns. CoreCivic's earnings surge is a direct function of federal immigration enforcement policy that management does not control — a change in administration or executive action (as occurred with the 2021 order to phase out DOJ use of private prisons) could reverse the ICE-driven growth cycle as quickly as it appeared [1]. Glassdoor ratings of 2.4-2.5/5 for work-life balance and culture point to organizational strain, and a broad pattern of insider selling — including a 120,000-share sale by the Chief Innovation Officer and a 50,000-share sale by the CFO in August 2026 — occurred during this record growth period with no offsetting purchases, suggesting leadership may view current conditions as a temporary window [3]. The company's for-profit model, which benefits financially from higher detention volumes, also excludes it from many ESG-screened and faith-based institutional portfolios despite the CSV-sourced Inspire score landing in ALIGNED territory, creating a persistent valuation and ownership-base constraint.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued expansion of ICE detention capacity utilization across CoreCivic's facility portfolio, supported by contracts already signed by year-end 2025 underpinning 2026 guidance of $437-$445M EBITDA [1]
  2. Potential additional facility reactivations or new government contract awards as federal detention capacity needs continue to grow, extending the current earnings re-acceleration
  3. Reasonable valuation (Forward P/E 13.56, Forward PEG 1.06) relative to 27.3% YoY revenue growth could support a re-rating if the current demand environment persists through 2026-2027
Key Risks
  1. Policy reversal risk — CoreCivic's growth is almost entirely dependent on federal immigration enforcement policy; a change in administration, executive order, or budget action could rapidly compress ICE-driven revenue and occupancy, as occurred with the 2021 phase-out order [1]
  2. Broad-based insider selling during a record earnings period — the Chief Innovation Officer, CFO, and other executives sold shares in mid-2026 with no offsetting purchases, a pattern that raises questions about leadership's own conviction in the durability of current growth [3]
  3. Low Glassdoor culture and work-life-balance scores (2.4-2.5/5) combined with a for-profit incarceration model that structurally excludes the stock from many ESG and faith-based institutional portfolios, limiting the potential investor base and valuation ceiling [2]
