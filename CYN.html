Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Cyngn develops autonomous vehicle technology for industrial customers: its DriveMod kit retrofits tuggers and forklifts for autonomous material handling, monetized through Enterprise Autonomy Suite (EAS) software subscriptions. Q2 2026 revenue was $144,459 (up ~4x YoY) against a $6.4M net loss, and management acknowledged that commercial execution lagged expectations, prompting a restructuring. The stock trades near $0.35, received a Nasdaq minimum-bid deficiency notice with a compliance deadline of April 12, 2027, and is seeking authority for a second reverse split after a 1-for-150 split in February 2025.

Investment Thesis
The bull case rests on optionality in industrial autonomy: Q2 2026 revenue rose about fourfold to $144,459 from EAS subscriptions on DriveMod tugger deployments, the DriveMod Forklift is now with select customers, and management says liquidity of roughly $39.7M is sufficient for at least 12 months [1][3]. The restructuring of sales processes around qualified tugger opportunities and wider use of AI-assisted tools could lower the ~$6.9M quarterly cost base [1].
The bear case dominates. Trailing revenue is $387K against a $26.9M trailing net loss, management admits commercial execution has lagged, and the share count rose from 7.97M to 13.6M in one quarter through a $1.93 offering [2][7]. The stock trades near $0.35, Nasdaq has given it until April 12, 2027 to regain a $1.00 bid, and a second reverse split is being sought after the February 2025 1-for-150 split [7]. Daily dollar volume of about $66,000 fails IOWN's $1M liquidity floor, so the stock cannot be traded at institutional size [3].
