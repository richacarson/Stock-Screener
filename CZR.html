Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Resorts & Casinos ·NASDAQ ·United States
Caesars Entertainment operates one of the largest U.S. casino-resort and entertainment portfolios, including Las Vegas properties, regional casinos, and the Caesars Digital online sports-betting and iGaming segment. On May 28, 2026, Caesars agreed to be acquired by Tilman Fertitta's Fertitta Entertainment in an all-cash deal worth approximately $17.6 billion (including ~$11.9B of assumed debt), with shareholders to receive $31.00 per share; the deal awaits antitrust and gaming-regulatory clearance and is expected to close by the end of 2027 [1][5]. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.99B (+3% YoY) with a net loss of $62M; Digital segment Adjusted EBITDA declined to $68M from $80M a year earlier.

Investment Thesis
Caesars agreed in May 2026 to be acquired by Fertitta Entertainment for $31.00 per share in cash, an approximately 4.2% premium to the current $29.76 price, in a transaction valued at ~$17.6B including assumed debt [1][5]. The deal validates real underlying asset value in Caesars' Las Vegas real estate portfolio and its growing Digital sports-betting/iGaming segment, and management has demonstrated shareholder-return discipline through $420M of buybacks since mid-2024 even amid heavy leverage [3].
The fundamentals independent of the deal are weak: D/E of 730%, negative ROE of -10.38%, a -3.99% profit margin, and TTM net loss of $465M reflect a business still carrying the debt load from its 2020 Eldorado merger, with $25B+ in total debt and $11B in net debt [3][4]. The stock's current discount to the $31 deal price reflects real closing risk — the transaction requires antitrust and multi-state gaming-regulatory approval and is not expected to close until end of 2027, an 18+ month window during which financing conditions, litigation, or regulatory objections could delay or derail the deal [5]. On a standalone basis, weak Infrastructure, negligible Innovation and Inspiration evidence, and a finite-game corporate posture culminating in a sale to an outside buyer make this a SELL for a long-term ownership framework like IOWN, independent of any short-term merger-arbitrage spread.
Key Catalysts
  1. Antitrust notice was submitted September 14, 2026; a clean regulatory clearance would reduce deal-completion risk and narrow the spread to the $31.00 cash consideration [5]
  2. Continued Las Vegas Strip visitation recovery and Digital segment stabilization could support standalone valuation if the Fertitta deal were to fall through [1]
  3. A daily 'ticking fee' increase to the deal price if closing occurs after June 26, 2027 provides some downside protection for shareholders during an extended regulatory review [5]
Key Risks
  1. Deal-completion risk: the transaction requires majority shareholder approval plus antitrust and multi-state gaming-regulatory clearance, with closing not expected until end of 2027 — an extended window for the deal to be delayed, renegotiated, or terminated [5]
  2. Extreme leverage (D/E of 730%, $25B+ total debt, $11B net debt, $1.2B+ annual lease obligations) leaves the standalone company with minimal financial flexibility if the acquisition does not close [3][4]
  3. Las Vegas Strip net revenue fell 3.5% year-over-year and Digital segment Adjusted EBITDA declined to $68M from $80M, indicating underlying operating softness beneath the deal narrative [1][4]
