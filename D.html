Company Profile
Utilities ·Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Dominion Energy is a regulated electric (and legacy gas) utility serving Virginia, North Carolina, and South Carolina, positioned at the center of the U.S. AI data-center buildout — Northern Virginia's Loudoun County corridor, in Dominion's territory, is a global hub for AI infrastructure investment. Under CEO Bob Blue, the company sold billions in natural gas midstream assets, is building the $9.8B Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind farm (81% complete as of Q2 2026), and is exploring small modular nuclear reactors. Q2 2026 operating EPS of $0.79 beat estimates, revenue rose 17.6% YoY to $4.48B, and the company reaffirmed full-year guidance ($3.45-$3.69 operating EPS, 5-7% long-term growth through 2030) with over 53 GW of data center capacity in various stages of contracting [1].

Investment Thesis
Dominion Energy sits at the physical epicenter of the AI infrastructure buildout: Northern Virginia's 'Data Center Alley' already drives 27% of the company's Virginia electric sales, and Dominion has over 53 GW of data-center capacity in various stages of contracting (12 GW under signed agreements, +5 GW since year-end), with management expecting demand to double by 2039 [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 17.6% YoY to $4.48B, beating estimates, and full-year guidance (5-7% long-term operating EPS growth through 2030) was reaffirmed. As essential regulated infrastructure, AI cannot disrupt Dominion's core business — AI can only increase demand for it, and the $9.8B Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind project (81% complete) adds a second structural growth leg as it approaches completion.
The bear case centers on balance sheet strain and governance credibility. Payout ratio of 93.3% leaves minimal dividend cushion, and 5-year dividend growth CAGR of just 1.16% reflects the 2020 dividend reset that followed the gas-asset sale — income investors are not being compensated with meaningful dividend growth despite a 4.01% yield. D/E of 160% and ROE of 8.28% (well below the 15% target) reflect the capital intensity of simultaneously funding offshore wind, grid expansion for data centers, and a legacy debt load. Governance overhang is real: allegations that Dominion overcharged Virginia customers by $1B, a CEO who collected $3M in ESG bonuses despite a ~30% stock decline, and an inadvertent political donation all raise questions about regulatory and public trust for a monopoly utility. This is a HOLD — the AI-driven demand tailwind is exceptional and structurally durable, but valuation, leverage, and governance concerns cap upside near-term.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued conversion of the 53 GW data-center contracting pipeline into signed electric service agreements would extend visibility on the 5-7% long-term operating EPS growth target through 2030 [1][2]
  2. Completion of the $9.8B Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind project (81% complete as of Q2 2026) would add a fully operational, rate-based clean generation asset supporting both growth and decarbonization goals [1]
  3. Progress on small modular nuclear reactor development could provide a third structural growth leg for firm, carbon-free capacity to match accelerating AI-driven load growth [2]
Key Risks
  1. Payout ratio of 93.3% leaves little cushion for a dividend increase or an earnings shortfall, and 5yr dividend growth CAGR of only ~1.16% signals continued income-growth stagnation for dividend-focused holders
  2. Regulatory and reputational risk from allegations that Dominion overcharged Virginia customers by $1B, alongside CEO ESG-bonus controversy amid stock underperformance, could invite tighter rate-case scrutiny from Virginia regulators [3]
  3. Elevated leverage (D/E of 160%) funding simultaneous offshore wind completion, nuclear exploration, and grid buildout for data centers creates execution and financing risk if data-center demand growth decelerates from current trajectory [1][2]
