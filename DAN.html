Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto Parts ·NYSE ·United States
Dana Incorporated provides power-conveyance and energy-management solutions for vehicles worldwide. The company supplies axles, driveshafts, transmissions, e-axle and e-transmission systems, inverters, electric motors, and thermal management products to light vehicle and commercial vehicle OEMs across North America, Europe, South America, and Asia Pacific. Dana divested its off-highway segment to refocus on higher-margin drivetrain operations.

Investment Thesis
Dana Incorporated at 13.58x forward earnings has undergone a meaningful transformation — the off-highway divestiture, 400bps Q1 2026 EBITDA margin expansion to 9.2%, $125M in share repurchases, and $35M cost savings demonstrate operational progress [1]. Revenue of $1.87B in Q1 (up 4.9% YoY) beat estimates, and full-year guidance of ~$7.5B revenue with ~$800M adjusted EBITDA (10-11% margin) and $300M free cash flow represents a step-change improvement from the prior year's $610M EBITDA [1]. The $750M three-year new business backlog with $200M incremental growth in 2026 from next-gen OEM platforms provides revenue visibility, and the Dana 2030 strategy targets $10B in sales — a 33% increase. E-axle and thermal management capabilities position Dana as a secular beneficiary of the electrification trend.
The bear case centers on leadership instability, leverage, and execution risk. Two CEO transitions in 18 months — Kamsickas to McDonald (Nov 2024) and McDonald to Foster (Jul 2026) — signal strategic uncertainty and board dissatisfaction that undermines confidence in the restructuring plan [3]. D/E of 285% remains dangerously elevated (though improved from 394%), and ROE of 5.31% is well below the 15% target, indicating the business is barely earning its cost of equity [5]. Revenue growth of ~3% YoY and a flat 5-year CAGR reveal that Dana is not yet a growth company — the improvement is driven by cost cuts and divestiture, not organic revenue expansion [5]. The trailing PE of 77.68 (inflated by one-time divestiture gains creating a 15.12% TTM net margin) masks the normalized net margin of ~3.0%. The EV transition creates binary risk — if EV adoption slows, Dana's electrification investments may not generate returns; if EVs accelerate, margin compression from new platform launches could persist. The stock's 127.5% gain over 52 weeks prices in substantial recovery expectations, leaving limited margin of safety.
