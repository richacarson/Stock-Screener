Company Profile

Darling Ingredients is the world's largest publicly traded processor of animal byproducts and food waste, operating 250+ plants in 17 countries and repurposing roughly 15% of the world's meat-industry waste streams into feed, food, fuel, fertilizer, and bioscience products. The Diamond Green Diesel JV with Valero delivered a record Q2 2026 with 350M gallons sold at $2.23 EBITDA/gallon, contributing $389.2M EBITDA. Q2 2026 net income surged to $387.3M ($2.41/share) from $12.7M prior year, with combined adjusted EBITDA of $741.7M nearly tripling YoY. CEO Randall Stuewe has led the company since 2003.

Investment Thesis
Darling Ingredients delivered a record Q2 2026 that validates the long-term DGD thesis: net income surged to $387.3M ($2.41/share) from $12.7M ($0.08/share), and combined adjusted EBITDA nearly tripled to $741.7M [1]. DGD produced 350M gallons at $2.23 EBITDA/gallon ($389.2M contribution) while Global Ingredients EBITDA rose 70% to $353M on fat and protein price recovery [1]. At forward P/E of 9.83x on consensus recovery EPS and PEG of 0.86, the stock offers compelling value for a global category leader with 250+ plants, a 23-year CEO, and a genuine circular-economy franchise processing 15% of the world's meat-industry waste [4][5]. The stock's 111% 52-week gain to $62.50 reflects the earnings inflection but the forward multiple remains undemanding relative to the infrastructure score of 9/10. Inspire Insight score of +53 (ALIGNED) with 12 positive ESG attributions and no negatives makes DAR suitable for faith-based portfolios [4].
The bear case rests on cyclicality and sustainability of the earnings surge. The 2025 earnings collapse (net income fell 77% from $278.9M to $62.8M) demonstrated how vulnerable DGD is to biofuel-credit policy shifts, and renewable fuel credits (RINs, Section 45Z, LCFS pricing) remain volatile — Q2 2026's record EBITDA benefited from favorable credit pricing that may not persist through 2027 [1]. D/E of 79% with significant debt load limits flexibility if the cycle reverses. The trailing profit margin of ~9% (TTM), while improved from 3.54%, reflects that the business generates thin returns on massive revenue even in a strong quarter [5]. Glassdoor reviews (3.3/5, 'horrible upper management,' 'culture is bad') reveal cultural weaknesses that could hinder the AI strategy and innovation efforts [3]. Key-man risk is elevated: CEO Stuewe has led for 23 years with no visible succession plan, and the circular-economy vision has never been tested through a leadership transition.
