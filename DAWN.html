Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NMS ·United States
Day One Biopharmaceuticals is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on developing and commercializing targeted therapies for patients with genetically defined cancers, beginning with pediatric oncology. Its lead product, OJEMDA (tovorafenib), is the first FDA-approved oral therapy for pediatric low-grade glioma (pLGG), approved in April 2024. The company reported FY2025 net product revenue of $155.4M in its first full commercial year, with 2026 guidance of $225-$250M, and acquired Mersana Therapeutics in January 2026 to expand its pipeline into antibody-drug conjugates [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Day One Biopharmaceuticals at approximately $21.48 with a $2.2B market cap is a rare commercial-stage pediatric oncology company executing a strong drug launch — OJEMDA (tovorafenib) generated $155.4M in its first full commercial year (FY2025), beating revenue expectations with Q4 2025 net product revenue of $52.8M and prescriptions growing 16% QoQ to over 900 [1]. The 2026 guidance of $225-$250M in U.S. net product revenue implies 45-61% growth from a product with no direct oral competitor in pediatric LGG. OJEMDA's near-monopoly position as the first and only FDA-approved oral RAF inhibitor for pediatric LGG, combined with label expansion into adult gliomas and other RAF-driven tumors, provides a clear multi-year revenue growth trajectory [1][2].
The bear case centers on unprofitability and pipeline concentration risk. FY2025 net loss of $107M against $155M in revenue yields a -69% profit margin as the company simultaneously funds OJEMDA commercial launch, clinical label expansion, and Mersana ADC pipeline integration [1]. The Mersana acquisition was a significant bet on an early-stage ADC platform with no approved products, adding execution and dilution risk. The pediatric LGG addressable market, while OJEMDA dominates it, is inherently small — the $225-250M 2026 guidance may approach the ceiling for U.S. pediatric use before adult indications (which face much more competition) can offset. Sustainalytics rates Day One's ESG management as 'weak,' ranking 742 of 853 in pharmaceuticals, creating a potential issue for ESG-mandated investors [4]. Finally, yfinance cannot retrieve current market data for DAWN as of October 2026, potentially signaling delisting, acquisition, or data issues that merit investigation [2].
