Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·Germany
Deutsche Bank is Germany's largest bank, operating Corporate Bank, Investment Bank, Private Bank, and Asset Management (DWS) divisions. Under CEO Christian Sewing, who restructured the bank in 2018–2019 including a retreat from parts of global trading, it has delivered record profits: Q3 2026 profit before tax rose 8% to €2.6B, post-tax profit was ~€1.9B, and nine-month net profit reached an all-time high of €5.9B [1][2][4]. Q3 net revenues rose 4% to ~€8.3B with record Corporate Bank revenues, post-tax RoTE was 10.6%, and the bank targets RoTE above 13% and a cost/income ratio below 60% by 2028 [1]. The dividend has been reinstated and raised each year since 2022 [8].

Investment Thesis
Deutsche Bank is a value-priced turnaround that keeps delivering. Q3 2026 profit before tax rose 8% to €2.6B, post-tax profit was ~€1.9B, and nine-month net profit hit an all-time high of €5.9B with a 61.4% cost/income ratio and 11.5% RoTE [1][2]. The shares trade at 7.3x forward earnings and 8.7x trailing [6]. Shareholder returns are growing fast: the per-ADR dividend rose from $0.21 in 2022 to $1.17 in 2026, with a 30% payout ratio and a 3.5% yield [6][8]. Management targets RoTE above 13% and cost/income below 60% by 2028 [1].
The bear case is mediocre returns and a weak culture record. Q3 RoTE of 10.6% is still below the 2028 target, and ROE is 9.0% [1][6]. Revenue growth is modest (Q3 +4%), and the Investment Bank was broadly flat [1]. The dividend history is short — payments were suspended in 2020–2021 — and DWS paid a €25M greenwashing fine in Germany plus $25M to the SEC ($19M for misleading ESG statements, $6M for AML program failures) [7][8]. Faith alignment is MISALIGNED (Inspire score -67, with Abortion Activism and LGBT Activism as negative attributions and no positives).
Key Catalysts
  1. 2028 targets: delivering RoTE above 13% (from 10.6% in Q3 2026) and cost/income below 60% (from 62.4%) would justify a re-rating from 7.3x forward earnings [1][6]
  2. Corporate Bank momentum: record Corporate Bank revenues in Q3 2026 provide stable, fee- and deposit-driven growth less dependent on trading [1]
  3. Rising capital returns: the dividend per ADR rose from $0.77 in 2025 to $1.17 in 2026 with a 30% payout ratio, leaving room for further increases [6][8]
Key Risks
  1. Sub-target profitability: Q3 2026 RoTE of 10.6% was slightly below the prior year's 10.7%, and missing the 13% 2028 target would undercut the re-rating thesis [1]
  2. Conduct and regulatory risk: DWS paid a €25M German greenwashing fine and $25M in SEC penalties, including $6M for AML program failures, and further compliance lapses could bring larger penalties [7]
  3. Investment Bank cyclicality: Investment Bank revenues were flat against a record Q3 2025, and a downturn in trading or underwriting would hit group earnings [1]
