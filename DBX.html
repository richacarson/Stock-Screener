Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Dropbox provides cloud file backup, sync and sharing, plus adjacent products including Dropbox Sign, DocSend, Reclaim.ai and the AI-powered universal search and knowledge tool Dropbox Dash [3]. Q2 2026 revenue was $631.5M (+0.9% YoY; +1.7% ex-FormSwift, which is being wound down by end-2026), with 18.19M paying users and $2.566B of ARR (+1.0%) [1]. Co-founder Drew Houston announced in May 2026 that he is stepping down as CEO after 19 years to become executive chairman, with product chief Ashraf Alkarmi (ex-Vimeo) succeeding him [4][5]. Aggressive buybacks have cut the weighted diluted share count from 395.8M in 2021 to 226.8M in Q2 2026 and left stockholders' equity negative [3].

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 3 /10
Through Sinek's lens, Dropbox has a real cause on paper, but its recent behavior is more finite than infinite. The company has used most of its cash generation to shrink the share count by about 43% since 2021, which pushed equity negative, while revenue went flat and R&D was cut [3]. The founder handing over the CEO role in the middle of an AI-driven competitive squeeze now tests whether 'a more enlightened way of working' outlives him [4][5]. The Dash pivot and the FormSwift wind-down show some willingness to adapt [1].

Investment Thesis
Bull case: Dropbox is a cash machine. TTM operating cash flow is about 38.7% of revenue, the free-cash-flow yield is 12.1% on a ~$7.9B market cap, and the stock trades at roughly 10.9x FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.32 [3]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.75 beat consensus, management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $2.513-2.523B, and paying users grew for a third straight quarter to 18.19M [1][8]. If Dash lifts ARPU, even modest growth on top of continued share shrinkage could compound per-share earnings.
Bear case: reported revenue growth is about 1%, constant-currency ex-FormSwift growth was just 0.1% in Q2, and FY2025 revenue actually declined about 1% [1]. Buybacks have left equity negative and doubled interest expense, and Microsoft Copilot and Google bundling are compressing the standalone file-storage category just as the founder steps back from the CEO role [3][4][5]. Without quantified Dash revenue, Dropbox risks becoming a slowly melting annuity.
Key Catalysts
  1. Dash monetization: disclosure of attach rates or ARR from Dash bundled into Core plans would show whether AI can re-accelerate ARPU above the current ~1% ARR growth [1]
  2. Completion of the FormSwift wind-down by end-2026, removing a ~80-100bps drag from reported growth in 2027 [1]
  3. Strategic direction under new CEO Ashraf Alkarmi, including any portfolio pruning or M&A after the founder transition [4]
Key Risks
  1. Microsoft embedding Copilot across OneDrive, Teams and 365 makes Dropbox's AI search a free feature inside a bundle customers already pay for [5]
  2. Negative stockholders' equity and rising interest expense ($50.0M in Q2 2026 vs $27.3M in Q2 2025) limit flexibility if cash flow weakens [3]
  3. Paying-user churn if AI assistants reduce the need for a separate file-sync subscription; FY2025 revenue already fell ~1% [1]
