Q2 2026 revenue was $94.6M (-0.3% YoY, +1.4% ex-divestitures) with a $12.9M GAAP net loss and adjusted EBITDA of -$0.8M. CEO Jeffrey Graves announced on August 4, 2026 that he will step down once a successor is found. Headquartered in Rock Hill, South Carolina.

Investment Thesis
The bull case is a cyclical recovery: Q2 2026 printer sales grew double digits in both metal and polymer, Healthcare revenue rose 6.8% to $48.1M, revenue ex-divestitures grew 1.4%, and adjusted EBITDA improved by $4.6M to -$0.8M on prior cost cuts [1]. The Q2 equity raise of $53.2M and debt-to-equity of 56% give runway, and exposure to aerospace and defense, data center infrastructure and dental gives several routes to growth if additive manufacturing demand recovers [1][2][4].
The bear case is structural decline and leadership uncertainty. Revenue has fallen at a -7.0% annual rate from $557M in 2020 to $387M in 2025, the trailing profit margin is -14.2% and ROE is -21.3%, and Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA is guided negative at -$3M to -$1M [1][2][3]. The CEO is leaving with no successor named, 18.9M new shares were issued in Q2 2026, and the company still faces a consolidated securities class action after paying ~$15M in 2023 to settle export-control violations [1][4][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Appointment of a permanent CEO to replace Jeffrey Graves, who announced his departure on August 4, 2026 [4]
  2. Continued double-digit printer system growth in metal and polymer translating into higher-margin recurring materials revenue [1]

Key Risks

  2. Leadership vacuum during the CEO search, slowing strategic decisions in a downturn [4]
  3. Legal overhang from the pending securities class action in the Eastern District of New York [5]
