Company Profile
Industrials ·Farm & Heavy Construction Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Deere & Company designs, manufactures, and distributes agricultural, construction, forestry, and turf care equipment worldwide through four segments: Production and Precision Agriculture, Small Agriculture and Turf, Construction and Forestry, and Financial Services. Technologies include the John Deere Operations Center, See & Spray precision-agriculture computer vision, and a growing fleet of autonomous tractors. Founded in 1837 and headquartered in Moline, Illinois, Deere reported FY2025 revenue of $44.7B (down 12% amid an agricultural equipment downcycle) while its Construction and Forestry order book strengthens on data-center and infrastructure-driven demand.

Investment Thesis
Deere is the dominant global player in production agriculture and a fast-growing beneficiary of AI-driven infrastructure spend through its Construction & Forestry segment, where order backlogs are over 80% filled for FY2026 with visibility into FY2027 and segment sales are guided up roughly 20% [2][3]. The ag equipment business appears to be bottoming in 2026 per management's own commentary, and Deere's precision-ag technology stack (See & Spray, autonomous tractors, Operations Center) gives it a durable, AI-enabled moat that peers have struggled to replicate [5].
The bear case centers on cyclicality and trust: FY2025 revenue fell 12% to $44.7B and the trailing 3-year revenue CAGR is negative, EPS has fallen from $34.63 (FY2023) to $18.50 (FY2025), and Forward P/E of 29.5x already prices in a meaningful recovery that depends on farm income improving from currently depressed levels [1][6]. The settled FTC/state right-to-repair litigation is a reputational and regulatory overhang that could pressure high-margin parts and service revenue going forward as farmers gain more independent repair access [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Construction & Forestry order book over 80% filled for FY2026 with backlog visibility into FY2027, driven by AI data-center and infrastructure construction demand [2][3]
  2. Management's own guidance that 2026 marks the bottom of the multi-year agricultural equipment downcycle, with consensus FY2027 EPS growth of nearly 28%
  3. Continued precision-ag subscription attach (John Deere Operations Center, See & Spray) expanding higher-margin recurring software and data revenue
Key Risks
  1. Persistently weak farm income and high global grain/oilseed inventories could extend the ag equipment downcycle beyond management's 2026 bottom-call
  2. Right-to-repair settlement terms requiring open diagnostic and repair access could compress high-margin aftermarket parts and service revenue over time [4]
  3. Elevated D/E (230%) tied to the captive Financial Services leasing book increases sensitivity to credit-quality deterioration among ag and construction dealers
