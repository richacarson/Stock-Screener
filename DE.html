Company Profile

Deere & Company manufactures and distributes agricultural, construction, forestry, and turf care equipment worldwide. The company operates through Production & Precision Ag, Small Ag & Turf, Construction & Forestry, and Financial Products segments. Deere is the dominant global leader in precision agriculture technology, autonomous machinery, and AI-powered farming solutions, with 189 years of continuous operation.

Investment Thesis
Deere is a generational franchise positioned at the intersection of agriculture and AI, with 189 years of brand equity, ~60% North American large equipment market share, and a transformative precision agriculture platform [1][2]. Q2 FY2026 delivered $13.37B revenue (up 5%) with Construction & Forestry surging 29% and Small Ag & Turf up 16%, signaling cyclical recovery in key segments [1]. Net income of $1.773B and maintained full-year guidance of $4.5B-$5.0B demonstrate operational stability. Autonomy 2.0 entered full release in 2026, See & Spray AI weed detection reduces herbicide 77%, and the OpenAI partnership positions Deere to capture recurring software/subscription revenue on top of equipment sales [2]. ROE of 18.2%, 53+ consecutive years of dividends, 15.3% five-year dividend CAGR, and $635M returned to shareholders in Q2 demonstrate durable capital allocation discipline [1][4].
The bear case rests on valuation, right-to-repair structural impact, and cyclical risk. Forward P/E of 25.84 and PEG of 2.24 embed significant recovery expectations; Production & Precision Ag revenue declined 14% in Q2 FY2026 and the agricultural equipment cycle may have a longer trough than consensus expects [1][4]. The FTC right-to-repair settlement requires Deere to provide repair tools, software, and diagnostic access to farmers and independent shops for 10 years — a structural change that could erode the aftermarket data moat and dealer network economics over time [3]. The $99M class-action settlement adds to the financial impact. D/E of 376% (driven by John Deere Financial's $60B+ lending book) creates leverage risk if agricultural credit quality deteriorates. Profit margin of 10.1% has compressed from cycle-peak levels above 16%, and tariffs contribute to buyer uncertainty. CEO May's $20.8M insider sale raises questions about near-term conviction. Technology democratization risk persists as AGCO and CNH Industrial deploy comparable AI/autonomous capabilities [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Agricultural cycle recovery — Small Ag & Turf up 16% and Construction & Forestry up 29% in Q2 FY2026, with full-year guidance of $4.5B-$5.0B net income maintained, signaling the production cycle trough may be ending [1]
  2. Autonomous farming milestones — Autonomy 2.0 in full 2026 release for tillage, autonomous corn/soybean production cycle targeted by 2030, See & Spray expansion, and OpenAI partnership creating new recurring revenue streams [2]
  3. Precision agriculture platform monetization — software subscriptions, AI-powered prescriptive farming advice, and data analytics layered on the installed equipment base, shifting revenue mix toward higher-margin recurring streams [2]
Key Risks
  1. Right-to-repair structural impact — FTC settlement requires 10 years of open repair tool/software access, plus $99M class-action settlement, potentially eroding the aftermarket data moat and dealer network economics that underpin Deere's competitive advantage [3]
  2. Extended agricultural downturn — Production & Precision Ag revenue declined 14% in Q2 FY2026, and tariff-driven uncertainty could defer equipment purchases beyond the expected cycle recovery, invalidating the guidance [1]
  3. Technology democratization — AGCO, CNH Industrial, and precision agriculture startups deploying comparable AI/autonomous capabilities, narrowing Deere's technology premium as computer vision and sensors commoditize [2]
