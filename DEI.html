Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Douglas Emmett is a fully integrated, self-administered REIT that owns and operates Class A office and multifamily properties in high-barrier coastal submarkets of Los Angeles and Honolulu. The portfolio includes approximately 17.6 million square feet of office space and 4,381 multifamily units. CEO Jordan Kaplan has led the company since its 2006 IPO. Q2 2026 revenue was $283.4M with adjusted core revenue up 8.6% YoY excluding the divested property management business, though the company remains in net loss territory with 2026 FFO guidance of $1.39-$1.43 per share.

Investment Thesis
Douglas Emmett at $13.36 trades at a steep discount to NAV, reflecting the market's deep skepticism toward office REITs amid AI disruption fears and elevated interest rates. The bull case rests on the irreplaceability of the company's coastal LA and Honolulu portfolio — high-barrier submarkets with structural supply constraints where Class A office serves entertainment, legal, and financial services tenants. Q2 2026 showed positive absorption of 60,000 sq ft with 3.2% straight-line rent increases and core revenue up 8.6% YoY excluding the divested property management business [3]. The 6.51% dividend yield ($0.76/year) provides income while investors wait for a potential office recovery, and the founder-led management team has navigated multiple real estate cycles over 21 years [2]. Multifamily occupancy at 98% provides a ballast against office headwinds.
The bear case is severe and structural. D/E of 297.49% reflects $5.59B in debt against a $2.24B market cap — a leveraged capital structure where higher-for-longer interest rates directly compress FFO, which fell from $1.71 to $1.45 per share in 2025 and is guided to $1.39-$1.43 in 2026 [3]. Net income remains negative with 2026 EPS guided at -$0.16 to -$0.20. The dividend has been frozen at $0.19/quarter since the 2020 cut from $0.28, offering zero growth and a 0% 5-year dividend CAGR. AI-driven automation of knowledge work threatens structural demand decline for office space — the market has already priced this in with office REIT multiples at deep discounts [2]. ROE is negative at -1.41% and profit margin at -6.84%, indicating the company is not generating adequate returns. With a SELL recommendation at an overall score of 33, the combination of near-floor leverage, negative earnings, zero dividend growth, and secular AI headwinds makes this a value trap rather than a recovery story.
