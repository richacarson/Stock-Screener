Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Wineries & Distilleries ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Diageo is the world's largest premium spirits company, owning brands including Johnnie Walker, Smirnoff, Guinness, Captain Morgan, Baileys, Tanqueray, Ketel One, and Don Julio. Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) organic net sales declined 2.0% amid continued weakness in U.S. spirits and Chinese white spirits, with reported revenue of $19.64B (-2.97% YoY) [1][2]. The company is mid-turnaround: CEO Debra Crew departed in July 2025, CFO Nik Jhangiani served as interim CEO, and Sir Dave Lewis (former Tesco Group CEO) was appointed permanent CEO effective January 1, 2026, alongside a $1.2B two-year restructuring plan and $300M in Accelerate programme cost savings [1][3].

Investment Thesis
Diageo trades at a depressed 12.99x forward earnings with a 2.23% dividend yield and a 61.81% payout ratio, backed by a 20+ year record of consecutive dividend increases and a portfolio of category-leading brands (Johnnie Walker, Guinness, Don Julio) that retain real pricing power and brand equity [1][2]. New CEO Sir Dave Lewis, who previously led a successful Tesco turnaround, has been installed alongside a $1.2B restructuring plan targeting $300M in Accelerate programme cost savings, and management guided FY2027 to broadly flat organic net sales with low-to-mid-single-digit organic operating profit growth and ~$2B of free cash flow — a plausible stabilization path if execution holds [1]. Diageo's alcohol-production business is structurally immune to AI disruption, and the balance sheet, while levered, generates $4.39B of operating cash flow to support the dividend through the turnaround [2].
The bear case is that this is a company currently playing a finite, defensive game: organic net sales have now declined for multiple consecutive periods (FY2026 -2.0% organic, -2.97% reported), the prior CEO departed amid the underperformance, and a 6.4% workforce reduction alongside $900M of restructuring charges signals real operational strain rather than confident long-term investment [1][5]. Debt-to-Equity of 171% and net debt of $20.42B against an Altman Z-Score of 2.21 leave less balance-sheet flexibility than the target profile, and continued weakness in U.S. Spirits and Chinese white spirits — two of the largest historical growth engines — raises real execution risk for the new CEO's turnaround [1][2]. The 61.81% payout ratio, above the 40% target, leaves less cushion to sustain the 20-year dividend-growth streak if earnings deterioration continues. The Inspire Impact Score of -53 (MISALIGNED, driven by Alcohol and LGBT Activism attributions) is a structural, non-financial exclusion factor for faith-based allocators regardless of turnaround success.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO Sir Dave Lewis executing the $1.2B restructuring plan and $300M Accelerate cost-savings programme, targeting FY2027 guidance of broadly flat organic net sales and ~$2B free cash flow [1]

Key Risks
  1. Elevated leverage (171% Debt-to-Equity, $20.42B net debt, Altman Z-Score of 2.21) reduces flexibility to fund both the restructuring and the 20-year dividend-growth streak if organic sales keep declining [2]
  2. Leadership transition risk: a new external CEO (Dave Lewis) must execute a turnaround in a company that has just been through a disruptive CEO exit and workforce reduction, with no guarantee the new operating framework restores growth [1][3]
  3. Continued category share loss in U.S. Spirits and Chinese white spirits, the segments explicitly cited as FY2026 organic net sales drags, could persist if underlying consumer trends (moderation, down-trading) prove structural rather than cyclical [1]
