Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
D.R. Horton is the largest homebuilder in the United States by volume, founded in 1978 and headquartered in Arlington, Texas. It acquires and develops land and builds detached and attached homes under the D.R. Horton, Express Homes, Emerald Homes and Freedom Homes brands, with majority-owned lot developer Forestar, DHI Mortgage and title services, and single- and multi-family rental operations. FY2026 (ended September 30, 2026) revenue was $33.0B (-4%) on 84,202 closings, with diluted EPS of $10.11, $3.5B of operating cash flow and $3.6B of buybacks; FY2027 guidance calls for $30-32B revenue and 79,000-84,000 closings. Paul Romanowski has been CEO since October 2023, with David Auld as Executive Chairman.

Investment Thesis
D.R. Horton is the scale leader in U.S. housing with a fortress balance sheet: D/E of 25.5%, consolidated debt-to-capital of 20.8%, $3.5B of FY2026 operating cash flow and $3.6B returned through buybacks plus $513M of dividends [1][5]. Net orders rose 2% to 84,995 homes in FY2026 and year-end backlog grew 8% to $4.5B (11,714 homes), while the stock trades at 13.1x forward earnings and 1.8x book [1][5]. Any easing in mortgage rates would hit a company that already controls land, lots (Forestar) and financing for the entry-level buyer.
The bear case is that affordability keeps squeezing margins and volume. FY2026 revenue fell 4% to $33.0B, diluted EPS fell to $10.11 from $11.57, Q4 net orders dropped 7% in units and 9% in value with cancellations up to 23%, and FY2027 guidance of $30-32B revenue and 79,000-84,000 closings points lower again [1]. Executive Chairman David Auld expects incentives to remain elevated in FY2027 [1], and litigation over DHI Mortgage escrow quotes and Forestar related-party dealings (alleged $700M investor harm) adds legal and reputational risk [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage-rate relief lifting entry-level demand and allowing incentives to fall from elevated FY2026 levels [1]
  2. Continued ~$3.0B FY2027 buyback against a 13.1x forward P/E, shrinking the share count by roughly 7% per year at current prices [1][5]
  3. Scaling AI-enabled off-site panel manufacturing with Promise Robotics to reduce build cycle times and construction cost [3]
Key Risks
  1. FY2027 guidance implies revenue of $30-32B (down from $33.0B) as Q4 FY2026 orders fell 7% and cancellations rose to 23% [1]
  2. Persistently elevated incentives compressing home-sales gross margin below FY2026's ~21% [1]
  3. Class-action litigation alleging DHI Mortgage understated property-tax escrow in quotes, and a suit alleging Forestar minority holders were shortchanged by at least $700M [4]
