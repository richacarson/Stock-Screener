Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
D.R. Horton, Inc. operates as the largest homebuilder in the United States by volume, constructing and selling single-family homes across 125 markets in 36 states. The company operates through multiple brands — D.R. Horton, Express Homes, Freedom Homes, and Emerald Homes — spanning entry-level through luxury segments. Its subsidiary Forestar Group operates as a residential lot development company. Founded in 1978 and headquartered in Arlington, Texas, DHI closed approximately 90,000 homes in FY2025 with revenue of approximately $36.8B and is navigating elevated mortgage rates while benefiting from structural housing undersupply.

Investment Thesis
D.R. Horton at 11.2x forward earnings with a fortress 26.5% D/E balance sheet and 18.9% ROE offers compelling value as America’s largest homebuilder positioned to benefit from a structural housing shortage estimated at 3-5 million units [1]. Q2 FY2026 net sales orders surged 11% to 24,992 homes ($9.2B), demonstrating demand resilience even with elevated mortgage rates. The company’s vertically integrated model — combining homebuilding, lot development (Forestar), rental operations, and financial services — provides competitive advantages in cost control and cycle time. Capital return is substantial: $2.5B in annual buybacks plus $500M in dividends. The Inspire Impact score of 36 (ALIGNED) and focus on affordable homeownership through Express Homes align with IOWN values [2].
The bear case centers on cyclical risk and rate sensitivity. Revenue declined 3.8% YoY as elevated mortgage rates compressed affordability, and FY2026 consolidated revenue guidance of $33.5-$34.5B reflects ongoing headwinds [1]. Sales incentives remain elevated through FY2026, compressing margins and limiting earnings upside. Profit margin of 11.5% is below the IOWN 20% target, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of homebuilding. The company’s performance is heavily correlated to mortgage rates, which remain elevated above 6.5%, and any further rate increases would directly impact demand. The cyclical nature of homebuilding means DHI’s current fortress balance sheet could be tested in a prolonged downturn. Land inventory risk is inherent — DHI’s aggressive land acquisition strategy creates leverage to price declines if demand deteriorates.
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage rate normalization toward the 5.5-6.0% range would significantly improve housing affordability and drive incremental demand, potentially accelerating DHI’s order growth beyond the 11% Q2 FY2026 pace [1]
  2. Structural U.S. housing undersupply of 3-5 million units provides a multi-year demand tailwind that persists regardless of economic cycles, channeling demand toward new construction as existing-home inventory remains constrained
  3. Forestar lot development pipeline expansion provides vertically integrated cost advantages and ensures land supply for sustained high-volume production at industry-leading cycle times [1]
Key Risks
  1. Sustained mortgage rates above 7% would further compress housing affordability, potentially slowing order growth and requiring even higher sales incentives that erode margins below current levels [1]
  2. Cyclical recession risk — homebuilding is inherently procyclical, and a broad economic downturn combined with elevated rates could reduce housing starts materially, stranding land inventory acquired at current prices
  3. Regulatory risk from potential changes to housing policy, building codes, or tariffs on construction materials (lumber, steel) that could increase construction costs and compress profitability
