Company Profile
Industrials ·Marine Shipping ·NYSE ·Bermuda
DHT Holdings owns and operates crude oil tankers through subsidiaries in Monaco, Singapore, Norway and India, and also provides technical management services. As of December 15, 2025 its fleet was 22 very large crude carriers (VLCCs) [3]. A tanker-rate upcycle produced the company's strongest quarter ever in Q2 2026: shipping revenue was $284.8M (vs $127.9M a year earlier) and net profit $198.3M ($1.23/share) [1]. H1 2026 net profit of $362.9M already exceeds the prior full-year record of $266.3M set in 2020 [4]. DHT pays a quarterly variable dividend tied to earnings, most recently $1.22 per share in August 2026 [3].

It is built to harvest tanker upcycles and hand the cash back through a variable dividend, not to pursue a cause beyond shareholder returns [1][3]. Stable leadership under CEO Harfjeld since 2010 and a conservative 32.7% D/E show that it is built to survive the cycle [3][5]. Surviving is not the same as advancing a Just Cause, though, and the company has never tried to reinvent itself beyond crude shipping.

Investment Thesis
Bull case: DHT is riding a powerful VLCC rate upcycle. Q2 2026 net profit was $198.3M ($1.23/share), the strongest quarter in company history, and H1 2026 profit of $362.9M already beats the 2020 full-year record [1][4]. With D/E of 32.7%, interest coverage of 27x and a 65.5% TTM net margin, the balance sheet can carry the cycle. FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.70 implies a 5.3x P/E and supports dividends of $1.22 per quarter or more [3].
Bear case: earnings are almost purely a function of spot VLCC rates. Consensus EPS falls from $4.70 in 2026 to $3.27 in 2027 and $1.89 in 2028, a 60% decline in two years, and the variable dividend will fall with it, as calendar-year payouts dropped from $1.35 in 2020 to $0.74 in 2025 [3]. Free cash flow is thin (FCF yield 0.9% TTM) because capex absorbs 92% of operating cash flow. Insiders sold 583,803 shares in Q3 2026 near the stock's 52-week high of $26.91 [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results: consensus revenue of about $243M and a Q4 estimate of about $333M point to continued record earnings and large variable dividends [8]
  2. Sanctions enforcement on shadow-fleet crude flows and OPEC+ output increases, which raise compliant-VLCC ton-mile demand
  3. Opportunistic vessel sales at elevated secondhand values, like the $60M Q1 2026 gain, to recycle capital [2]
Key Risks
  1. VLCC rate normalization: consensus EPS falls ~60% from 2026 to 2028, which would cut the variable dividend proportionally [3]
  2. Capex intensity: capex consumed 92% of TTM operating cash flow, leaving FCF of only $0.22/share against $2.45 of dividends per share [3]
  3. Long-term crude demand decline from the energy transition shrinking seaborne crude volumes, with DHT undiversified beyond VLCCs
