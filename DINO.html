Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
HF Sinclair Corporation is an independent energy company that produces and markets high-value light products including gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, renewable diesel, and specialty lubricants. The company operates through four segments: Refining, Renewables (370M+ gallons/year renewable diesel capacity delivering 87.5% margins and 21% of consolidated EBIT), Marketing, and Lubricants & Specialties. Formed from the 2022 merger of HollyFrontier Corporation and Sinclair Oil, DINO operates refineries across the western and mid-continent United States. Q2 2026 delivered EPS of $5.31 (vs $1.70 prior year) on revenue of $10.39B, with refining adjusted EBITDA of $1.02B as gross margins surged to $25.95/bbl from $16.50.

Investment Thesis
HF Sinclair delivered a blowout Q2 2026 — EPS of $5.31 crushed the $4.39 consensus (+21%), revenue of $10.39B beat estimates by 38.55%, and refining adjusted EBITDA hit $1.02B as gross margins surged 57% to $25.95/bbl [1][2]. The renewables segment pivoted from cash drag to profit engine, now delivering 87.5% margins and contributing 21% of consolidated EBIT. At 7.58x forward earnings with a 2.21% dividend yield and 17.7% payout ratio, DINO offers deep value with significant margin of safety. FY2026 EPS consensus of $11.85 has been revised up 19.8% over four weeks, reflecting accelerating earnings momentum. The West Coast refining market is tightening as competing refineries face closures and capacity constraints, which should sustain above-mid-cycle margins through 2026-2027 [1]. D/E of 27.78% and ROE of 19.41% demonstrate balance sheet strength and capital efficiency.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, leadership instability, and long-term demand risk. Refining margins are inherently volatile — the 57% gross margin improvement from $16.50 to $25.95/bbl could revert as global refining capacity additions in the Middle East and Asia come online, and the 2022 revenue peak of $38.2B was followed by three consecutive years of decline before the current recovery [1][2]. CEO Tim Go's abrupt voluntary departure in February 2026, followed by executive restructuring, raises unresolved governance questions about internal dynamics that have not been publicly disclosed [4]. Profit margin of 6.15% is thin even by refining standards and leaves little cushion if crack spreads compress. Long-term secular decline in refined product demand from EV adoption and emissions regulation poses existential risk for pure-play refiners over a 10-15 year horizon — though renewable diesel and tightening West Coast dynamics provide some offset. The 5yr revenue CAGR of ~18% is heavily inflated by the 2022 Sinclair Oil merger, masking organic revenue volatility [3].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Oil refining is a physical-world, capital-intensive industrial operation that AI structurally cannot replace [1][2]. Refineries require massive physical infrastructure, complex chemical processing, logistics networks, and regulatory compliance that exist entirely in the physical domain. AI may improve refinery optimization and predictive maintenance (incremental efficiency gains), but the core business of converting crude oil into fuels and lubricants is immune to AI substitution. The renewable diesel segment adds an energy transition dimension that remains physical infrastructure-dependent. AI data center buildout indirectly supports refined fuel demand through construction activity and power generation. The only subtle risk is that AI-powered efficiency improvements in downstream consumption (autonomous vehicles, optimized logistics) could modestly reduce demand over time. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI-related revenue. HF Sinclair does not develop or deploy AI products, though the new President of Growth, Technology and Transformation (Valerie Pompa) is exploring operational AI applications [4].
