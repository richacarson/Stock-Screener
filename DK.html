Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
Delek US Holdings, Inc. is a downstream energy company that operates petroleum refineries, markets fuels, and manages logistics assets. The Refining segment operates three refineries with approximately 302,000 barrels per day of crude oil throughput capacity in Tyler, TX; El Dorado, AR; and Big Spring, TX. The Logistics segment operates through Delek Logistics Partners, LP, managing pipelines, storage tanks, and wholesale marketing. Revenue is primarily driven by crack spreads and refining throughput volumes. Headquartered in Brentwood, Tennessee.

Investment Thesis
Delek US offers deep-value characteristics with a 7.81x forward P/E, 12.3% free cash flow yield ($477.8M on $3.87B market cap), and Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $211.7M that surged 530% YoY on improved crack spreads and small refinery exemption benefits [1][2]. The stock has appreciated 156% over the past year as refining economics recovered and the company demonstrated the operating leverage inherent in its three-refinery, 302,000 bpd platform. The $1.02 annual dividend provides a 1.61% yield, and aggressive buybacks reduce the share count, amplifying per-share economics. Physical refining assets are irreplaceable infrastructure with high barriers to new entry.
Delek US is fundamentally a commodity business with extreme earnings volatility driven by crack spreads outside management's control — FY2025 revenue declined 9.5% and TTM net profit margin is negative at -0.48% [2]. The D/E ratio of 1,125% reflects dangerous leverage that amplifies both upside and downside through commodity cycles, with an Altman Z-Score of 1.47 indicating elevated bankruptcy risk [2]. Glassdoor reviews reveal serious cultural problems including CEO dismissiveness, executive turnover, and diversity concerns that undermine organizational stability [3]. The Innovation and Inspiration scores are among the lowest in the IOWN universe — this is a company with no competitive moat beyond physical refinery assets and no purpose beyond processing crude oil. A sustained downturn in crack spreads would rapidly compress both earnings and the equity cushion supporting the leveraged balance sheet.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained improvement in Gulf Coast crack spreads above $15/bbl supporting positive refining margins and EBITDA recovery toward $800M+ annually
  2. Successful debt reduction from the $477.8M free cash flow, reducing D/E ratio and improving the Altman Z-Score toward investment-grade territory
  3. Small refinery exemption benefits continuing to provide regulatory tailwinds that improve refining economics relative to larger peers
Key Risks
  1. Crack spread compression below $10/bbl for multiple quarters would produce significant losses given the negative net profit margin baseline and extreme leverage at 1,125% D/E with Altman Z-Score of 1.47
  2. Executive leadership instability and CEO cultural dismissiveness creating organizational dysfunction that impairs operational execution during commodity downturns
  3. Environmental regulatory tightening or reversal of small refinery exemption policies eliminating a key competitive advantage that boosted Q1 2026 EBITDA by hundreds of millions
