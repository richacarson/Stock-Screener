Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Delek Logistics Partners is a midstream master limited partnership focused on gathering, transporting, and storing crude oil, natural gas, and water primarily in the Permian Basin. The partnership operates pipelines, storage terminals, and water midstream infrastructure under fee-based contracts with parent Delek US Holdings (DK) and third-party customers. CEO Avigal Soreq has led since June 2022. Q2 2026 revenue was $384.8M (+56.2% YoY) with EBITDA of $120.0M, and the partnership announced its 54th consecutive quarterly distribution increase. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance is $520-$560M. Market cap approximately $3.1B.
800 Employees ·www.deleklogistics.com

Investment Thesis
Delek Logistics at 13.77x forward earnings offers high-yield midstream infrastructure income backed by Permian Basin fee-based contracts and a 54-quarter track record of consecutive distribution increases [1]. The 7.4% distribution yield with ~1.5x coverage ratio provides attractive risk-adjusted income. Q2 2026 revenue surged 56.2% to $384.8M with EBITDA of $120.0M, and management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $520-$560M — a record level [1]. The H2O Midstream acquisition positions the partnership in the growing produced water infrastructure market, diversifying revenue beyond crude oil and gas gathering. The Inspire Impact Score of 36 (ALIGNED) with 10 positive attributions and zero negatives makes DKL suitable for faith-based income portfolios. AI disruption risk is essentially zero for physical pipeline infrastructure, and AI-driven data center buildout may increase long-term energy demand.
The bear case centers on structural MLP limitations, leverage, and parent dependency. Net income declined to $28.87M from $44.57M YoY in Q2 2026 despite strong revenue and EBITDA growth, suggesting rising depreciation and interest costs from acquisition-driven expansion [1]. The MLP capital structure with very high ROE (439%) masks significant leverage — debt levels are elevated as the partnership finances growth through borrowing. Parent Delek US Holdings controls the general partner and can influence asset dropdown pricing, distribution policy, and strategic decisions that may not always align with limited partner interests. Glassdoor reviews of the parent describe a 'run by a few, for a few' culture, and the MLP structure's inherent focus on quarterly cash distributions represents a finite game mindset that limits long-term strategic flexibility [2]. Energy transition risk is a secular headwind: while Permian Basin demand is robust today, long-term crude oil demand decline could impair the value of pipeline infrastructure. Revenue growth of 56% is acquisition-driven and not indicative of organic growth trajectory.
Key Catalysts
  1. Reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $520-560M represents record profitability and supports continued distribution increases — the 55th consecutive quarterly increase would extend one of the longest growth streaks in midstream [1]
  2. H2O Midstream integration synergies and growing Permian Basin produced water volumes could drive organic revenue growth as operators increase completion activity [1]
  3. AI-driven data center buildout increasing natural gas and power demand in the Permian Basin could support long-term throughput volumes and contract renewals at favorable rates
Key Risks
  1. Parent Delek US Holdings controls the general partner with incentive distribution rights, creating potential conflicts of interest in dropdown pricing, capital allocation, and distribution decisions that may prioritize parent economics over limited partner returns [1]
  2. Net income declining 35% YoY despite 56% revenue growth signals rising cost of debt and depreciation from acquisition-fueled expansion — if leverage ratios deteriorate, distribution coverage could compress [1]
  3. Long-term energy transition risk as crude oil demand peaks could impair the terminal value of Permian Basin pipeline infrastructure, though near-term demand remains robust through 2030+ [3]
