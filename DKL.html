Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Delek Logistics Partners, LP is a midstream master limited partnership that gathers, transports, stores, and markets crude oil, natural gas, and water in the Permian Basin and other regions. The partnership operates crude oil and natural gas gathering systems, pipeline transportation, storage facilities, and wholesale marketing operations. Controlled by general partner Delek US Holdings, DKL reported Q2 2026 net revenues of $384.8M (+56.2% YoY), driven by the DPG dropdown acquisition. Adjusted EBITDA was $143.5M with a quarterly distribution of $1.135 per unit. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance is $520-560M.

Investment Thesis
Delek Logistics Partners posted Q2 2026 net revenues of $384.8M (+56.2% YoY) driven by the DPG dropdown acquisition, with adjusted EBITDA of $143.5M and full-year guidance of $520-560M reaffirmed [1]. The partnership offers an 8.0% distribution yield with 1.3x coverage, providing meaningful current income for investors. Forward P/E of 14.75 is attractive relative to midstream MLP peers, and the Permian Basin focus positions DKL in the most productive oil basin in the U.S. with continued volume growth expected through the decade [4]. The New Energy strategy elevation in the C-suite suggests management is at least thinking about energy transition positioning.
The bear case centers on structural MLP risks, governance, and earnings quality. DKL is controlled by general partner Delek US Holdings, which means unitholder interests are subordinate to the parent company's strategic priorities — dropdown transactions and intercompany pricing may not always optimize for DKL unitholders [2]. GAAP net income fell from $44.6M to $28.9M in Q2 2026 despite 56% revenue growth, driven by higher interest expense, and Q2 EPS of $0.54 missed the $1.12 consensus by 52% [1]. The 56% revenue growth is largely non-organic (DPG dropdown), and organic volume growth is harder to assess. Frequent executive turnover — three major leadership changes since 2022 — raises governance concerns [2]. The profit margin of 12.8% is below typical midstream peers, and the partnership's reliance on parent company volumes creates concentration risk if Delek US faces refining headwinds [4]. The energy transition poses a decades-long structural risk to hydrocarbon gathering volumes, and DKL's New Energy strategy remains unexecuted [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $520-560M, if achieved, would demonstrate the DPG dropdown is fully integrated and generating accretive cash flow, supporting continued distribution growth [1]
  2. Permian Basin volume growth — continued production increases in the basin's core acreage provide organic throughput tailwinds for DKL's gathering and transportation systems
  3. New Energy strategy execution under newly elevated C-suite leadership could diversify revenue streams and attract ESG-conscious MLP investors seeking energy transition exposure [2]
Key Risks
  1. General partner control by Delek US Holdings creates structural governance risk — dropdown pricing, intercompany transactions, and distribution policy are set by the parent, potentially at the expense of minority unitholders [2]
  2. GAAP earnings deterioration despite revenue growth: Q2 2026 net income fell 35% YoY to $28.9M while revenue surged 56%, driven by rising interest expense and debt burden from acquisition financing [1]
  3. Concentration risk in Delek US refining volumes — if the parent company reduces throughput or faces refining margin compression, DKL's captive gathering and transportation volumes decline proportionally [4]
