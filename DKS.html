Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Dick's Sporting Goods is the largest omnichannel sporting goods retailer in the United States, operating approximately 850+ stores including Dick's Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, and the experiential 'House of Sport' concept. The company completed its transformative acquisition of Foot Locker in 2025, nearly doubling revenue and expanding into footwear/streetwear retail. Q2 FY2026 revenue reached $5.6B (+53.2% YoY, acquisition-driven), with organic comparable sales growth of 2.5-4.0%. FY2026 guidance: $22.1-22.4B revenue, $13.27-$14.27 EPS.

Investment Thesis
Dick's at 10.16x forward earnings offers compelling value for the dominant US sporting goods retailer post-Foot Locker integration. FY2026 guidance of $22.1-22.4B revenue and $13.27-$14.27 EPS reflects a transformed business with massive scale advantages — the combined entity operates 850+ stores with unmatched physical distribution in sporting goods and athletic footwear [1]. Organic comp sales growth of 2.5-4.0% demonstrates underlying health beyond acquisition-driven topline growth. The 'House of Sport' experiential format creates competitive differentiation against e-commerce, while the ~3.3% dividend yield and ~20% 5yr dividend growth CAGR provide attractive income characteristics [4]. ROE of 18.48% confirms strong capital efficiency despite integration-related margin compression.
The bear case centers on acquisition integration risk and margin pressure. Earnings declined 25.7% YoY despite 53.2% revenue growth, indicating the Foot Locker integration is dilutive to profitability — profit margin compressed to 3.97% versus Dick's historical 7-9% range, reflecting integration costs and amortization of acquisition intangibles [1][4]. D/E of 139.08% nearly doubled from pre-acquisition levels, constraining financial flexibility and creating interest rate sensitivity. The consumer discretionary sector faces macroeconomic headwinds from persistent inflation and potential consumer spending pullback, while Nike's direct-to-consumer push and Amazon's athletic category expansion could erode Dick's market position over time. The stock has declined from ~$168 to $134 (-20%) since the acquisition, suggesting the market is skeptical about integration execution. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) may limit faith-based investor allocation.
Key Catalysts
  1. Foot Locker integration synergies projected to deliver $200M+ in annual cost savings by FY2028, with potential for margin normalization back toward Dick's historical 7-9% profit margin as integration costs fade [1]
  2. House of Sport experiential format expansion driving higher per-store revenue and customer engagement, with potential to convert legacy Dick's and Foot Locker locations to the premium concept
  3. Organic comparable sales growth of 2.5-4.0% in FY2026 demonstrates the combined entity's underlying demand strength, which should accelerate as Foot Locker brand repositioning takes hold [1]
Key Risks
  1. Foot Locker integration consuming management bandwidth and capital — 25.7% EPS decline despite 53.2% revenue growth indicates the acquisition is near-term dilutive, with risk of prolonged margin compression if synergies underdeliver [1][4]
  2. Consumer discretionary spending vulnerability in a potential economic slowdown — sporting goods and athletic footwear are deferrable purchases that could contract sharply in recession, amplifying the leverage from acquisition-related debt (D/E 139.08%)
  3. Nike's accelerating direct-to-consumer strategy and Amazon's athletic category expansion could pressure Dick's market position and negotiating leverage with key brands over time
