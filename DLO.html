Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·Uruguay
DLocal is an emerging markets payments technology platform that enables global enterprise merchants to accept and disburse payments in 44+ countries across Africa, Asia, and Latin America. The company processes local payment methods including cards, bank transfers, cash, mobile money, and alternative payment methods, serving commerce, streaming, ride-hailing, financial services, gaming, crypto, and travel industries. Founded in 2016, headquartered in Montevideo, Uruguay. Q2 2026 delivered record $400M revenue (+56% YoY) and TPV of $18B (+92% YoY), marking the 7th consecutive quarter of 50%+ growth.

Investment Thesis
DLocal delivered record Q2 2026 results with $400M revenue (+56% YoY) and TPV of nearly $18B (+92% YoY), marking the 7th consecutive quarter of 50%+ growth [1]. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to 60-70% TPV growth and 25-30% gross profit growth, signaling sustained momentum. At a forward P/E of 12.14x and estimated PEG of 0.42, DLocal is one of the most attractively valued high-growth fintechs globally — trading at a fraction of peers like Adyen (40x+) despite superior revenue growth [4]. D/E of just 12.46%, ROE of 41.29%, and AI-driven operational efficiency (7% headcount equivalent savings) demonstrate a capital-light, scalable business model [2]. The 44+ country network across emerging markets creates a widening competitive moat as each new payment method integration increases switching costs for 600+ enterprise merchants.
The bear case centers on margin compression, governance risk, and emerging market volatility. Profit margin of 15.04% is below the 20% target and has compressed as geographic mix shifts toward lower-take-rate regions in Africa and Asia — Q2 2026 revenue topped estimates but EPS missed, signaling that volume growth is not fully translating to bottom-line profitability [1]. Recurring governance controversies — Muddy Waters fraud allegations (2022), Argentine government investigation (2023), Hollenden Square money laundering report (2025) — create a permanent credibility discount even though investigations found allegations 'not substantiated' [3]. Employee sentiment is weak with Glassdoor ratings of 3.1-3.3 and complaints of micromanagement, suggesting organizational growing pains. Emerging market concentration creates simultaneous currency, regulatory, and political risks across 44+ jurisdictions — a single capital control event in a major market (Brazil, Mexico, Nigeria) could materially impact revenue.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Cross-border payment processing in emerging markets requires regulatory licenses, local banking partnerships, correspondent banking relationships, and compliance infrastructure across 44+ countries — regulatory and physical moats that AI structurally cannot replicate [1][2]. Payment method fragmentation (cards, bank transfers, cash, mobile money, BNPL) demands boots-on-the-ground integrations that take years to build. Switching costs are high for enterprise merchants with integrated payment flows. However, AI could accelerate competitors' ability to replicate local payment integrations, and AI-driven automation could commoditize the technology layer of payment routing and fraud detection over time. AI Opportunity (0/1): DLocal does not generate revenue from AI-related products or services sold to customers. The company uses AI internally for operational efficiency (7% headcount equivalent savings in 2025) but this is cost optimization, not AI revenue [2].
