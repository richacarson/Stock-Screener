Investment Thesis
Digital Realty is a top-two global data center REIT riding the AI infrastructure buildout: Q2 2026 revenue of $1.92B (+29% YoY) and record Core FFO of $2.13/share drove a raise in full-year Core FFO guidance to $8.15-$8.20/share, backed by a record $1.9B backlog and a development pipeline that doubled to 1.4GW under construction, 63% pre-leased at an 11.5% stabilized yield [1]. AI and hyperscale cloud demand is structurally additive — DLR cannot be disrupted by AI because it supplies the physical power, cooling, and interconnection infrastructure AI requires — and interconnection bookings tied to enterprise AI hit a record $108M in the zero-to-one megawatt category [1]. D/E of 68% is within target and leverage has been improving even as the company funds a $20B pipeline, aided by a capital-partnership funding model that avoids over-reliance on balance-sheet debt.
The bear case centers on valuation and income quality: at a Forward P/E of 79.7 (reflecting AFFO/FFO being the more relevant REIT metric, still rich on that basis) and ROE of just 2.91%, DLR is priced for continued flawless execution of its development pipeline, and the GAAP payout ratio of 234.8% reflects that GAAP earnings understate REIT cash flow but also limits headline dividend safety optics [6]. The quarterly dividend has been frozen at $1.22/share since March 2022 — over four years without a raise — as capital is redirected to growth capex, meaning income investors are not being compensated with dividend growth despite the AI-driven revenue acceleration. Execution risk is real given the pipeline's scale: 1.4GW under construction at $20B represents a near-doubling of committed capital in six months, and any slowdown in hyperscaler capex, a well-documented industry-wide swing factor, would compress the pre-leasing and yield assumptions underpinning the growth thesis.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued lease-up of the record 1.4GW development pipeline (63% pre-leased at an 11.5% stabilized yield) converting into recognized FFO as capacity is delivered through 2026-2028 [1]
  2. Resumption of dividend growth if 2026-2027 Core FFO growth (guided at double digits) provides enough coverage cushion to move off the four-year dividend freeze [1][6]
  3. Record $1.9B backlog and accelerating zero-to-one megawatt AI/enterprise interconnection bookings ($108M, a company record) converting into higher-margin recurring revenue [1]
Key Risks
  1. Heavy reliance on continued hyperscaler AI/cloud capex; any industry-wide pause or reallocation of AI infrastructure spending would directly compress DLR's pre-leasing pace and development yields [1]
  2. Elevated valuation (Forward P/E ~80x, ROE under 3%) leaves little room for execution missteps on the $20B, 1.4GW pipeline, which represents a near-doubling of committed development capital in a single half-year [1]
  3. Dividend has not grown in over four years (frozen at $1.22/share since March 2022), and the 234.8% GAAP payout ratio leaves limited headline cushion despite AFFO coverage, a risk if development yields disappoint [6]
