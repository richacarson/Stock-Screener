Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Discount Stores ·NASDAQ ·United States
Dollar Tree is a discount variety retailer headquartered in Chesapeake, Virginia, operating the Dollar Tree banner across the U.S. and Canada after divesting Family Dollar in 2025 (now reported as discontinued operations). Under CEO Mike Creedon, who joined as COO in 2022 and became permanent CEO after an interim stint, it is shifting from a single-price-point model to multi-price assortments and replacing decades-old legacy systems with AI-enabled planning, inventory and workforce platforms. Q2 FY2026 (ended August 1, 2026) total sales grew 7.0% to $4.89B with comparable sales +3.7%; adjusted EPS was $1.39 (+80.5%) and GAAP EPS of $2.70 included a one-time tariff refund. Full-year FY2026 guidance is $20.5-20.7B net sales and adjusted EPS of $7.70-8.05. The company pays no dividend.

Investment Thesis
Post-Family Dollar, Dollar Tree is a simpler, higher-return business. Q2 FY2026 sales grew 7.0% to $4.89B with comps +3.7% on top of +6.5% last year, adjusted EPS rose 80.5% to $1.39, and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $7.70-8.05 on $20.5-20.7B of sales [1]. ROE of 46%, an 8.0% net margin and TTM free cash flow of roughly $9.72 per share (P/FCF ~14x) at 17.6x forward earnings support the case that multi-price assortments and AI-enabled inventory and workforce systems can keep compounding earnings [2][3][5].
The bear case is earnings quality and leverage. Q2 GAAP EPS of $2.70 was inflated by a one-time tariff refund, FY2026 guidance includes about $0.60 of net tariff-refund benefit, and Q3 adjusted EPS guidance of $0.80-0.95 (including ~$0.50 of refund reinvestment) missed estimates [1]. Growth is coming from ticket rather than traffic, D/E is 224% and price-to-book is 7.7x after Family Dollar write-downs [3][5], and the stock has rallied from a 52-week low of $85.88 to $138.60, near its $142.40 high, leaving little margin for execution slips by a leadership team that is still new [2][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 FY2026 results (early December) confirming comps of 3-4% and net sales of $5.0-5.1B, with progress toward the raised $7.70-8.05 adjusted EPS guide [1]
  2. Margin gains from AI-enabled assortment planning, inventory visibility and workforce-management platforms replacing legacy systems [2]
  3. Further rollout of multi-price assortments lifting average ticket at the core Dollar Tree banner [1]
Key Risks
  1. Normalization of earnings once one-time tariff refunds (about $0.60 of FY2026 adjusted EPS) roll off, with Q3 guidance already below consensus [1]
  2. Traffic stagnation — Q2 growth was driven mostly by ticket — as multi-price moves test the brand's value perception [1]
  3. Leverage of 224% D/E and residual compliance obligations from the DOJ DC 202 resolution [4][5]
