Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NASDAQ ·United States
Dorchester Minerals, L.P. acquires, owns, and administers royalty properties across the United States. The partnership holds producing and nonproducing mineral, royalty, overriding royalty, net profits, and leasehold interests in 594 counties and parishes in 28 states. Founded in 1982 and headquartered in Dallas, Texas, DMLP operates as a pure-play mineral royalty company with minimal operating costs and only 26 employees. Q2 2026 net income reached $30.9M ($0.62/unit) on operating revenues of $56.1M, with royalty receipts of $50.4M and NPI receipts of $16.6M [1]. H1 2026 net income doubled YoY to $60.0M on revenues of $114.9M versus $75.6M [1].

CEO Ehrman's 22-year tenure and the board's 11.9-year average tenure provide exceptional leadership continuity [4]. The mineral royalty business model is maximally immune to AI disruption while benefiting from AI-driven natural gas demand for data center power generation. The bear case centers on commodity price dependence, distribution volatility, and structural revenue unpredictability. While H1 2026 revenue surged 52%, the 5-year revenue CAGR is -2.0%, reflecting the fundamental challenge of a royalty business tied to volatile commodity prices — revenue declined from $170M+ (2022) to $152.8M (2025) before the current recovery [1][3]. Distributions are extraordinarily volatile: Q2 2026's $1.273/unit is nearly 3x the Q1 2026 distribution of $0.475, making income planning impossible for yield-oriented investors. The payout ratio of 182.8% of GAAP earnings (typical for MLPs that distribute cash flow rather than accounting income) may concern investors unfamiliar with MLP accounting. With only 26 employees and no operational control over drilling activity, DMLP is entirely dependent on third-party operators' capital allocation decisions — a decline in drilling activity on DMLP acreage would reduce royalty volumes regardless of mineral rights quality [2]. The trailing P/E of 18.72 is relatively elevated for a commodity-dependent business with no growth levers beyond waiting for higher prices.
