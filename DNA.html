Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NYSE ·United States
Ginkgo Bioworks is a synthetic biology and bioengineering platform company pioneering the concept of 'programming biology.' Founded in 2008 by MIT scientists including CEO Jason Kelly, the company provides cell engineering services and is pivoting to AI-driven autonomous labs that accelerate bioengineering across biopharma, agriculture, and industrial biotech. After losing approximately $6B cumulatively, Ginkgo divested its biosecurity business in Q1 2026 and refocused on autonomous lab offerings. FY2025 revenue was $170M (down 25% YoY), Q1 2026 revenue was $19M (down 49% YoY), and the company guided 2026 cash burn of $125-150M. Market cap is approximately $718M.

The company reduced cash burn by 55% from 2024 to 2025, guided 2026 cash burn to $125-150M, and refocused entirely on autonomous lab offerings after divesting biosecurity [1]. The founding team's 18-year commitment to the 'programming biology' vision, with co-founders Kelly (CEO), Shetty (COO), and Canton (CTO) all still leading, provides strategic continuity rare among loss-making biotechs [2]. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Ginkgo's core business involves physical biological engineering — wet-lab work, cell libraries, strain development, and biological manufacturing processes that AI cannot substitute [2]. Synthetic biology requires physical experimentation with living organisms, regulatory compliance for biological products, and proprietary cell programming expertise. AI is fundamentally an enabler for Ginkgo's platform (faster design cycles, better predictions of biological behavior) rather than a substitution threat. However, AI-native biotech startups could build competing platforms, and large pharma companies investing in internal AI-driven drug discovery could reduce demand for Ginkgo's contract services. The company's pivoting TO AI (autonomous labs) means AI defines its competitive strategy rather than threatening it. AI Opportunity (1/1): Ginkgo is actively selling AI-driven autonomous lab products — lab robots with AI capabilities for bioengineering workflows — and has made this the centerpiece of its strategic pivot [2]. The company generates revenue from AI-enhanced cell engineering services, though specific AI revenue is not separately disclosed.
