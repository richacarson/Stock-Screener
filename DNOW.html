Company Profile
Industrials ·Industrial Distribution ·NYSE ·United States
DNOW Inc. is a Houston-based distributor of pipe, valves, fittings, flanges, MRO supplies, artificial lift, pumping, process and measurement equipment, plus supply-chain and materials-management services, to the upstream, midstream, downstream, utility and industrial markets [4]. Founded in 1862 and spun off from National Oilwell Varco in 2014, the company closed its all-stock acquisition of MRC Global on November 6, 2025, creating the largest energy and industrial PVF distributor in North America [2]. CEO Dave Cherechinsky (since 2020) is leading integration, including stabilizing MRC Global's legacy Oracle ERP and migrating U.S. locations onto DNOW's SAP platform [2]. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.31B (+108% YoY, +10% sequentially), and the company reports Q3 2026 results on November 5, 2026 [1].

Investment Thesis
DNOW at $15.80 offers an earnings-recovery story built on scale. The MRC Global combination produced Q2 2026 revenue of $1.31B (+108% YoY, +10% sequentially), management raised its 2026 outlook, and Q3 guidance calls for low-to-mid single-digit sequential growth with EBITDA margins of 5-5.5% [1]. The annualized cost-synergy run rate expected by year-end 2026 was raised to ~$30M from an original $17M target [2]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E 30.9%, current ratio 2.1x), TTM free cash flow yield is ~4.8%, and the stock trades at 19.2x forward earnings and ~0.7x sales [4][5]. Data-center and midstream build-outs offer new demand on top of the core energy business, and the Inspire Impact Score of 41 (ALIGNED, no negative attributions) supports faith-aligned ownership.
The bear case is execution and cyclicality. The business is unprofitable on a GAAP basis (TTM profit margin -4.6%, ROE -11.6%) because of merger charges, and management called MRC Global's ERP problems 'a much heavier lift than previously known'; half of U.S. revenue runs on the legacy platform being migrated to SAP [2][5]. Organic momentum is thin: management said Q2 revenue was roughly flat excluding a large non-recurring Q1 project and share-recovery efforts [1]. Revenue remains tied to oil and gas capital spending, which fell sharply in 2015-2016 and 2020 (FY2020 net loss of $427M) [5]. The Q3 report on November 5, 2026 is a near-term test of the margin guide [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results on November 5, 2026 — delivering the guided 5-5.5% EBITDA margin and sequential growth would confirm that MRC integration is moving from remediation to recovery [1]
  2. Synergy delivery — the year-end 2026 annualized cost-synergy run rate was raised to ~$30M from $17M; further upside as U.S. MRC locations finish migrating to SAP [2]
  3. New end markets — data-center construction and midstream gas infrastructure are being pursued as growth vectors beyond traditional upstream oil and gas [2]
Key Risks
  1. ERP and integration execution — legacy MRC Global Oracle instability has already disrupted operations and share; a botched SAP migration could cost customers and delay synergies [2]
  2. Energy capex cyclicality — DNOW lost $427M in FY2020 when oil and gas spending collapsed, and a renewed downturn in drilling or midstream budgets would hit volumes directly [5]
  3. Weak organic growth — excluding acquisition effects and a non-recurring Q1 project, Q2 revenue was roughly flat, so the 108% YoY headline overstates underlying demand [1]
