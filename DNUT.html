Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NASDAQ ·United States
Krispy Kreme is a global doughnut and beverage company operating across approximately 30 countries through company-owned shops, franchise locations, and delivered fresh daily (DFD) points of access. The company is executing a turnaround under CEO Josh Charlesworth, pursuing a refranchising strategy, recovering from the terminated McDonald's USA partnership ($28.9M in charges), and addressing legacy debt. Q2 2026 revenue was $331M (-12.8% YoY) but adjusted EBITDA surged 43% to $28.8M with margin expanding 340 bps to 8.7%. Average weekly US sales per door rose 33% YoY to $697. FY2026 guidance: $1.25-$1.35B net revenue, $140-$150M adjusted EBITDA.
22,000 Employees ·www.krispykreme.com

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Krispy Kreme shows elements of infinite game thinking — a distinctive brand purpose centered on joy, genuine investment in employee development through the Centre for Peeps, and willingness to make difficult decisions like exiting the McDonald's partnership and pursuing refranchising. Q2 2026's EBITDA surge of 43% suggests the turnaround strategy is gaining traction. However, the company's recent history of financial turbulence, leadership transitions, and restructuring reflects reactive survival rather than proactive purpose-driven strategy. Investment Thesis
Krispy Kreme's turnaround is gaining momentum — Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA surged 43% to $28.8M with margin expanding 340 basis points to 8.7%, following Q1's 38% EBITDA growth [2]. US average weekly sales per door jumped 33% YoY to $697, digital sales grew 8% to 22% of US retail sales, and the refranchising strategy is systematically reducing capital intensity and improving profitability metrics. D/E improved from 208% to 140% as the balance sheet deleverages. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $140-$150M represents substantial year-over-year improvement, and the forward P/E compressed from 141x to 53x as analysts raise forward estimates on the improving trajectory [6]. At $3.20 per share, the stock is priced for distress — any sustained GAAP profitability or further EBITDA acceleration could drive significant re-rating. The Inspire score of 47 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions and 16 positive categories makes DNUT one of the best faith-alignment profiles in the consumer sector.
The bear case remains substantial. Revenue continues to decline — Q2 fell 12.8% YoY to $331M, and FY2026 guidance of $1.25-$1.35B represents another 10%+ contraction from 2025 as refranchising transfers revenue to franchise partners [2]. ROE of -58.15% and profit margin of -6.26% reflect ongoing GAAP losses from impairment charges and restructuring costs. The stock has fallen over 80% from its $17 IPO price in 2021 to $3.20, destroying massive shareholder value through the cumulative damage of the McDonald's partnership failure, cybersecurity incident, Insomnia Cookies divestiture, and persistent negative earnings [6]. D/E of 140% means the balance sheet remains overleveraged with limited margin for error. Forward P/E of 53x assumes a return to profitability that remains unproven on a full-year GAAP basis. The class action lawsuit over the McDonald's partnership disclosure creates ongoing legal and financial risk. The turnaround depends on flawless execution of the refranchising strategy and sustained margin expansion in an inflationary cost environment where input costs (flour, sugar, dairy) and labor costs remain elevated.

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Krispy Kreme's core business — manufacturing and selling fresh doughnuts through retail shops and delivered fresh daily points of access — faces minimal direct AI substitution risk [1].
