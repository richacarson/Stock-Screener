Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Healthpeak Properties is an S&P 500 real estate investment trust focused on healthcare discovery and delivery, owning and operating 703+ properties across outpatient medical, lab, and senior housing segments. The company merged with Physicians Realty Trust in March 2024 and completed the Janus Living senior housing REIT IPO in March 2026, retaining 81.6% ownership. In Q2 2026, Healthpeak raised FFO guidance to $1.73-1.77/share, reported lab occupancy surging 80bps to 78.5%, and closed a Brookfield joint venture generating $1B in proceeds. Founded in 1985, headquartered in Denver, Colorado.
411 Employees ·www.healthpeak.com

Investment Thesis
Healthpeak offers a 5.80% dividend yield supported by FFO adjusted guidance of $1.73-1.77/share (well above the ~$1.22 annual dividend), with Q2 2026 delivering beat-and-raise results — revenue of $749.81M beat estimates, FFO of $0.46/share beat consensus, and same-store NOI guidance was raised 75bps at midpoint [1]. The Brookfield joint venture generated $1B in proceeds, and Janus Living is on pace to double in size with 19% same-store NOI growth. Lab portfolio recovery is accelerating dramatically, with Torrey Pines submarket surging from ~65% leased in December to 97% including LOIs, and total lab occupancy gaining 140bps since year-end to 78.5% [1]. Outpatient medical leasing remains robust with 1.2M square feet executed and +5% cash re-leasing spreads. Aging U.S. demographics with 10,000 Baby Boomers turning 65 daily through 2030 create structural demand tailwinds for healthcare facilities.
The bear case centers on structurally weak financial metrics despite the attractive yield. ROE of 2.80% is very low even by REIT standards, the forward P/E of 100.46x and GAAP payout ratio of 359% — while distorted by REIT depreciation accounting — deter investors using traditional fundamental screens [4]. The dividend has shown zero growth over 5 years, offering yield without capital appreciation — a potential value trap for income investors seeking total return. Life science lab vacancy rates, while recovering at Torrey Pines, remain elevated in broader markets as biotech funding has tightened and construction pipelines deliver new inventory across San Francisco, Boston, and San Diego [1]. D/E of 118.2% creates leverage risk with $10.71B in total debt against $1.18B in cash, and net debt leverage of approximately 5x adjusted EBITDA leaves limited buffer against credit deterioration. With only 411 employees managing 703+ properties, operational depth is thin compared to vertically integrated healthcare companies [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Lab portfolio recovery accelerating — Torrey Pines submarket leased percentage surging from 65% in December to 97%, with total lab occupancy up 140bps since year-end to 78.5%, signaling inflection in the life science real estate cycle [1]
  2. Janus Living subsidiary delivering 19% same-store NOI growth and on pace to double in size, providing a high-growth senior housing platform within the healthcare REIT portfolio [1]
  3. Agentic AI operating platform deployment under Palantir veteran Omkar Joshi could unlock operational efficiencies across 703+ properties, improving NOI margins and tenant experience [2]
Key Risks
  1. Zero dividend growth over 5 years and GAAP payout ratio of 359% create value trap dynamics — high current yield but no income growth or capital appreciation for investors requiring total return [4]
  2. D/E of 118.2% with $10.71B total debt creates interest rate sensitivity — sustained higher rates increase borrowing costs and reduce the relative attractiveness of the 5.80% yield versus risk-free alternatives [4]
  3. Broader life science lab vacancy rates remain elevated outside Torrey Pines as biotech funding constraints and construction pipeline deliveries create supply-demand imbalance in key innovation markets [1]
