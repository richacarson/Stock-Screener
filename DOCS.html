Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NYSE ·United States
Doximity, Inc. operates the largest professional medical network in the United States, reaching over 80% of U.S. physicians. The platform provides clinical workflow tools including secure messaging, telehealth, e-faxing, e-signatures, credentialing, and AI-powered clinical tools (Scribe ambient notetaking, Ask medical search engine). Revenue is generated primarily through pharmaceutical and health system marketing solutions. FY2026 revenue reached $644.9M (+13% YoY) with 30.4% net margins and $317.5M free cash flow. Q2 CY2026 revenue of $156.6M grew 7.3% YoY, signaling deceleration. Founded in 2010 by Jeff Tangney and Nate Gross, headquartered in San Francisco, California.
830 Employees ·www.doximity.com

Track Record (2/3): Doximity has successfully expanded from a physician social network to a comprehensive clinical platform, tripling revenue from $207M (FY2021) to $644.9M (FY2026) in five years [1]. CEO Tangney declared 2026 an 'AI investment year' with partnerships with Aledade and Photon to scale AI delivery. These are products being actively used by physicians, not vaporware. Investment Thesis
Doximity is a high-quality, asset-light healthcare platform with exceptional financial metrics: 30.4% profit margin, 19.3% ROE, virtually zero debt (D/E 0.01%), and $748.6M cash on a $3.8B market cap [1][4]. The company dominates physician networking with 80%+ U.S. physician penetration and is pivoting aggressively into AI with shipped products (Scribe, Ask, PeerCheck) that position it as the leading physician AI platform [2]. FY2026 revenue of $644.9M grew 13% with $317.5M free cash flow (49% FCF margin), and the 43% R&D workforce allocation signals sustained innovation. At 14.56x forward P/E, the stock trades at a meaningful discount to healthcare IT peers after a 65% decline in the past year, suggesting the market has heavily priced in the growth deceleration but may be undervaluing the AI platform optionality and the long-term value of the physician network moat.
The primary concern is growth deceleration and the path to AI monetization. FY2027 guidance of $664-676M (3-5% growth) represents a sharp deceleration from 13% in FY2026 and 23% in Q2 FY2026, raising questions about whether the core pharma advertising business is maturing [1]. Net income declined 12.2% YoY despite revenue growth, suggesting margin pressure from AI investments. The PEG ratio of 3.79 reflects the market's concern about growth sustainability. Net insider selling of $105M in the last 12 months — particularly notable with the stock down 71% from its $76.51 high — signals limited insider conviction at current levels. The pharma advertising model faces structural questions: pharma companies are experimenting with AI-driven physician outreach that could bypass Doximity's network. The company is small (880 employees, $4B market cap) and dependent on a single market (U.S. physicians), limiting geographic and customer diversification.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI platform monetization breakthrough — Scribe, Ask, and PeerCheck adoption by health systems and pharma companies could create new subscription revenue streams beyond the core advertising model, potentially reaccelerating growth above the 3-5% FY2027 guidance
  2. Physician AI platform network effects — with 85% of U.S. physicians already on the platform, new AI tools trained on physician workflow data could create a flywheel of adoption, engagement, and monetization that competitors cannot replicate
  3. Stock price recovery from deeply oversold levels — at 14.56x forward P/E with 30%+ profit margins and $749M cash, DOCS trades at a significant discount to healthcare IT peers, creating potential for multiple expansion if FY2027 results exceed conservative guidance
Key Risks
  1. FY2027 revenue growth deceleration to 3-5% (from 13% in FY2026) suggests the core pharma advertising business may be approaching maturity — if AI tools cannot create new revenue streams quickly enough, the company could face secular growth stagnation
  2. Net insider selling of $105M and the 71% decline from the 52-week high of $76.51 signal persistent institutional concern about the growth trajectory and AI monetization timeline
  3. Concentration risk in a single market (U.S. physician advertising) with 880 employees makes Doximity vulnerable to pharma budget cuts, regulatory changes in pharma marketing, or competitive disruption from health system EHR vendors (Epic, Oracle Health) adding AI features

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Doximity's core moat — the largest U.S. physician network reaching 85%+ of doctors — is a data and network effect that AI cannot replicate [2]. The platform's value comes from physician identity verification, credentialing, peer relationships, and clinical workflow integration, all of which require trust and professional validation that AI alone cannot provide. Pharmaceutical companies need verified access to physicians for marketing (Doximity's primary revenue source), and this access depends on the physician network, not AI-substitutable content. However, the pharma advertising model faces some exposure: AI could enable more targeted, automated physician outreach that bypasses Doximity's network, and large health systems could build competing AI clinical tools. The company's proactive AI pivot mitigates this risk. AI Opportunity (1/1): Doximity has shipped multiple AI products — Scribe (ambient notetaking), Ask (clinical AI assistant), and PeerCheck (physician-verified AI) — and CEO Tangney has declared Doximity aims to become 'the largest physician AI platform' [2]. The 380-person R&D team is heavily focused on AI, and partnerships with Aledade and Photon are scaling AI delivery. AI is generating measurable engagement and is positioned as the company's primary growth driver.
