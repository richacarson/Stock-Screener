Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NMS ·United States
DocuSign is the market leader in electronic signatures and the pioneer of Intelligent Agreement Management (IAM), an AI-native platform that automates the entire agreement lifecycle from creation through signing, management, and analytics. The company serves over 1.5 million customers across industries with $3.3B in annual recurring revenue as of FY2026 (ending January 2026). DocuSign's IAM platform now represents approximately 12.6% of total ARR and is growing rapidly, with over 10,000 IAM customers. TTM revenue of $3.36B reflects 9.4% YoY growth with improving profitability. Founded in 2003 and headquartered in San Francisco, California [1][2].

Investment Thesis
DocuSign at $80 with a $15B market cap is the dominant e-signature platform successfully pivoting to AI-native Intelligent Agreement Management (IAM), creating a larger addressable market. FY2026 revenue of $3.22B grew 8% with $1B+ in free cash flow, and Q2 FY2027 revenue of $875.7M signals accelerating growth toward the $3.48-$3.50B FY2027 guidance [1][2][3]. The IAM platform now represents 12.6% of ARR ($350M+) with over 10,000 customers and is growing significantly faster than the core business. The financial profile is exceptional for a SaaS company — D/E of just 10.7%, forward P/E of 15.4, PEG of 0.74, and 30%+ non-GAAP operating margin guidance suggest the stock is attractively valued relative to its growth rate and profitability trajectory [5]. The $778M cash position and $1B authorized buyback provide capital return optionality.
The bear case focuses on growth deceleration and competitive pressure. Revenue growth of 9.4% is below the 10% IOWN target and well below the company's pandemic-era 40%+ growth rates, raising questions about whether the IAM pivot can sustainably reaccelerate growth or merely maintain mid-single-digit organic expansion. GAAP profit margin of 9.8% is well below the 20% target, and trailing P/E of 49x GAAP earnings obscures the gap between adjusted and reported profitability [5]. CEO Thygesen's pattern of consistent insider selling totaling over $11M in 2025 alone undermines the narrative of management conviction in the long-term story [4]. Competitively, Microsoft is embedding agreement features in Copilot 365, Adobe is building AI into Acrobat, and AI startups are attacking the CLM space — DocuSign's brand dominance in e-signature does not automatically extend to the broader IAM market where it must compete against larger platforms with existing enterprise relationships.
Key Catalysts
  1. IAM platform acceleration — 12.6% of ARR and growing, with over 10,000 customers adopted and consumption-based pricing launching in Q1 FY2027, potentially driving higher net revenue retention and expanding TAM beyond the $50B+ agreement management market [2][3]
  2. Margin expansion trajectory — FY2027 guidance of 30.0-30.5% non-GAAP operating margin combined with $1B+ annual free cash flow positions DocuSign for significant earnings power as IAM scales and operating leverage improves [2]
  3. Developer ecosystem and AI agent integration — MCP server for LLM integration and expanded developer tools position DocuSign as essential infrastructure for AI-powered business workflows, creating a new growth vector as AI agents increasingly manage agreements [3]
Key Risks
  1. Growth deceleration risk — revenue growth of 9.4% is below the 10% IOWN target and has decelerated from 40%+ pandemic-era rates, and the IAM platform must demonstrate it can sustainably reaccelerate growth beyond the maturing core e-signature business [1][5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): DocuSign's core e-signature business is PROTECTED by substantial moats: legal compliance frameworks (ESIGN Act, eIDAS regulation), deep enterprise integrations with hundreds of third-party applications, network effects requiring both parties to use compatible signing, and FedRAMP authorization for government work [1]. These regulatory and switching-cost barriers make the e-signature core defensible against AI substitution — AI tools cannot replace the legal validity and audit trail that regulated e-signatures provide. However, the IAM expansion space faces more direct AI competition: Microsoft Copilot, Adobe Acrobat AI, and emerging startups are building AI-powered agreement tools that could erode DocuSign's expansion opportunity. The company's broadest risk is commoditization of e-signature as a feature embedded in larger platforms rather than a standalone product. AI Opportunity (1/1): DocuSign generates quantifiable AI revenue through its IAM platform — approximately $350M in ARR (12.6% of total ARR as of April 2026), up from 10.8% at the end of FY2026 [2]. Over 10,000 customers have purchased IAM, the Navigator product uses AI to analyze agreement repositories, and the consumption-based pricing model directly monetizes AI capabilities. The company's MCP server for LLM integration positions DocuSign as AI infrastructure for agreement workflows [2][3].
