Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Amdocs Limited provides software and services to communications, entertainment, media, and other service providers worldwide. The company designs, develops, operates, and markets cloud-native telecom platforms including CES25 (a GenAI-led customer experience suite) and aOS (an agentic operating system for telecommunications). Operates through managed services, software licensing, and system integration. Founded in 1982, headquartered in Chesterfield, Missouri, with major operations in Israel.

Investment Thesis
Amdocs offers deep value at 8.9x forward earnings with a 3.4% dividend yield backed by 14 consecutive years of growth and a ~10% five-year dividend CAGR [6]. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1.172B grew 3.9% YoY with non-GAAP EPS of $1.78 beating guidance, and the $4.28B twelve-month backlog provides strong revenue visibility [1]. The fortress balance sheet (D/E 27%) supports continued capital returns, and the aOS agentic AI platform positions Amdocs to capture telecom AI modernization spending as carriers automate customer service and network operations. FY2026 guidance calls for 2.6-4.6% revenue growth and 5-7% EPS growth, with Europe recording a record $192M quarterly revenue (up 6.2%) [1].
The bear case centers on structural growth constraints and workforce disruption. Five-year revenue CAGR of approximately 2% reflects an industry where carrier consolidation limits the customer base and spending is cyclical [1]. The 7,200 positions eliminated since 2023 — roughly 25% of the workforce — raises questions about organizational capability and morale even as it supports near-term margins [4]. Profit margin of 12.5% is capped by the managed-services-heavy model (65% of revenue), limiting upside even with AI efficiency gains. The CEO transition to Shimie Hortig introduces execution risk during a critical AI platform buildout, and the aOS must prove it can generate material revenue before hyperscalers and AI-native competitors disintermediate Amdocs' integration role [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Commercial traction of the aOS agentic operating system with tier-1 carriers converts the platform from cost center to incremental revenue growth driver, proving the AI pivot thesis
  2. European expansion continues at record pace ($192M quarterly, up 6.2%) as new Western European carrier wins build pipeline beyond the $4.28B twelve-month backlog
  3. AI-driven margin expansion as the 7,200-position workforce reduction fully annualizes, potentially pushing non-GAAP operating margins above the 21.5% current level toward 23-24%
Key Risks
  1. Telecom carrier spending weakness — particularly from large customers like T-Mobile — could compress organic revenue growth below the low end of 2.6% FY2026 guidance
  2. AI commoditization of telecom integration services by hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) and AI-native startups erodes managed services pricing power, threatening the 65% of revenue from that segment
  3. Workforce reduction of 7,200 positions degrades institutional knowledge and delivery capability, creating execution risk on complex multi-year carrier modernization projects during the CEO transition
