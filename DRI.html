Investment Thesis
Darden Restaurants delivered a strong FY2026 with $13.21B revenue (up 9.4%), EPS of $10.38 (up 17%), and blended same-restaurant sales growth of 4.5%, powered by Olive Garden (+4.0%) and LongHorn Steakhouse (+7.2%) [1]. FY2027 guidance projects $13.6-13.75B revenue with EPS of $11.10-$11.35, 75-80 new restaurant openings, $875M capex, and an 8% dividend increase to $1.52/quarter — signaling management confidence in the growth trajectory [1]. At 18.6x forward earnings with a 3.07% dividend yield, Darden offers a reasonable valuation for the largest full-service restaurant franchise in the US with consistent cash flow generation. AI-driven efficiency tools across 2,000+ locations continue to improve labor productivity and food waste management [3].
The bear case centers on balance sheet leverage, margin pressure, and consumer cyclicality. D/E of 401% reflects significant debt load amplified by lease obligations under ASC 842, creating vulnerability in an elevated rate environment [5]. Profit margin of 9.2% leaves limited room for cost absorption if food inflation or labor costs spike. The COVID-era dividend suspension resets the consecutive years paid clock to just 5 years, undermining the dividend track record for income investors, and payout ratio of 54% is above the ideal 40% target [5]. Consumer spending on full-service dining is inherently cyclical — Darden's revenue dropped 36% during COVID. Competition from fast-casual chains (Chipotle, CAVA, Sweetgreen) continues to pressure the full-service dining segment, particularly among younger demographics who increasingly favor faster, cheaper formats.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2027 guidance of $13.6-13.75B revenue with $11.10-$11.35 EPS and 75-80 new restaurant openings demonstrates management confidence in sustained growth [1]
  2. 8% dividend increase to $1.52/quarter and $1.5B share repurchase authorization signal strong capital return commitment and management conviction [1]
  3. Chuy's integration adding a high-growth Tex-Mex brand with potential for accelerated unit expansion under Darden's operational infrastructure and scale advantages [1]
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 401% creates significant balance sheet risk from lease-heavy restaurant operations, particularly if interest rates remain elevated or a consumer recession compresses cash flows [5]
  2. Full-service dining secular headwinds as fast-casual chains capture share with faster service and lower price points, pressuring traffic at traditional sit-down restaurants across all Darden brands
  3. Consumer cyclicality — Darden's revenue is correlated with discretionary spending and dropped 36% during COVID, making the stock vulnerable to any recession or consumer confidence deterioration
