Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NYSE ·United States
Darden Restaurants owns and operates full-service restaurant brands including Olive Garden, LongHorn Steakhouse, Cheddar's Scratch Kitchen, Chuy's, Yard House, Ruth's Chris Steak House, The Capital Grille, Seasons 52, Eddie V's Prime Seafood, and Bahama Breeze across the U.S. and Canada. Founded in 1938 and headquartered in Orlando, Florida, Darden reported fiscal 2026 revenue of $13.2B, up 9.4% year-over-year, led by same-restaurant sales strength at Olive Garden and LongHorn Steakhouse.

Investment Thesis
Darden is executing well across its portfolio, with fiscal 2026 revenue up 9.4% to $13.2B, LongHorn Steakhouse same-restaurant sales up 9.5% in Q4, and Olive Garden continuing to take share from smaller casual-dining operators [1]. The company just raised its dividend 8% (a sixth straight year of increases) and authorized a new $1.5B buyback, reflecting confident free cash flow generation and a shareholder-friendly capital allocation policy backed by 32 consecutive years of uninterrupted dividend payments [2][3].
The bear case centers on valuation support and structural risk: D/E of 365% (largely lease-related) leaves limited balance-sheet flexibility if same-restaurant sales momentum reverses, and profit margin of 9.1% is thin for a company trading near 20x trailing earnings [6]. A recurring pattern of wage-and-hour and tipped-wage discrimination litigation at premium brands like Capital Grille creates ongoing legal and reputational risk, and casual dining remains structurally exposed to any pullback in discretionary consumer spending [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. LongHorn Steakhouse same-restaurant sales momentum (+9.5% in Q4 FY2026) continuing to outpace the casual-dining category
  2. Fiscal 2026 total sales growth guided up to 8.5-9.3%, with 60-65 new restaurant openings planned to sustain unit growth
  3. New $1.5B share repurchase authorization alongside a sixth consecutive annual dividend increase signaling confidence in free cash flow
Key Risks
  1. High D/E (365%) driven by lease obligations and buyback-funded capital structure limits flexibility in a sales downturn
  2. Recurring wage-and-hour and tipped-wage discrimination litigation at Capital Grille and other premium brands creates legal/reputational overhang [5]
  3. Casual and fine dining remain discretionary spending categories exposed to a consumer pullback if labor markets weaken
