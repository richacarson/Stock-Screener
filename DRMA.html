Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Dermata Therapeutics is a San Diego micro-cap that in September 2025 pivoted from prescription dermatology drug development (its Spongilla-based XYNGARI acne candidate) to over-the-counter, direct-to-consumer skincare, withdrawing the XYNGARI IND [3][1]. Its Tome Skincare brand (a once-weekly foundational treatment and a clearing treatment for acne) was scheduled to launch commercially on August 25, 2026 [1]. The company had $4.4M of cash at June 30, 2026, guided to fund operations only into Q4 2026, has 9 employees and a market cap of roughly $3.8M [2][6]. The CEO is Gerald (Gerry) Proehl [6].

Investment Thesis
The bull case is optionality on a tiny base: Dermata carries no debt, launched its first commercial product (Tome Foundational Treatment) in August 2026, and at a ~$3.8M market cap even modest DTC traction would be material [1][6]. The OTC route removes FDA approval risk and costly Phase 3 trials, letting the company generate revenue far sooner than its prior XYNGARI path [3].
The bear case dominates. Cash was $4.4M at June 30, 2026 after $4.9M of H1 operating cash burn, and management guided that it would fund operations only into Q4 2026 — i.e., now — so further dilutive financing or a going-concern event is likely [1][2]. ROE is -184%, there is no reported revenue yet, the company has used three reverse splits to stay listed, and the stock trades at $0.77, below Nasdaq's $1 minimum bid again [5][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. First reported Tome Skincare sales in the Q3 2026 report, establishing whether DTC customer acquisition is economic [1]
  2. Additional Tome product launches planned after the foundational treatment, broadening the DTC basket [4]
  3. Any non-dilutive partnership or retail distribution deal that extends the runway beyond Q4 2026 [2]
Key Risks
  1. Cash runway only into Q4 2026 ($4.4M cash at June 30, 2026) makes another highly dilutive raise or going-concern doubt near-certain [1][2]
  2. Nasdaq listing risk: shares at ~$0.77 are below the $1 minimum bid, and a prior delisting determination in 2025 followed repeated reverse splits [5][6]
  3. DTC skincare is crowded and marketing-intensive; a 9-person company with no brand history may be unable to acquire customers profitably [2]
