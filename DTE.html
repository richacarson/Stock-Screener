Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
DTE Energy is a Detroit-based diversified energy company operating through Electric, Gas, DTE Vantage, and Energy Trading segments. It generates, purchases, distributes, and sells electricity to 2.3 million customers and natural gas to 1.4 million customers in Michigan. The company posted Q1 2026 operating EPS of $1.95 on revenue of $5.30B (+23.5% YoY) and guides for full-year operating EPS of $7.59-$7.73 with a $36.5B five-year capital plan through 2030.

Investment Thesis
DTE Energy is a well-managed regulated utility delivering consistent earnings growth with an attractive income profile. Q1 2026 operating EPS of $1.95 positions the company for the high end of $7.59-$7.73 full-year guidance, up from $7.36 in FY2025 [1][3]. The $36.5B five-year capital plan through 2030 targets grid modernization, 2,300 MW of renewables, and data center load growth anchored by the 1.4 GW Oracle contract, supporting the 6-8% long-term EPS growth outlook with bias to the upper end [1]. The 3.01% dividend yield with 60+ years of consecutive payments and 16 consecutive years of growth provides reliable income, supported by a manageable 62.9% payout ratio [5]. At 19.2x forward earnings, DTE offers fair value for a utility with above-average growth visibility, strong regulatory relationships in Michigan, and direct AI infrastructure demand tailwinds. The Inspire score of 45 (ALIGNED) and comprehensive ESG positive attributions support faith-based investment criteria [4].
The bear case centers on DTE's elevated leverage and the limits of its regulated model. Debt-to-equity of 214% and negative free cash flow reflect the capital-intensive nature of the $36.5B buildout plan, creating refinancing risk if interest rates remain elevated [5]. Profit margin of 9.24% is thin even for a utility, and ROE of 12.18% lags the 15% target for strong infrastructure scores — constrained by regulatory return caps. The Forward P/E of 19.21x, up from 17.6x at last screen, leaves limited upside if the EPS growth trajectory merely meets rather than exceeds guidance. Revenue volatility from the Energy Trading segment (2022 revenue was $19.2B vs. $12.5B in 2024) makes underlying utility growth hard to assess [5]. Environmental controversies including Zug Island permit disputes and renewable siting opposition risk project delays and reputational damage [4]. The 5.0% five-year dividend growth CAGR trails the 6% inflation-adjusted target, and regulatory lag on rate recovery could compress returns if capital costs rise faster than approved increases.
