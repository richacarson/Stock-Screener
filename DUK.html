Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Duke Energy is one of the largest energy holding companies in the United States, serving approximately 8.4 million electric and natural gas customers across six states through its regulated utilities. Under CEO Harry Sideris, Duke announced an industry-record $103 billion five-year capital plan to meet surging power demand driven by AI data centers, with 7.8 GW of service agreements signed and 15 GW of new capacity planned by 2031. H1 2026 revenue was $16.77B (+6.4% YoY) with EPS of $3.35 vs $3.00 prior year, and the company reaffirmed full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.55-$6.80 with 5-7% earnings growth through 2030.

Investment Thesis
Duke Energy at 16.9x forward earnings offers a regulated utility uniquely positioned to benefit from the AI-driven power demand surge. The $103B five-year capital plan drives 9.6% earnings base growth through 2030, backed by 7.8 GW of signed data center service agreements and 15 GW of new capacity planned by 2031 [1][2]. CEO Sideris states AI demand is growing at 10x the historic pace, transforming Duke from a 0-0.5% load growth utility into a mid-single-digit growth story. The 3.51% dividend yield with 98 consecutive years of payments and 19 years of increases provides income stability, and management reaffirmed 5-7% EPS growth through 2030 [1].
The bear case centers on execution risk, leverage, and environmental liabilities. D/E of 162% is high even by utility standards, and the $103B capital plan requires sustained regulatory approval for cost recovery — any resistance could compress returns. The 2.0% 5-year dividend growth CAGR is well below inflation, eroding real income value despite 19 years of nominal increases [4]. ROE of 9.9% and profit margin of 16% reflect regulatory return caps limiting upside. The coal ash legacy (110 million tons in unlined pits, 24 sites violating federal regulations, $202M in fines) represents open-ended environmental liability potentially requiring billions in remediation [3]. PEG ratio of 2.85 suggests the current valuation already prices in the AI growth story.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center demand driving power load growth at 10x historic rates, with 7.8 GW of service agreements signed and pipeline toward 15 GW by 2031 — each GW translates to billions in regulated rate base growth [1][2]
  2. $103B five-year capital plan providing 9.6% earnings base growth through 2030, with management signaling the number will grow as data center demand accelerates [2]
  3. Management guidance to earn in the top half of 5-7% EPS growth range beginning 2028, suggesting earnings acceleration in later capital plan years [1]
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 162% and $103B capital plan requiring sustained regulatory cost recovery approval — pushback from state public utility commissions could compress returns and force plan reductions
  2. Coal ash environmental liabilities (110 million tons, 24 sites violating federal regulations) represent open-ended remediation costs potentially reaching billions not in current investment plan [3]
  3. 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 2.0% is below inflation, eroding real income value — if dividend growth cannot accelerate, income-focused investors may reallocate to utilities with stronger growth profiles [4]
