Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYQ ·United States
Duke Energy is one of the largest regulated electric and natural gas utilities in the United States, serving approximately 8.4 million customers across six states through its Electric Utilities and Infrastructure (EU&I) and Gas Utilities and Infrastructure (GU&I) segments. The company generates, transmits, and distributes electricity from a diversified fuel mix including nuclear, natural gas, coal, hydroelectric, and renewables. Full-year 2025 revenue of $32.2B with adjusted EPS of $6.31 reflects the regulated utility model. Duke has secured 7.8 GW of data center service agreements with an additional 15.4 GW pipeline. Founded in 1904 and headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina [1][3].

Investment Thesis
Duke Energy at $121 with a $94.6B market cap is one of America's largest regulated utilities positioned as a direct beneficiary of AI-driven electricity demand growth. The company has secured 7.8 GW of data center service agreements with a 15.4 GW late-stage pipeline, representing potential $5-10B in capital plan additions — these agreements include minimum-take provisions that underpin revenue growth projections [1][2]. Adjusted EPS of $6.31 in 2025 grew 7% YoY, with 2026 guidance of $6.55-$6.80 and a reaffirmed 5-7% long-term EPS growth target. The dividend profile offers predictability — 43 consecutive years paid, 20 consecutive years of growth, 3.58% current yield, and a $103B five-year capital plan that supports continued regulated earnings growth [3][4]. Forward P/E of 16.9 is attractive for a utility with improving growth visibility from data center demand.
The bear case for Duke Energy centers on the tension between its capital-intensive growth plan and its financial constraints. D/E of 162% reflects the massive capital requirements of a regulated utility executing a $103B investment plan, and the company's free cash flow is deeply negative (-$4.5B TTM) as capital spending far exceeds operating cash flow [4]. The 2.0% five-year dividend growth CAGR significantly trails inflation, meaning real income is declining for dividend investors — the 3.58% yield must compensate for minimal growth. ROE of 9.9% is constrained by the regulated return model, and revenue growth of 1.1% in the most recent quarter highlights the slow organic growth reality between rate cases. Regulatory execution risk is significant — the data center agreements and capital plan depend on state utility commissions approving rate increases, and any pushback could compress returns on the massive investment. The CEO transition from Lynn Good to Harry Sideris introduces incremental execution risk during the most capital-intensive period in the company's history [1][3].
