Company Profile
Communication Services ·Advertising Agencies ·NYSE ·United States
DoubleVerify Holdings, Inc. is a leading software platform for digital media measurement, data, and analytics. The company provides independent verification and authentication of digital advertising across channels including programmatic, social media, connected television (CTV), and retail media, helping brands ensure their ads are viewed by real people in brand-safe and suitable environments within the correct geography. Founded in 2008 and headquartered in New York City, DV processes billions of data transactions daily across the digital advertising ecosystem.

Investment Thesis
DoubleVerify is a rare AI beneficiary trading at value-stock multiples — Forward P/E of 9.5x and PEG of 0.68 for a company with 10% revenue growth, minimal debt (D/E 8.8%), and a product suite that becomes more essential as AI proliferates through digital advertising [1][5]. Q1 2026 revenue of $180.8M and full-year guidance of $810M-$826M demonstrate steady growth, while the AI product portfolio (SlopStopper applied to 40% of impressions, DV Neura cognitive engine, Agent ID Measurement) positions the company at the center of three secular tailwinds: AI-generated content detection, agentic ad buying verification, and LLM ad placement measurement projected at $25B by 2029 [1][3]. The company's independence as a third-party verification standard gives it structural advantages that platform-owned measurement cannot replicate.
The primary concern is profitability: 7.16% net margin and 5.18% ROE are weak for a software company with minimal capital requirements, suggesting that AI product investments are compressing margins without yet generating returns at scale [1][2]. Revenue growth has decelerated from 14% in 2025 to 10% in Q1 2026, and the $810M-$826M full-year guidance implies 8-10% growth — solid but not the acceleration investors typically demand from growth-stage software companies [1]. The securities investigation by Bronstein, Gewirtz & Grossman, while targeting pre-2023 historical activity, creates an overhang on investor sentiment [5]. Additionally, the risk of major advertising platforms (Google, Meta) internalizing verification functions could structurally reduce demand for third-party measurement, though historical precedent suggests advertisers prefer independent verification.
