Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
DaVita is one of the two dominant U.S. dialysis providers, operating approximately 2,700 outpatient dialysis centers serving over 200,000 patients domestically and internationally. The company also provides integrated kidney care, home dialysis modalities, and value-based care arrangements. Under CEO Javier Rodriguez, DaVita reported FY2025 revenue of $13.64B (+6.5% YoY), operating income of $2,044M, and adjusted EPS of $10.78. Q2 2026 revenue accelerated to $3.554B with EPS of $4.02. Market cap is approximately $11.4B, and the company targets 3-7% adjusted OI growth and 8-14% adjusted EPS growth annually.

DaVita has one of the most deliberately articulated organizational cultures in corporate America — the 'village' identity, 'community first, company second' purpose, and Trilogy of Care framework have been maintained for over 25 years since the 2000 rebrand. Through Sinek's lens, this cultural consistency is genuine and differentiating. However, DaVita operates in a regulatory-protected duopoly where the infinite game is partly played for them — the barriers to entry and CMS reimbursement structure reduce the need for the kind of courageous, cause-driven decisions that define true infinite players. The culture is real, but the competitive context doesn't demand the same level of existential flexibility or worthy rival engagement.

Investment Thesis
DaVita at 10.41x forward earnings represents a compelling value opportunity in defensive healthcare. The company operates in a regulatory-protected duopoly controlling ~70% of U.S. dialysis with approximately 2,700 centers serving 200,000+ patients [1]. FY2025 revenue of $13.64B (+6.5%) and Q2 2026 EPS of $4.02 demonstrate steady, predictable growth, with management targeting 8-14% annual adjusted EPS growth through a combination of modest revenue growth, operating leverage, and aggressive share buybacks [1]. ROE of 88.5% reflects capital efficiency, and the forward P/E of 10.41 prices in minimal growth premium for a business with exceptional barriers to entry, mission-critical services (patients die without dialysis), and a growing patient population driven by rising diabetes and hypertension prevalence. AI-driven predictive algorithms for early kidney disease identification could expand the patient funnel [2]. The company's 25+ year 'community first' culture provides organizational stability.
The bear case centers on reimbursement risk, leverage, and growth limitations. D/E of 1242% reflects extreme financial leverage driven by share repurchases — while the absolute debt is manageable for a company generating $2B+ in operating income, any CMS reimbursement cut or adverse regulatory change could stress the balance sheet [1][3]. Revenue growth of 5.2% and a 5yr CAGR of only 3.3% reflect the structural limitations of a dialysis business — the patient population grows slowly, reimbursement rates are negotiated, and home dialysis adoption could shift volume away from higher-reimbursement center-based treatment. Profit margin of 6.1% is thin for a company with this leverage, leaving limited buffer for labor cost inflation or reimbursement pressure. The Inspire MISALIGNED score (-49) limits allocation for faith-based investors despite otherwise strong ESG practices and patient care focus [3]. Earnings growth of 55.8% YoY is largely buyback-driven (reducing share count rather than growing operating profits proportionally), making EPS growth unsustainable if the company exhausts buyback capacity.
Key Catalysts
  1. CMS reimbursement rate increases for dialysis services in 2027 could accelerate revenue growth above the current 5.2% rate, particularly if the Biden administration's kidney care priorities are continued [1]
  2. AI-driven predictive algorithms identifying undiagnosed chronic kidney disease patients earlier could expand DaVita's patient funnel and increase treatment volume across the existing center network [2]
  3. Continued aggressive share buybacks at current 10.41x forward P/E compress the share count and drive 8-14% annual adjusted EPS growth, rewarding patient shareholders with steady capital appreciation [1]
Key Risks
  1. CMS reimbursement cuts or adverse regulatory changes to the ESRD (End-Stage Renal Disease) program could directly compress revenue and margins for the entire dialysis industry, with DaVita's D/E of 1242% amplifying the financial impact [1][3]
  2. Home dialysis adoption acceleration could shift patient volume from higher-reimbursement center-based treatments to lower-cost home modalities, reducing revenue per treatment even as total patient volume grows [1]
  3. Labor cost inflation for nurses and dialysis technicians in a tight healthcare labor market pressures the already-thin 6.1% profit margin, with limited ability to pass costs through to CMS-regulated reimbursement rates [2]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): DaVita's core business — operating physical dialysis centers where licensed nurses and technicians perform life-sustaining medical procedures on patients — is structurally immune to AI displacement [1]. Dialysis treatment requires physical presence, medical equipment, sterile environments, and hands-on clinical care that AI cannot substitute. The business is further protected by CMS reimbursement regulation, state licensure requirements, certificate-of-need laws, and the massive capital and operational barriers to opening and staffing dialysis centers. AI may actually expand DaVita's addressable market by enabling earlier identification of chronic kidney disease patients. No analysts have flagged AI as a substitution risk for dialysis services. AI Opportunity (1/1): DaVita generates value from AI-enhanced healthcare services through predictive algorithms that identify undiagnosed kidney disease patients, optimize treatment protocols, and improve patient outcomes [2]. While AI revenue is not separately reported, these capabilities directly support patient acquisition and care quality — the core revenue drivers of the business.
