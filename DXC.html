Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
DXC Technology Company is a global IT services provider specializing in consulting, engineering, cloud infrastructure, and insurance software solutions. Formed in 2017 from the merger of Computer Sciences Corporation and Hewlett Packard Enterprise Services, DXC operates across three segments: Consulting and Engineering Services, Global Infrastructure Services, and Insurance Software and Services. The company serves enterprise clients in over 70 countries with approximately 115,000 employees. Headquartered in Ashburn, Virginia, with a market cap of approximately $1.57B.

Investment Thesis
DXC Technology trades at a deeply discounted forward P/E of 3.79x and forward PEG of 0.31x, reflecting the market's skepticism about a turnaround under CEO Raul Fernandez [1][4]. Non-GAAP adjusted EPS of approximately $3.48 for FY2026 and robust free cash flow of $1.04B significantly exceed the $18M GAAP net income, suggesting the underlying business is more profitable than headline numbers indicate [1]. Q3 FY2026 showed sequential improvement with bookings of $3.6B (1.12x book-to-bill), adjusted EBIT margin expanding to 8.2%, and non-GAAP EPS growing 4.3% YoY, signaling stabilization [1]. The AI Center of Competence and Anthropic partnership position DXC to participate in enterprise AI adoption.
The bear case is compelling: DXC has failed to achieve organic revenue growth for over five years, with consistent 4% organic decline reflecting structural share loss to more innovative competitors [1]. The Altman Z-Score of 1.06 places the company in the financial distress zone, and the 132% debt-to-equity ratio limits strategic flexibility [4]. Four CEO transitions since 2017 have produced no lasting strategic direction, and the IT services labor-arbitrage model faces existential threat from AI automation that could commoditize large portions of DXC's service delivery. GAAP profit margin of 0.22% leaves zero buffer against execution missteps, and the $1.57B market cap on $12.64B revenue implies the market sees terminal value risk in the current business model.
Key Catalysts
  1. CEO Raul Fernandez achieving organic revenue stabilization (flattening the 4% annual decline) through AI services and cloud migration wins, which would trigger significant multiple expansion from the 3.79x forward P/E
  2. AI Center of Competence and DXC OASIS platform gaining traction with enterprise clients, creating a new growth engine to offset legacy IT infrastructure decline
  3. Continued margin expansion from Q3 FY2026 levels (8.2% adjusted EBIT margin) driven by operational efficiency, AI-enabled automation, and higher-value service mix shift
Key Risks
  1. Persistent organic revenue decline of 4% annually over the past five years with no clear inflection point, suggesting structural competitive displacement by Accenture, Infosys, and cloud-native providers
  2. AI disruption of the IT services labor-arbitrage model could accelerate revenue decline as enterprise clients adopt AI coding assistants, autonomous infrastructure management, and GenAI tools that reduce demand for outsourced IT labor
  3. Altman Z-Score of 1.06 indicates financial distress risk, and the 132% debt-to-equity ratio constrains strategic investment capacity needed for the AI pivot while GAAP profit margin of 0.22% provides no earnings buffer

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
5
AI Risk Protection (4/9): DXC's core IT services business faces direct AI disruption risk across multiple revenue lines. Global Infrastructure Services, which includes help desk, data center management, and routine IT operations, is highly vulnerable to AI automation that can handle Tier 1-2 support tickets, monitor infrastructure, and auto-remediate issues [3]. The labor-arbitrage model underpinning offshore IT services is being disrupted as AI coding assistants and autonomous agents reduce the need for large human workforces. However, complex enterprise consulting, regulated industry solutions (insurance software), and legacy system migration provide some protection as they require deep domain expertise and human judgment. AI Opportunity (1/1): DXC is actively monetizing AI through its AI Center of Competence, Anthropic partnership, DXC OASIS platform, and AI-enhanced security operations, generating measurable revenue from enterprise AI adoption services even if total AI revenue is not separately disclosed [3].
