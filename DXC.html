Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
DXC Technology is a global IT services provider formed from the 2017 merger of Computer Sciences Corporation and Hewlett Packard Enterprise Services. The company operates across three segments: Consulting and Engineering Services ($1.26B, +1.7% YoY), Global Infrastructure Services ($1.55B, -5.0% YoY), and Insurance Software and Services ($325M, +7.3% YoY). FY2026 total revenue was $12.64B (down 1.8% reported, down 4.8% organic), with non-GAAP diluted EPS of $3.23 and adjusted EBIT margin of 7.7%. CEO Raul Fernandez has led since February 2024. Market cap approximately $1.8B.

Investment Thesis
DXC Technology at 4.58x forward earnings and $11.33 stock price offers deep value optionality on a legacy IT services turnaround under CEO Raul Fernandez [5]. Operational profitability improved meaningfully in FY2026: non-GAAP EPS of $3.23 on $12.64B revenue, adjusted EBIT margin of 7.7%, and ROE recovering to 12.93% from near-zero levels suggest the business is stabilizing beneath the negative top-line trajectory [1]. Insurance Software & Services grew 7.3% organically, providing a higher-margin anchor. DXC OASIS and the AI Center of Competence position the company to participate in enterprise AI adoption. Free cash flow generation supports the $1.8B market cap, and the stock trades at a fraction of revenue.
The bear case is structural and severe. Revenue has declined for five consecutive years (-7.0% five-year CAGR), with FY2026 organic revenue down 4.8% reflecting persistent share loss to more innovative competitors [1]. GAAP diluted EPS of $0.10 (down 95.2%) reveals the extent of restructuring charges masking the non-GAAP picture. PitchGrade Research identifies DXC as facing 'structural disruption' from AI, noting that AI-powered migration tools directly attack DXC's lock-in moat with 'no meaningful data moat, network effects, or proprietary AI platform' to defend against disruption [3]. Glassdoor ratings of 3.0/5 (22% below industry average) signal a cultural deficit that impedes talent attraction precisely when AI talent is most critical [2]. D/E of 127% constrains strategic investment capacity. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors. With no competitive moat, declining revenue, and existential AI disruption risk, the turnaround thesis requires both flawless execution and a favorable macro environment.
