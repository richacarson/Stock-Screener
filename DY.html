Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Contracting / Engineering & Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Dycom Industries is a leading provider of specialty contracting services to the telecommunications infrastructure and utility industries across the United States. Services include program management, planning, engineering and design, aerial/underground/wireless construction, maintenance, and fulfillment for telecom providers. The company expanded into data center construction through the Power Solutions acquisition, accelerating growth. FY2026 revenue reached $5.55B (+18% YoY) with FY2027 guidance of $6.85-7.15B. New CEO Daniel Peyovich succeeded 25-year veteran Steven Nielsen in November 2024.

Investment Thesis
Dycom at 17.61x forward earnings and 0.82 PEG offers compelling growth at a reasonable price — FY2026 revenue of $5.55B (+18% YoY) with FY2027 guidance of $6.85-7.15B (+24-29%) demonstrates accelerating demand driven by fiber buildout, 5G densification, and data center construction [1][2]. The Power Solutions acquisition positioned Dycom at the intersection of two secular megatrends: telecom infrastructure modernization and AI-driven data center expansion. ROE of 19.20%, EPS growth of 20.7%, and a Forward PEG of 0.82 indicate the stock is attractively valued relative to its growth trajectory. With ~56% revenue growth in Q1 FY2027 and 85% adjusted EPS growth, the acquisition integration is delivering ahead of expectations [2]. AI infrastructure demand provides a multi-year revenue tailwind as hyperscalers commit hundreds of billions to data center construction.
The bear case centers on margin compression risk, leverage, and customer concentration. Profit margin of 4.98% reflects the inherently low-margin nature of specialty contracting, and any cost inflation in labor or materials could squeeze already thin margins [4]. D/E of 136.62% is elevated from the Power Solutions acquisition, creating financial risk if the infrastructure spending cycle decelerates or interest rates remain elevated. Dycom derives a significant portion of revenue from a handful of telecom customers (AT&T, Lumen, Charter), creating concentration risk — a capital spending pullback by any major customer would disproportionately impact results. The FY2027 growth rate of 24-29% includes significant inorganic contribution; organic growth guidance of only 6.6-10.3% is more modest [1]. New CEO Daniel Peyovich has been in the role less than two years and has not yet navigated an economic downturn. Glassdoor ratings of 3.1/5 for culture suggest potential workforce quality and retention challenges as the company scales to meet surging demand.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center construction boom providing a multi-year revenue tailwind as hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) commit $200B+ annually to data center buildout, with Dycom's Power Solutions acquisition positioning it to capture this demand [2]
  2. Fiber-to-the-home deployments accelerating nationwide as telecom providers (AT&T, Lumen) invest in last-mile connectivity, driving sustained demand for Dycom's core aerial and underground construction services [1]
  3. FY2027 guidance of $6.85-7.15B with Q1 already showing 56.1% revenue growth suggests the Power Solutions integration is running ahead of plan, with potential for guidance raises through the year [2]
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration risk as top telecom clients (AT&T, Lumen, Charter) represent a disproportionate share of revenue — a capital spending pullback or contract loss by any major customer would materially impact results [5]
  2. Profit margin compression from labor cost inflation, materials pricing, or competitive bidding pressure in a business that already operates at sub-5% net margins, with limited pricing power in a competitive contracting market [4]
  3. Integration and leverage risk from the Power Solutions acquisition, with D/E at 136.62% — a deceleration in data center spending or infrastructure investment could leave Dycom overleveraged during a downturn [4]
