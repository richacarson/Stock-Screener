Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Integrated ·NYSE ·Colombia
Ecopetrol S.A. is the largest integrated energy company in Colombia and one of the principal integrated energy companies in the Americas. Majority state-owned (~88.5% by the Colombian government), the company operates across exploration, production, refining, transportation, and marketing of oil, gas, and petrochemical products. Under CEO Ricardo Roa Barragan (since April 2023), Ecopetrol delivered record Q2 2026 results with revenue of COP 40.2 trillion (+35% YoY), EBITDA of COP 17.7 trillion (44% margin), and net income of COP 6.1 trillion (+235% YoY) [1]. Production guidance for 2026 is 730-740 kboe/d, with the Brava Energia acquisition expected to consolidate in Q3 2026, adding 42,000 boe/d [2]. The company trades on the NYSE as an ADR.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Ecopetrol plays a primarily finite game. As a state-owned enterprise, its strategic direction is heavily influenced by Colombian government priorities that shift with political cycles — a fundamentally finite constraint. The company has a national development mandate that provides purpose beyond quarterly earnings, and early energy transition investments show forward thinking. However, the ~88.5% government ownership creates structural governance challenges: CEO selection is political, the 112% payout ratio prioritizes government revenue extraction over long-term reinvestment, and strategic flexibility is constrained by sovereign fiscal needs [4].

Investment Thesis
Ecopetrol at 9.3x forward earnings offers deep value exposure to a recovering Colombian oil major riding elevated Brent prices ($97/bbl in Q2 2026). Q2 2026 results were exceptional — revenue surging 35% to COP 40.2T, EBITDA margin expanding to 44%, and net income exploding 235% to COP 6.1T [1]. H1 2026 delivered total EBITDA of COP 31.1T and net income of COP 9.0T, already approaching some full-year estimates. The Brava Energia acquisition (42K boe/d consolidating Q3 2026) adds production growth beyond the current 730-740 kboe/d guidance [2]. The ~4% dividend yield provides income while the stock trades at a significant discount to global integrated oil peers. For income investors willing to accept emerging market and commodity price risk, EC offers a compelling yield-plus-value combination at less than 10x forward earnings.
The bear case centers on three structural risks: sovereign governance, commodity cyclicality, and dividend sustainability. The Colombian government's ~88.5% ownership means Ecopetrol's strategic direction is hostage to political priorities — energy transition mandates could constrain production growth while government dividend demands may be unsustainable [3]. The 112% payout ratio signals the dividend exceeds current earnings capacity, making it dependent on continued high oil prices; a sustained Brent decline below the $83/bbl planning assumption would compress both earnings and the dividend [2][4]. The 5-year history includes dramatic revenue and earnings swings tied to oil prices and COP/USD exchange rate movements (planning band COP 3,600-4,000). The H1 2026 revenue surge was substantially driven by Brent averaging $97/bbl — a level well above historical norms that may not persist. Infrastructure scored well (8/10) mechanically, but the 112% payout ratio floor (0.0 score) and only 2 consecutive years of dividend growth reveal structural income quality weaknesses beneath the headline valuation discount.
