Company Profile
Financials ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Eagle Point Credit Company is an externally managed, non-diversified closed-end fund that invests primarily in equity and junior debt tranches of collateralized loan obligations (CLOs) consisting of below investment-grade U.S. senior secured loans. Managed by Eagle Point Credit Management LLC, the fund reported Q1 2026 NAV of $4.17/share, down 26.8% from year-end, with total investment income of $42.4M. The stock has declined approximately 50% over the past 52 weeks amid CLO market dislocation. Market cap is approximately $505M.

Investment Thesis
Eagle Point Credit at $3.78 trades at a 9% discount to its March 2026 NAV of $4.17 (which rebounded to $4.49-$4.59 in April), offering potential NAV recovery upside alongside a forward dividend yield of approximately 19% ($0.06/month) [1]. Recurring cash flows of $0.47/share in Q1 2026 exceeded the combined common distributions and total expenses by $0.14/share, suggesting the reduced distribution level is sustainable and potentially positioned for a future increase if CLO market conditions normalize [1]. CLO equity tranches benefit from the structural leveraged return profile of CLO waterfalls, and the current dislocation in credit markets may represent a buying opportunity for contrarian investors willing to accept elevated volatility. The fund's 12-year track record of continuous distributions through multiple credit cycles (including COVID-19) demonstrates institutional durability.
The bear case is severe and dominates the investment picture. NAV collapsed 26.8% in Q1 2026 alone, driving GAAP ROE to -18.07% and total return on equity to -20.2% for the quarter [1]. The stock has lost 50% of its value over the trailing 52 weeks, the dividend was cut by 57% (from $0.14 to $0.06/month), and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR is approximately -16% — this is a fund in secular distribution decline, not a temporary dip [3]. The GAAP profit margin of -83.95% reflects massive unrealized losses on CLO equity positions that may or may not recover. As a single-strategy CLO equity vehicle, ECC has no ability to pivot away from deteriorating CLO market conditions, and external management fees create a structural drag that compounds in adverse environments. The Innovation score of 1/10, Inspiration of 0/10, and Infinite Game of 1/10 reflect that this is a purely financial vehicle with no strategic differentiation, no purpose beyond income, and no flexibility to adapt. For IOWN's framework, which values purpose-driven businesses built to endure, a leveraged CLO equity fund in secular decline scores among the lowest in the universe.
