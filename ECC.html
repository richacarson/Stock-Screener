Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Eagle Point Credit Company is a publicly traded closed-end fund managed by Eagle Point Credit Management LLC ($14 billion AUM) that invests primarily in equity and junior debt tranches of collateralized loan obligations (CLOs) consisting predominantly of below investment grade U.S. senior secured loans. The fund also allocates to regulatory capital relief (9.2%), ABS/specialty finance (5.2%), infrastructure credit (8.9%), and strategic credit (1.5%). Founded by Tom Majewski in 2014, ECC's NAV per share declined from $5.70 at year-end 2025 to $4.17 as of March 31, 2026 — a 26.8% decline — before recovering to an estimated $4.49-$4.59 by April month-end. Monthly common distributions were reduced to $0.06 per share for Q3 2026.

Investment Thesis
Eagle Point Credit offers a 19.6% current yield ($0.06/month) backed by 11 consecutive years of distribution payments since the fund's 2014 inception, and the stock trades at a 12% discount to the most recent estimated NAV of $4.49-$4.59 per share [1]. D/E of 41% is conservative at the fund level, and Eagle Point Credit Management's $14 billion AUM platform and Tom Majewski's 31 years of structured credit experience provide deep CLO market expertise [2]. The portfolio diversification into regulatory capital relief (9.2%), infrastructure credit (8.9%), and ABS/specialty finance (5.2%) reduces concentration risk relative to historical pure CLO equity exposure. In a supportive credit environment where CLO issuance reached $47 billion in Q1 2026 alone, ECC's equity tranches benefit from elevated loan spreads and strong recovery assumptions.
The bear case is severe and well-documented. ECC's NAV collapsed 26.8% in Q1 2026 from $5.70 to $4.17 per share, generating a -20.2% GAAP return on equity [1]. Monthly distributions have been slashed from $0.20/share (2019-2021) to $0.06/share (2026) — a 70% reduction that signals the fund's income-generating capacity has been structurally impaired. The stock has fallen from double-digit prices to $3.67, and the 19.6% yield is a function of price collapse rather than income strength. CLO equity is the riskiest tranche in the CLO capital structure — first loss, last recovery — and in a credit downturn, further NAV erosion and distribution cuts are virtually certain. ROE of -18.07% and profit margin of -83.95% reflect an investment vehicle that is destroying capital, not generating it [4]. The Inspire score of 0 (MIXED) with no attributions in either direction means there is no faith-based argument for or against ECC. The SELL recommendation reflects a fundamentally impaired income vehicle with negative returns, declining distributions, and structural risks inherent in CLO equity investing.
