Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Consolidated Edison is one of the largest investor-owned energy companies in the United States, providing electric service to approximately 3.5 million customers and gas service to approximately 1.1 million customers in New York City and Westchester County. Through its subsidiary Orange and Rockland Utilities, it also serves customers in southeastern New York and adjacent areas of New Jersey and Pennsylvania. The company sold its Clean Energy Businesses to RWE AG for approximately $6.8 billion in 2023, refocusing exclusively on regulated utility operations.

Investment Thesis
Consolidated Edison offers one of the most reliable income streams in the utility sector, with 52 consecutive years of dividend increases (Dividend King status) and 141 years of continuous dividend payments dating to 1885 [4]. The company reported 2025 adjusted EPS of $5.70 on $16.9B revenue (+10.9% YoY), and reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $6.00-$6.20 adjusted EPS with a five-year CAGR target of 6-7% [1][2]. Serving the most densely populated and economically critical metro area in the United States provides structural demand stability — ConEd's 3.5 million electric customers and 1.1 million gas customers in NYC and Westchester represent irreplaceable franchise value. The regulated utility model delivers predictable earnings growth supported by constructive rate case relationships, and the stock trades at a reasonable 18.6x forward earnings for a premium service territory with AI-immune revenue streams [4].
The bear case centers on ConEd's modest growth profile relative to peers. The 6-7% EPS CAGR lags utilities with strong data center tailwinds like AEP (9%) and NextEra (10%+), and the $6.8B sale of Clean Energy Businesses to RWE in 2023 removed the company's renewables optionality, leaving a pure regulated utility with limited growth levers beyond rate base expansion [1][2]. D/E of 117% and ROE of 8.77% are manageable but unremarkable, and the 2.5% five-year dividend growth CAGR barely exceeds inflation, making real income growth negligible for dividend investors [4]. New York's regulatory environment faces political pressure on rate increases given cost-of-living sensitivities, and a net profit margin of just 12% leaves limited buffer against cost overruns. The 2013 East Harlem gas explosion ($153M+ settlement, 8 deaths) and subsequent $2.3B in infrastructure remediation highlighted operational safety risks inherent to aging urban utility infrastructure that could recur [3]. Forward P/E of 18.6x prices in steady-state performance with minimal upside optionality.
Key Catalysts
  1. Rate base growth from ongoing grid modernization and infrastructure resilience investments across the NYC metro area, with ConEd's five-year capital plan supporting 6-7% adjusted EPS CAGR through the end of the decade [1][2]
  2. Indirect AI data center demand tailwind as NYC-area electricity consumption increases, with ConEd positioned as the sole regulated provider for the densely populated service territory [2]
  3. Dividend King status with 52 consecutive increases provides a floor valuation during market downturns, and potential acceleration of dividend growth rate from 2.5% CAGR as EPS growth of 6-7% creates room for higher payout growth [4]
Key Risks
  1. New York regulatory risk — political pressure to limit rate increases in one of the highest cost-of-living cities in the US could compress returns on rate base investment and slow EPS growth below the 6-7% target [1][2]
  2. Aging urban infrastructure safety risk — the 2013 East Harlem gas explosion resulted in 8 deaths and $153M+ in costs, and ConEd's century-old gas and electric infrastructure in NYC requires ongoing investment to prevent similar incidents [3]
  3. Below-peer growth profile with 6-7% EPS CAGR and 2.5% dividend growth CAGR versus peers offering 8-10% EPS growth with data center tailwinds, creating risk of relative multiple compression as investors rotate to higher-growth utilities [1][4]
