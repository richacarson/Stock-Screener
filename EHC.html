Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Encompass Health is the largest owner and operator of inpatient rehabilitation hospitals in the United States, providing post-acute care through approximately 160+ hospitals across 37 states. The company focuses on helping patients recover from strokes, brain injuries, spinal cord injuries, and other complex medical conditions. Encompass spun off its home health and hospice business (Enhabit) in 2022 to sharpen its focus on inpatient rehabilitation. Q2 2026 revenue grew 9.6% YoY driven by 5.6% discharge growth and 3.9% higher net revenue per discharge. FY2026 guidance raised to $6.41-6.49B revenue and $6.02-6.25 adjusted EPS, with 7 new hospitals planned.
42,000 Employees ·www.encompasshealth.com

Investment Thesis
Encompass Health at 18.41x forward earnings is a high-quality healthcare compounder with dominant market position in a structurally growing niche — the aging US population and rising stroke/brain injury incidence drive secular demand for inpatient rehabilitation that is immune to AI disruption and resistant to economic cycles [1]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 9.6% YoY with raised FY2026 guidance of $6.41-6.49B revenue and $6.02-6.25 adjusted EPS, reflecting consistent execution. ROE of 24.97% demonstrates strong capital returns, and 7 new hospital openings in 2026 provide visible organic growth. The Enhabit spin-off sharpened strategic focus, and growing emphasis on medically complex categories (stroke, brain injury) drives higher revenue per discharge (+3.9% in Q2) and improved acuity mix [2]. Inspire Impact Score of 59 (ALIGNED) makes this suitable for faith-based allocations.
The bear case centers on valuation, healthcare regulatory risk, and labor constraints. Forward P/E of 18.41x prices in sustained high-single-digit growth for a healthcare facility operator that faces inherent margin constraints from Medicare reimbursement rates — CMS payment rule changes could compress profitability if annual rate increases fall below labor cost inflation [4]. D/E of 82.12% is manageable but limits financial flexibility for accelerating new hospital openings. Revenue growth of 9.6% is strong for the sector but relies on discharge volume growth (5.6%) that is constrained by physical hospital capacity and licensed therapist availability — the healthcare labor shortage (nurses, physical therapists, occupational therapists) is a structural headwind that limits expansion speed and pressures wages. Innovation score of 4 reflects a company that dominates its niche through operational excellence and scale rather than technology differentiation, leaving it potentially vulnerable to more tech-enabled competitors or alternative care delivery models over the long term [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Aging US population (65+ cohort growing 3%+ annually) driving secular increase in stroke, brain injury, and joint replacement rehabilitation volumes — structural demand tailwind independent of economic cycles [1][2]
  2. 7 new hospital openings in 2026 with a multi-year pipeline for geographic expansion into underserved markets, adding ~4% annual capacity growth on top of same-hospital discharge improvements
  3. Revenue per discharge growth of 3.9% in Q2 2026 driven by higher patient acuity and mix shift toward medically complex cases (stroke, brain injury) — improving unit economics without relying solely on volume growth [2]
Key Risks
  1. Medicare reimbursement risk — CMS payment rule changes could compress margins if annual rate increases fall below healthcare labor cost inflation, directly impacting the ~70% of revenue derived from Medicare/Medicare Advantage [4]
  2. Healthcare labor shortage (physical therapists, occupational therapists, nurses) constraining expansion speed, pressuring wages, and limiting same-hospital discharge growth capacity — a structural headwind that cannot be quickly resolved
  3. Forward P/E of 18.41x requires sustained high-single-digit revenue growth — any deceleration from discharge volume slowdowns, capacity constraints, or regulatory headwinds could trigger multiple compression from the current premium healthcare valuation [4]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Encompass Health's core business — operating inpatient rehabilitation hospitals where licensed physical, occupational, and speech therapists help patients recover from strokes, brain injuries, and complex medical conditions — is structurally immune to AI disruption. AI cannot replace the hands-on, physical work of helping a stroke patient relearn to walk, talk, and perform daily activities. The business requires physical hospital facilities, licensed clinical staff, and deeply regulated healthcare processes (Medicare/CMS oversight, state licensing, accreditation) that create structural barriers AI cannot circumvent [1][2]. AI may enhance diagnostics, treatment planning, and operational efficiency, but increases productivity rather than threatening the business model.
