Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Edison International is the parent company of Southern California Edison (SCE), one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, serving approximately 5 million customer accounts across a 50,000-square-mile service territory in central, coastal, and southern California. Under CEO Pedro Pizarro, the company is navigating significant wildfire litigation while executing a grid modernization and clean energy transition strategy. Q2 2026 core EPS jumped 59% to $1.54, bringing YTD core EPS to $2.97, and management reaffirmed FY2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90-$6.20 with a long-term 5-7% growth target through 2030. The Eaton Fire Wildfire Recovery Compensation Program has extended $775 million in offers to over 2,200 community members.

Investment Thesis
Edison International at ~12x forward core earnings offers a deeply discounted regulated utility with significant upside if wildfire liabilities resolve favorably. Q2 2026 core EPS surged 59% to $1.54, and FY2026 guidance of $5.90-$6.20 puts the stock at ~12x forward — a substantial discount to utility peers trading at 18-20x [1]. ROE of 24.10% and profit margin of 23.08% are among the strongest in the utility sector [4]. The 5-7% long-term EPS growth target through 2030, supported by 7% rate base growth and the $2.5B RAMP grid hardening investment, provides a clear earnings trajectory [1]. The ALIGNED Inspire Impact Score (37) with no negative attributions and 14 positive ESG categories makes EIX accessible to faith-based mandates. The Eaton Fire judge has balked at holding Edison liable, and a dismissed shareholder lawsuit suggests the legal risk may be more manageable than the stock price implies [2].
The bear case is dominated by wildfire liability risk that overshadows otherwise strong fundamentals. LA County sued Edison alleging the company 'deliberately prioritized profits over safety' in the Eaton Fire, and over 12,300 individuals have presented claims through the voluntary compensation program with $775M in offers already extended — total liabilities could be multiples of this amount [2]. D/E of 216% is the highest among major U.S. utilities, reflecting $41.55B in total debt against $158M in cash, leaving minimal financial cushion for adverse wildfire outcomes [4]. The trailing P/E of 7.37x vs. forward P/E of 12.3x signals the market expects earnings to decline from the trailing period (which likely included one-time gains), normalizing to a more modest growth profile. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 7.36% is solid but reflects California rate increases rather than organic demand growth, and regulatory resistance to rate hikes could constrain future growth. Southern California's wildfire risk creates a unique business model vulnerability — no other major U.S. utility faces the same existential equipment liability risk from climate-driven events. The inverse condemnation doctrine in California means Edison can be held liable even without negligence, creating tail risk that is difficult to quantify.
Key Catalysts
  1. Favorable wildfire litigation outcomes — a judge has already balked at holding Edison liable for the Eaton Fire, and a shareholder lawsuit was dismissed, suggesting the legal environment may be more favorable than the stock discount implies [2]
  2. California wildfire reform legislation, if enacted, could restructure the inverse condemnation framework and reduce Edison's future equipment liability exposure, removing the primary valuation overhang [1]
  3. Rate base growth of 7% through the $2.5B RAMP investment and grid hardening program, providing steady regulated earnings growth that compounds through the 5-7% EPS growth target to 2030 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Eaton Fire wildfire liabilities remain unresolved — LA County alleges Edison 'deliberately prioritized profits over safety,' and 12,300+ individuals have presented claims with total exposure potentially in the billions, which could overwhelm the California Wildfire Fund and require direct Edison liability [2]
  2. D/E of 216% ($41.55B debt vs $158M cash) leaves minimal financial cushion for adverse wildfire outcomes, and additional capital raises or securitizations could dilute shareholders or increase interest costs [4]
  3. California's inverse condemnation doctrine holds utilities liable for wildfire damages regardless of negligence, creating a unique and structurally uninsurable business model risk that no operational improvements can fully eliminate [2]
