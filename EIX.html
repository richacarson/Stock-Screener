Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Edison International is the parent company of Southern California Edison (SCE), one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, serving approximately 5 million customer accounts across a 50,000-square-mile service territory in central, coastal, and southern California. Founded in 1886, the company operates under CEO Pedro Pizarro while navigating significant wildfire litigation. Q2 2026 core EPS was $1.54 (up 59% YoY), with year-to-date core EPS of $2.97, and management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $5.90-$6.20 with a long-term 5-7% EPS growth target [1]. Wildfire-related charges of $1.3B were recorded in Q1 2026, partially offset by $917M in self-insurance recoveries [2].

Investment Thesis
Edison International at $56.53 and 8.7x forward core earnings offers a deeply discounted regulated utility with significant upside if wildfire liabilities resolve favorably. Q2 2026 core EPS surged 59% to $1.54, bringing YTD core EPS to $2.97, and FY2026 guidance of $5.90-$6.20 implies a forward P/E well below utility peers trading at 18-20x [1]. ROE of 19.7% and profit margin of 19.3% are strong for the utility sector. The 5-7% long-term EPS growth target through 2030, supported by rate base growth and the RAMP grid hardening investment, provides a visible earnings trajectory [1]. The dividend yield of 6.21% with a conservative 35.7% payout ratio and 20 consecutive years of growth offers substantial income [1]. The ALIGNED Inspire Impact Score (37) with 14 positive ESG attributions and no negatives makes EIX accessible to faith-based mandates.
The bear case is dominated by wildfire liability risk that overshadows otherwise strong fundamentals. Edison recorded $1.3B in wildfire losses in Q1 2026 alone, management estimates 30,000 claims are currently in litigation, and the company is at the lowest possible investment-grade credit ratings [2][4]. D/E of 228% reflects $43.5B in total debt against only $242M in cash, leaving minimal financial cushion. CEO Pizarro warned that failed wildfire reform through SB 492 could leave utilities facing hundreds of millions in additional costs, and the legislation's final form was narrower than expected [4]. The stock has declined from $74.66 to $56.53 (down 24%) since August 2026, reflecting deteriorating investor confidence. California's inverse condemnation doctrine holds utilities liable regardless of negligence, creating a structural and essentially uninsurable business model risk. The trailing P/E of 5.83x includes one-time wildfire recovery gains that inflate earnings, and quarterly revenue declined 4.1% in the most recent period. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~7% reflects rate increases rather than organic demand growth [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Favorable wildfire litigation outcomes or California legislative reform could restructure the inverse condemnation framework and reduce Edison's future equipment liability exposure, removing the primary valuation overhang that depresses the stock 50%+ below utility peer multiples [2][4]
  2. Rate base growth of 7% through grid hardening and clean energy investments, supporting the 5-7% EPS growth target through 2030 with regulatory cost recovery mechanisms [1]
  3. AI data center expansion in Southern California driving incremental electricity demand, increasing SCE's utilization and rate base growth opportunity while the AWARE AI platform improves grid reliability [3]
Key Risks
  1. Wildfire liabilities with 30,000 estimated claims in litigation and $1.3B in Q1 2026 losses alone could overwhelm the California Wildfire Fund and self-insurance, with total exposure potentially in the billions given the inverse condemnation doctrine [2][4]
  2. D/E of 228% ($43.5B debt, $242M cash) at the lowest investment-grade credit rating — a downgrade to junk would trigger covenant violations and severely constrain capital access during the most capital-intensive period of grid modernization [1][4]
  3. SB 492 wildfire reform legislation passed in narrower form than expected, failing to substantially reduce utilities' liabilities — CEO Pizarro warned this could mean hundreds of millions in additional cost exposure that the stock has not yet priced in [4]
