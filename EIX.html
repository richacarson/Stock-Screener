Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Edison International is the parent company of Southern California Edison (SCE), one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, serving approximately 5 million customer accounts across a 50,000-square-mile service territory in central, coastal, and southern California. Under CEO Pedro Pizarro, the company is navigating significant wildfire litigation while executing a grid modernization and clean energy transition strategy. The stock has declined to $56.53 as wildfire liability overhang intensifies, while FY2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90-$6.20 implies a forward P/E of just 8.69x. The Eaton Fire Wildfire Recovery Compensation Program has extended $775 million in offers to over 2,200 community members [1].

Investment Thesis
Edison International at $56.53 with a $21.8B market cap trades at a deeply discounted 8.69x forward core earnings — among the lowest valuations in the US utility sector — reflecting ongoing wildfire liability overhang from the January 2025 LA fires. The fundamental business remains strong: TTM revenue of $19.4B with 19.3% profit margin, ROE of 19.7%, and a growing rate base driven by SCE's $6B+ annual capital program in grid hardening, clean energy, and electrification infrastructure [1][4]. The dividend yield of 6.2% with a conservative 35.7% payout ratio provides substantial income support — Edison has increased dividends for 22 consecutive years, with the current quarterly rate at $0.878/share ($3.51 annualized). The secular tailwind from AI-driven electricity demand, California's aggressive electrification mandates, and massive grid modernization spending create a multi-decade growth runway for the regulated rate base [3].
The bear case centers squarely on wildfire liability risk. The stock has declined 24% from $74.66 in August to $56.53 as the Eaton Fire litigation intensifies — LA County sued Edison alleging the company 'deliberately prioritized profits over safety,' and over 12,300 individuals have presented claims through the voluntary compensation program with $775M in offers already extended, with total liabilities potentially multiples of this amount [2]. The $43.5B debt load (228% D/E ratio, worsening from 216% in August) reflects both the capital-intensive business model and wildfire-related financing needs, creating meaningful balance sheet risk if liability estimates worsen. Revenue declined 4.1% YoY due to rate case timing, profit margin compressed from 23% to 19.3%, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of only 5.5% highlights limited organic growth for a utility burdened by liability uncertainty [4]. The forward PEG of 2.49 reflects the market's skepticism about long-term growth prospects. California's inverse condemnation doctrine means Edison can be held liable for wildfire damages even without negligence, creating tail risk that is difficult to quantify or insure against [2].
