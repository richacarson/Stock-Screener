Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Emerson Electric Co. is a global automation technology leader providing intelligent devices, software, and control systems across process, hybrid, and discrete industries. Following the transformative $11B+ AspenTech acquisition and divestiture of Climate Technologies (Copeland), Emerson has repositioned as a pure-play automation company serving power, LNG, life sciences, semiconductor, and AI data center industries. Under CEO Lal Karsanbhai (since February 2021), the company reported Q3 FY2026 EPS of $1.71 (beating consensus by $0.03), raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to ~$6.55, and ended June 2026 with a $9.6B backlog [1]. The company is a Dividend King with 69 consecutive years of dividend increases. Market cap approximately $85B.

Investment Thesis
Emerson has executed one of the most ambitious industrial portfolio transformations in recent history, divesting legacy businesses and acquiring AspenTech for $11B+ to become a pure-play automation technology leader with a self-reinforcing AI data flywheel [1][2]. Q3 FY2026 delivered EPS of $1.71 (beating consensus by $0.03) with underlying orders up 7% for the fourth consecutive quarter. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to ~$6.55 with ~$3.6B in free cash flow, and the backlog grew to $9.6B — up 17% sequentially — with 75% expected to convert within 12 months [1]. Software & Systems orders surged 23% and Ovation power-related orders jumped 74% driven by AI data center demand. The ASPIRE targets of $21B revenue, 30% EBITA margin, and $8.00 EPS by FY2028 outline a clear path to margin expansion. With 69 consecutive years of dividend growth, Emerson is a Dividend King operating at the intersection of industrial reliability and AI-driven transformation.
At 20.17x forward earnings (on adjusted EPS), Emerson's valuation assumes successful execution of the margin expansion from 13.35% profit margin toward the 30% EBITA target — a significant gap that carries real execution risk [4]. GAAP trailing P/E of 31.73 reflects restructuring charges from the portfolio transformation. Revenue growth of ~5% GAAP (3.5% underlying) is modest for a premium automation valuation, and the portfolio transformation makes historical comparisons difficult [1]. The AspenTech cultural integration — merging software-first talent with a traditional industrial workforce of 67,000 — remains an ongoing challenge that could drive talent attrition. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED due to LGBT Activism) may limit allocation for faith-based investors, despite 16 positive ESG attributions including Business Resilience, Product Safety, and GHG Emissions [3]. The 1.39% dividend yield, while backed by 69 years of increases, falls below the 2% threshold for Dividend sleeve classification.
