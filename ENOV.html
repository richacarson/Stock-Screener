Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Enovis is a medical technology company developing clinically differentiated orthopedic solutions across two segments: Prevention & Recovery (orthopedic bracing, hot/cold therapy, bone growth stimulators, electrical stimulators) and Reconstructive (hip, knee, shoulder, and extremities implants, instrumentation, and enabling technologies such as the ARVIS augmented-reality surgical guidance system). Formerly Colfax Corporation, Enovis was rebranded in 2022 to focus exclusively on medical devices, and CEO Damien McDonald (former LivaNova CEO) took over in May 2025.

Investment Thesis
Enovis trades at just 6.8x forward earnings and a 0.63 PEG despite headline Q2 2026 results that beat expectations — revenue of $582.8M and adjusted EPS of $0.90 both topped consensus, and full-year 2026 guidance was reaffirmed at $2.31-2.37B revenue with 4-6% organic growth [1]. The Reconstructive segment continues to outgrow the overall business (11% reported / 6% organic in Q1 2026, Extremities up 10% organic), and a steady cadence of named product launches (Nebula hip stem, ARVIS augmented-reality surgical guidance, Augmented Reverse Glenoid) shows the LimaCorporate-driven portfolio expansion is translating into real commercial traction [2]. Positive free cash flow of $63.7M and a 1.98 current ratio indicate the underlying cash-generative business remains sound beneath the GAAP losses.
The market's skepticism is well-founded: shares fell 12.8% after the Q2 2026 report as adjusted operating margin contracted 6.7 percentage points YoY to just 4.5%, and the company booked a $501.3M goodwill impairment — the second consecutive year of a nine-figure-plus impairment (following $645M a year earlier) tied to the same LimaCorporate acquisition [1]. That drives TTM net income to -$1.1B and floors both ROE (-54.1%) and profit margin (-48.0%) in the mechanical Infrastructure score, and an Altman Z-Score of 0.73 signals elevated balance-sheet stress. Repeated large impairments on one deal raise a real question about whether management overpaid and is now recognizing that mistake in installments, which should temper confidence in the 'cheap on forward earnings' framing until margins genuinely stabilize.
