Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NMS ·United States
Enovix is an advanced battery technology company that designs, develops, and manufactures silicon-anode lithium-ion batteries using a proprietary 3D cell architecture. The company targets high-performance applications including defense and industrial (drone batteries with a $183M pipeline), smart eyewear (AI-1 battery shipping to customers), and next-generation smartphones (1,000-cycle life demonstrated). Enovix operates Fab-2 in Malaysia for volume manufacturing and maintains facilities in the U.S. and South Korea. Q2 2026 revenue was $9.0M (up 21% YoY), with H1 2026 revenue of $16.6M (up 32% YoY), and the company holds $552M in cash and marketable securities [1][2]. CEO Raj Talluri departed in August 2026; CFO Ryan Benton serves as interim CEO while founder T.J. Rodgers moved to executive chairman [3].
664 Employees ·www.enovix.com

Investment Thesis
Enovix at $2.68 with a $588M market cap offers exposure to a proprietary silicon-anode battery technology scaling through multiple high-growth end markets. Q2 2026 revenue of $9.0M (up 21% YoY) and Q3 guidance of $9-10M demonstrate continued commercial traction, with H1 2026 revenue of $16.6M up 32% YoY [1][2]. The $183M drone/defense pipeline provides multi-year revenue visibility, AI-1 smart eyewear shipments are expected to increase 9x sequentially in Q3, and the 1,000-cycle life achievement on smartphone-class batteries opens the largest addressable market in consumer electronics. With $552M in cash and marketable securities, Enovix has approximately 7+ years of runway at the current $75M annual burn rate, eliminating near-term financing risk that plagues most pre-profitability hardware companies. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately 55% validates the technology's commercial potential.
The bear case centers on execution risk, leadership instability, and the path to profitability. CEO Raj Talluri's August 2026 departure — followed by COO Ajay Marathe's earlier retirement — creates a leadership vacuum at a critical scaling juncture, and the stock's 83% decline from its 2023 highs to $2.68 reflects eroded market confidence [3]. Q2 2026 gross margin compressed to 14.4% from 26.0% a year earlier due to product mix shift, suggesting Enovix is achieving volume growth at the expense of margin quality. Adjusted EBITDA of negative $18.9M means the company is still burning significant cash despite $35.9M TTM revenue. The D/E ratio of 255.5% reflects $541.7M in convertible debt, and while this is offset by the cash position today, a sustained revenue miss or margin compression could shift the balance sheet dynamic. The fundamental question is whether Enovix can achieve manufacturing economics competitive with Samsung SDI, LG, and CATL — companies with orders-of-magnitude greater scale and decades of manufacturing optimization. The stock's decline from $43 to $2.68 over three years suggests the market increasingly doubts this trajectory.
