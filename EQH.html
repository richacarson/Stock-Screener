Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Equitable Holdings is a New York-based retirement, protection and asset-management company founded in 1859 and known as AXA Equitable Holdings until January 2020 [4]. It operates Individual Retirement (variable and buffered annuities), Group Retirement (tax-advantaged plans for educators, municipalities and nonprofits), Investment Management and Research (AllianceBernstein), and Protection Solutions (life and group benefits) [4]. CEO Mark Pearson leads the company [2]. In Q3 2026 it reported non-GAAP operating earnings of $595M ($2.13/share, +19% YoY) and GAAP net income of $1.1B, with net inflows of $1.1B in Retirement, $2.7B in Wealth Management and $6.6B in Asset Management [1].

Investment Thesis
Equitable trades at $54.26, or just 6.1x forward earnings, while Q3 2026 non-GAAP operating EPS rose 19% to $2.13 and year-to-date operating EPS is up 22%, above the 15%+ growth guided for 2026 [1][4]. TTM non-GAAP operating earnings reached $2.07B, with a non-GAAP operating ROE (ex-AOCI) of 39%. All three growth engines saw positive net flows in Q3: $1.1B in Retirement, $2.7B in Wealth Management and $6.6B in Asset Management [1]. The dividend is well covered at a 24.8% payout, and the quarterly rate has risen every year since the 2018 IPO, from $0.13 to $0.30 (9.7% 5-year CAGR) [7]. The Stifel Independent Advisors deal adds 110+ advisors and ~$9B of client assets to the higher-multiple wealth business [2].
The bear case is earnings quality and balance-sheet opacity. GAAP results swing widely with hedging and derivative marks: FY2025 had a $1.44B net loss to common after $1.23B of profit in FY2024, revenue fell 29.8% YoY in the latest quarter, and the 5-year revenue CAGR is -1.2% [4][5]. Book equity is very thin or negative depending on the measure (D/E 586% on yfinance), which leaves little cushion if equity markets or interest rates move sharply against the variable-annuity book. Litigation over cost-of-insurance increases and volatility-management strategies has been ongoing for years [3]. For faith-aligned investors, the Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED, citing LGBT activism) is a significant negative.
Key Catalysts
  1. Delivering above 15% non-GAAP operating EPS growth for full-year 2026; year-to-date growth of 22% through Q3 sets up a beat [1]
  2. Wealth Management scaling — $2.7B of Q3 net inflows plus Stifel Independent Advisors (~$9B in assets) shifting the mix toward capital-light, higher-multiple fee earnings [1][2]
  3. Continued dividend growth — the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.30 in 2026, with only 24.8% of TTM earnings paid out [7]
Key Risks
  1. Market-sensitive GAAP earnings — hedge and derivative marks on the variable-annuity book drove a $1.44B FY2025 net loss to common despite positive operating earnings, and a sharp equity or rate move could repeat this [5]
  2. Thin or negative book equity (D/E 586%) leaves little capital cushion and makes the company dependent on regulatory capital and reinsurance arrangements [4]
  3. Policyholder litigation — class actions over cost-of-insurance increases on 2004-2007 universal life policies and the variable-annuity volatility-management strategy carry legal and reputational risk [3]
