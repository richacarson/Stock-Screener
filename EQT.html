Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
EQT Corporation is the largest natural gas producer in the United States, operating as the only large-scale vertically integrated natural gas company following its 2024 acquisition of Equitrans Midstream. The company produces over 2.1 trillion cubic feet equivalent annually from the Appalachian Basin across Pennsylvania, West Virginia, and Ohio, representing approximately 6% of total U.S. natural gas output. Founded in 1888 and headquartered in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, EQT generated TTM revenue of approximately $9.5B with a 29.2% profit margin.

Investment Thesis
EQT at $52.94 is the largest and lowest-cost natural gas producer in the United States, uniquely positioned as the only vertically integrated large-scale gas company following the 2024 Equitrans Midstream acquisition. The company generated $2.46B in free cash flow over the trailing twelve months on a clean balance sheet with just 19.6% D/E, while raising 2026 production guidance by 90 Bcfe and lowering capex by $25M simultaneously [1]. New commercial agreements — a 10-year CPV power-generation gas supply deal and 5-year Asian LNG offtake — diversify revenue beyond volatile domestic spot prices, while AI-driven data center buildouts create a secular demand tailwind for natural gas as the primary dispatchable fuel source. At 14x forward earnings with $9.5B in TTM revenue, EQT offers compelling value for an asset-rich commodity leader.
The bear case centers on natural gas price volatility and near-term earnings weakness. Q2 2026 revenue fell 29% YoY to $1.81B with EPS of just $0.39 as Henry Hub prices averaged $2.89/MMBtu, and trailing earnings growth of -74% reflects the cyclical trough [1]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 22% is misleading — it is heavily inflated by the Equitrans acquisition rather than organic growth, and EQT remains fundamentally a commodity producer whose earnings are dictated by natural gas prices it does not control. Environmental advocacy groups have documented methane emissions from EQT facilities that contradict the company's net-zero claims, creating reputational and regulatory risk as EPA enforcement evolves [3]. The departure of CFO David Khani removes a key architect of the financial turnaround, and EQT's low dividend yield of 1.25% offers minimal income protection during commodity downturns.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-driven data center electricity demand creating secular tailwind for natural gas — EQT's new 10-year CPV power-generation supply deal and 5-year Asian LNG offtake position it as a direct beneficiary of the structural demand increase for dispatchable generation fuel [1]

  3. MVP Southgate pipeline acceleration — $85M in capital contributions pulled forward from 2027 to 2026 to speed construction, expanding market access for Appalachian gas to Southeast U.S. demand centers [1]
Key Risks

  3. CFO departure and leadership continuity risk — David Khani's exit removes the executive credited with restoring EQT to investment-grade credit, and the company has not yet named a permanent replacement during a period of complex integration execution [2]
