Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NMS ·Sweden
Telefonaktiebolaget LM Ericsson is a Swedish multinational providing mobile connectivity solutions to communications service providers, enterprises, and the public sector globally. Operating through Networks, Cloud Software and Services, Enterprise, and Other segments, Ericsson is one of three major global 5G infrastructure vendors alongside Nokia and Huawei. Founded in 1876 and headquartered in Stockholm, Sweden, Ericsson generated approximately 237B SEK (~$23B USD) in FY2025 revenue with a 10.8% profit margin across 86,536 employees.

Investment Thesis
Ericsson at $9.50 offers a value entry into one of only three global 5G infrastructure providers at 15.5x forward earnings and 3.4% dividend yield, with a clean balance sheet (37.7% D/E) and 26% ROE demonstrating capital efficiency despite revenue headwinds [1]. The Q1 2026 results showed 6% organic growth beneath the FX-distorted headline, with contributions from all segments driven by continued 5G rollouts and 5G Core uptake. The CEO transition to Per Narvinger (20+ year Ericsson veteran, Head of Networks) signals continuity, and the Cognitive Labs AI initiative and T-Mobile AI-native Scheduler partnership position Ericsson at the intersection of AI and connectivity infrastructure [2]. As AI data centers proliferate, demand for high-performance mobile connectivity and private 5G networks should create a secular tailwind for the company's core capabilities.
The bear case is dominated by secular revenue decline and margin compression. Revenue in local currency has been essentially flat over five years (~0.5% CAGR), and headline USD revenue declined 6.1% YoY as SEK strength amplified underlying weakness [1]. The AI boom is a double-edged sword — while it may eventually drive network demand, the immediate impact is rising semiconductor and memory chip costs that squeeze hardware margins, with management acknowledging Ericsson is 'not a near-term AI beneficiary' [2]. The bribery scandal legacy remains a governance overhang — $1B+ in FCPA penalties, Iraq corruption revelations involving alleged payments near ISIS-controlled territories, DOJ breach determination, and ongoing Swedish and Chinese investigations create unquantifiable legal tail risk [3]. The Enterprise segment's 19-30% revenue decline and widening losses suggest the private 5G growth thesis is failing to materialize on management's timeline. At a market cap of $31B, Ericsson is priced for modest recovery, but continued revenue erosion and margin pressure from AI-driven input costs could push the stock lower.
