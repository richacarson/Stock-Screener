Company Profile
Utilities ·Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Eversource Energy is New England's largest energy delivery company, serving approximately 4.4 million electric, natural gas, and water customers across Connecticut, Massachusetts, and New Hampshire. Following the 2025 exit from offshore wind, Eversource is a pure-play regulated transmission and distribution utility with a $26.5 billion 2026-2030 capex plan focused on grid modernization and AI data-center load growth.

Investment Thesis
Eversource is a cleaner pure-play regulated utility post-offshore-wind exit with improving fundamentals: Q1 2026 revenue of $4.504B (+9.37% YoY), ROE recovering to 10.97% (from 5.4% in FY2024), and a 27-year Dividend Aristocrat streak yielding 4.26% with 5.81% 5-year CAGR [1][4]. The $26.5B 2026-2030 capex plan supports 5-7% EPS growth through 2030, anchored in grid modernization and AI data-center load growth across New England [1]. Forward P/E of 15.92 is attractive for a regulated monopoly with Dividend Aristocrat status, and AI tools like SmartInspect and digital-twin outage prevention are reducing operating costs [2]. Structurally immune to AI disruption while benefiting from AI-driven electricity demand growth. Inspire alignment of 45 (ALIGNED) with 18 positive ESG attributions reflects the utility's essential infrastructure role.
The bear case centers on regulatory headwinds, balance sheet leverage, and residual offshore wind exposure. FERC's 100bps reduction in allowed transmission base ROE forced 2026 EPS guidance down to $4.57-$4.72 (from $4.80-$4.95), directly compressing near-term returns [5]. D/E of 182% remains elevated and finances a $26.5B capex plan competing with dividend coverage. The potential Aquarion sale and Revolution Wind stop-work order create lingering liability uncertainty, with Fitch flagging financial risk [3]. Connecticut rate politics remain adversarial following 2025 bill-shock episodes, threatening future rate-case outcomes in a key territory. The stock's 10% appreciation from $67.60 to $74.44 since the prior screen has compressed the yield from 4.66% to 4.26%, making entry less compelling for pure income investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data-center load growth across New England driving accelerated transmission capex and rate base expansion under the $26.5B 2026-2030 plan [1]
  2. ROE recovery from 5.4% (FY2024) to 10.97% (current TTM) demonstrating post-wind-exit earnings normalization with further improvement expected as impairment drag fully subsides [5]
  3. 27-year dividend growth streak continuing with 5-7% EPS growth through 2030, supporting a 4.26% yield with ~32.5% coverage cushion at current payout ratio [4]
Key Risks
  1. FERC allowed-ROE cut already forced 2026 EPS trim to $4.57-$4.72; further regulatory reductions would directly pressure the dividend growth trajectory and 5-7% long-term guidance [5]
  2. D/E of 182% finances heavy capex in a rising-rate environment — interest expense growth competes with dividend coverage, and any credit downgrade would increase financing costs [5]
  3. Connecticut rate politics and residual Revolution Wind stop-work liability create ongoing regulatory and legal uncertainty that caps the stock's multiple relative to peers [3]
