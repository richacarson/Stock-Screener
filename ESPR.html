Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - Specialty & Generic ·NASDAQ (Delisted) ·United States
Esperion Therapeutics was a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on non-statin LDL-cholesterol-lowering therapies, marketing NEXLETOL and NEXLIZET (bempedoic acid-based oral medicines) in the U.S. and NILEMDO/NUSTENDI internationally through partners including Daiichi Sankyo and Otsuka. On July 13, 2026, ARCHIMED's MED Platform II fund completed its take-private acquisition of Esperion for $3.16/share cash plus up to $100M in contingent value rights, valuing the deal at up to $1.1B on a fully diluted basis. Esperion shares were subsequently delisted from NASDAQ and the company filed Form 15 to terminate SEC reporting obligations [1]. Full-year 2025 revenue grew 21% to $403.1M, and Q1 2026 revenue reached $80.1M (+23.2% YoY) prior to the acquisition close.

Investment Thesis
Esperion's underlying franchise was genuinely accelerating before the take-private — full-year 2025 revenue grew 21% to $403.1M and Q1 2026 revenue rose 23.2% YoY to $80.1M, with U.S. net product revenue up 38% as NEXLETOL/NEXLIZET prescription volume climbed and the Otsuka-partnered Japan launch added a new royalty stream [2]. Management projected $205M in 2026 U.S. product sales and $321M for 2027. The bempedoic acid franchise addressed a genuine unmet need for statin-intolerant patients, and the Inspire Impact Score of 39 (ALIGNED) made it one of the few pharma names meeting faith-based investment criteria. Forward P/E of 12.35x looked inexpensive for a company compounding revenue at a ~75% three-year CAGR.
This is no longer an investable equity story. ARCHIMED completed its take-private acquisition of Esperion on July 13, 2026 at $3.16/share cash, and the stock was delisted from NASDAQ [1]. Shareholders received $3.16 per share plus contingent value rights: up to $40M if annual NEXLETOL/NEXLIZET net sales exceed $350M in 2027, and up to $60M if annual Enbumyst net sales reach $160M in any year through 2030. The balance sheet was weak on a standalone basis (negative shareholders' equity of ~-$308M against $543.8M of total debt), which explains why the board accepted a private equity offer rather than continuing to compound independently. At least one shareholder law firm (Ademi LLP) investigated whether the $3.16 offer was fair, noting substantial insider benefits under change-of-control arrangements and a deal penalty limiting competing transactions [5]. The CVR payments remain the only potential residual value for former shareholders.
Key Catalysts
  1. Contingent value right payments: up to $40M if 2027 NEXLETOL/NEXLIZET annual net sales exceed $350M (vs. management's pre-deal forecast of $321M), payable to former shareholders [1]
  2. Second CVR tranche: up to $60M if Enbumyst (bumetanide nasal spray) annual net sales reach $160M in any year through 2030 under ARCHIMED ownership [1]
  3. Potential inclusion of bempedoic acid in U.S. Dyslipidemia Guidelines, which could drive incremental prescriber adoption and accelerate the $350M sales threshold triggering the first CVR payment [2]
Key Risks
  1. Stock is delisted and no longer tradeable — the only remaining value for former shareholders is through the non-tradeable contingent value rights, which are capped and dependent on commercial execution under new private ownership [1]
  2. CVR milestone risk: NEXLETOL/NEXLIZET must exceed $350M in 2027 sales (vs. $205M projected for 2026) to trigger the first $40M payment, requiring ~71% growth that may be difficult to achieve under new ownership transition [1][2]
  3. Negative shareholders' equity (~-$308M) and $543.8M of total debt meant the standalone business was financially fragile, validating ARCHIMED's low acquisition price of $3.16/share
