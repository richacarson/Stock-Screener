Company Profile
Energy ·Uranium ·NASDAQ ·United States
enCore Energy Corp. is the largest in-situ recovery (ISR) uranium producer in the United States, operating two Central Processing Plants in South Texas (Alta Mesa CPP and Rosita CPP) with combined licensed capacity approaching 2.6 million pounds of U3O8 per year. The company extracted 699,807 pounds of uranium in 2025 (+242% YoY) and has secured 12 uranium sales agreements with US nuclear power plants totaling 8.3 million pounds through 2033. Expansion pipeline includes Upper Spring Creek satellite (Texas), Dewey Burdock ISR (South Dakota, Fast-41 permitting), and Gas Hills (Wyoming). Market cap approximately $253M.
168 Employees ·www.encoreuranium.com

Investment Thesis
enCore Energy is the most direct pure-play on US domestic uranium supply growth, operating as the largest US ISR uranium producer with 699,807 lbs extracted in FY2025 (+242% YoY) and production scaling over 100% further in 2025 [1][2]. The company has secured 12 uranium sales agreements with US nuclear power plants totaling 8.3M lbs through 2033, providing contracted revenue visibility. Q1 2026 showed the first quarterly profit ($5.4M net income, $0.03/share), and analysts estimate $94.5M revenue for FY2026 versus $43.16M in FY2025 [1]. The confluence of AI data center nuclear power demand, the ADVANCE Act accelerating reactor licensing, and Fast-41 permitting for Dewey Burdock creates structural demand tailwinds for domestic uranium that cannot be quickly replicated by competitors.
The investment case carries significant risks. FY2025 revenue declined 26% from FY2024 ($43.16M vs $58.33M), profit margin is -63%, and the Q1 2026 profit was driven by a $34.4M one-time gain on property sales rather than operational profitability [1][5]. The CEO transition from experienced ISR veteran Paul Goranson to Acting CEO Robert Willette introduces leadership uncertainty at a critical production scaling phase. At $1.35/share and $253M market cap (down from $1.82 and $355M in April), the stock has declined 26% in three months, reflecting market concern about execution pace. The company needs uranium prices to remain above ~$60/lb and production to scale toward 3M lbs annually before achieving sustained operating profitability — outcomes that are not guaranteed and depend on commodity price dynamics, wellfield performance, and regulatory timelines.
Key Catalysts
  1. Upper Spring Creek satellite ISR project production feed to Rosita CPP in 2026, incrementally increasing extraction toward the 3M lb annual target and demonstrating multi-site operational scaling
  2. Dewey Burdock ISR project Fast-41 permitting progress in South Dakota — the first critical minerals project on this federal fast-track — potentially compressing the timeline to a multi-million pound production expansion
  3. AI hyperscaler nuclear power procurement agreements driving long-term uranium offtake demand at premium prices, with enCore's 12 contracted nuclear plant agreements positioning it to capture this structural demand shift
Key Risks
  1. Uranium price correction below $50/lb would push ISR production costs above revenue, forcing further cash burn and potentially requiring dilutive equity raises at the depressed $1.35 share price
  2. CEO leadership transition from Goranson to Acting CEO Willette creates execution risk at a critical production scaling phase — the 'Acting' designation suggests permanent leadership selection is unresolved
  3. Q1 2026 profitability was driven by a $34.4M one-time property sale gain rather than operational earnings — underlying ISR operations remain loss-making and require significant production scaling before sustainable profitability

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Uranium ISR mining is a physical extraction process requiring licensed wellfields, injection/recovery systems, Central Processing Plants, and Nuclear Regulatory Commission compliance — none of which can be automated away or substituted by AI [3]. enCore's business depends on geological assets, licensed infrastructure, and chemical processing that AI structurally cannot replicate. More importantly, AI is a direct demand driver for enCore's product: hyperscale AI data center operators including Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are signing nuclear power purchase agreements to meet baseload energy requirements, creating the largest emerging source of US nuclear power demand and directly benefiting domestic uranium producers [3]. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI data centers represent the single largest emerging driver of US nuclear power demand, making enCore's contracted uranium supply directly enabled by AI infrastructure buildout. The US government's Fast-41 permitting acceleration for uranium (designating it a critical mineral) explicitly reflects AI-driven energy security priorities. enCore's 12 nuclear plant sales agreements totaling 8.3M lbs through 2033 capture this demand.
