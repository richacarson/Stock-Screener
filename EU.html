Company Profile
Energy ·Uranium ·NASDAQ ·United States
enCore Energy Corp. is the largest in-situ recovery (ISR) uranium producer in the United States, operating Central Processing Plants in South Texas with licensed capacity approaching 2.6 million pounds of U3O8 per year. The company underwent a major leadership reset in April 2026, appointing Richard H. Little as CEO (replacing Robert Willette), with founder William Sheriff returning as Executive Chair [3]. H1 2026 revenue rose 55% YTD to $34.0M, with Q1 marking the first quarterly profit ($5.4M net income on $18.3M revenue), though the profit was primarily driven by a Verdera asset sale gain [1]. Uranium extraction from own operations rose 22% YoY to 90K lbs in Q1 2026, with production costs of $46.43/lb versus $78.82/lb for purchased material. Phase 1 of Upper Spring Creek-Brown satellite is complete with plant capacity now at 1,600 gpm [2].

Investment Thesis
enCore Energy is the most direct pure-play on US domestic uranium supply growth, operating as the largest US ISR uranium producer with extraction up 22% YoY and H1 2026 revenue surging 55% to $34.0M [1][2]. The company's production costs of $46.43/lb for own extraction versus $78.82/lb for purchased material demonstrate improving unit economics as production scales [1]. Q1 2026 marked the first quarterly profit ($5.4M net income). The structural demand thesis is compelling: AI data center operators are signing nuclear power purchase agreements, the ADVANCE Act is accelerating reactor licensing, and US energy security concerns are driving demand for domestic uranium production that cannot be quickly replicated by competitors. Phase 1 of Upper Spring Creek is complete with capacity expansion potential to 3,200 gpm [2].
The investment case carries significant risks that dominate the picture. Q1 2026 profitability was driven by a Verdera asset sale gain rather than operational earnings; underlying ISR operations remain loss-making with TTM profit margin of -113% and ROE of -21% [1][4]. Uranium price risk is acute — a sustained decline below $50/lb would push production costs above revenue, requiring dilutive capital raises at depressed share prices. The company needs sustained uranium prices above ~$60/lb and production scaling beyond current levels before achieving operational breakeven.
