Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NASDAQ ·United States
Evergy is a regulated electric utility serving approximately 1.7 million customers in Kansas and Missouri. Formed in 2018 from the merger of Great Plains Energy and Westar Energy, the company generates, transmits, and distributes electricity through a diversified generation fleet including coal, natural gas, nuclear, wind, and solar. Evergy is executing a generation fleet transition toward renewables and grid modernization while benefiting from emerging data center demand in its Midwest service territory.

Investment Thesis
Evergy offers dependable utility income with 23 consecutive years of dividend increases, a 3.69% yield, and improving growth prospects. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.69 (+25.5% YoY) demonstrated strong earnings momentum, and full-year 2026 guidance of $4.14-$4.34 implies 8-13% growth over 2025 adjusted EPS of $3.83 [1]. The company's long-term target of 6-8%+ adjusted EPS growth through 2030 (exceeding 8% from 2028+) provides visibility into an accelerating earnings trajectory, driven by rate base growth from generation fleet transition and emerging data center demand in the Midwest. Trading at 17.4x forward earnings, Evergy is reasonably valued for a regulated utility with improving growth characteristics. The stock carries an Inspire score of 47 (ALIGNED), making it attractive for faith-aligned income investors [4].
The bear case centers on Evergy's modest current growth and Midwest-focused market position. Full-year 2025 GAAP EPS declined 3.4% to $3.66, and revenue growth of approximately 2% significantly lags peers with data center tailwinds like AEP (11.3%) [1]. The 6-8% EPS growth target, while solid, is mid-pack for utilities and relies on constructive regulatory outcomes in both Kansas and Missouri — any adverse rate case decision could impair the growth trajectory. D/E of 148% on total debt of $14.8B versus just $27.8M in cash creates meaningful interest rate sensitivity, particularly if rising rates compress utility valuations or increase refinancing costs [4]. Payout ratio of 71.71% is above the 60% IOWN target, leaving limited room for dividend acceleration without earnings growth materializing. The company is relatively young as a combined entity (formed 2018) with a CEO in place only since 2021, and the Midwest service territory, while stable, lacks the premium growth dynamics of Sun Belt or data center-heavy regions. ESG stakeholder expectations across both progressive and conservative constituencies create potential reputational risk in either direction [3].
