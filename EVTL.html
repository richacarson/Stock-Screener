Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Vertical Aerospace designs, manufactures, and develops zero operating emission electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft for the advanced air mobility market. The company's VX4 aircraft is a piloted, four-passenger eVTOL targeting dual CAA/EASA certification by 2028. Headquartered in Bristol, UK, Vertical went public via SPAC in 2022 and is led by CEO Stuart Simpson, who succeeded founder Stephen Fitzpatrick in 2024. The company is pre-revenue with a market cap of approximately $165M and annual cash burn of $132M.

Investment Thesis
Vertical Aerospace offers high-risk, high-reward exposure to the nascent eVTOL urban air mobility market through one of only two companies globally to have completed a piloted transition flight [1]. The $850M comprehensive financing package provides a funded path to the 2028 CAA/EASA dual certification target, and the appointment of former EASA Executive Director Patrick Ky to the board strengthens regulatory credibility [2]. At $1.30 per share with $96.51M in cash ($0.73 per share in net cash), the stock trades at a modest premium to liquidation value. If VX4 achieves certification, the zero-emission, four-passenger aircraft would position Vertical in a market projected to reach $30B+ by 2035.
The bear case is severe for a portfolio allocation. Vertical Aerospace is a pre-revenue company burning $132M annually with going concern risk explicitly flagged in SEC filings — £69M in cash against £145M in projected 12-month outflows creates a survival timeline measured in months without additional fundraising [3][4]. Shares outstanding surged 169.8% YoY, and the $850M financing package will require further massive dilution. The stock has declined 78.9% over 52 weeks, and EPS of -$1.60 on $0 revenue demonstrates no path to profitability before 2029 at the earliest. Certification risk is existential: any VX4 testing failure, regulatory delay, or change in certification standards could strand all development investment. Competitors Joby and Archer are further advanced in FAA certification, and the eVTOL market may not develop at the scale or timeline required to justify current investment. The 8.41% short interest reflects bear conviction about survival probability.
Key Catalysts
  1. VX4 certification by CAA and EASA in 2028 would validate Vertical's technology and unlock commercial revenue, with the piloted transition flight milestone demonstrating technical feasibility [1]
  2. Securing additional commercial pre-orders from airlines and air mobility operators would de-risk the revenue outlook and could trigger a valuation re-rating from speculative to pre-commercial pricing
  3. Strategic partnership or acquisition by a larger aerospace OEM (Embraer, Airbus, Boeing) could provide manufacturing scale, distribution, and capital that Vertical lacks independently
Key Risks
  1. Going concern risk — SEC filings flag that projected 12-month cash outflows of £145M significantly exceed available cash of £69M, making continued operation dependent on additional financing that may further dilute shareholders by 50%+ [3][4]
  2. Certification failure or delay beyond 2028 could exhaust available funding before the VX4 generates any revenue, stranding $500M+ in development investment
  3. Massive share dilution — shares outstanding increased 169.8% YoY and the $850M financing package ensures continued dilution, compressing per-share value even if the enterprise succeeds [3]
