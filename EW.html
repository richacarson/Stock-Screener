Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Edwards Lifesciences designs, manufactures, and markets medical devices for advanced cardiovascular diseases, including transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), transcatheter mitral and tricuspid therapies (TMTT), and surgical structural heart solutions. The company derives approximately 60% of sales internationally and is the global leader in TAVR with nearly 70 years of structural heart innovation. Q2 2026 revenue grew 13.6% YoY to $1.74B, and the company raised FY2026 guidance to $6.5-6.9B (10-11% growth) with TMTT guidance raised to $760-780M [1].

Investment Thesis
Edwards Lifesciences is the undisputed global leader in transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) and is rapidly building a second growth engine in TMTT. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $1.74B (+13.6% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.78 beating the $0.74 consensus, prompting management to raise full-year guidance to $6.5-6.9B (10-11% growth) and TMTT guidance to $760-780M [1]. At $89.95 and 28.65x forward earnings, EW offers a reasonable entry for a dominant medtech franchise with multi-decade structural heart growth tailwinds. A pristine balance sheet (D/E 7%, $4.3B cash vs $704M debt), R&D investment at 17% of sales, and TMTT's path to $2B by 2030 create a visible long-duration growth trajectory. International markets at 60% of revenue remain under-penetrated for both TAVR and TMTT, and TAVR full-year guidance was raised to 8-9% growth [1]. The Ethisphere Most Ethical Companies recognition and Inspire Impact Score of 57 (ALIGNED) support faith-based allocation.
The bear case centers on three risks. First, the FTC antitrust action against Edwards' acquisition of JenaValve raises regulatory risk around the company's M&A strategy — if the FTC prevails, Edwards may face constraints on future acquisitions in the TAVR market, limiting its ability to build competitive moats through technology acquisition [3]. Second, the CEO transition from long-tenured Michael Mussallem to Bernard Zovighian in 2023 has created cultural turbulence, with Glassdoor reviews flagging layoffs, departing senior leaders, and a shift to a more metrics-driven culture that could impair innovation velocity and talent retention [2]. Third, GAAP EPS of $1.72 (-75.5% YoY) reflects the distorted comparison to FY2024's massive one-time gains from the Critical Care divestiture, making earnings quality difficult to assess; TTM profit margin of 15.4% is well below the 28% levels seen in FY2023. Forward P/E of 28.65x and PEG of ~2.3x price in significant growth — any TMTT scaling disappointment or TAVR deceleration beyond the 8-9% guidance could trigger multiple compression [4].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Edwards' core business — designing, manufacturing, and implanting physical heart valves via catheter-based procedures — is fundamentally protected from AI disruption [2]. TAVR and TMTT require physical devices, interventional cardiologists, catheterization labs, and regulated surgical environments that AI cannot substitute. The structural heart market is expanding as TAVR penetrates lower-risk patient populations, and AI-driven diagnostics may actually increase detection of aortic stenosis and valve disease, expanding Edwards' addressable market. Regulatory barriers (FDA PMA approval process) and decades of clinical trial data create deep moats that AI startups cannot breach. Competitive risks come from other device makers (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific), not AI substitution [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): While Edwards is building internal AI capabilities with 11 patents and Palantir Foundry deployment for operational efficiency and clinical decision support, these do not yet generate quantifiable AI revenue [2].
