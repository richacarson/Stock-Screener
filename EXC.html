Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NMS ·United States
Exelon Corporation is the largest regulated electric distribution utility in the United States, serving more than 10 million customers through six operating subsidiaries: ComEd (Illinois), PECO (Pennsylvania), BGE (Maryland), Pepco (D.C./Maryland), DPL (Delaware), and ACE (New Jersey). Following the 2022 spin-off of its generation business as Constellation Energy, Exelon operates as a pure-play regulated utility focused on electricity and natural gas distribution and transmission. Headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, Exelon generated $25.3B in TTM revenue with a 11.0% profit margin.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Exelon operates within a fundamentally finite game construct — regulated utilities earn returns set by public utility commissions, compete through rate cases rather than innovation, and optimize for regulatory outcomes and shareholder returns. While the essential nature of electricity distribution gives the company permanence, the ComEd bribery scandal revealed that Exelon's actual competitive playbook involved corrupting the regulatory process to secure favorable legislation, the antithesis of playing an infinite game with integrity. Butler's leadership post-spinoff shows signs of improvement, but the organizational culture that enabled systematic bribery from 2011-2019 casts a long shadow.

Investment Thesis
Exelon at $41.73 is the largest regulated electric distribution utility in the United States, positioned at the epicenter of AI-driven electricity demand growth. CEO Calvin Butler has quantified a 900% potential increase in Chicago-area data center power demand, with 36 GW of data center projects in the pipeline across Exelon's service territories — doubled from H1 2024 to H1 2025 [2]. The company reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.81-$2.91 with 5-7% compound annual growth through 2029, supported by a $38B capital investment plan for 2025-2028 that grows rate base and earns regulated returns [1]. At 13.7x forward earnings with a 4.0% dividend yield, Exelon offers a defensive income stream backed by monopoly service territories serving 10M+ customers, with AI providing a secular growth catalyst that could push EPS growth toward the high end of guidance.
The bear case centers on regulatory risk, governance scars, and capital-intensive growth requirements. The ComEd bribery scandal ($200M fine, $173M shareholder settlement, former CEO convicted) demonstrated that Exelon's relationship with regulators was built on corruption rather than trust, and the reputational damage persists in the regulatory proceedings that determine the company's earnings power [3]. D/E of 177% reflects the heavy capital structure required for rate-base growth, and negative free cash flow of -$2.7B in the trailing twelve months means the $38B capex plan requires continued debt issuance that increases leverage in a rising-rate environment. Consumer and legislative pushback on rate increases — with intervention underway in New Jersey, New York, and Maryland — could constrain the regulatory returns Exelon needs to fund grid modernization [1]. The dividend growth CAGR of just 1.9% over five years (distorted by the Constellation spinoff reset) is below inflation, and the 60% payout ratio leaves limited room for acceleration without earnings growth. Butler's own blackout warnings by 2027 expose reliability risk that could trigger regulatory penalties if grid failures materialize before capital investments are completed.
