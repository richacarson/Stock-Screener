Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Exelixis is an oncology-focused biopharmaceutical company that discovers, develops, and commercializes treatments for difficult-to-treat cancers. Its lead franchise is cabozantinib (CABOMETYX/COMETRIQ), a multi-kinase inhibitor used in renal cell carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, thyroid cancer, and neuroendocrine tumors. The company is advancing a diversifying pipeline including zanzalintinib (NDA accepted by FDA for mCRC, PDUFA Dec 3, 2026), XL309, and antibody-drug conjugates, with collaboration partners including Ipsen, Takeda, Roche, and Bristol-Myers Squibb. Q2 2026 total revenues reached $628.7M (+11% YoY) with GAAP net income of $212.1M.

Investment Thesis
Exelixis is a highly profitable oncology franchise trading at a deeply discounted valuation with a de-risking pipeline catalyst approaching. Q2 2026 revenue of $628.7M (+11% YoY) generated $212.1M GAAP net income (+15% YoY), with CABOMETYX prescriptions growing 12% (2x the broader TKI market) and prescription share expanding from 45% to 47% [2]. At 14.87x forward P/E and PEG of 0.56, the stock trades at a fraction of biotech peers, with a fortress balance sheet (~8% D/E, substantial net cash) and a $750M buyback authorization providing downside protection [6]. The zanzalintinib NDA has been accepted by the FDA with a PDUFA date of December 3, 2026 for metastatic colorectal cancer — a potential franchise-diversifying approval based on positive phase 3 overall survival data that is not reflected in the current multiple [4]. The STELLAR-316 phase 3 trial for kidney cancer initiating mid-2026 provides additional pipeline visibility.
The bear case centers on franchise concentration, toxic culture, and decelerating growth. CABOMETYX generates the vast majority of revenue, and competitive displacement from emerging therapies (IO combinations, next-gen TKIs, ADCs in RCC) would be existential before pipeline diversification is achieved [1][2]. Management lowered FY2026 revenue guidance by $50M to $2.50-2.55B due to slower neuroendocrine tumor adoption, signaling that label expansion economics can underperform expectations [2]. The critically poor employee culture (2.0/5 Glassdoor, 11% recommend, culture rated 1.9/5) creates retention risk for the specialized scientific talent needed to advance the pipeline [3]. Significant insider selling ($7.8M+ by officers and directors in February 2026) signals limited insider conviction [1]. Pipeline candidates face typical biotech binary risk — zanzalintinib could receive a complete response letter, and the ADC programs could fail clinically. Revenue growth deceleration (18.5% in 2023 to ~10% in 2026) suggests the cabo franchise is approaching maturity.

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Exelixis's core business of discovering, developing, and commercializing oncology drugs is PROTECTED from AI disruption. Drug development requires decades of clinical expertise, FDA-regulated clinical trials, physician relationships, and manufacturing capabilities that AI cannot replace [1][3]. The regulatory pathway — Phase I through Phase III trials, FDA approval, post-market surveillance — is a structural moat that AI tools cannot circumvent. However, AI-powered drug discovery companies (Recursion, Insilico Medicine) could accelerate competitive pipeline development in oncology, and the single-franchise concentration on cabozantinib creates exposure if AI-discovered competitors emerge faster. The zanzalintinib NDA acceptance partially mitigates franchise concentration risk [4].
