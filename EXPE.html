Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Travel Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Expedia Group operates a portfolio of travel brands including Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity, and Trivago, plus a rapidly growing B2B platform (Expedia for Business) that powers travel distribution for airlines, financial services, and retailers. Revenue generated via merchant, agency, and advertising models across lodging, air, and experiences. Currently navigating its third major technology disruption (AI) under CEO Ariane Gorin, with an August 2026 reorganization centralizing AI under Chief AI and Data Officer Xavier Amatriain.

Investment Thesis
Expedia delivered a strong H1 2026 with Q2 revenue of $4.3B (+14% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $1.1B (+23%), and diluted EPS of $7.16 (vs $2.48 prior year), raising full-year guidance to $16.05-16.22B revenue (+9-10%) with 1.5-1.75 ppt EBITDA margin expansion [1][2]. The August 2026 AI reorganization under Chief AI Officer Xavier Amatriain (ex-Google VP) signals deep commitment to AI transformation, with CEO Gorin quantifying AI as driving 9% revenue growth [3]. At 15.3x forward earnings with PEG of ~1.0, the stock is reasonably valued for a company executing its AI pivot while the B2B segment grows 22-25%, diversifying from the AI-exposed consumer OTA core. Gross bookings guidance of $129.5-130.8B (+8-9%) provides visibility into sustained travel demand.
The bear case centers on existential AI disruption risk and cultural deterioration. Generative AI trip planners from Google, OpenAI, and native OS assistants are building end-to-end travel flows that could bypass OTA aggregation — Expedia's CEO acknowledges this as the company's 'third tech disruption' and the outcome remains structurally uncertain despite strong H1 execution [3]. Four rounds of significant workforce cuts since 2024 — including 8 senior tech leaders in August 2026 — a WARN Act investigation, and deteriorating Glassdoor sentiment describe a fear-driven culture that may struggle to retain the AI talent needed to win this transition [5]. D/E of 257% from aggressive buybacks limits financial flexibility if travel demand softens. The stock's 22% rally from $270 to $329 since the prior screen has compressed the forward PE from 12.3 to 15.3, reducing the margin of safety.
