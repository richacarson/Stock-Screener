Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Travel Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Expedia Group operates a portfolio of travel brands including Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity, and Trivago, plus a rapidly growing B2B platform (Expedia for Business) that powers travel distribution for airlines, financial services, and retailers. Revenue generated via merchant, agency, and advertising models across lodging, air, and experiences. Currently navigating its third major technology disruption (AI) under CEO Ariane Gorin.

However, the infinite-game read is undermined by severe trust erosion from three rounds of layoffs in two years (with WARN Act investigation), aggressive financial engineering ($700M quarterly buybacks alongside cuts), and a purpose that remains generic despite decades of operation. The company plays to survive and adapt rather than playing from deep purpose.

Investment Thesis
Expedia delivered a breakout Q1 2026 with $3.426B revenue (up 15% YoY), adjusted EPS of $1.96 (beating consensus by 42%), and 83% EBITDA growth with 591 bps of margin expansion [1]. The B2B segment grew 25% and now provides meaningful diversification from the AI-exposed consumer OTA core. At 12.3x forward earnings with a PEG near 0.8, the stock is compellingly valued for a company executing successfully on its AI integration — CEO Gorin quantified AI as directly contributing 9% revenue growth [3]. Full-year 2026 guidance of $15.6-16.0B revenue (+6-9%) with 1.0-1.25 ppt EBITDA margin expansion appears conservative given Q1's 15% top-line growth. The $700M Q1 buyback demonstrates capital return conviction backed by strong FCF generation.
The bear case centers on existential AI disruption risk and cultural deterioration. Generative AI trip planners from Google, OpenAI, Perplexity, and native OS assistants are building end-to-end travel flows that could bypass OTA aggregation entirely — Expedia's own CEO acknowledges this as the company's 'third tech disruption' and the outcome remains structurally uncertain despite strong Q1 execution [3]. Three rounds of layoffs in two years (595 jobs in January 2026), a WARN Act investigation, and deteriorating Glassdoor sentiment describe a fear-driven culture that may struggle to retain the AI talent needed to win this transition [5]. D/E of 255% from aggressive buybacks limits financial flexibility if travel demand softens or AI disruption accelerates faster than adaptation. The Inspire score of -67 (MISALIGNED) presents faith-alignment concerns. Full-year guidance of only 6-8% gross bookings growth vs. Q1's 13% suggests management expects deceleration in the back half.
Key Catalysts
  1. B2B platform (Expedia for Business) sustaining 22-25% growth rate, diversifying revenue away from AI-exposed consumer OTA core and embedding Expedia as infrastructure in partner workflows [1]
  2. EBITDA margin expansion of 1.0-1.25 ppt in 2026 (conservative given Q1's 591 bps surge), demonstrating AI and platform unification are delivering structural cost leverage not just one-time gains [1]
  3. AI Activity Planner and conversational trip planning adoption driving higher engagement, repeat usage, and revenue per traveler — potentially widening the moat against AI-native competitors [3]
Key Risks
  1. Generative AI disintermediation from Google Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Apple Intelligence building complete travel planning-to-booking flows that bypass OTA aggregation — structural threat to the 30-year intermediation model [3]
  2. Cultural erosion from three rounds of layoffs in two years (WARN Act investigation pending) — fear-driven environment may struggle to attract and retain the AI engineering talent essential to winning the technology transition [5]
  3. D/E of 255% from aggressive buybacks reduces financial flexibility if macroeconomic conditions weaken travel demand while the company needs to sustain AI investment intensity to remain competitive [4]
