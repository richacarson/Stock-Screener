Company Profile
Industrials ·Staffing & Employment Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
First Advantage Corporation is the world's largest provider of employment background screening, digital identity verification, and workforce risk management solutions. The company serves 80,000 organizations including 66% of Fortune 100 companies, screening 200M+ candidates annually across 200+ countries. Following the $2.2B acquisition of Sterling Check Corp. in October 2024, First Advantage approximately doubled its revenue base. CEO Scott Staples has led the company since 2017. TTM revenue of $1.61B with a market cap of $3.53B.

Investment Thesis
First Advantage at $20.56 with Forward P/E of 16.33 offers a reasonable entry into the world's largest employment background screening company, serving 66% of Fortune 100 companies across 200+ countries [3]. Q2 2026 record revenue of $448.8M (+14.9% YoY) and raised full-year guidance to $1.67-1.71B in revenue with $472-486M in adjusted EBITDA demonstrate successful Sterling integration and accelerating organic growth [2]. The company's proprietary AI-powered platform, access to 1B+ records, and regulatory compliance moats (FCRA, GDPR) create durable competitive advantages. Adjusted EPS guidance of $1.23-1.29 implies adjusted P/E of ~16, and the analyst consensus is 'Buy' with a $19.43 target. Free cash flow of $216M (13.5% margin) supports deleveraging from the $2.07B acquisition debt.
The bear case centers on acquisition integration risk and balance sheet leverage. D/E of 160% with $2.07B in total debt against only $226M in cash creates $1.84B in net debt from the Sterling acquisition [4]. GAAP profitability is near zero (0.53% profit margin, ROE 0.66%) due to massive intangible amortization, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.22 places the company in the distress zone [4]. The background screening business is cyclical — revenue correlates with hiring volumes, which are sensitive to economic downturns. If a recession reduces hiring activity by 15-20%, FA's high fixed-cost base and debt servicing obligations could severely compress cash flows. Short interest of 8.36% reflects some skepticism about the integration execution and leverage profile. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~15% is almost entirely M&A-driven rather than organic, and organic growth of 5-8% may be the steady-state rate once Sterling synergies are fully captured.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued Sterling integration synergies and organic growth acceleration — raised 2026 guidance to $1.67-1.71B revenue ($472-486M adjusted EBITDA) with Q2 showing 14.9% growth versus Q1's 8.6% suggests momentum building [2]
  2. AI-powered platform expansion into adjacent workforce management and identity verification services could open new revenue streams beyond traditional screening [3]
  3. Deleveraging from $2.07B acquisition debt using $216M annual FCF could reduce D/E from 160% toward 100% within 2 years, unlocking capital return flexibility and valuation re-rating [4]
Key Risks
  1. Cyclical hiring volume exposure — background screening revenue directly correlates with employer hiring activity, and a macroeconomic downturn reducing U.S. hiring volumes by 15-20% would compress revenue and cash flow against a $2.07B debt load [4]
  2. Sterling integration execution risk — merging two large global screening companies with overlapping clients, platforms, and employee bases could result in higher-than-expected costs, client attrition, or operational disruptions that delay synergy realization [1]
  3. GAAP profitability near zero (0.53% margin, $8.54M net income on $1.61B revenue) reflects massive acquisition-related amortization that will persist for years, masking underlying profitability and potentially deterring GAAP-focused investors [4]
