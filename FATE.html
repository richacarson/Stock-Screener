Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Fate Therapeutics is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company developing programmed cellular immunotherapies for cancer and autoimmune disorders using its proprietary induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) platform. Key programs include FT819 (off-the-shelf CAR T-cell therapy advancing toward registrational trial in lupus nephritis), FT839 (multi-antigen dual-CAR T-cell with cleared IND for autoimmune basket trial), and FT836 (solid tumors). Headquartered in San Diego, California.
161 Employees ·www.fatetherapeutics.com

Investment Thesis
Fate Therapeutics is at a clinical inflection point with its iPSC platform advancing toward potential registrational status. FT819 is preparing for a Phase 2 potentially registrational trial in refractory SLE with lupus nephritis (dosing H2 2026), FT839 received IND clearance for a Phase 1 autoimmune basket trial, and the stock has doubled to $2.80 on these catalysts [1]. The 500+ patent portfolio, proprietary Sword and Shield chemotherapy-free dosing technology, and first-mover position in off-the-shelf iPSC CAR T-cells create a meaningful scientific moat. Cash of $174.8M provides runway into 2028, operating expenses fell 20% YoY to $34.3M per quarter, and the $326M market cap offers asymmetric upside if clinical data supports approval in the multi-billion-dollar autoimmune cell therapy market [1].
The bear case is severe. Fate is burning $31M per quarter on $1.3M in revenue, with no approved products and a stock still down 97%+ from its 2021 highs above $100 [1][4]. The $174.8M cash runway to 2028 means dilutive financing will be needed before any product reaches market, and the $326M market cap provides limited equity cushion. Clinical risk is acute — FT819's registrational trial in SLE could fail, and the 20-patient autoimmune dataset from earlier dosing is too small for definitive conclusions [1]. Glassdoor reviews describing 'toxic culture' with a 3.0/5 rating based on 122 reviews suggest organizational dysfunction that could impair execution during the critical clinical development phase [2]. Competition from autologous CAR-T (BMS Breyanzi, Gilead Yescarta) and other allogeneic platforms (Allogene, CRISPR) is intensifying in the autoimmune space. ROE of -52.4% and zero profit margin with no path to profitability before 2030+ make this a speculative option on clinical success.
Key Catalysts
  1. FT819 Phase 2 potentially registrational trial in refractory SLE with lupus nephritis commencing patient dosing in H2 2026 under RMAT designation, with potential to accelerate approval path [1]
  2. FT839 IND cleared by FDA for Phase 1 autoimmune basket trial, adding a multi-antigen dual-CAR T-cell approach to the autoimmune pipeline and broadening the platform's clinical validation [1]
  3. Autoimmune cell therapy market emerging as multi-billion-dollar opportunity with Fate's off-the-shelf iPSC approach offering manufacturing scalability and cost advantages over patient-specific autologous CAR-T [2]
Key Risks
  1. Cash runway to 2028 with $31M quarterly burn rate makes dilutive financing inevitable before any product commercialization, potentially devastating the $326M market cap [1]
  2. Clinical-stage binary risk — FT819 registrational trial failure in SLE would be catastrophic given the concentrated pipeline and small prior autoimmune dataset of ~20 patients [1]
  3. Glassdoor rating of 3.0/5 with 'toxic culture' reviews and 122 negative employee assessments create execution risk during the critical phase of registrational trial design and enrollment [2]
