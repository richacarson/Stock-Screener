Investment Thesis
First BanCorp delivered Q2 2026 net income of $96.1M (+24% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.62 beating consensus by 15%, driven by all-time high pre-tax pre-provision income of $138M, 21% YoY loan origination growth, and $274M in deposit expansion [1]. The balance sheet is conservatively managed with ROE of 19.5% and a 39% net margin, while the stock trades at just 10.8x forward earnings — a significant discount to mainland U.S. regional banks [4]. The dividend yields 2.3% with a low 27% payout ratio and ~15% five-year dividend growth CAGR, creating a compelling income-plus-growth profile. Puerto Rico's economic recovery, driven by federal disaster funds, Opportunity Zone investments, and population stabilization, provides a multi-year tailwind for FBP's dominant local franchise.
The bear case centers on geographic concentration and macro sensitivity. First BanCorp derives virtually all revenue from Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and the British Virgin Islands — economies that remain vulnerable to hurricanes, fiscal instability, and population decline [3]. Puerto Rico's government debt restructuring under PROMESA may limit public sector borrowing that could otherwise drive loan growth. At 11.6x trailing earnings, the stock's discount to mainland banks reflects legitimate sovereign and catastrophe risk premiums. Loan growth guidance of only 3-5% for 2026 contrasts with the strong Q2 origination pace, suggesting management caution about sustainability [1]. The bank has minimal technology differentiation, no AI strategy, and faces competitive pressure from larger institutions with greater digital capabilities. The Innovation score of 2 reflects a company executing well on a traditional playbook without building for the next era of banking.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued Puerto Rico economic recovery driven by $60B+ in unspent federal disaster reconstruction funds flowing through the local economy, directly supporting commercial lending and deposit growth for FBP's dominant local franchise [1]
  2. Aggressive dividend growth trajectory — 15% five-year CAGR from a low 27% payout ratio leaves substantial room for continued dividend increases as earnings grow, potentially driving yield-oriented investor interest [4]
  3. Pre-tax pre-provision income reaching all-time highs with 21% loan origination growth suggesting FBP is gaining meaningful market share against larger competitors in Puerto Rico's recovering economy [1]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme geographic concentration in Puerto Rico and the Caribbean — a single major hurricane or fiscal shock could simultaneously impair the loan portfolio, disrupt operations, and reduce the deposit base with no geographic diversification buffer [3]
  2. Puerto Rico's structural demographic headwinds — population declined from 3.7M (2010) to ~3.2M (2026), reducing the addressable market for consumer and mortgage lending even as commercial activity recovers [3]
  3. Competitive pressure from technology-enabled national banks and fintech challengers — FBP has no disclosed digital banking innovation or AI strategy, and larger competitors' technology investments may erode the relationship-banking advantages that support FBP's market share [2]
