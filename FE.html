Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYQ ·United States
FirstEnergy is a regulated electric utility operating distribution, integrated, and stand-alone transmission segments across Ohio, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, West Virginia, Maryland, and New York. The company operates 252,959 distribution line miles and 24,157 transmission line miles, serving approximately 6 million customers. FirstEnergy reported H1 2026 GAAP earnings of $693M ($1.20/share) on $7.9B revenue, reaffirmed 2026 Core EPS guidance of $2.62-$2.82, and has contracted 6.4 GW of data center demand. Founded in 1996 and headquartered in Akron, Ohio.

Investment Thesis
FirstEnergy at $45.72 with a $26.5B market cap is a regulated utility in recovery mode, rebuilding after the HB 6 bribery scandal while positioning for data-center-driven demand growth — H1 2026 GAAP earnings reached $693M ($1.20/share) on $7.9B revenue, with Core EPS guidance reaffirmed at $2.62-$2.82 for 2026 [1]. The $36B Energize365 capital program implies 10% annual rate base growth through 2030, and 6.4 GW of contracted data center demand (WV up 137% to 4.3 GW) provides a secular growth vector rare among regulated utilities. Forward P/E of 15.5x with a 4.07% dividend yield offers attractive income for a utility expected to deliver 6-8% Core EPS growth through 2030, and the transmission segment's 22% rate base growth in Q2 demonstrates the capital deployment thesis is working [1].
The bear case centers on financial fragility, governance risk, and stretched dividend sustainability. D/E of 200.9% with $29B total debt against only $63M cash leaves minimal balance sheet flexibility, and the 96.3% payout ratio means virtually every dollar of earnings goes to dividends, leaving no cushion for earnings misses [1]. The bribery scandal — resulting in $230M in criminal fines, fired executives, and a deferred prosecution agreement — represents a governance failure that took years to remediate and continues to shadow the company through ESG-focused investor divestments like Pictet's exit [3]. Negative free cash flow of -$2.0B in the TTM period reflects the massive capital investment program that requires continued access to debt markets. The company's 6.9% profit margin ranks among the lowest of regulated utilities, and the concentration of data center demand in West Virginia (4.3 GW of 6.4 GW total) creates geographic and customer concentration risk [1][2].
