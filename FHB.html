Company Profile
Financials ·Regional Banks ·NASDAQ ·United States
First Hawaiian, Inc. is the bank holding company for First Hawaiian Bank, the largest financial institution in Hawaii with $23.6 billion in total assets. The bank provides a full range of retail and commercial banking services through approximately 44 branches across Hawaii, Guam, and Saipan. Founded in 1858, First Hawaiian is the oldest and largest bank in the state of Hawaii, with 168 years of continuous community banking operations. Formerly a subsidiary of BNP Paribas, became fully independent in 2019. Headquartered in Honolulu, Hawaii.

Investment Thesis
First Hawaiian Bank delivered a strong Q2 2026 with $73.4M net income ($0.60 EPS), beating consensus by 2.9%, on revenue of $231.3M that exceeded estimates by 1.7% [1]. Net interest margin expanded to 3.25% with the efficiency ratio improving to 56.2%, demonstrating operating leverage in a stabilizing rate environment. The bank's fortress balance sheet — CET1 of 13.27%, D/E of just 0.65%, and $20.2B deposit base — supports a 3.47% dividend yield with a sustainable 43.3% payout ratio. At 12.3x forward P/E with 6.3% revenue growth, FHB offers compelling relative value among community banks. The 168-year franchise in Hawaii provides a natural geographic moat with limited competitive entry points [5].
First Hawaiian's strengths are also its limitations. The Hawaiian market is small and mature, constraining the bank's growth ceiling — 6.3% revenue growth in Q2 represents a cyclical NIM expansion rather than structural growth acceleration [1]. ROE of 10.26% trails the 15% target and lags well-managed peer banks, reflecting conservative lending practices and the lower-growth island economy. Innovation is minimal — digital banking and AI initiatives are in early stages with no quantified business impact [2]. The bank carries concentration risk in Hawaii's tourism-dependent economy, exposure to natural disaster risk (volcanic activity, hurricanes), and secular headwinds from digital banking adoption that could erode the branch-based model over time. A score of 53 reflects solid but unremarkable fundamentals with limited upside catalysts.

  2. ROE of 10.26% trails well-managed peer banks, and the conservative Hawaiian market constrains growth potential — the stock may remain a 'value trap' at perpetual discount multiples
  3. Natural disaster risk (volcanic activity, hurricanes, wildfires) represents an unhedgeable concentration risk for the bank's $14.6B loan portfolio, heavily weighted to Hawaiian real estate
