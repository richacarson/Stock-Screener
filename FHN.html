Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·United States
First Horizon Corporation is a Memphis, Tennessee-based regional bank holding company with approximately $83B in assets, operating under the First Horizon Bank brand across the Southeast and beyond. Q2 2026 revenue reached $887M (+7% YoY) with net income available to common shareholders of $260M (+12% YoY) and diluted EPS of $0.54 (+20% YoY), supported by a net interest margin of 3.49% and strong loan growth [1]. The company has been led by Chairman, President, and CEO D. Bryan Jordan since 2008, and in 2023 saw its proposed $13.4B acquisition by TD Bank collapse due to TD's own AML remediation issues, resulting in a $200M breakup fee and a return to an independent growth strategy [2].

Investment Thesis
First Horizon is a well-capitalized $83B-asset regional bank delivering accelerating profitability — Q2 2026 revenue of $887M (+7% YoY), net income of $260M (+12% YoY), and diluted EPS growth of 20% YoY — backed by a stable 3.49% net interest margin and strong loan growth [1]. The stock trades at an undemanding 11.34x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.97, a 2.76% dividend yield, and a conservative 32.68% payout ratio, leaving ample room for continued dividend growth after the company resumed raising its payout in 2026 (+13.3% to $0.68/share) following a flat 2021-2025 stretch [1][4][5]. CEO Bryan Jordan's nearly two-decade tenure through the 2008 financial crisis and the 2023 TD Bank merger collapse provides unusual leadership continuity for a regional bank, and the $200M TD breakup fee strengthened the balance sheet for an independent growth strategy [2].
The bear case centers on modest structural growth and integration/regulatory scar tissue. Return on equity of 11.45% remains below the 15% target for a top-tier bank, and 7% revenue growth, while solid, is not exceptional for the sector [4]. The company's dividend was held flat for five consecutive years (2021-2025) before the 2026 increase, producing a weak 5-year dividend growth CAGR of roughly 2.5% — below the 6% (2x inflation) target — suggesting capital allocation discipline has only recently shifted back toward shareholder returns after the TD Bank uncertainty [5]. The failed TD Bank acquisition (which would have delivered a large premium to shareholders) and past SEC Regulation Best Interest violations at its broker-dealer subsidiary ($325K fine) and individual employee misconduct cases (Fed-enforced prohibition orders for embezzlement) point to residual execution and compliance risk that could resurface [2][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued net interest margin expansion (3.49% in Q2 2026, up 9bps YoY) alongside strong loan growth could sustain the current double-digit EPS growth trajectory [1]
  2. Further dividend increases following the 2026 resumption of payout growth (+13.3% to $0.68/share) would improve the currently modest 5-year dividend growth CAGR and attract income-focused capital [5]
  3. Capital deployment of the retained $200M TD Bank breakup fee into organic growth, buybacks, or bolt-on M&A could accelerate the independent growth strategy [2]
Key Risks
  1. Return on equity of 11.45% trails the 15% target, and a 7% revenue growth rate is solid but unremarkable, limiting the case for premium valuation multiples versus higher-growth regional bank peers [4]
  2. A flat dividend from 2021-2025 followed by only one year of renewed growth leaves the 5-year dividend growth CAGR (~2.5%) well below the 6% inflation-adjusted target, a risk factor for dividend-growth-oriented investors [5]
  3. Past regulatory findings — including a $325K SEC Regulation Best Interest fine at its broker-dealer subsidiary and Federal Reserve enforcement actions against former employees for embezzlement and data misappropriation — highlight ongoing compliance and operational risk in a heavily regulated industry [6]
