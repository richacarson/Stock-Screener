Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Apparel Manufacturing ·NYSE ·United States
FIGS, Inc. is a digitally native, direct-to-consumer healthcare apparel and lifestyle brand founded in 2013 by Heather Hasson and Trina Spear. The company designs and sells technically advanced scrubwear, lab coats, outerwear, underscrubs, and lifestyle apparel for healthcare professionals. FIGS reported Q2 2026 revenue of $196.6M (+29% YoY) — its third consecutive quarter exceeding 25% growth — with net income of $28.4M (14.4% net margin) and record quarterly scrubwear sales of $161.2M [1]. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to ~20% revenue growth (from 14-16%) with adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.8-15.0% (from 13.0-13.2%) and authorized a $200M share repurchase program [2]. Market cap approximately $2.5B.

Investment Thesis
FIGS is executing a powerful growth acceleration out of its 2023-2024 trough, with Q2 2026 revenue surging 29% YoY to $196.6M — its third consecutive quarter exceeding 25% growth — while net income quadrupled to $28.4M (14.4% net margin vs. 4.7% prior year) and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 18.6% [1]. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to ~20% revenue growth and 14.8-15.0% adjusted EBITDA margin, demonstrating that the DTC healthcare apparel model can generate both rapid growth and meaningful profitability simultaneously [2]. The balance sheet is pristine with D/E of 14% and no significant financial debt, and the $200M share repurchase authorization signals management confidence. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~15% with accelerating growth and margin expansion suggests FIGS is inflecting toward a higher-profitability operating model. The Inspire Impact Score of 17 (MIXED) with no negative attributions makes FIGS accessible to faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on valuation and structural risk. At 42.23x forward earnings and 55.64x trailing P/E, FIGS is priced as a premium growth company but operates in the competitive apparel industry with historically thin margins [4]. The stock has surged 157% over the past 52 weeks, pricing in significant future growth. TTM profit margin of ~3.9% — despite Q2's impressive 14.4% — remains well below the 20% IOWN target and raises questions about margin sustainability through seasonal fluctuations. Forward PEG of ~2.0 is reasonable but not cheap. The DTC-only distribution model limits addressable market penetration compared to omnichannel competitors and creates vulnerability to rising digital customer acquisition costs. AI-powered competitors could enter premium healthcare apparel more easily, and FIGS' success could attract well-capitalized incumbents (Nike Medical, lululemon Healthcare) to enter the category. The company is too young (13 years) and has not navigated a leadership transition or major crisis, leaving the organizational resilience untested.
