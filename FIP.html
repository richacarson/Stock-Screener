Company Profile
Industrials ·Infrastructure Operations ·NASDAQ ·United States
FTAI Infrastructure Inc. develops, acquires, owns, and operates transportation and energy infrastructure businesses across North America. Key assets include Jefferson Terminal (crude oil storage and processing in Beaumont, Texas), Transtar Railroad (short-line railroad serving the Pittsburgh industrial corridor), Repauno (deep-water port and LNG facility in New Jersey), and Power and Gas operations including the Long Ridge Energy Terminal (pending sale). Spun off from FTAI Aviation Ltd. in August 2022, externally managed by Fortress Investment Group. Market cap approximately $333M with $2.89B total debt.

Investment Thesis
FTAI Infrastructure offers a deep-value bet on physical infrastructure assets at distressed pricing — $333M market cap for operations generating $76.1M adjusted EBITDA in Q2 2026, including the Transtar Railroad (record quarterly revenues), Jefferson Terminal (crude processing hub with $50M+ incremental annual EBITDA from new contracts), and the Repauno LNG facility (Phase Two targeting early 2027) [1]. Revenue grew 52.7% YoY to $186.8M in Q2 2026. The Long Ridge sale should eliminate $1.46B in combined debt, potentially unlocking significant equity value and reducing interest expense substantially. The 4.25% dividend yield at current prices provides some income while waiting for the deleveraging thesis to play out. The Inspire Impact Score of 42 (ALIGNED) reflects positive attributes in business resilience and energy management.
The bear case is severe and structural. FIP has negative book value (-$2.79/share), D/E of 453%, $2.89B in debt, and interest expense that often exceeds adjusted EBITDA [1][4]. The company lost $321M in H1 2026 (vs. $24.4M profit in H1 2025), driven partly by $63.2M asset impairments and $9.6M non-cash warrant losses, but operational losses are also substantial. The Long Ridge sale, while necessary for survival, eliminates a high-EBITDA asset — the company is selling its best business to service debt on weaker ones. The stock has collapsed 58% from $6.69 to $2.82 and carries 26.8% short interest, reflecting market skepticism about the capital structure's viability [4]. The external management structure by Fortress Investment Group creates governance risk: management fees are based on assets, not returns, incentivizing asset accumulation and leverage over shareholder value [2]. The $0.03/quarter dividend ($0.12/year) is not covered by earnings or operating cash flow and represents cash the company cannot afford to distribute.
