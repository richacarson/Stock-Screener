Company Profile
Industrials ·Infrastructure Operations ·NASDAQ ·United States
FTAI Infrastructure Inc. develops, acquires, owns, and operates transportation and energy infrastructure businesses across North America. Key assets include Jefferson Terminal (crude oil storage and processing in Beaumont, Texas), Transtar Railroad (short-line railroad serving the Pittsburgh industrial corridor), Repauno (LNG and industrial gas facility in New Jersey), and Power and Gas operations including the Long Ridge Energy Terminal. Spun off from FTAI Aviation Ltd. in August 2022, externally managed by Fortress Investment Group.

Investment Thesis
FTAI Infrastructure offers a deep-value bet on physical infrastructure assets at distressed pricing — $504M market cap for $5.75B in total assets including the Transtar Railroad (record $42.4M quarterly EBITDA), Jefferson Terminal (crude processing hub with $50M+ incremental annual EBITDA from new contracts), and the Repauno LNG facility (Phase Two targeting early 2027) [1][2]. Revenue grew 72.3% YoY to $659M TTM, and four-segment adjusted EBITDA reached $83M in Q2 2026. The Long Ridge sale should eliminate $1.46B in combined debt, potentially unlocking $300-500M in equity value and reducing interest expense by $40M+ annually. At $4.27/share with analyst targets of $9.94 (+133% upside), FIP offers asymmetric upside if deleveraging succeeds and Jefferson Terminal crude volumes ramp as projected.
The bear case is severe and structural. FIP has negative stockholders' equity of -$518.7M, $2.76B in debt, and interest expense of $105.5M per quarter that consumed more than the $76.1M adjusted EBITDA in Q2 2026 [1]. The company lost $603.5M TTM including $63.2M in asset impairments, and H1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $30.3M — the company is burning cash, not generating it. The Long Ridge sale, while necessary for survival, eliminates the Power and Gas segment ($27.4M Q2 EBITDA) — the company is selling its best asset to service debt on weaker assets. The external management structure by Fortress Investment Group creates governance risk: management fees are based on assets, not returns, incentivizing asset accumulation and leverage over shareholder value [3]. The $0.03/quarter dividend ($0.12/year) is nominal but unsustainable at current loss levels.
Key Catalysts
  1. Long Ridge Energy Terminal sale closing would eliminate $1.16B in debt and repay approximately $300M additional obligations, potentially transforming the balance sheet from distressed to manageable
  2. Jefferson Terminal crude volume ramp from new contracts expected to add over $50M in annual incremental EBITDA, driven by the completed SSP bi-directional pipeline enabling Gulf Coast crude access
  3. Repauno Phase Two LNG facility targeting early 2027 operations commencement could add a significant new revenue stream to diversify beyond railroad and terminal operations
Key Risks
  1. Negative equity of -$518.7M and $2.76B in debt with $105.5M quarterly interest expense create existential leverage risk — if the Long Ridge sale fails to close, the company faces potential covenant violations or restructuring
  2. External management by Fortress Investment Group creates structural conflicts — management fees incentivize asset accumulation rather than shareholder returns, and the 3.3-year average management tenure provides limited accountability track record
  3. Quarterly $0.03/share dividend is not covered by earnings or operating cash flow (negative $30.3M in H1 2026), and continued payment represents a choice to distribute cash the company cannot afford to lose
