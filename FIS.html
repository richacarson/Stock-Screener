Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
FIS provides core banking, payments, and capital-markets technology to more than 20,000 financial-institution clients worldwide, processing over $75B in transactions annually. In 2026, the company completed a major portfolio restructuring: acquiring Global Payments' Issuer Solutions business (formerly TSYS) for $13.5B enterprise value while selling its remaining 45% Worldpay stake, refocusing around banking software and card-issuer processing. Under CEO Stephanie Ferris (since 2022), FIS was recognized among Fast Company's Best Workplaces for Innovators for the third time in 2025 and is actively deploying generative AI across its banking platform. Q1 2026 revenue rose 30% YoY to $3.3B and Q2 2026 reached $3.38B (+29.1%), with full-year 2026 guidance of $13.6-13.7B adjusted revenue and pro forma organic growth of 4.5-5.0%.

Investment Thesis
FIS at $36 trades at a deeply discounted 5.4x forward P/E and 0.19 PEG despite strong profitability (22.3% ROE, 27.6% profit margin) and a transformed strategic footprint following the $13.5B TSYS acquisition and full Worldpay exit, repositioning FIS around higher-margin, recurring banking-technology and card-issuer revenue [1][2]. The company is actively monetizing AI through an industry-first agentic AI transaction platform for banks and an AI-powered insurance risk suite, positioning FIS as an AI enabler to 20,000+ financial-institution clients rather than a disruption target [3][4]. The 4.89% dividend yield — backed by a conservative 25.8% payout ratio with room for continued growth after the 2024 reset — offers compelling income alongside the re-rating opportunity from portfolio simplification. FIS was recognized as a Fast Company Best Workplace for Innovators for the third consecutive year in 2025, and FY2026 guidance calls for 32-34% adjusted EBITDA growth [1].
The bear case centers on integration risk, capital-allocation credibility, and slowing organic growth. The 2024 dividend cut (from $0.52 to $0.36/q) showed FIS is willing to disrupt its income-investor base for balance-sheet flexibility, and D/E of 132.5% reflects acquisition leverage. FIS has operated without a permanent CTO since March 2026 — a notable gap at a company whose value proposition depends on technology execution during a major systems integration [5]. The stock's 48% decline from its 52-week high of $68.98 suggests the market questions the strategic repositioning's success. Pro forma organic revenue growth of only 4.5-5.0% — versus the reported 29.1% headline — reveals that the acquisition accounts for nearly all the growth, and organic momentum has actually decelerated from the earlier 5.1-5.7% guidance [1]. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.15-6.24 trails consensus of $6.28, and the TSYS integration adds execution complexity atop the Worldpay divestiture.
