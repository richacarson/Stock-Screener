Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
FIS provides core banking, payments, and capital-markets technology to more than 20,000 financial-institution clients worldwide, processing over $75B in transactions annually. In 2026 the company completed a major portfolio restructuring: it acquired Global Payments' Issuer Solutions business (formerly TSYS) for an enterprise value of $13.5B while selling its remaining 45% stake in Worldpay, refocusing the company around banking software and card-issuer processing rather than merchant acquiring. Q2 2026 revenue rose 29.1% YoY to $3.38B (boosted by the TSYS acquisition), and FY2026 guidance calls for $13.6-13.7B in revenue [1][2].

Investment Thesis
FIS trades at a deeply discounted 6.04x forward P/E and 0.24 PEG despite double-digit profitability metrics (22.3% ROE, 27.6% profit margin) and a transformed strategic footprint following the $13.5B TSYS acquisition and full Worldpay exit, which together reposition FIS around higher-margin, recurring banking-technology and card-issuer revenue [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 29.1% YoY to $3.38B, and the company is actively monetizing AI through an industry-first agentic AI transaction platform for banks and an AI-powered insurance risk suite, positioning FIS as an AI enabler to 20,000+ financial-institution clients rather than an AI disruption target [3][4]. The 4.25% dividend yield, backed by a conservative 25.8% payout ratio, offers meaningful income alongside the re-rating opportunity from the portfolio simplification.
The bear case centers on integration risk and capital-allocation credibility. The 2024 dividend cut (from $0.52 to $0.36 per quarter) tied to the Worldpay stake monetization shows FIS has been willing to disrupt its own income-investor base for balance-sheet flexibility, and debt-to-equity of 132.5% reflects the leverage taken on to fund the TSYS deal. FIS has operated without a permanent Chief Technology Officer since March 2026, a notable gap at a company whose entire value proposition depends on technology execution during a major systems-integration effort [5]. The pre-TSYS organic 3-year revenue CAGR of just 3.17% (2022-2025) suggests the reported 29.1% growth rate is heavily M&A-driven and may normalize once the TSYS acquisition annualizes, and net income of $382M against $10.7B of 2025 revenue reflects thinner bottom-line conversion than the margin metrics alone suggest.
