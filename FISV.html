Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
Fiserv, Inc. provides financial technology solutions to merchants, banks, and financial institutions worldwide through two segments: Merchant Solutions (Clover POS, payment processing) and Financial Solutions (core banking, digital banking, risk management). Following the 2019 First Data acquisition, the company generated approximately $19B in trailing twelve-month revenue with ~16% profit margins, though 2026 has seen revenue declines of 3-4% and adjusted EPS falling 16-26% amid operational challenges. CEO Mike Lyons departed in September 2026 after 13 months, replaced by Takis Georgakopoulos.

Investment Thesis
Fiserv at $45.94 with a forward P/E of 6.93x and PEG under 1.0 is a deep value opportunity in critical financial infrastructure. The company processes payments and provides core banking technology for thousands of financial institutions with multi-year integration cycles that create enormous switching costs [1]. New CEO Takis Georgakopoulos brings three decades of payments, technology, and AI experience and has already driven progress on modernizing the Clover merchant platform [3]. The medium-term outlook targeting 4-6% annual adjusted revenue growth through 2029 with 37%+ operating margins, combined with a ~$24B market cap for a company generating $19B+ in revenue, suggests significant upside if operational execution stabilizes [4].
The bear case centers on deepening operational deterioration and leadership instability. Q2 2026 revenue declined 4% YoY with adjusted EPS down 26% and GAAP EPS down 37%, driven by a 6% organic decline in the Financial Solutions segment [1][2]. Full-year 2026 guidance was slashed from $8.00-$8.30 to $7.20-$7.40 adjusted EPS, with organic revenue now expected to decline 0-1%. CEO Mike Lyons departed after just 13 months, making Georgakopoulos the third CEO in recent years — a pattern that suggests structural governance problems rather than execution issues [3]. Argentina macro volatility, delayed client implementations, and 'slower execution on growth initiatives' may reflect deeper competitive disadvantages against tech-native payment platforms like Stripe and Adyen. The 70%+ stock decline from 2025 highs has created deep value, but value traps exist when operational trends are deteriorating and management is unstable.
Key Catalysts
  1. Deep value at 6.93x forward earnings with PEG under 1.0 — significant upside if new CEO Takis Georgakopoulos stabilizes operations and executes the 'One Fiserv' turnaround plan [3]

  3. Medium-term outlook targets 4-6% adjusted revenue growth 2026-2029 with operating margins exceeding 37% by 2029, implying substantial earnings recovery from current depressed levels [4]
