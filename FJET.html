Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE American ·United States
Starfighters Space operates a privately owned fleet of flight-ready Lockheed F-104 supersonic jets from NASA's Kennedy Space Center, positioned as the only commercial Mach 2+ fleet, for pilot and astronaut training, hypersonic flight testing and future air-launch services [2][4]. The company raised about $40M in a Regulation A IPO at $3.59 per share and began trading on NYSE American on December 18, 2025 [3]. Founder Rick Svetkoff resigned as CEO and Chairman in February 2026 amid a dispute with the board, and Tim Franta, who led development of the STARLAUNCH air-launch system, became CEO [2][3]. The company has reported no revenue in 2025-2026, a Q2 2026 net loss of $11.5M, and a going-concern warning [1][3].

Investment Thesis
The bull case for Starfighters is optionality on a unique asset. It operates what it describes as the world's only commercial Mach 2+ fleet, based at Kennedy Space Center, as demand for in-flight hypersonic testing grows and defense programs move testing from wind tunnels to the sky [2]. The Blackstar Orbital SpaceDrone captive-carry partnership and the STARLAUNCH suborbital and orbital roadmap offer paths to revenue [2]. At June 30, 2026 the balance sheet held $23.2M of cash and short-term investments against only $464K of debt, giving the new CEO time to win first contracts, and the ~$45M market cap is near that cash level [1][4].
The bear case dominates. The company has reported $0 revenue, losses are accelerating (Q2 2026 net loss of $11.5M versus $4.3M in Q1), and filings carry a going-concern warning that implies further dilutive equity raises [1][3]. The stock has collapsed from a 52-week high of $31.50 and the $3.59 IPO price to $0.82, and remains near the $1M/day liquidity floor [4]. The February 2026 founder exit, reported misappropriation findings, material weaknesses in controls, and multiple shareholder investigations create serious governance and legal risk, and continued trading below $1 risks NYSE American listing-compliance issues [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. First revenue-generating hypersonic test contracts, such as Blackstar Orbital SpaceDrone captive-carry flights, proving the F-104 testbed can generate revenue [2]
  2. A dated STARLAUNCH 1 suborbital flight demonstration, giving the air-launch roadmap a concrete milestone [2]
  3. Resolution of the governance dispute and remediation of internal-control weaknesses under CEO Tim Franta, reducing legal overhang [3]
Key Risks
  1. Going-concern and dilution risk — $0 revenue and Q2 2026 burn of ~$6.3M in operating expenses against $23.2M of cash and investments means more equity raises are likely [1][3]
  2. Governance and litigation — the founder's resignation dispute, reported $1.9M misappropriation finding, material weaknesses and multiple law-firm investigations could produce lawsuits, restatements or penalties [3]
  3. Liquidity and listing risk — the stock trades at ~$0.82 with ~$1.0M of daily dollar volume, at the IOWN liquidity floor and below the $1 level that triggers exchange deficiency notices [4]
