Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Manufacturing Services ·NASDAQ ·Singapore
Flex Ltd. provides technology innovation, supply chain, and advanced manufacturing solutions to data center, communications, enterprise, consumer, automotive, healthcare, industrial, and power industries globally. The company operates through three segments: Integrated Technology Solutions (ITS), Regulated Manufacturing Solutions (RMS), and Cloud and Power Infrastructure (CPI). In May 2026, Flex announced the planned spin-off of CPI as an independent publicly traded company ('SpinCo') in Q1 CY2027, with CEO Revathi Advaithi moving to lead SpinCo and targeting 65-75% CPI revenue growth in FY2027. FY2026 revenue of $27.91B (+8% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $3.30 (+25%). Headquartered in Singapore with operations in 30+ countries.

Investment Thesis
Flex delivered a record FY2026 with $27.91B revenue (up 8.14% YoY), adjusted EPS of $3.30 (up 25%), and adjusted operating margins expanding 70bps to 6.3% — the sixth consecutive year of double-digit adjusted EPS growth [1]. The standout story is the planned CPI spin-off in Q1 CY2027, with CEO Revathi Advaithi moving to lead the standalone AI infrastructure company targeting 65-75% revenue growth in FY2027, accelerating to 80%+ in FY2028 [3][8]. A multi-year contract with Google and hyperscaler data center expansion underpin the CPI growth trajectory. The stock has corrected 28% from its $161+ peak to $115.76, bringing the forward PEG to 0.66 — well below the market average — and creating a more attractive entry point [7]. The CPI segment meets 80% of data center requirements for large-scale customers and contributes over 25% of total earnings, directly benefiting from the $200B+ annual AI capex cycle [2].
The primary concern is execution risk around the CPI spin-off and CEO transition. Advaithi's move to SpinCo leaves Flex's remaining ITS and RMS businesses without their transformative CEO during a period of strategic separation [3][8]. At 58.7x trailing P/E and 30.1x forward P/E (even after the 28% correction), the stock prices in aggressive growth expectations, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of only 2.9% reflects the reality that the core manufacturing business has been stagnant. Profit margin of 3.15% is structurally thin, meaning any revenue shortfall or cost overrun directly impacts the bottom line. Heavy insider selling ($82.9M over 12 months with zero purchases) — including CEO Advaithi's $16.2M sale in May 2026 — signals that leadership viewed peak prices as fully valued [6]. Customer concentration with hyperscale data center operators creates revenue risk, and potential insourcing represents a longer-term threat.
