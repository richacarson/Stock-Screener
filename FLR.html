Company Profile
Industrials ·Engineering & Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Fluor Corporation provides engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) services through three segments: Urban Solutions (advanced technologies, life sciences, mining, infrastructure), Energy Solutions (oil and gas, LNG, nuclear, energy transition), and Mission Solutions (U.S. government, nuclear remediation, defense). The company operates globally with a $25.7B backlog and was founded in 1912, headquartered in Irving, Texas.

Investment Thesis
Fluor is a top-tier global EPC firm with a $25.7B backlog (82% reimbursable) positioned at the intersection of data center construction, LNG expansion, nuclear energy revival, energy transition, and defense modernization [1]. The balance sheet is exceptionally strong with $2.27B cash against $1.07B debt (D/E just 18%), and the $1.4B buyback program targets a 14% share reduction in 2026 [1][5]. At 15.6x forward P/E on consensus forward EPS of ~$3.25, the stock is attractively valued for a company with improving earnings trajectory and AI-driven infrastructure tailwinds. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $110M was the strongest Q1 in nine years, and management expects the 2026 book-to-burn ratio to exceed 1.0 as significant new awards materialize across nuclear power, data centers, mining, and uranium enrichment [1][2].
The bear case centers on profitability challenges, execution risk, and cultural concerns. Q1 2026 was operationally weak — consolidated segment profit of only $8M (vs $131M prior year), adjusted EBITDA of $61M (vs $155M), and EBITDA guidance trimmed to $525-560M — reflecting a $37M mining project cost increase and $96M Mission Solutions litigation impact [1]. Revenue declined 8% YoY despite the massive backlog. FY2025 was a GAAP net loss (-$51M) with negative profit margin (-2.1%) and negative ROE (-1.7%), meaning the turnaround to sustainable profitability remains incomplete [5]. Glassdoor reviews describe 'toxic, abusive behavior from middle management' and a 'high school culture,' raising talent retention concerns for a business that depends on specialized engineering talent [3]. The CEO transition (Jim Breuer, May 2025) introduces leadership uncertainty during execution-critical backlog conversion. EPC project execution inherently carries risk — the 18% of backlog that is non-reimbursable could generate additional cost overruns similar to the Q1 mining project charge [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Backlog conversion — $25.7B backlog with book-to-burn ratio expected to exceed 1.0 in 2026, with significant new awards across nuclear power, data centers, mining, and uranium enrichment providing a diversified growth pipeline [1][2]
