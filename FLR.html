Company Profile
Industrials ·Engineering & Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Fluor Corporation provides engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) services through three segments: Urban Solutions (advanced technologies, life sciences, mining, infrastructure), Energy Solutions (oil and gas, LNG, nuclear, energy transition), and Mission Solutions (U.S. government, nuclear remediation, defense). The company operates globally with a $26.9B backlog, was founded in 1912, and is headquartered in Irving, Texas. CEO Jim Breuer took over from David Constable (now Executive Chairman) in May 2025. Q2 2026 revenue was $4.3B (+9% YoY) with $6.1B in new awards and adjusted EBITDA of $149M.

Investment Thesis
Fluor is a top-tier global EPC firm with a $26.9B backlog positioned at the intersection of data center construction, nuclear energy revival, LNG expansion, energy transition, and defense modernization [1][2]. Q2 2026 demonstrated a sharp recovery from Q1 weakness — revenue of $4.3B (+9% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $149M (vs $96M prior year), and $6.1B in new awards drove backlog expansion with management guiding to a full-year book-to-bill well above 1.0 [2]. The balance sheet is exceptionally strong with D/E of 18%, net cash position, and a $1.4B buyback program targeting 14% share reduction. At 17.5x forward P/E on consensus $2.75 EPS, the stock is attractively valued for a company with improving earnings trajectory, AI-driven infrastructure tailwinds (data center and power generation construction), and 6+ quarters of revenue visibility from the backlog [6].
The bear case centers on profitability volatility, thin margins, and cultural concerns. Despite Q2's recovery, FY2025 was a GAAP net loss (-$51M) and Q1 2026 generated only $8M in segment profit — demonstrating the quarterly volatility inherent in project-based businesses where a single cost overrun or litigation charge can wipe out a quarter's earnings [1]. EBITDA guidance was adjusted to $500-525M (from the previous $525-560M range). The 2.6% profit margin in Q2 2026 is structurally thin — this is a high-revenue, low-margin business where project execution risk is omnipresent, particularly on the 18% non-reimbursable backlog [2]. Glassdoor reviews describe 'toxic, abusive behavior from middle management' and a culture of disrespect, threatening talent retention for a business dependent on specialized engineering expertise [3]. Net insider selling ($1.5M+ in recent months, zero purchases) signals limited insider conviction. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 3.1% means the company has not yet demonstrated sustained top-line growth despite the massive backlog [6].
