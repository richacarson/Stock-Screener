Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Flowserve Corporation is a leading provider of flow control products and services for the global infrastructure markets, manufacturing pumps, valves, seals, and related aftermarket parts and services. The company serves process industries including oil & gas, chemical, power generation, water management, and general industries. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.95 beat consensus of $0.86 with revenue of $1.17B, marking 14 consecutive quarters of margin expansion. FY2026 guidance targets adjusted EPS of $4.05-$4.20 with organic sales growth of ~-1% and total sales growth of ~+3% including the Mogas Industries acquisition. TTM revenue is approximately $4.63B.

Investment Thesis
Flowserve delivered Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.95 beating consensus by 10%, marking 14 consecutive quarters of margin expansion under CEO Rowe's operational transformation [1]. FY2026 guidance of $4.05-$4.20 adjusted EPS implies double-digit earnings growth, and at 15.9x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.66, the stock is attractively valued for an industrial company with improving profitability [4]. The $1.3B Mogas acquisition strengthens the severe service valve portfolio, and secular tailwinds from AI-driven data center buildout (cooling and water management), energy transition (hydrogen, carbon capture, nuclear), and global infrastructure investment provide multi-year demand visibility. The balance sheet is manageable with D/E of 99.6% post-acquisition, and TTM revenue of $4.63B provides substantial scale in the flow control market.
The bear case centers on organic growth challenges and execution risk. Organic revenue growth is approximately -1% for 2026, masked by acquisitive growth, suggesting the core business faces headwinds from Middle East conflict (12-13% of revenue), sluggish global industrial capex, and project timing delays [1]. Profit margin of 8.0% remains structurally low for an industrial company despite 14 quarters of improvement, limiting earnings power relative to revenue scale [4]. D/E of 99.6% is elevated post-Mogas and constrains financial flexibility for future acquisitions or share repurchases. The Mogas integration carries execution risk, and Glassdoor reviews suggest organizational culture challenges that could impede the transformation. Innovation scored only 4/10 — Flowserve is optimizing a mature business rather than building differentiated technology, and competitors like Emerson are investing more aggressively in digital and AI capabilities.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-driven data center buildout creating sustained demand for cooling systems, pumps, and water management infrastructure — analysts specifically cite Flowserve as a beneficiary of the AI electricity demand surge [2]
  2. Continued margin expansion trajectory: 14 consecutive quarters of improvement with FY2026 guidance implying double-digit EPS growth, and the Mogas acquisition adding higher-margin severe service valve revenue [1]
  3. Energy transition secular tailwind — Flowserve's pivot toward hydrogen, carbon capture, nuclear, and clean energy flow control positions the company for multi-decade demand growth as global energy infrastructure transforms [3]
Key Risks
  1. Organic revenue decline of approximately -1% for 2026 masked by acquisitive growth — if Middle East disruptions persist and global industrial capex remains sluggish, underlying demand weakness could compress margins despite operational improvements [1]
  2. Mogas integration execution risk — the $1.3B acquisition is significant relative to Flowserve's $9.4B market cap, and integration missteps could disrupt the 14-quarter margin expansion streak while increasing D/E leverage [1]
  3. Profit margin structurally limited at ~8% for an industrial manufacturer — even with continued improvement, Flowserve's margin profile caps earnings power and makes the company vulnerable to raw material cost inflation and pricing pressure from larger competitors [4]
