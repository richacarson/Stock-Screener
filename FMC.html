Company Profile

FMC Corporation develops, markets, and sells crop protection chemicals — insecticides, herbicides, fungicides, biologicals, crop nutrition, and seed treatment products — across Latin America, North America, Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and Asia. The company is the fifth-largest global innovator in crop protection, with a network of 800+ scientists. FMC is in severe financial distress: TTM revenue of $3.25B is down 17.5% YoY, trailing EPS is -$21.46 due to massive impairments, and the dividend was cut 86% from $0.58/q to $0.08/q in late 2025. Founded in 1883 and headquartered in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

Investment Thesis
FMC at $5.31 with an $826M market cap is a deeply distressed turnaround candidate trading at 3.81x forward earnings, with the market pricing in a significant probability of further deterioration against management's expectation of a Q4 2026 return to growth [1]. The company maintains 800+ scientists, a patent portfolio including the newly launched fluindapyr fungicide, and a position as the fifth-largest global crop protection innovator — assets that could drive substantial upside if the agricultural chemicals cycle recovers. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA came in above the high end of guidance, Q2 adjusted EBITDA also beat, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $1.19-$1.49 implies a forward P/E under 4x, suggesting the stock is priced for permanent impairment rather than cyclical recovery [1]. The 86% dividend cut to $0.08/q preserves cash, and free cash flow turned positive at $166M TTM.
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. Revenue has collapsed from ~$5.8B to $3.25B with full-year 2026 guidance cut to $3.50-$3.70B (down 7% at the midpoint), while organic revenue declined 22% in Q2 and volumes fell 10% as North American growers face margin compression from low crop prices [1]. The balance sheet is stretched at 265% D/E with $4.4B debt against declining earnings power, and GAAP trailing EPS of -$21.46 reflects massive impairment charges that destroyed book value. Q3 2026 guidance is particularly weak at adjusted EBITDA of $120-140M (down 45% YoY) as North American distributors shift orders to Q4, creating concentration risk in the final quarter. The leadership carousel — CEO Douglas out, Brondeau returned, President Pereira departed — signals organizational stress, and the EPA has previously cited FMC for federal pesticide law violations ($1M settlement in 2017), while Greenpeace identified 41% of leading agrochemical products containing highly hazardous pesticides [2][3]. The dividend cut from $2.32/yr to $0.32/yr destroyed income investor trust.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q4 2026 return to growth — management guides revenue of $1.06-$1.20B (up ~4% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $275-$315M (up ~5%), driven by increased direct-to-grower sales in Brazil, new active ingredients including fluindapyr, and deferred North American distributor orders from Q3 [1]
  2. Forward P/E of 3.81x prices in permanent impairment rather than cyclical recovery — if FMC achieves consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.55, the stock trades at just 3.4x forward earnings, offering substantial upside on any normalization of agricultural chemical demand [1]
  3. Patent portfolio and R&D pipeline provide differentiation — 800+ scientists across five countries and proprietary active ingredients like fluindapyr create barriers to generic competition and potential for new product-driven revenue growth [2]
Key Risks
  1. Balance sheet distress at 265% D/E with $4.4B debt and GAAP losses of -$21.46/share trailing — continued revenue declines could trigger covenant violations or require dilutive equity raises, and the company's ability to service debt depends on the Q4 recovery materializing [1]
  2. Imminent dividend cut risk at 223% payout ratio — the $0.32/year dividend is mathematically unsustainable on current earnings and its elimination would remove the primary bull case for income investors [1]
  3. Patent cliff on key diamide insecticides (Rynaxypyr/cyantraniliprole) as exclusivity periods expire, exposing FMC's highest-margin products to generic competition that could accelerate revenue and margin decline [2]
