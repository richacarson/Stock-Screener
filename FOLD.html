Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Amicus Therapeutics was a rare disease biopharmaceutical company focused on developing and commercializing therapies for Fabry disease (Galafold/migalastat) and Pompe disease (Pombiliti+Opfolda). The company generated $634M in FY2025 revenue growing 17% YoY, with non-GAAP net income of $96.8M. BioMarin Pharmaceutical acquired Amicus for $14.50/share ($4.8B) in an all-cash transaction that closed April 27, 2026, after which FOLD was delisted from NASDAQ.

Investment Thesis
Amicus Therapeutics was a rare disease-focused biotech generating $634M in FY2025 revenue (+17% YoY) across two commercial products: Galafold ($521.7M, +12% CER) for Fabry disease and Pombiliti+Opfolda ($112.5M, +56% CER) for Pompe disease [1]. The company was approaching a GAAP profitability inflection with non-GAAP net income of $96.8M ($0.31/share) and strengthening unit economics as the Pombiliti+Opfolda launch scaled. The patient-first mission rooted in founder John Crowley's personal story, strong Inspire Impact Score of 48 (ALIGNED), and rare disease positioning — where unmet medical need is high and competitive dynamics favor specialty players — supported a constructive fundamental thesis.
BioMarin's acquisition for $14.50/share ($4.8B) closed April 27, 2026, ending FOLD's independent trading life [2]. The deal valued Amicus at roughly 7.6x trailing revenue, reasonable for a rare disease biotech approaching profitability but below multiples commanded by faster-growing peers. Key risks at time of sale included concentrated revenue in two products serving small patient populations (~10,000-15,000 patients each globally), ongoing competition from Sanofi's established Fabrazyme franchise, and the capital-intensive nature of rare disease commercial infrastructure that limited near-term margin expansion. The GAAP net loss of $27M despite $634M revenue reflected continued investment in geographic expansion and gene therapy pipeline. The FOLD ticker is no longer tradeable — this report reflects the company's fundamentals at its final standalone reporting period.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Amicus's core business — manufacturing and distributing FDA-approved biologic therapies for rare genetic diseases — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. Enzyme replacement therapies require complex biologics manufacturing, multi-year clinical trials with small patient populations, and regulatory approvals that AI cannot substitute. Rare disease drug development depends on deep patient phenotyping, genetic testing, and specialized physician relationships that create durable competitive moats. The highly regulated nature of pharmaceutical manufacturing and distribution provides additional structural protection. AI may modestly accelerate clinical trial design for competitors, but this represents an incremental efficiency gain rather than a substitution threat to existing approved therapies.
