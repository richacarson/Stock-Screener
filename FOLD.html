Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Amicus Therapeutics was a rare disease biopharmaceutical company focused on developing and commercializing therapies for Fabry disease (Galafold/migalastat) and Pompe disease (Pombiliti+Opfolda). The company generated $634M in FY2025 revenue growing 17% YoY. BioMarin Pharmaceutical acquired Amicus for $14.50/share ($4.8B) in an all-cash transaction that closed April 27, 2026, after which FOLD was delisted from NASDAQ. Results are now consolidated within BioMarin's rare disease portfolio.

Investment Thesis
Amicus Therapeutics was a rare disease-focused biotech generating $634M in FY2025 revenue (+17% YoY) across two commercial products: Galafold ($522M, +12% CER) for Fabry disease and Pombiliti+Opfolda ($113M, +56% CER) for Pompe disease [1]. The company was approaching a GAAP profitability inflection with non-GAAP net income of $96.8M ($0.31/share) and strengthening unit economics as the Pombiliti+Opfolda launch scaled. The patient-first mission, strong Inspire Impact Score of 48 (ALIGNED), and rare disease focus — where unmet medical need is high and competitive dynamics favor specialty players — supported a constructive fundamental thesis.
BioMarin's acquisition for $14.50/share ($4.8B) closed April 27, 2026, ending FOLD's independent trading life [2]. The deal valued Amicus at roughly 7.6x trailing revenue, reflecting the growth trajectory and strategic value of its rare disease portfolio. Investors who held through received a ~30% premium to pre-announcement levels. Going forward, Amicus's products are consolidated within BioMarin's rare disease portfolio, where Q2 2026 combined revenue grew 20% YoY. Key residual risks include BioMarin integration execution, potential disruption to the patient-focused culture Amicus had built, and pipeline investment decisions now subject to BioMarin's broader portfolio priorities. The FOLD ticker is no longer tradeable — this report reflects the company's fundamentals at the time of its final standalone reporting period.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience DEVELOPING
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Rare disease therapeutics face relatively low AI disruption risk — the core business of developing, manufacturing, and distributing enzyme replacement therapies requires deep biological expertise, regulatory navigation, and specialized manufacturing that AI cannot easily replicate or commoditize [1]. Patient relationships and physician trust in established brands provide switching cost protection. However, AI-accelerated drug discovery could enable competitors to develop rival therapies faster, and AI-powered clinical trial design could advantage larger pharma companies with more data assets. The acquisition by BioMarin partially mitigates this by providing scale and data resources. AI Opportunity (1/1): While Amicus did not generate direct AI revenue, the broader BioMarin platform could leverage AI for clinical trial optimization, patient identification in rare disease populations (where diagnosis is notoriously delayed), and manufacturing process optimization for biologic therapies.
