Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment & Materials ·NASDAQ ·United States
FormFactor is the world's leading provider of semiconductor wafer probe cards and test solutions used in the manufacturing and testing of integrated circuits. The company serves DRAM, HBM, Foundry & Logic, and Flash memory markets. Q2 2026 revenue reached a record $258.2M (+31.9% YoY, +14.2% QoQ) driven by record DRAM revenue from High Bandwidth Memory demand and significant Foundry & Logic growth from networking applications. Gross margins expanded 1,500 basis points over four quarters as FormFactor tripled EPS. FY2026 EPS is expected to grow 94% to approximately $1.36.

Investment Thesis
FormFactor is the dominant global probe card manufacturer positioned at the epicenter of the AI semiconductor testing cycle. Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $258.2M (+31.9% YoY) with gross margins expanding 1,500bps over four quarters and EPS tripling, driven by record DRAM revenue from HBM applications and significant Foundry & Logic growth from AI networking chips [1]. The virtually debt-free balance sheet (D/E 2.7%) provides maximum financial flexibility, and at a forward PEG of 0.36 (Forward P/E 34.1 / 94% expected EPS growth), the growth is attractively priced for a technology leader with no debt and dominant market share [4]. The HBM roadmap (HBM3, HBM3E, HBM4) and Co-Packaged Optics adoption create multi-year visibility for increasing probe card complexity and content per wafer.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, valuation, and organizational culture. Semiconductor equipment is deeply cyclical, and FormFactor's 502.5% trailing EPS growth reflects recovery from a severe 2024 downturn rather than sustainable growth rates — the trailing P/E of 82x reflects this distortion [4]. Forward P/E of 34x is elevated relative to broader semi equipment peers, pricing in continued HBM demand acceleration that could disappoint if AI capex slows. Profit margin of 12.8%, while rapidly improving, remains below the 20% target and reflects the company's manufacturing-intensive business model [4]. The Glassdoor rating of 3.2/5 and 'good ole boys club' cultural criticism raise concerns about the company's ability to attract and retain top engineering talent in a competitive semiconductor labor market [2]. ROE of 11.0% below the 15% target suggests the conservative capital structure may be under-optimizing returns. The company's fortunes are heavily concentrated in HBM and AI — any slowdown in AI semiconductor investment would disproportionately impact FormFactor.
Key Catalysts
  1. HBM roadmap progression — each generation (HBM3E to HBM4) increases probe card complexity and content per wafer, driving secular revenue growth independent of unit volume, with NVIDIA, AMD, and memory manufacturers all expanding HBM production capacity [1]
  2. Co-Packaged Optics adoption for AI networking — CPO integration into switch ASICs and interconnects creates a new, high-value probe card demand category beyond memory, diversifying FormFactor's AI exposure [1]
  3. Continued margin expansion — 1,500bps of gross margin improvement over four quarters suggests structural operating leverage as revenue scales, with FY2026 EPS expected to grow 94% to $1.36 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Semiconductor cyclicality — FormFactor's 502.5% trailing EPS growth reflects recovery from a deep downturn, and the next cycle correction could compress earnings and the 34x forward multiple simultaneously, amplifying downside [4]
  2. AI capex concentration risk — FormFactor's growth is heavily levered to HBM and AI networking demand; any slowdown in hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending or a shift to alternative memory architectures would disproportionately impact the company [1]
  3. Organizational culture concerns — Glassdoor rating of 3.2/5 and 'good ole boys club' criticism risk talent attrition in a tight semiconductor engineering labor market, potentially undermining the technology leadership that drives market share [2]
