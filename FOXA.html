Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Fox Corporation produces and distributes news, sports and entertainment content. Its Cable Network Programming segment includes FOX News, FOX Business, FS1/FS2, FOX Deportes and the Big Ten Network. Its Television segment includes the FOX broadcast network, 29 owned local stations and the free ad-supported streamer Tubi [3]. Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) revenue rose 5% to $17.13B. Q4 revenue jumped 28% to $4.21B on World Cup matches, political advertising and Tubi growth, and Tubi reached 110M monthly active users [1][2]. The company is led by Executive Chair and CEO Lachlan Murdoch [4].

Investment Thesis
Bull case: Fox owns scarce live-news and live-sports franchises that hold value as the rest of linear TV erodes. FY2026 revenue grew 5% to $17.13B, Q4 grew 28% on the World Cup, and Tubi's 110M MAUs give it a scaled streaming ad platform [1][2]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E 65%, interest coverage 10x), and the stock trades at 10.7x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.01 with a forward PEG of 0.78 [3]. Consensus sees EPS of $6.64 by FY2029.
Bear case: FY2026 GAAP net income fell 26% and diluted EPS fell from $4.91 to $3.90, and FY2027 lacks both the World Cup and the Super Bowl comparisons that shape ad revenue [1][2][3]. Cord-cutting pressures the 4% distribution growth, Fox News carries ongoing defamation and reputational litigation risk ($787.5M Dominion precedent), and insiders have been net sellers [3][6]. Fox filed S-4 and 424B3 registration documents in 2026 for a share-issuance transaction that investors should review [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q1 FY2027 results and the 2026 U.S. midterm political advertising cycle, following higher political ad spend in Q4 FY2026 [2]
  2. Tubi monetization as MAUs scale beyond 110M, narrowing the gap between engagement and ad revenue [1]
  3. Continued buybacks at ~10.7x forward earnings, supported by ~$3.46/share of TTM free cash flow [3]
Key Risks
  1. Defamation and reputational litigation at FOX News; the $787.5M Dominion settlement shows the scale of potential liabilities [6]
  2. Accelerating cord-cutting eroding affiliate fees and the 4% distribution revenue growth [2]
  3. Event-driven revenue volatility: the loss of the Super Bowl to NBC and the absence of World Cup and Olympic-type events can swing ad revenue sharply [2]
