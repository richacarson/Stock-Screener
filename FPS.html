Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NYSE ·United States
Forgent Power Solutions engineers and manufactures electrical power distribution equipment — low- and medium-voltage switchgear, transformers, PDUs, eHouses, transfer switches and switchboards — plus testing, commissioning and retrofit services for data centers, utilities/power generators and industrial customers. Formed in 2023 and rebranded in 2025 as the parent of MGM, PwrQ, States and VanTran, the company IPO'd on the NYSE in February 2026 and is headquartered in Dayton, Minnesota. FY2026 (June year-end) revenue was $1.42B (+89%) with an all-time-high backlog of $3.0B.

Investment Thesis
Forgent is one of the fastest-growing pure plays on AI-driven power infrastructure: Q4 FY2026 revenue rose 94% to $462M with adjusted EBITDA up 163% to $113M, full-year revenue reached $1.42B (+89%), and Q4 bookings of $1.50B lifted backlog to a record $3.0B (+256% YoY, +53% QoQ) [1]. FY2027 guidance of $2.4-2.6B revenue (~76% growth at the midpoint) and $575-625M adjusted EBITDA is more than 90% covered by backlog, giving unusual visibility [1][6]. At $28.35 — down from a 52-week high of $66 — the stock trades around 23x forward earnings with a forward PEG near 1.0, and TTM ROE of 18.4% shows the business already earns attractive returns as margins scale [2][3].
The bear case is that this is a levered, sponsor-controlled roll-up priced off a single demand cycle. D/E is 124.7% with $57M of annual interest expense, full-year GAAP net margin was just 5.8%, and heavy capacity capex left TTM free cash flow negative [1][2]. The stock's 3.9 beta and fall of more than 55% from its high show how sensitive it is to sentiment about hyperscaler capex; a slowdown in data center orders would hit a backlog that is now 6.5x quarterly revenue, raising execution and cancellation risk. Governance is also a concern: the controlling holder (Neos Partners) retains majority influence and the company pays related-party expenses to other portfolio companies, and secondary sales could weigh on the shares [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Execution on FY2027 guidance of $2.4-2.6B revenue and $575-625M adjusted EBITDA, starting with Q1 FY2027 guidance of $445-465M revenue and $90-100M adjusted EBITDA [1][6]
  2. Converting the record $3.0B backlog through the new Dayton, MN manufacturing capacity, which should lift GAAP net margin from 5.8% for the full year toward the 14.3% reached in Q4 [1][4]
  3. Deleveraging from 124.7% D/E as EBITDA roughly doubles, cutting the $57M interest burden and improving free cash flow once the capacity build-out peaks [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Concentration in hyperscaler/data center demand: a capex pause would hit a backlog equal to ~6.5 quarters of revenue and a stock with a 3.9 beta that has already fallen from $66 to $28 [1][2]
  2. Sponsor overhang and related-party dealings with Neos Partners portfolio companies ($9.2M in nine months to March 2026), with possible secondary offerings [5]
  3. Execution risk scaling four integrated legacy businesses from $753M to ~$2.5B of revenue in two years while TTM free cash flow is negative and leverage is above 120% D/E [1][2]
