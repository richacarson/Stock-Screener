Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NYSE ·United States
Forgent Power Solutions, Inc. designs and manufactures electrical distribution equipment — switchgear, busway, power distribution units — used in data centers, power grids, renewable energy facilities, and industrial applications. Formed from a combination of four legacy electrical equipment companies (MGM, PwrQ, States, VanTran) by Platinum Equity, IPO'd in February 2026 at $27/share raising $1.5B. FY2026 guidance raised to $1.35-1.39B revenue (+82% YoY) with record backlog of $1.98B driven by AI data center demand. Headquartered in Maple Grove, Minnesota.

Investment Thesis
Forgent Power Solutions is riding the most powerful demand cycle in electrical equipment history — Q3 FY2026 revenue surged 103% YoY to $379M, record bookings of $867M (+308%), and all-time-high backlog of $1.98B (+157%) [1]. FY2026 guidance was raised to $1.35-1.39B revenue (+82%) and $310-320M adjusted EBITDA (+86%), with sequential margin expansion to 22.4% demonstrating operating leverage as the company scales [1]. Every AI data center requires electrical distribution equipment, and Forgent's U.S. manufacturing base with 'highest levels of customization and shortest lead times' positions it to capture domestic infrastructure build. At $31.60 (down 37% from July highs), the stock has corrected from the peak euphoria, and the $1.98B backlog provides 5.2 quarters of revenue visibility — unusual for a $12B market cap company. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) with zero negative attributions provides a neutral faith-based profile.
The primary concern is that Forgent remains a PE-assembled rollup priced at 63.25x forward earnings on trailing GAAP profit margin of just 2.17%, despite the 37% stock correction from $50.54 [3]. D/E of 121% with $707.6M debt against $93.8M cash creates balance sheet risk if demand moderates [3]. The company has operated as a unified entity for less than one year, and the four legacy businesses have never been tested as an integrated platform through a demand cycle downturn. ROE of ~4.4% and trailing PE at extreme levels underscore how early the earnings ramp truly is — the gap between 'revenue momentum' and 'proven earnings power' remains wide. PE sponsors retain significant holdings and could execute secondary offerings that pressure the stock. If the AI data center buildout decelerates, shifts to alternative architectures, or moves to geographies where Forgent lacks manufacturing presence, the growth thesis collapses rapidly. The forward PE of 63.25 breaching the 60 floor for the first time (it was 40.13 in July) reflects eroding market confidence in the valuation despite strong fundamental execution.
