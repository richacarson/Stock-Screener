Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Industrial ·NYSE ·United States
First Industrial Realty Trust is a U.S.-only owner, operator, developer, and acquirer of logistics properties concentrated in 15 target MSAs. The company owns and has under development approximately 72.1 million square feet of industrial space serving multinational corporations and regional firms for supply chain operations. Established in 1993 and headquartered in Chicago, FR delivered FY2025 FFO of $2.96/share (+11.7% YoY) and guided 2026 FFO of $3.08-$3.16/share. Q2 2026 lease revenue was $193.9M vs $177.5M in Q2 2025. Market cap is approximately $8.4B.
151 Employees ·www.firstindustrial.com

Investment Thesis
First Industrial Realty Trust at a 3.27% dividend yield and Forward P/FFO of approximately 19x (P/E distorted by REIT depreciation accounting) offers a solid combination of income and growth in U.S. logistics real estate. FY2025 FFO of $2.96/share (+11.7% YoY) demonstrates consistent execution, and 2026 guidance of $3.08-$3.16/share implies continued mid-single-digit FFO growth [1]. The 72.1M sq ft portfolio concentrated in 15 high-barrier-to-entry U.S. MSAs benefits from secular tailwinds including e-commerce fulfillment demand, supply chain reshoring, and AI data center buildout. The 68.73% payout ratio provides dividend coverage with room for continued growth. AI disruption risk is negligible as physical logistics infrastructure sees increased demand from technology-driven economic activity.
The primary risk is interest rate sensitivity — as a leveraged REIT with D/E of 90.1%, FR's cost of capital and property valuations are directly impacted by interest rate movements, and the Forward P/E of 32.45x (inflated by REIT depreciation accounting) makes the stock appear expensive on traditional metrics [4]. Revenue growth YoY of 8.4% and ROE of 13.63% are solid but not exceptional, suggesting FR is a steady compounder rather than a high-growth opportunity. The U.S.-only strategy provides geographic focus but limits diversification if domestic industrial demand weakens. Competition from Prologis (which is 15x larger by sq ft) could pressure rents and occupancy in target MSAs. The lean 151-employee structure, while efficient, limits the organizational depth for scaling operations significantly. IOWN's framework penalizes FR on Innovation, Inspiration, and Infinite Game — appropriate for a REIT that prioritizes operational efficiency over transformative purpose.
Key Catalysts
  1. E-commerce and supply chain reshoring driving sustained demand for U.S. logistics space — FR's 15-MSA concentration in high-barrier markets positions it to benefit from both trends as nearshoring and last-mile delivery networks expand [1]
  2. FFO growth trajectory of $2.96 (FY2025) to $3.08-$3.16 (FY2026 guidance) at the midpoint represents ~6% growth with visibility into continued lease revenue increases (Q2 2026 lease revenue up 9.2% YoY to $193.9M) [1]
  3. Development pipeline in target MSAs provides organic growth opportunity at attractive development yields, with 72.1M sq ft portfolio scale creating tenant relationship advantages for build-to-suit projects
Key Risks
  1. Interest rate sensitivity as a leveraged REIT with D/E of 90.1% — rising rates increase cost of capital, compress property valuations, and make the 3.27% dividend yield less attractive relative to risk-free alternatives [4]
  2. Slowing industrial leasing activity — after years of strong demand, national industrial vacancy rates are normalizing, and speculative development across the sector could pressure rents in FR's target MSAs [1]
  3. Competitive pressure from Prologis (15x larger by sq ft) and other industrial REITs with greater scale, lower cost of capital, and broader geographic reach could limit FR's ability to win tenants and development opportunities
