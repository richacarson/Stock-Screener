Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Specialty ·NASDAQ ·United States
Fermi Inc. is a pre-revenue data center and power infrastructure company that IPO'd on NASDAQ in October 2025. The company's flagship effort is Project Matador, targeting 11-17 GW of combined data center and power generation capacity to serve AI-driven electricity demand. Fermi has a $2B market cap at $3.16/share but has generated zero revenue, reporting a FY2025 net loss of $486M. The company has experienced severe governance turmoil: founder/CEO Toby Neugebauer was fired in April 2026 for abusive behavior, and Lee McIntire (former CH2M HILL and TerraPower CEO) was appointed CEO in August 2026. A securities class action is pending after a $150M tenant agreement was cancelled in December 2025.

Investment Thesis
Fermi Inc. represents a speculative bet on AI-driven power demand through its Project Matador, targeting 11-17 GW of combined data center and power generation capacity. The company recently partnered with Hillcore Energy Capital on a 2.6 GW power complex and appointed Lee McIntire — former CEO of CH2M HILL and leader at TerraPower — as CEO in August 2026, bringing credible operational leadership to a company that desperately needs it [2][4]. At $3.16/share ($2B market cap) with a forward P/E of 24.3x, analysts project a path to profitability with consensus estimates showing initial revenue of $6M in Q3 2026 and $25M in Q4 2026, suggesting the commercial ramp may finally be beginning [1].
The bear case is overwhelming at this stage. Fermi is pre-revenue with $486M in cumulative net losses, zero operational infrastructure, and a governance crisis that includes a fired CEO, an active securities class action (Lupia v. Fermi Inc.), and a hostile proxy battle with its largest shareholder [3]. The first $150M tenant agreement was cancelled in December 2025, destroying the initial commercial thesis and triggering a 33% stock decline. The stock has fallen from its post-IPO highs above $5 to $3.16, reflecting eroded investor confidence. The 11-17 GW capacity target is aspirational with no construction timeline, financing plan, or contracted offtake to support it. Competing against established data center operators and power companies with decades of operational history and billions in installed capacity, Fermi's competitive position is extraordinarily weak. The IOWN framework's emphasis on proven execution, financial durability, and organizational trust produces a SELL rating that reflects the pre-revenue reality and governance dysfunction.
