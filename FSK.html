Company Profile
Financials ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
FS KKR Capital Corp. is a publicly traded business development company (BDC) managed by FS/KKR Advisor LLC, a partnership between FS Investments and KKR Credit Advisors. The company provides customized credit solutions to private middle-market U.S. companies, with approximately $19B in AUM. Q2 2026 showed modest stabilization: NII of $0.44/share beat guidance (9.6% annualized yield), nonaccruals fell to 7.1% from 8.1%, and net D/E declined to 122% from 131%, but NAV still fell 2.8% to $18.30/share. Faces a securities class action and Moody's junk rating (Ba1).

Investment Thesis
FSK trades at ~$10.80, a 41% discount to Q2 2026 NAV of $18.30, and offers a 19.3% dividend yield with a forward P/E of 6.35 [1][6]. Q2 2026 showed stabilization: NII of $0.44/share beat the 8-9% annualized guidance, nonaccruals improved to 7.1% from 8.1%, and net D/E fell to 122% from 131% as the company generated $1.3B in sales/repayments against $590M in originations — actively de-risking the portfolio [1]. KKR's $300M capital support ($150M tender offer at $11 + $150M convertible preferred) provides a price floor and managing partner commitment [4]. If nonaccrual trends continue improving and the de-leveraging strategy works, the deep NAV discount could narrow meaningfully from the current 41% level.
The bear case remains severe. Moody's Ba1 junk rating and the securities class action alleging misleading portfolio disclosures create ongoing institutional overhang and litigation risk [5]. Despite Q2 improvement, nonaccruals remain elevated at 7.1% (3.8% at fair value), and NAV continued declining (down 2.8% QoQ to $18.30 after Q1's 9.9% collapse), reflecting ongoing credit deterioration in the $12.3B portfolio [1]. Total investment income fell $14M QoQ to $290M as lower interest rates and a shrinking portfolio compress earnings. The software company exposure — 16.4% of the portfolio — remains structurally at risk from AI disruption, and CNBC reported many write-downs are tied to AI-disrupted borrowers [2]. The 41% discount to NAV may reflect appropriate risk pricing given the Moody's junk status, litigation overhang, and the possibility that further credit deterioration will necessitate additional dividend cuts below the current $0.44/quarter [5]. The JPMorgan-led credit facility tightening limits financial flexibility during the recovery [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q2 2026 nonaccrual improvement (7.1% from 8.1%) and NII beat ($0.44 vs 8-9% guidance) suggest the portfolio may be stabilizing, and continued improvement could narrow the 41% NAV discount [1]

Key Risks

  2. Securities class action alleging misleading disclosures about portfolio health and dividend sustainability creates material litigation liability and management distraction
  3. AI disruption of software portfolio companies driving nonaccruals higher — this is a structural risk that may worsen as AI capabilities expand and more middle-market software companies face disruption from AI alternatives
