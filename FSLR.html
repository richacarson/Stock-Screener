Company Profile
Technology ·Solar ·NASDAQ ·United States
First Solar is the leading American manufacturer of photovoltaic solar modules using proprietary cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film semiconductor technology. The company designs, manufactures, and sells solar modules to developers, system integrators, and utilities globally. First Solar operates the largest solar manufacturing and R&D footprint in the Western Hemisphere, with three fully vertically integrated facilities in Ohio, one in Alabama, and a new $1.1B AI-enabled facility in Louisiana. Q1 2026 delivered record revenue of $1.04B (+24% YoY) with 47% gross margin and Q2 followed with $1.06B revenue, 57% gross margin, and $423M net profit. Full-year 2026 guidance: $4.9-5.2B revenue, $2.6-2.8B adjusted EBITDA. Contracted backlog of 47.9 GW at $14.4B through 2030. Market cap approximately $20B+.

Investment Thesis
First Solar is executing at an elite level: Q1 2026 delivered record revenue of $1.04B (+24% YoY) with 47% gross margin, followed by Q2 at $1.06B revenue with gross margin expanding to 57% and net profit of $423M [1]. The balance sheet is fortress-grade: 6% D/E, $2.4B+ cash, and 30.73% profit margins that rival software companies despite being a manufacturer [4]. Forward P/E of 8.88 and PEG of 0.67 dramatically under-price a company growing revenue at 24% with world-class profitability and multi-year backlog visibility (47.9 GW contracted at $14.4B through 2030). The AI data center buildout creates a structural demand tailwind, with CEO Widmar positioning First Solar as essential infrastructure for America's AI dominance [3]. Tandem Cell technology (CdTe + Perovskite) targeting 25%+ efficiency for 2027-2028 provides a clear next-generation innovation pipeline.
The primary risk is policy dependence: First Solar's domestic manufacturing premium is significantly supported by IRA Section 45X advanced manufacturing production credits, and any legislative modification or repeal would materially impact profitability [1]. Section 45X credits are set to decline after 2029, making current peak margins potentially unsustainable long-term. Chinese solar manufacturers continue to expand capacity aggressively, maintaining 80%+ global market share with dramatically lower production costs — First Solar's CdTe technology represents less than 5% of global module production, making it a niche player dependent on trade protection [3]. Q2 2026 net sales were actually down 4% YoY due to customer contract terminations, introducing execution risk beneath the headline growth story. The stock has exhibited extreme volatility driven by political sentiment around clean energy policy and tariff uncertainty. The Inspire score of 59 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based allocation, but the policy concentration risk is the fundamental bear case for this otherwise exceptional business.
