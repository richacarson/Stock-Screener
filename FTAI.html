Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NASDAQ ·United States
FTAI Aviation is an integrated aerospace products and services company focused on CFM56 jet engine aftermarket maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO). The company operates through two segments: Aerospace Products (engine module MRO with proprietary PMA parts, producing 270 CFM56 modules in Q1 2026) and Aviation Leasing (aircraft and engine leasing through special purpose vehicles). FTAI internalized management from Fortress Investment Group in 2024. A third segment, Power (repurposed jet engine gas turbine power generation), targets commercial launch in Q4 2026. Revenue surged 65% YoY in Q1 2026 to $830.7M. Market cap is approximately $18.5B.

Investment Thesis
FTAI Aviation at 20x forward earnings is a unique aerospace growth compounder -- Q1 2026 revenue of $830.7M (+65% YoY) was driven by CFM56 module production doubling to 270 units, with Aerospace Products EBITDA surging 70% YoY to $222.6M at a 30% margin [1]. The 2026 segment EBITDA target of $1.625B ($1.05B Aerospace Products + $575M Aviation Leasing) implies continued rapid scaling, and FY2026 consensus EPS of $7.10 (+90% from FY2025) reflects the operating leverage of the manufacturing ramp. Forward PEG of 0.57 suggests the market is not fully pricing the growth trajectory, and the Power business (commercial launch Q4 2026, nearly sold-out 2027 production) represents an optionality call on a new revenue stream. Leverage has declined from ~5x to 2.3x, and the revolving credit facility upsized to $2.025B provides ample liquidity for continued growth investment.
The bear case centers on financial transparency, extreme leverage, and culture risk. The Muddy Waters and Snowcap short-seller reports alleged that FTAI characterizes whole engine sales as module sales to inflate reported volumes and manipulates expense categorization to inflate EBITDA margins [3]. While the board's independent review found these claims meritless, the company provided no specific rebuttals, and an ongoing investor lawsuit (Hagens Berman) seeks damages for the class period. D/E of 865% reflects a capital structure dependent on warehouse debt facilities ($3.5B) and revolving credit ($2.025B) that could seize during a credit market dislocation. Negative free cash flow of -$962M despite $477M in net income reveals the capital intensity of the growth strategy. Glassdoor reviews describing engineers pressured to 'bend their moral code' are particularly concerning alongside the short-seller allegations about financial reporting [4]. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Aviation MRO is a physical-world, heavily regulated activity that AI structurally cannot displace. Jet engine module disassembly, inspection, repair, and reassembly require FAA Part 145 certification, hands-on skilled technicians, specialized tooling, and physical infrastructure that no software can replicate. FTAI's proprietary PMA parts provide additional regulatory and IP moats -- each PMA requires FAA engineering approval that takes years to obtain [1]. The aviation leasing business is protected by capital intensity, long-term contractual relationships, and physical asset ownership. AI could eventually assist in predictive maintenance diagnostics and visual inspection, but the core MRO labor and regulatory framework remains structurally immune to AI substitution. AI Opportunity (0/1): FTAI generates no revenue from AI products or services. The company may benefit from AI-assisted maintenance scheduling and diagnostics in the future but has not disclosed any AI-related revenue.
