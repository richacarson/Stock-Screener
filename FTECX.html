Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management — Mutual Fund ·NASDAQ ·United States
FTECX is a share class of the Fidelity Advisor Technology Fund, an open-end mutual fund investing primarily in securities of companies in the technology sector. The fund's initial offering took place on 11/6/2025, with returns prior to that date representing those of the Fidelity Select Technology Portfolio [1]. Sub-advised by Fidelity Management & Research Company. As a mutual fund vehicle, FTECX is structurally incompatible with the IOWN Return on Intention framework, which is designed to evaluate individual operating companies on their innovation, leadership, financial durability, and AI resilience — none of which are meaningful for a pooled investment vehicle that holds a diversified portfolio of other companies' stocks [2].

Investment Thesis
FTECX provides exposure to technology sector stocks through Fidelity's active management expertise, inheriting the track record of the Fidelity Select Technology Portfolio which has delivered strong long-term returns in the technology sector [1]. For investors who prefer active management and work with financial advisors in the Class C fee structure, the fund provides a packaged solution for technology sector allocation. The Fidelity brand carries institutional credibility and deep research resources.
However, FTECX is structurally incompatible with the IOWN Return on Intention framework, which evaluates operating companies rather than fund vehicles. Mutual funds cannot be scored on innovation, infrastructure, inspiration, or infinite game thinking because these dimensions measure company-specific attributes that funds do not possess. The Class C share structure layers ongoing distribution fees that create persistent cost drag compared to lower-cost alternatives like the Fidelity MSCI Information Technology Index ETF (FTEC) at ~0.08% expense ratio [1][2]. The SELL rating reflects framework incompatibility rather than a judgment on the fund's investment merit — investors seeking technology sector exposure should evaluate FTECX on fund-specific criteria (expense ratio, manager track record, tax efficiency) rather than the IOWN framework.
