Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
TechnipFMC designs, manufactures, and services subsea equipment and systems for the oil and gas industry worldwide, operating through Subsea and Surface Technologies segments. The company pioneered the integrated EPCI (iEPCI) model that combines engineering, procurement, construction, and installation into a single contract structure. Q2 2026 revenue reached $2.76B (up ~9% YoY) with full-year guidance of $10.35B-$10.90B and adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.19B.

Investment Thesis
TechnipFMC at $70 with a $27.5B market cap is the dominant pure-play subsea equipment and services company executing a strong growth trajectory — Q1 2026 revenue surged 11.6% YoY to $2.49B and Q2 delivered $2.76B (up ~9%), with full-year guidance of $10.35B-$10.90B and raised adjusted EBITDA expectations of ~$2.19B [1][2]. The iEPCI integrated delivery model is a structural competitive advantage that improves project economics for operators and deepens customer lock-in, while the subsea backlog of $15.8B provides over 6 quarters of revenue visibility. Balance sheet strength (D/E of 37.9%, ROE of 35.8%) supports the $10B expected Subsea inbound orders for 2026, and Forward P/E of 19.3 offers reasonable valuation for a business compounding revenue at ~10% annually [1].
The bear case centers on commodity cyclicality and margin compression. TechnipFMC's revenue is ultimately tied to deepwater oil and gas capital expenditure decisions by major operators, making it vulnerable to crude price declines and OPEC+ production cut extensions that could defer FID decisions on new subsea developments [4]. Profit margin of 11.3% remains structurally low for the project-based engineering model, and the raised EBITDA guidance of $2.19B implies only ~20% EBITDA margin, limiting earnings leverage. The company's history of bribery settlements — including a $214M payment to Brazilian authorities and prior Nigeria corruption charges — represents ongoing governance risk [4]. Q2 book-to-bill of 1.0x and Q1 at 0.9x suggest order momentum is decelerating from the 2023-2024 upcycle peak, and the flat subsea backlog of $15.8B could signal the cycle is nearing its plateau. The minimal dividend ($0.20/year, 0.29% yield) provides negligible downside protection in a cyclical downturn.
Key Catalysts
  1. Subsea backlog of $15.8B with ~$10B expected 2026 inbound orders provides multi-year revenue visibility, with backlog conversion of $3.8B in H2 2026, $5.2B in 2027, and $6.8B in 2028+, de-risking near-term earnings estimates [1]
  2. iEPCI integrated delivery model continues gaining adoption as operators seek cost efficiency in deepwater developments, driving higher project margins and customer retention versus traditional fragmented contracting approaches [2]
  3. Balance sheet strength with 37.9% D/E and growing free cash flow supports potential shareholder returns expansion beyond the current $0.20/year dividend, with management signaling increased capital returns as backlog converts to cash [1]
Key Risks
  1. Crude oil price decline below $60/bbl would trigger deepwater FID deferrals and order cancellations, as subsea project economics are highly sensitive to commodity prices — the 2014-2016 downturn saw FTI predecessor revenue decline over 40% [4]
  2. Governance overhang from history of bribery settlements including $214M Brazilian payment and prior Nigeria corruption charges creates ongoing legal risk and reputational damage that could affect government-linked contract awards [4]
  3. Book-to-bill deceleration from 1.1x in Q4 2025 to 1.0x in Q2 2026 and 0.9x in Q1 2026 may signal the subsea upcycle is peaking, with flat backlog at $15.8B suggesting order growth is no longer exceeding revenue conversion [1]
