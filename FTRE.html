Investment Thesis
Fortrea trades at a compelling 13.9x forward P/E with PEG of 0.34, offering deep value if the turnaround gains traction under new CEO Anshul Thakral [1][4]. The stock has surged 69% from $10.50 to $17.76 as operational improvements materialize: Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled to $47.0M (7.4% margin vs 4.7%), adjusted EPS rose to $0.16 from near-zero, and the $7.8B backlog (up 1.6% YoY) with 1.15x book-to-bill provides multi-quarter revenue visibility [1]. FY2026 guidance of $190-220M adjusted EBITDA implies continued margin expansion as the Fuel for Growth cost program delivers $120M+ in cumulative annualized savings. Fortrea Intelligent Technology (FIT) positions the company to compete on AI-enabled trial delivery as the CRO industry undergoes digital transformation [3].
The bear case centers on deteriorating revenue trends and elevated leverage. Revenue declined 2.3% YoY in Q1 2026 with FY2026 guidance of $2.55-2.65B implying further contraction from $2.72B in 2025, suggesting structural demand weakness rather than a cyclical trough [1]. D/E of 213% with $1.12B debt against $174.6M cash creates meaningful balance sheet risk if revenue declines persist or accelerate. The $797.9M goodwill impairment confirmed Labcorp overvalued the business at separation [1]. CEO turnover after just two years (Tom Pike replaced by Thakral in August 2025) undermines organizational stability during a critical turnaround phase. Glassdoor reviews citing that 'culture is resistant to feedback' and 'management does not empower' employees (3.4/5) suggest the cost-cutting may be degrading service quality in a people-intensive business [2]. CRO competition from larger, better-capitalized peers (IQVIA at $15B+ revenue, PPD/Thermo Fisher) and the emergence of AI-native clinical trial platforms create market share risk.
