Company Profile
Communication Services ·Broadcasting ·NYSE ·United States
Fubo operates a live TV streaming platform delivering sports, news, and entertainment in the U.S., Canada, France, Spain, and internationally. In 2025 Fubo completed a transformative business combination with Disney's Hulu + Live TV, with Disney taking an approximately 70% ownership stake and founder-CEO David Gandler transitioning out in favor of former Disney+ president Alisa Bowen. The combined entity is now the second-largest virtual pay-TV (vMVPD) provider in the U.S. with roughly 5.7 million North America subscribers, trailing only Google's YouTube TV.

Fubo's story is one of survival-driven transformation rather than infinite-game conviction. The company fought Disney in court over the earlier Venu Sports joint venture on antitrust grounds, then agreed to be majority-acquired by that same rival — a pragmatic pivot that secured the company's future but subordinated its independent Just Cause to Disney's strategic interests, with the founding CEO departing as part of the deal. Sinek would note the transformative flexibility on display, but the shallow, generic mission and lack of any documented pattern of courageous, cause-driven capital allocation keep this squarely in mixed territory.

Investment Thesis
Fubo has been transformed by the 2025 Hulu + Live TV combination into the clear #2 U.S. vMVPD with ~5.7M North America subscribers, record Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1.574B, and Adjusted EBITDA of $37.7M versus just $1.4M pro forma a year earlier — a dramatic profitability inflection [1]. Disney's ~70% ownership and $145M committed term loan provide a capital backstop and content-cost synergy opportunities that an independent Fubo never had, and management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance plus a long-term target of at least $300M Adjusted EBITDA by FY2028 [1]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E of just 15%) and daily liquidity (~$17M) is more than adequate for a small-cap position.
The bear case centers on unresolved profitability, subscriber softness, and diminished independence. Net loss narrowed but remained negative (-$6.2M in Q2), profit margin is still slightly negative, and North America subscribers actually declined slightly year-over-year (5.7M vs 5.9M) even after the Hulu Live TV consolidation — evidence that gross subscriber growth remains elusive in a saturated vMVPD market [1]. Revenue growth of only ~1% pro forma understates how much of Fubo's scale now comes from consolidation rather than organic demand. Governance risk is real: founder-CEO David Gandler is exiting after 11 years, Disney holds a 70% controlling stake and effectively sets strategic direction, and legacy shareholders now hold a minority position with diminished influence over the company's future. Innovation and Inspiration scores are both weak, reflecting limited evidence of AI-driven differentiation or a deeply lived organizational purpose, and analyst price targets (~$19.50) imply optimism that has not yet been matched by fundamentals.
Key Catalysts
  1. Execution of Disney/Hulu + Live TV cost, revenue, and advertising synergies could accelerate the path toward the long-term $300M+ Adjusted EBITDA target by FY2028 [1]
  2. Continued Adjusted EBITDA inflection (from $1.4M pro forma to $37.7M in Q2 FY2026) could re-rate the stock if management demonstrates a credible path to sustained GAAP profitability [1]
  3. New CEO Alisa Bowen (former Disney+ president) brings large-scale streaming operating experience that could sharpen product strategy and subscriber retention as the combined entity scales [2]
Key Risks
  1. North America subscribers declined year-over-year (5.7M vs 5.9M) even post-merger, signaling continued competitive pressure from YouTube TV and other vMVPD/skinny-bundle alternatives [1][2]
  2. Disney's ~70% ownership stake concentrates strategic control with a party whose incentives (protecting the broader Disney+/Hulu/ESPN bundle) may not always align with legacy Fubo minority shareholders
  3. Persistent negative profit margin and reliance on continued content-cost renegotiation leave limited margin for error if subscriber trends do not stabilize or advertising synergies underdeliver [1][4]
