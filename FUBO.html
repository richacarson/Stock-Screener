Company Profile
Communication Services ·Broadcasting ·NYQ ·United States
fuboTV operates a live TV streaming platform featuring sports, news, and entertainment programming through Fubo-branded and Hulu Live-branded services in the United States, Canada, France, Spain, and internationally. The company completed a transformative combination with Disney's Hulu + Live TV business in early 2025, with Disney holding a 70% economic and voting interest, making Fubo a subsidiary of The Walt Disney Company. Q2 FY2026 reported revenue was $1.574B (+40% YoY reported, +1% pro forma), with Adjusted EBITDA of $37.7M and 5.7M North America subscribers. New CEO Alisa Bowen replaced co-founder David Gandler in July 2026.
510 Employees ·fubo.tv

Investment Thesis
Fubo at $9.25 with a $279M market cap is a live TV streaming platform approaching an inflection point toward profitability after the transformative Hulu Live combination. Q2 FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA reached $37.7M (vs. pro forma $1.4M a year earlier), and management raised fiscal 2026 pro forma Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $90-$100M with positive free cash flow targeted for fiscal 2027-2028 [1]. The Forward P/E of 16.76 is attractive for a company with a pathway from $5.7B+ in TTM revenue to profitability, supported by Disney's 70% backing which provides content access, scale, and operational expertise. New CEO Alisa Bowen has prioritized technology and AI investment to improve user experience and operational efficiency [2].
The bear case centers on weak organic growth and structural concerns. Pro forma revenue growth was only 1% in Q2 FY2026, and North America subscribers fell to 5.7M from 5.9M, suggesting the combined platform is not organically growing [1]. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $1.482B missed the Street estimate of $1.504B, and Adjusted EBITDA declined to $19.1M from pro forma $31M a year earlier [2]. The profit margin of -1.0% means GAAP profitability remains elusive despite the massive revenue base. Disney's 70% controlling interest limits Fubo's strategic autonomy and raises governance concerns — the company's purpose and direction are ultimately subordinate to Disney's media strategy. The CEO transition from founder Gandler to Bowen introduces execution risk at a critical integration phase. The stock's decline from a 200-day moving average of $14.12 to $9.25 (-35%) reflects market skepticism about the path to sustainable growth under the combined structure.
Key Catalysts
  1. Path to positive free cash flow in fiscal 2027-2028 with pro forma Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $90-$100M for FY2026, demonstrating that the Hulu Live combination can generate real economic returns at scale [1]
  2. Live sports mega-events including World Cup and NBA Finals drove Q3 engagement and could generate subscriber acquisition momentum if the platform can retain viewers post-event through improved AI-powered content discovery [2][3]
  3. New CEO Alisa Bowen's technology and AI investment priorities could unlock operational efficiencies and differentiate the user experience from competitors like YouTube TV and Sling [2]
Key Risks
  1. Organic revenue growth of 1% pro forma and declining North America subscribers (5.9M to 5.7M) suggest the combined platform may be in secular subscriber decline as cord-cutting matures and live TV streaming competition intensifies from YouTube TV, DirecTV Stream, and Peacock [1]
  2. Disney's 70% controlling interest means Fubo lacks strategic autonomy — capital allocation, content strategy, and technology investment are ultimately governed by Disney's media priorities, creating governance risk for minority shareholders [1]
  3. Content cost inflation in live sports rights (NFL, NBA, MLB, FIFA) could compress margins and prevent the company from achieving sustainable GAAP profitability, as content costs exceed 80% of revenue and are subject to escalating multi-year contracts [2]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Fubo's core business — live sports streaming — is structurally protected from AI substitution because live sporting events are physical-world activities that cannot be replicated or generated by AI [1][2]. Content rights and broadcast licenses create regulatory and contractual moats that AI cannot bypass. The platform layer (content discovery, personalization, voice search) benefits from AI enhancement rather than facing displacement. However, Fubo is not fully IMMUNE because AI-powered competitors could build superior streaming interfaces, and AI-driven content aggregation or agentic AI assistants could eventually disrupt the bundling model that underpins subscription economics. Fubo's 10-Q discloses AI-specific risk factors around execution quality and third-party AI dependence [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): Fubo uses AI internally for content discovery, voice search, personalization, and marketing creative generation, but generates no quantifiable revenue directly from AI products or services [3]. The AI Instant Headlines feature and planned conversational AI assistant are user experience enhancements, not standalone revenue streams.
