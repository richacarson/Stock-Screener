Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Leisure ·NYSE ·United States
Six Flags Entertainment Corporation is the largest regional amusement park operator in North America, operating 42 theme parks, water parks, and resort properties across the United States, Mexico, and Canada. The company was formed through the July 2024 merger of Cedar Fair, L.P. and legacy Six Flags Entertainment Corp. Previously known as Cedar Fair, the company rebranded to Six Flags Entertainment Corporation in July 2024 and is headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina. The combined portfolio includes iconic parks such as Cedar Point, Six Flags Magic Mountain, Knott's Berry Farm, and Six Flags Great Adventure. Market cap approximately $1.93B.

Investment Thesis
Six Flags represents a compelling turnaround opportunity following the transformative July 2024 merger of Cedar Fair and Six Flags, creating the dominant regional theme park operator with 42 parks, $3.1B revenue, and $792M adjusted EBITDA [1]. New CEO John Reilly (30 years of theme park experience) is implementing structural improvements including empowered local park leadership and employee-driven innovation that could unlock significant operational upside [2]. Revenue grew 11.19% YoY, and full merger synergies (estimated at $200M+) are still being realized. At an EV/EBITDA of 9.26x, the stock offers reasonable value for a dominant consumer franchise with high barriers to entry.
The balance sheet is the critical concern: $5.4B in debt with 1,076% debt-to-equity, -123% ROE, and an Altman Z-Score of 0.31 place the company in severe financial distress territory by traditional measures [3]. While much of the headline loss reflects non-cash goodwill impairment from the merger, the underlying leverage is real and limits strategic flexibility. Full year 2025 attendance of 47.4M guests fell short of management expectations due to weather and fewer season pass sales, and Q4 2025 revenue declined 5% YoY [1]. The company suspended dividends, and the 36% stock price decline over 52 weeks reflects investor concern about the integration trajectory. Competition from Disney, Universal, and expanding entertainment alternatives creates persistent pressure on per-capita spending growth, and any economic downturn would disproportionately impact a highly leveraged leisure company.
Key Catalysts
  1. Realization of $200M+ in merger synergies from the Cedar Fair-Six Flags combination, including corporate cost elimination, shared best practices across 42 parks, and procurement leverage from industry-leading scale
  2. CEO John Reilly's operational transformation with empowered Park Presidents and employee-driven innovation initiatives driving attendance recovery and per-capita spending growth toward pre-merger trend levels
  3. Debt reduction using free cash flow generation from the combined portfolio, potentially de-levering from 6.8x net debt/EBITDA toward a more sustainable 4-5x range that would unlock meaningful equity value
Key Risks
  1. Extreme leverage with 1,076% debt-to-equity, $5.4B debt load, and Altman Z-Score of 0.31 creates bankruptcy risk if attendance declines persist or an economic recession reduces discretionary leisure spending
  2. 2025 full year attendance of 47.4M guests fell below expectations due to weather impacts and fewer season pass sales, and Q4 revenue declined 5% YoY, raising questions about the combined company's demand trajectory
  3. No dividend paid and stock down 36% over 52 weeks, with the massive goodwill impairment signaling that the merger may have been completed at too high a price, potentially destroying shareholder value
