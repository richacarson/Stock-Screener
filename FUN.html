Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Leisure ·NYSE ·United States
Six Flags Entertainment Corporation is the largest regional amusement park operator in North America, operating 42 theme parks, water parks, and resort properties across the United States, Mexico, and Canada. Formed through the July 2024 merger of Cedar Fair, L.P. and legacy Six Flags, the combined portfolio includes Cedar Point, Six Flags Magic Mountain, Knott's Berry Farm, and Six Flags Great Adventure. Q1 2026 revenue was $225.6M (+12% YoY) with attendance up 4% to 2.9M visits and per capita spending up 6% to $69.26, though net loss widened to $268.6M due to asset impairments and heavy interest expense. TTM revenue ~$3.1B. CEO John Reilly (December 2025) is restructuring with empowered park leadership. CapEx guided $425-450M for 2026. Headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina.

Investment Thesis
Six Flags represents a turnaround opportunity at the dominant regional theme park operator following the transformative July 2024 Cedar Fair merger. Q1 2026 showed operational improvement: revenue up 12% to $225.6M, attendance up 4% to 2.9M visits, and per capita spending up 6% to $69.26, demonstrating pricing power and early operational traction under CEO Reilly [1]. The underlying business generates approximately $792M adjusted EBITDA (~25% margin) on $3.1B TTM revenue from 42 irreplaceable physical assets with high barriers to entry. New leadership (empowered Park Presidents, fresh C-suite appointments) and $425-450M CapEx are investing in guest experience improvement. Merger synergies (estimated $200M+) are still being realized. At ~$17 per share, the stock is pricing in near-terminal value despite a viable operating business.
The balance sheet is the overwhelming risk: D/E of 1,455% reflects $5.4B+ in debt with -143% ROE and deeply negative GAAP profitability, placing the company in severe financial distress territory [3]. Q1 2026 net loss widened to $268.6M due to asset sale and trade name impairments alongside $120M+ in quarterly interest expense, and the dividend has been suspended. The board shake-up (Executive Chairman Bassoul and Lead Independent Director Hanrahan departing December 2025) raises governance questions during a critical integration period. Reports of planned park sales suggest the merger may have been completed at too high a price, potentially requiring asset divestitures to service debt rather than optimize the portfolio strategically [2]. Any economic downturn would disproportionately impact a highly leveraged leisure company with extreme weather sensitivity — the stock has declined significantly from post-merger levels, reflecting investor skepticism about whether the combined company can generate sufficient free cash flow to de-lever before interest costs consume operating earnings.
Key Catalysts
  1. Realization of $200M+ in merger synergies from the Cedar Fair-Six Flags combination, including corporate cost elimination, shared best practices across 42 parks, and procurement leverage from industry-leading scale [1]
  2. CEO Reilly's operational transformation — empowered Park Presidents, $425-450M CapEx, 20 additional operating days — driving per capita spending and attendance growth beyond Q1's 6% and 4% respectively toward full-year momentum [2]
  3. Debt reduction through free cash flow generation and selective park divestitures, de-levering from extreme D/E levels toward a sustainable capital structure that unlocks meaningful equity value at the depressed stock price
Key Risks
  1. Extreme leverage with D/E of 1,455%, $5.4B+ debt, and ~$480M+ annual interest expense creates bankruptcy risk if attendance declines persist or an economic recession reduces discretionary leisure spending — the Q1 net loss of $268.6M demonstrates the cash flow burden [1][3]
  2. Board instability (Executive Chairman and Lead Independent Director departing, trade name impairments, potential park sell-offs) signals the merger integration may be underperforming original expectations, with asset sales driven by financial necessity rather than strategic optimization [2]
  3. High seasonal employee turnover, weather sensitivity, and competition from Disney, Universal, and expanding entertainment alternatives create persistent pressure on attendance and per capita spending growth needed to service the debt load
