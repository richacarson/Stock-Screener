Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Fusemachines Inc. is an enterprise AI company offering AI-as-a-service, AI Studio, AI Engines and AI Agents products, plus AI talent sourced and trained through its Fusemachines AI Fellowship in underserved regions such as Nepal. Founded in 2013 by Columbia AI lecturer Dr. Sameer Maskey, it listed on Nasdaq in October 2025 via a SPAC merger. FY2025 revenue was $7.71M (-12.45% YoY) and Q2 2026 revenue was $1.79M with a $2.85M net loss as total client count declined. The company faces multiple Nasdaq deficiency notices (MVPHS, MVLS, and sub-$1 bid price) and a delisting panel hearing scheduled for November 3, 2026; CFO Christine Chambers departed in August 2026 and David Wells was appointed CFO in September 2026.

Investment Thesis
The bull case is an option on a micro-cap pure-play AI services company: Fusemachines carries a ~55% gross margin, sells shipping AI Agents and AI-as-a-service products, and its AI Fellowship gives it a low-cost engineering talent pipeline [2][6]. Losses narrowed to $0.10 per share in Q2 2026 from $0.52 a year earlier, and management's 2026 plan targets vertical products in retail and real estate plus acquisitions such as Rake [1]. At a ~$12.5M market cap and ~1.1x sales, any client re-acceleration could re-rate the shares sharply.
The bear case is severe and near-term. Revenue fell 12.45% in 2025 and kept declining in 2026 as total clients decreased, equity is negative, the current ratio is ~0.11 with only ~$0.37M of cash, and the company faces a Nasdaq Hearing Panel on November 3, 2026 plus a bid-price deadline of March 17, 2027 [1][5][6]. Dilution risk from the pending S-1 offering is high, the CFO departed in August 2026, and daily dollar volume below $1M fails IOWN's liquidity floor.
Key Catalysts
  1. Nasdaq Hearing Panel decision following the November 3, 2026 hearing — a granted exception would remove the immediate delisting overhang [5]
  2. Capital raise via the 2026 S-1 registration that could fund operations and the vertical AI product roadmap, albeit with dilution [5]
  3. Client wins for AI Agents in retail and real estate verticals that reverse the declining client count reported in Q2 2026 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Delisting: multiple Nasdaq deficiencies (MVPHS, MVLS, sub-$1 bid) with a November 3, 2026 panel hearing could move the stock to OTC markets [5]
  2. Going-concern and dilution risk: negative equity, ~0.11 current ratio and ~$0.37M of cash against continuing quarterly losses of ~$2.8M [6][7]
  3. Commoditization of custom AI services by foundation-model providers' agent platforms, shrinking the addressable market for sub-scale integrators [1]
