Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Formula One Group, a tracking stock of Liberty Media Corporation, holds the commercial rights to the FIA Formula One World Championship and also owns MotoGP, the F2 and F3 feeder series, and F1 Academy. The company generates revenue from race promotion fees, broadcast rights, sponsorship, and the F1 Paddock Club hospitality program across a global race calendar. Full-year 2025 F1 revenue grew 14% to $3.9B, and Q1 2026 revenue surged 53% to $617M. Fan attendance reached 6.75M (+4%) with live viewership up 21%. Liberty Media acquired F1 in 2017; headquartered in Englewood, Colorado.

Investment Thesis
Formula One's commercial momentum remains strong: full-year 2025 F1 revenue grew 14% to $3.9B with operating income up 28% to $632M and Adjusted OIBDA up 20% to $946M, while Q1 2026 revenue surged 53% to $617M with Adjusted OIBDA more than doubling to $172M [1]. Fan attendance hit a record 6.75M (+4%), live viewership grew 21%, and new multi-year sponsorships (Marsh, FanDuel, Betway) alongside extensions (Salesforce, Allwyn) demonstrate accelerating commercial demand. The business is a structurally AI-resilient (score 9), effectively monopolistic global media and live-events franchise — AI cannot substitute live racing, exclusive broadcast rights, or hospitality experiences — and AWS-powered AI tools are actively enhancing the product rather than threatening it [2].
The bear case centers on earnings quality, leadership stability, and valuation. Trailing PE of 104.53x and Forward PE of 44.35x reflect weak profitability — ROE of 2.73% and profit margin of 5.50% are poor for the asset's scale, reflecting MotoGP integration costs and lumpy quarter-to-quarter revenue recognition tied to race-calendar timing [6]. Q1 2026 EPS growth was actually negative (-43.6% YoY) even as revenue surged 53%, highlighting the disconnect between top-line momentum and bottom-line translation. Greg Maffei — the executive most associated with the 'Drive to Survive' growth strategy — is the fourth senior Liberty Media leader to depart since 2017, and directors have been meaningful net sellers in 2026 (Chase Carey's $9.03M sale, Renee Wilm's 42.66%-of-position sale), raising questions about insider conviction at current prices [3][5].
