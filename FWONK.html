Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Formula One Group, a tracking stock of Liberty Media Corporation, holds the commercial rights to the FIA Formula One World Championship and also owns MotoGP, the F2 and F3 feeder series, and F1 Academy. The company generates revenue from race promotion fees, broadcast rights, sponsorship, and the F1 Paddock Club hospitality program across a global race calendar. Liberty Media acquired F1 in 2017; the company is headquartered in Englewood, Colorado.

Investment Thesis
Formula One's commercial momentum remains strong: full-year 2025 F1 revenue grew 14% to $3.9B with operating income up 28% to $632M and Adjusted OIBDA up 20% to $946M, while Q1 2026 revenue surged 53% to $617M with Adjusted OIBDA more than doubling to $172M [1]. Fan attendance hit a record 6.75M (+4%) and live viewership grew 21%, and the Turkish Grand Prix returns in 2027 under a new multi-year agreement alongside extended sponsorships (Salesforce, Allwyn, Marsh, FanDuel, Betway) [1]. The business is a structurally AI-resilient, effectively monopolistic global media and live-events franchise, and AWS-powered AI tools (F1 Insights, StatBot) are actively enhancing the broadcast product and production efficiency without threatening the core commercial-rights model [2].
The bear case centers on earnings quality, leadership stability, and valuation. Trailing earnings growth was actually negative in the most recent periods (-43.6% YoY, -49.7% quarterly) even as headline revenue surged, producing a Forward P/E of 48.0x and an extreme Forward PEG of 12.5x that score at the floor of the Infrastructure algorithm [1][6]. ROE of 2.7% and profit margin of 5.5% are weak for the asset's scale, reflecting MotoGP integration costs and lumpy quarter-to-quarter revenue recognition tied to race-calendar timing. Greg Maffei — the executive most associated with the 'Drive to Survive' growth strategy — is the fourth senior Liberty Media leader to depart since the 2017 acquisition, and directors have been meaningful net sellers of FWONK stock in 2026 (Chase Carey's $9.03M sale, Renee Wilm's 42.66%-of-position sale), raising questions about insider conviction at current prices [3][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Turkish Grand Prix returning in 2027 under a new multi-year agreement, adding to a race calendar already delivering record attendance (6.75M, +4%) and viewership (+21%)
  2. Continued AI-driven production efficiency (crew reductions from hundreds to 40-50 people per broadcast) and AWS F1 Insights fan-engagement tools supporting margin expansion
  3. Resolution of senior leadership transition following Greg Maffei's 2024 departure, providing clarity on strategic continuity for the 'Drive to Survive' commercial playbook
Key Risks
  1. Forward PEG of 12.5x and Forward P/E of 48.0x are extreme against recent negative trailing earnings growth (-43.6% YoY), leaving the stock vulnerable to multiple compression if profitability doesn't catch up to revenue growth
  2. Four senior Liberty Media executive departures since the 2017 F1 acquisition, including CEO Greg Maffei, plus meaningful director-level insider selling in 2026 (Chase Carey $9.03M, Renee Wilm 42.66% of her position)
  3. Speculation about a potential sale of F1 by Liberty Media introduces strategic uncertainty for FWONK tracking-stock holders independent of the underlying sport's commercial performance
