Company Profile

Genpact Limited is a global professional services firm delivering digital operations, analytics, and AI-powered solutions to enterprise clients across banking, insurance, manufacturing, healthcare, and life sciences. Originally spun off from General Electric in 2005, the company has transformed from a traditional business process outsourcing provider into an AI-first operations platform under CEO BK Kalra, with its Advanced Technology Solutions (ATS) segment growing 24.1% YoY in Q2 2026. Genpact reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.34B (+7.1% YoY) and adjusted EPS of $1.00, guiding at least 7% full-year revenue growth with 12%+ EPS growth to $3.48.

Investment Thesis
Genpact at 6.75x forward earnings represents a compelling value proposition in the IT services space, with the stock trading at a 53% discount to its 10-year average P/E of 17.1 [5]. The accelerating ATS segment (+24.1% in Q2, guided 25%+ for FY2026) validates the company's AI-first transformation strategy, while the core business delivers steady 7% revenue growth with margin expansion [1]. ROE of 23.12% demonstrates efficient capital deployment, and the dividend — yielding 2.03% with a 21.4% payout ratio and 10.5% five-year growth CAGR — offers a growing income stream backed by healthy cash generation. The Agentic Operations pivot positions Genpact to capture enterprise AI spending that is still in early innings, with 130,000 employees providing domain expertise that pure AI tools cannot replicate.
The bear case centers on AI disruption risk and modest top-line growth. Genpact's core BPO operations face genuine substitution risk as agentic AI platforms from Microsoft, Salesforce, and ServiceNow automate transactional workflows that generate a significant portion of current revenue [3]. The 7% revenue growth rate, while stable, is below IT services peers delivering double-digit growth, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of 5.7% suggests the AI transformation has not yet materially accelerated overall growth [2]. Profit margin of 11.04% is structurally constrained by the labor-intensive services model, and if AI reduces the need for human-in-the-loop operations, revenue could compress faster than cost savings materialize. The departure of long-tenured CEO Tiger Tyagarajan and the relative newness of BK Kalra's leadership introduces execution risk on a transformation that requires cultural change across 130,000 employees.
