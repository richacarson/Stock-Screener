Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·United States
Glacier Bancorp is a Montana-based bank holding company operating through Glacier Bank, providing commercial and retail banking services across the Northwestern United States. Founded in 1955 and headquartered in Kalispell, Montana, the company has grown through 17 community bank acquisitions into a $28B+ asset institution serving individuals, small to medium-sized businesses, and public entities. Q2 2026 revenue reached $317.5M (+32% YoY) with net income of $97.9M (+85% YoY), driven by net interest margin expansion and 15% loan portfolio growth.

Investment Thesis
Glacier Bancorp is experiencing a powerful earnings recovery driven by NIM expansion, with Q2 2026 net income surging 85% YoY to $97.9M on revenue of $317.5M (+32% YoY) [1]. First-half 2026 net income reached $180M, up 68% from the prior year, as the loan portfolio grew 15% and deposits rose 14% [1]. The forward P/E of 12.3x is attractive relative to the earnings growth trajectory, and the 3.18% dividend yield provides income while the franchise recovers [4]. Glacier's community banking model across eight Northwestern states, built through 17 acquisitions preserving local brand identities, creates relationship-based moats that are difficult to replicate. The company's conservative lending culture (CRE-focused with local expertise) and $1B+ cash position provide downside protection. CEO Chesler's 9.5-year tenure delivers leadership stability, and the Inspire Impact score of 38 (ALIGNED) with 8 positive attributions reflects strong ESG positioning [3].
The bear case centers on structural and competitive challenges. ROE of 7.9% remains well below the 15% community bank target, indicating the franchise has not yet fully leveraged its scale [4]. The dividend was cut from $1.42 to $1.32 in 2023 and has remained flat for three years, with a 67.6% payout ratio limiting capacity for increases until earnings fully recover. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately 4.0% reflects the NIM compression years (2023-2024) that temporarily depressed the franchise's earnings power. Glassdoor's 3.8 parent company rating with 3.3 culture score suggests organizational challenges that could impact talent retention as the banking industry competes for digital and technology skills [2]. The stock trades at 1.33x book value — reasonable but not cheap for a community bank with sub-8% ROE. Fintech disruption and digital banking adoption among younger demographics represent medium-term competitive risks to the branch-heavy model, and any return to the 2023-2024 rate compression environment would reverse the current earnings momentum.
