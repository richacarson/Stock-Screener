Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical - Healthcare Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
GoodRx operates the leading U.S. digital platform for comparing prescription drug prices and accessing negotiated discount codes usable at pharmacies nationwide, monetized primarily through fees from pharmacy benefit managers. It also runs Pharma Direct (manufacturer-sponsored savings and affordability programs), the GoodRx Gold subscription, and telehealth services. Under CEO Wendy Barnes (since January 2025), the mix is shifting toward Pharma Direct, which grew 76% YoY in Q2 2026 while the legacy prescription-transaction business declined. Market cap is roughly $1.2B at $3.55 per share.

Investment Thesis
GoodRx is moving from a PBM-dependent coupon business toward manufacturer-funded Pharma Direct and subscriptions. Pharma Direct revenue rose 76% to $61.6M and subscriptions rose 39% to $28.5M in Q2 2026, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $790-805M revenue and $240-250M adjusted EBITDA [1][2]. At $3.55 the stock trades at about 8x EV/EBITDA, roughly 12x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $0.30, and a 12% free-cash-flow yield [3]. Debt is modest at 83% of equity, and buybacks have cut the share count from about 412M in 2022 to about 339M [3].
The bear case is that the core prescription-transaction business keeps shrinking. Total revenue fell 1.3% YoY in Q2 2026 despite Pharma Direct's growth, and TTM GAAP net margin is only 2.1% with ROE of 2.6% [1][3]. Retail pharmacy closures, PBM contract renegotiations, and AI search tools that surface drug prices directly all threaten the traffic engine. Repeated CEO turnover and a past health-data privacy controversy raise governance and execution risk [6][9]. The Inspire score of -49 (MISALIGNED, abortion activism) is a significant concern for faith-based mandates.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results in early November: a return to YoY revenue growth, which management signaled could come earlier than planned [2]
  2. Pharma Direct reaching more than 70% growth for FY2026 as GLP-1 and other branded cash-pay programs scale [2][10]
  3. Continued share buybacks funded by about $146M of TTM free cash flow, a 12% FCF yield [3]
Key Risks
  1. Continued decline of the legacy prescription-transaction business as pharmacy closures and PBM economics shift [1]
  2. AI assistants and search answers surfacing drug prices directly and cutting consumer traffic [5]
  3. Leadership instability and privacy or regulatory scrutiny over the use of consumer health data [6][9]
