Company Profile
Technology ·Data Center Services ·NASDAQ ·China
GDS Holdings Limited is a leading developer and operator of high-performance data centers in China and Southeast Asia. Founded by William Wei Huang in 2006, the company operates dozens of data centers across Greater China including in Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen, Suzhou, Chengdu, and Hong Kong. Q1 2026 revenue was RMB 3,367M (US$488M, +23.6% YoY) and Q2 2026 revenue was RMB 3,088M (US$455M, +6.5% YoY) with the company swinging to net income of RMB 837.6M in Q2. FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to RMB 12.70-13.00B driven by AI-related data center demand. GDS secured a $1.2B Series B equity raise led by SoftBank Vision Fund to fund 1GW+ of new capacity. Market cap is approximately $6.5 billion.

Investment Thesis
GDS Holdings at $31.32 is a China-based data center operator riding the AI infrastructure supercycle — Q1 2026 revenue surged 23.6% YoY to RMB 3,367M, the company swung to Q2 net income of RMB 837.6M (vs. a year-ago loss), and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to RMB 12.70-13.00B explicitly driven by AI-related demand [1][2]. The $1.2B Series B raise from SoftBank Vision Fund and Kenneth Griffin validates the platform and funds 1GW of new AI-ready capacity [3]. As China's largest carrier-neutral data center operator, GDS holds a structural position in the AI infrastructure stack that is extremely difficult to replicate — the permitting, power procurement, and interconnection relationships represent years of accumulated advantage. The AI Resilience score of 9 reflects GDS's position as a direct beneficiary of AI proliferation rather than a disruption target.
The bear case centers on profitability sustainability, China risk, and capital intensity. The Forward P/E of 143.88x suggests analysts remain skeptical that the Q2 2026 profitability will sustain — the TTM net margin is still -0.90%, and the swing to profit may reflect one-time items rather than structural margin expansion [5]. Debt-to-equity of 142.64% is elevated for any business, and the data center industry's insatiable capital requirements mean GDS must continually raise debt and equity to fund growth, diluting existing shareholders. China geopolitical risk is the elephant in the room — U.S.-China tensions, potential data localization mandates, and regulatory uncertainty around foreign investment in Chinese technology infrastructure create a structural discount that may not resolve. The ROE of 4.84% confirms that despite revenue growth, GDS has not yet demonstrated it can generate adequate returns on the massive capital deployed. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) is neutral for faith-based investors.
