Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
GE Aerospace designs and produces commercial and defense aircraft engines, integrated engine components, electric power, and aircraft systems. The company operates through Commercial Engines & Services (LEAP, GE90, GEnx, CF6, CFM56) and Defense & Propulsion Technologies segments, with a $210B+ backlog serving airlines, lessors, and defense customers worldwide. CEO Larry Culp continues to run the company on the FLIGHT DECK lean operating model following the 2023-2024 GE HealthCare and GE Vernova spinoffs.

Investment Thesis
GE Aerospace continues to deliver an aerospace super-cycle beat, with Q1 2026 revenue up 25% YoY, orders up 87% to a record level, and Defense & Propulsion Technologies posting a book-to-bill above 2.0x for a second consecutive quarter [1]. The $210B+ total backlog — including a $170B commercial services annuity on an aging global fleet — provides multi-year revenue visibility that AI or economic cycles cannot easily erode, and full-year 2026 guidance was raised across revenue, EPS ($7.65-7.85), operating profit, and FCF [1]. The CFM LEAP engine's dominance (100% of 737 MAX, >55% of A320neo) anchors a durable competitive moat, and R&D spend rising 22% YoY (to $440M in Q1) signals continued investment in RISE next-gen propulsion and hypersonics [6]. The GE Aerospace Foundation's $22.5M 2025 giving and new $30M multi-year workforce program demonstrate genuine community reinvestment alongside the operating turnaround [3].
The bear case centers on valuation and governance friction. Forward P/E of 41.64x and PEG of 2.40 leave little margin of safety for any deceleration in aviation demand, and senior executives (SVP Mohamed Ali, EVP Robert Giglietti) have sold multi-million-dollar stakes in July/August 2026 near current levels with no offsetting purchases identified [4]. D/E of 113% is elevated, and a shareholder proxy battle over GE Aerospace's Israel defense sales has emerged as a governance flashpoint that could strain customer relationships or invite reputational scrutiny regardless of its outcome [5]. Rising engineering turnover among individual contributors, noted in recent employee reviews, is a soft signal worth monitoring as the company scales LEAP production toward 2,000 annual deliveries. The impact of the GE HealthCare and GE Vernova spinoffs makes historical 5-year growth trends not meaningful, requiring investors to underwrite the newer, narrower standalone entity on a shorter track record.
Key Catalysts
  1. Defense & Propulsion Technologies book-to-bill above 2.0x for a second consecutive quarter and record decade defense orders position the segment for accelerating multi-year revenue as the $210B+ total backlog converts [1]
  2. LEAP engine delivery ramp toward a 2,000-unit 2026 target, combined with R&D spend up 22% YoY funding RISE next-gen propulsion, sustains GE Aerospace's structural moat in narrowbody engines [1][6]
  3. $170B commercial services backlog from an aging global fleet provides a multi-decade maintenance annuity largely insulated from near-term macro or AI disruption risk [1]
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 41.64x and PEG of 2.40 leave minimal margin of safety — any deceleration in orders or a broader aviation demand slowdown would likely trigger a significant multiple compression [6]
  2. Senior-executive insider selling (SVP and EVP transactions totaling ~$6.6M in July/August 2026) with no offsetting purchases is a soft but persistent signal worth monitoring [4]
  3. A shareholder proxy battle over Israel defense contract sales and rising engineering turnover among individual contributors both represent governance and talent-retention risks that could affect execution as production scales [3][5]
