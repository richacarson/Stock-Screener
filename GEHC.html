Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
GE HealthCare Technologies designs, manufactures, and markets medical imaging equipment (MRI, CT, X-ray, ultrasound), patient monitoring systems, pharmaceutical diagnostics (contrast agents), and advanced visualization solutions. Spun off from GE in January 2023, the company operates globally under CEO Peter Arduini. GEHC leads the industry with 115 FDA-cleared AI-enabled medical devices. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $5.3B with 3.5% organic growth, record orders (11.1% organic growth), and a record $23.9B backlog. Market cap approximately $32B.

Investment Thesis
GEHC at 12.2x forward earnings offers compelling value for a healthcare equipment leader delivering record orders and backlog acceleration — Q2 2026 organic orders grew 11.1% (highest since spin) with total backlog reaching a record $23.9B, up $2.6B YoY and $2.1B sequentially, providing multi-year revenue visibility [1]. The company's 115 FDA-cleared AI-enabled devices — more than any competitor — create meaningful technology differentiation in purchasing decisions for hospital systems upgrading aging imaging fleets. Adjusted EPS guidance of $4.95-$5.15 (+8-13% YoY) with margin expansion of 50-80bps demonstrates improving profitability, while ROE of 19.46% and the conservative 3% dividend payout ratio signal capital efficiency and reinvestment capacity [2][5]. The secular tailwind of AI-enhanced imaging, pharmaceutical diagnostics growth, and global healthcare infrastructure investment support durable mid-single-digit organic growth with earnings growth leveraged to operating margin expansion.
The bear case centers on modest top-line growth, leverage, and execution risk. Organic revenue growth of 3-4% is below the 10% infrastructure scoring target and reflects the mature, capital-equipment nature of the healthcare imaging market — GEHC is not a high-growth business, and the stock's 12.2x forward P/E partially reflects this reality [2]. The $10.6B debt load (D/E 97%) inherited from GE limits financial flexibility and creates meaningful interest expense that constrains profit margin improvement from the current 9.10% [5]. The April 2026 guidance cut (crashing the stock 13%) revealed vulnerability to tariff impacts and margin pressure from input costs, and Q2 2026 still faced margin headwinds despite record orders [1]. Revenue concentration in imaging equipment creates cyclicality exposure to hospital capital spending budgets, which can be deferred during economic downturns.
