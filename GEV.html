Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
GE Vernova is a global energy company providing products and services that generate, transfer, orchestrate, convert, and store electricity. Operating through Power, Wind, and Electrification segments, it serves the global energy transition with gas turbines, wind turbines, grid solutions, and energy storage. Spun off from GE Aerospace in April 2024, the stock has risen over 600% since IPO. CEO Scott Strazik has driven a remarkable turnaround — FY2025 revenue of $38B grew to an expected $45.5-$46.5B in 2026, with a $176B backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility fueled by AI data center power demand.

Investment Thesis
GE Vernova is the premier pure-play energy infrastructure beneficiary of AI-driven power demand, with a $176B backlog providing over 15 quarters of revenue visibility [1][2]. The financial trajectory is exceptional: FY2025 revenue of $38B is guided to $45.5-$46.5B in 2026 (+20%), with 2028 targets of $56B revenue, 20% EBITDA margin, and $22B+ cumulative FCF [1]. Q1 2026 orders surged 71% organically to $18.3B — a book-to-bill ratio near 2.0x that signals accelerating demand [1]. ROE of 82.58%, profit margin of 23.04%, and a net cash position of $9B ($12.72B cash vs. $3.72B debt) demonstrate a balance sheet and profitability profile that is rare among large-cap industrials [5]. CEO Strazik's turnaround from GE legacy to focused energy growth story — evidenced by a 600%+ stock gain since the April 2024 IPO — and partnerships with NVIDIA and Blue Energy position the company at the intersection of AI infrastructure and the energy transition [3].
The primary risk is valuation — at 36.53x forward earnings and 1.12 PEG, the stock prices in substantial and sustained growth [5]. Any deceleration in AI data center buildout, utility capital spending cycles, or order growth could trigger significant multiple compression from the current industrial-sector premium. The Wind segment, while improving, has historically been the margin drag and faces offshore execution risks, supply chain challenges, and policy sensitivity to renewable energy subsidies [1]. Greenwashing criticism around the company's promotion of fossil gas while claiming green credentials creates reputational risk, particularly for ESG-oriented investors — though the Inspire Impact Score of 58 (ALIGNED) across 22 positive categories is strong [4]. Employee trust concerns ('leaders directly misleading teams,' 'broken promises') and the Power segment CEO resignation suggest cultural integration from the GE legacy is incomplete [3]. At $940 per share with a negligible 0.21% dividend yield, investors are paying for growth execution — any miss on the PROPEL 2028 targets would be severely punished.
