Company Profile
Energy ·Renewable Fuels ·NASDAQ ·United States
Gevo, Inc. is a next-generation diversified energy company focused on producing sustainable aviation fuel (SAF), renewable natural gas (RNG), and other renewable products. The company operates Gevo RNG, which generates renewable natural gas from dairy farm manure through anaerobic digesters, and is constructing the Net-Zero 1 (NZ1) commercial-scale SAF facility in Lake Preston, South Dakota, backed by a $1.46B DOE loan guarantee. Revenue reached $161M in FY2025.

Investment Thesis
Gevo is positioned at the intersection of two powerful secular trends: airline decarbonization mandates and U.S. energy security policy. The RNG business is generating real revenue ($161M FY2025, Q1 2026 up 48% YoY) and approaching profitability with $9M adjusted EBITDA in Q1 2026 and a run-rate target of $40M [1]. The NZ1 plant, backed by $1.46B in DOE loan guarantees, will produce 60M gallons of SAF annually with co-products generating additional revenue from protein, animal feed, and corn oil [2]. With airlines globally facing carbon reduction mandates and SAF supply severely constrained, Gevo's first-mover ATJ technology positions it to capture significant value as the market scales from <1% of jet fuel today toward 10%+ by 2030.
The bear case centers on execution risk, capital intensity, and political uncertainty. At $1.41 per share with a $335M market cap, GEVO is a speculative bet on the successful construction and operation of NZ1 — a technology that has never been proven at commercial scale [3]. The company carries $170M in debt against $79M in cash, and NZ1 construction will dramatically increase leverage through the $1.46B DOE facility [3]. Net losses of $22M in Q1 2026 (-$0.09/share) persist despite revenue growth, and the path to profitability depends entirely on NZ1 achieving design capacity. Political risk is significant as changes in SAF tax credits, renewable fuel standards, or DOE loan program priorities could undermine the project's economics. The stock's vulnerability to sentiment shifts is amplified by its low price and retail-heavy shareholder base.
Key Catalysts
  1. NZ1 construction milestones and progress toward commercial operation, with 60M gallon annual SAF capacity representing transformative revenue potential upon commissioning
  2. RNG business approaching run-rate adjusted EBITDA of $40M per year, potentially demonstrating standalone profitability and self-funding capability for corporate operations
  3. Airline SAF procurement mandates accelerating globally (EU ReFuelEU, CORSIA) creating binding demand for the limited SAF supply that NZ1 will produce
Key Risks
  1. NZ1 commercial-scale ATJ technology has never been proven at the planned 60M gallon capacity, creating execution risk on timeline, cost, and production yields
  2. Political risk from potential changes to SAF tax credits (Section 40B), renewable fuel standards, or DOE loan program priorities that could undermine NZ1 project economics
  3. Persistent net losses (-$22M in Q1 2026) and low stock price ($1.41) create dilution risk from potential capital raises and vulnerability to retail investor sentiment shifts
