New CEO Dr. Paul Bloom succeeded founder Dr. Patrick Gruber in April 2026, and the company is expanding with the Gevo North Dakota facility. Investment Thesis
Gevo at $1.41 per share offers asymmetric upside exposure to the decarbonization of transportation fuels, with the business crossing critical inflection points — Q2 2026 revenue of $47M, fourth consecutive quarter of positive adjusted EBITDA ($11M), and full-year EBITDA outlook raised to >$60M from the prior $30M target [1]. The carbon credit business is now running at $30M+ annual revenue, providing high-margin recurring income that could approach profitability on its own. Revenue trajectory from $0.5M (2021) to $160.6M (2025) to an estimated $188M (2026) demonstrates commercial scale-up is working [2]. The Gevo North Dakota expansion, new CEO Dr. Paul Bloom with deep renewable fuels expertise, and the secular tailwind of sustainable aviation fuel mandates (EU ReFuelEU, US SAF Grand Challenge) provide multi-year growth runway. The Inspire Impact Score of 61 (ALIGNED) with 15 positive attributions and zero negatives makes this one of the strongest faith-alignment profiles in the screening universe.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability, execution risk, and the micro-cap nature of the business. Despite $160.6M in 2025 revenue, Gevo posted a -19.4% GAAP profit margin and -6.79% ROE — adjusted EBITDA improvement masks ongoing GAAP losses that could persist through the North Dakota facility build-out [5]. At $1.41 per share and $343M market cap, the stock is a penny stock with significant dilution risk — the company has repeatedly issued equity (shares outstanding grew from ~35M to ~247M), and additional capital raises will likely be needed to fund the North Dakota expansion [5]. The CEO transition from founder Gruber to Bloom introduces execution risk at a critical juncture, followed by executive restructuring just four months later [3]. The sustainable aviation fuel market remains dependent on government mandates and subsidies; any policy reversal (particularly in the US) could undermine the fundamental economics. Revenue concentration risk exists as the 849% YoY growth in 2025 was acquisition-driven, and organic growth in H1 2026 is decelerating (Q1 +48%, Q2 +7%).
