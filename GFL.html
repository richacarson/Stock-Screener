GFL Environmental Inc. is the third-largest waste management company in North America, providing solid waste collection, recycling, and liquid waste management services across Canada and the United States. The company has grown through 100+ acquisitions since founding, recently divesting its Environmental Services unit for $8B to focus on core solid waste and liquid waste operations. Founded in 2007, headquartered in Vaughan, Ontario, Canada.

Investment Thesis
GFL Environmental is the third-largest waste management company in North America, delivering record Q1 2026 EBITDA margins with management raising adjusted EBITDA guidance by $90M to $2,230M (~30.6% margin) [1]. The $8B Environmental Services divestiture in 2025 is deleveraging the $7.1B debt load and refocusing the portfolio on higher-margin solid waste and liquid waste operations. Core pricing in the mid-5% range demonstrates strong pricing power in a rational oligopoly alongside Waste Management and Republic Services. The waste management industry is structurally advantaged — essential service, high barriers to entry through landfill permits and franchise agreements, recession-resistant demand, and complete immunity to AI disruption. AI route optimization and recycling sorting automation are driving incremental margin expansion [2].
The bear case centers on leverage, valuation, and organizational culture. D/E of 135% and $7.1B total debt create heavy interest expense that compresses net margins to 3.12% despite 30%+ EBITDA margins [4]. Forward P/E of 54.35 is extremely elevated for a company growing revenue in the high-single-digits organically, trading at a steep premium to Waste Management (~28x) and Republic Services (~30x) despite weaker margins and higher leverage. ROE of 3.28% does not generate adequate returns on equity relative to cost of capital. The trailing P/E of 5.35 reflects a one-time $3.83B gain from the Environmental Services divestiture and will normalize dramatically. Glassdoor culture scores of 3.0/5 with reports of hostility, sexism, and 'big egos' indicate organizational dysfunction that could drive field employee turnover and execution risk [2]. CEO Dovigi's dual-class share structure concentrating control until 2040 limits governance accountability.
Key Catalysts
  1. Raised FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance by $90M to $2,230M with record margins of ~30.6%, driven by mid-5% core pricing power and AI-enabled operational efficiency [1]

Key Risks

  2. Forward P/E of 54.35 is a steep premium to Waste Management (~28x) and Republic Services (~30x), creating significant multiple compression risk if organic growth disappoints mid-single-digit expectations [4]
  3. Glassdoor culture ratings of 3.0/5 with reports of hostility, sexism, and dysfunction indicate organizational trust deficits that could drive field employee turnover across 15,000+ workers, compounded by governance concerns over dual-class share structure [2]

Solid waste collection involves route-based truck operations across thousands of municipalities with local franchise agreements, environmental compliance requirements, and capital-intensive assets.
