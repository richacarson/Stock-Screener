Company Profile
Industrials ·Waste Management ·NYSE ·United States
GFL Environmental is the fourth-largest diversified environmental services company in North America, providing solid waste collection, transfer, recycling, and disposal services across Canada and the United States, alongside soil remediation and liquid waste management. Founded and led by CEO Patrick Dovigi, GFL has grown primarily through acquisition, most recently the $4.6B pending Secure Waste hazardous-waste deal, while divesting the non-core Green Infrastructure Partners business for $4.25B in 2025. FY2025 revenue reached $6.62B with record adjusted EBITDA margin of 30%, and 2026 guidance calls for ~$7.5B in revenue.

Investment Thesis
GFL Environmental has built a genuine #4 position in North American environmental services through disciplined acquisition, delivering a record 30% adjusted EBITDA margin and 23.6% adjusted free cash flow growth to $755.9M in 2025, with 2026 guidance calling for further margin expansion to ~30.6% on ~$7.5B in revenue [1]. The waste-collection business model is structurally insulated from AI disruption — physical collection and landfill capacity cannot be automated away — while RNG and EPR-related initiatives create incremental, higher-margin growth adjacencies [2]. The 2025 divestiture of the non-core Green Infrastructure Partners unit for $4.25B, redeployed toward the pending $4.6B Secure Waste hazardous-waste acquisition, shows disciplined portfolio management focused on core competency.
The bear case is twofold: financial and governance. GFL posted a GAAP net loss with ROE of -2.25% and profit margin of -2.87% despite record EBITDA margins, reflecting the heavy depreciation and interest burden of its debt-funded roll-up strategy, and D/E of 134.5% leaves less room for error if acquisition integration underperforms [4]. More seriously, governance disclosures reveal $14.3M in 2025 lease payments to related parties including entities controlled by CEO Dovigi and an unnamed director without full transparency, a dual-class share structure giving Dovigi 25.5% voting control on under 6% economic ownership, and shareholder opposition of 36% on Say-on-Pay and over 20% on four governance-committee directors [3]. These are the kinds of related-party and control-concentration red flags that historically precede either activist pressure or a governance-driven derating, and they weigh heavily on GFL's Infinite Game and Inspiration scores independent of the company's legitimate operational execution.
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful integration of the pending $4.6B Secure Waste hazardous-waste acquisition, extending GFL into a higher-margin adjacent category and supporting the ~$7.5B 2026 revenue guidance [1][2]
  2. Continued RNG project buildout and EPR policy tailwinds translating into incremental EBITDA margin expansion beyond the record 30% achieved in 2025 [2]
  3. Deleveraging from the $4.25B Green Infrastructure Partners divestiture proceeds improving the D/E ratio of 134.5% and creating capacity for GAAP profitability to catch up to EBITDA margin gains [1][4]
Key Risks

Undisclosed related-party lease payments ($14.3M in 2025) to entities controlled by CEO Dovigi and an unnamed director, combined with a dual-class structure giving Dovigi 25.5% voting control on under 6% economic ownership, are governance red flags that could trigger activist pressure, litigation, or a valuation derating [3]
  2. GAAP net losses (ROE -2.25%, profit margin -2.87%) despite record EBITDA margins show heavy debt-service and depreciation burden from the acquisition-funded growth model; a slowdown in accretive M&A could expose the gap between EBITDA and true earnings power [4]
  3. D/E of 134.5% leaves reduced flexibility if the pending Secure Waste integration underperforms or credit markets tighten, given GFL's continued reliance on debt-funded acquisitions [4]
