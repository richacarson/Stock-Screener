Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NYQ ·Brazil
Gerdau S.A. is the largest long steel producer in the Americas and the world's largest recycler of scrap steel, founded in 1901 in Porto Alegre, Brazil. The company operates across Brazil, North America, and South America segments, producing rebars, bars, wires, plates, and structural shapes primarily via electric arc furnaces (71% scrap-based). Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA reached BRL 3.4B — strongest since Q3 2023 — with net income of BRL 1.5B (+45% QoQ). TTM revenue of BRL 69.5B (~$12.7B USD), market cap $9.8B.

Investment Thesis
GGB at $5.01 with 8.7x forward earnings represents a deeply discounted play on North American infrastructure and steel cycle recovery. Q2 2026 delivered the strongest consolidated EBITDA since Q3 2023 at BRL 3.4B, with North American shipments up 7% YoY and EBITDA surging 15% sequentially, while net income rose 45% QoQ to BRL 1.5B [1]. The balance sheet is fortress-grade (D/E 28.2%) with ample liquidity, and first-half 2026 free cash flow exceeded H1 2025 by BRL 2.3B. As the Americas' largest long steel producer with 71% EAF/scrap-based production, Gerdau benefits from structural cost and environmental advantages, and AI-driven process optimization is delivering quantified savings of $3/ton in alloy costs [2]. The dividend yield of 3.6% provides income while investors wait for cyclical margin recovery.
The bear case centers on cyclical margin compression and dividend deterioration. Profit margin of 3.2% and ROE of 4.2% reflect trough conditions — trailing P/E of 22.8x versus forward 8.7x signals that consensus expects significant earnings improvement that may not materialize if steel demand weakens [1]. Dividends have collapsed from $0.56/share in 2022 to approximately $0.08 in 2025, an 85%+ decline that undermines the income thesis despite the headline yield. Revenue growth of just 2% YoY on a TTM basis demonstrates the mature, cyclical nature of the business. As a Brazilian ADR, investors bear BRL/USD currency risk — continued Real depreciation erodes USD-denominated returns even if BRL results improve. Chinese steel overcapacity and aggressive export dumping remain structural overhangs for global steel pricing, and potential U.S. tariff policy shifts could disrupt the North American margin advantage that drives 74% of EBITDA [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. North American infrastructure spending acceleration — 74% of EBITDA from North America, with the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act plus data center construction creating multi-year demand tailwind for long steel products, Q2 2026 shipments already up 7% YoY [1]
  2. Cyclical earnings recovery with forward P/E of 8.7x versus trailing 22.8x — Q2 2026 EBITDA at strongest since Q3 2023 and first-half FCF up BRL 2.3B YoY suggest inflection point in the steel cycle [1]
  3. AI-driven cost optimization scaling across operations — $3/ton alloy cost reduction and 15% quality variation improvement delivering measurable margin expansion as Chief Data & AI Officer scales deployment to additional plants [2]
Key Risks
  1. Steel cyclicality with 3.2% profit margin — commodity price swings can compress margins to breakeven within 2-3 quarters, and dividends have already collapsed 85%+ from 2022 peak with no guarantee earnings recovery will restore them [1]
  2. BRL/USD currency exposure — majority of revenue in BRL while the ADR trades in USD; continued Brazilian Real depreciation directly erodes USD-denominated returns and Gerdau cannot control Brazil's volatile economic and political environment
  3. Chinese steel overcapacity and trade tensions — China produces over 50% of global steel and has been aggressively exporting excess capacity, pressuring global steel prices and threatening margins for Americas-based producers including Gerdau [1]
