Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NASDAQ ·Israel
Gilat Satellite Networks Ltd. is a global provider of satellite-based broadband communications solutions, serving defense, homeland security, in-flight connectivity (IFC), and enterprise markets. The company's SkyEdge platform powers fixed and mobile satellite networks worldwide, and its DataPath subsidiary provides ruggedized SATCOM terminals for U.S. and allied military forces. In June 2026, Gilat announced the $157.5M acquisition of Comtech's Satellite & Space Communications segment, which will push combined annual revenue above $700M and more than double defense revenues. Headquartered in Petah Tikva, Israel.

Investment Thesis
Gilat is executing a transformative growth strategy anchored by the $157.5M Comtech Satellite & Space acquisition, which will push combined revenue above $700M and more than double defense revenues [4]. FY2025 revenue surged 48% to $451.7M, Q2 2026 delivered $122.7M (up 17% YoY), and 2026 guidance of $500-520M implies continued double-digit organic growth [1][2]. The defense SATCOM market is experiencing secular growth driven by satellite modernization programs and contested environment communications needs, with Gilat's DataPath securing new U.S. DoD orders [3]. AI-powered network management and interference detection differentiate the platform [5]. The balance sheet entering the Comtech deal was pristine — $171M cash, $7.5M debt — and the acquisition is funded entirely from cash.
The primary risk is integration execution and valuation. The Comtech deal consumes virtually all of Gilat's cash reserves, leaving no margin for integration missteps. Comtech's satellite segment generated only 8.6% EBITDA margins ($16.8M on $195.2M TTM revenue), which Gilat must improve to justify the $157.5M purchase price [4]. The forward P/E of 38.1 and PEG of 4.33 price in substantial growth expectations — any delay in Comtech integration, defense contract timing, or IFC market softness could trigger significant multiple compression. Net profit margin of 6.8% remains thin for a company trading at growth multiples, and the CFIUS review required for an Israeli company acquiring U.S. defense assets introduces regulatory uncertainty [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Comtech acquisition closing by year-end 2026, expected to double defense revenues and push combined annual revenue above $700M with synergy-driven margin improvement
  2. Defense SATCOM demand acceleration driven by military satellite modernization, contested environment requirements, and DataPath's expanding U.S. DoD relationship with $11M in recent orders
  3. AI-powered network management and interference detection/cancellation technology creating competitive differentiation in a market transitioning toward software-defined satellite communications
Key Risks
  1. Comtech integration risk — the $157.5M acquisition consumes nearly all cash reserves, and improving Comtech's thin 8.6% EBITDA margin is essential to value creation; any integration delays would strain both operations and the balance sheet
  2. Forward P/E of 38.1 and PEG of 4.33 price in sustained high-teens growth — any execution miss against $500-520M 2026 guidance or Comtech synergy shortfall could compress the multiple significantly
  3. CFIUS and regulatory review risk for an Israeli-based company acquiring U.S. defense satellite assets, with potential for conditions or delays that could alter the deal economics

Satellite communications infrastructure is fundamentally physical — ground stations, ruggedized military terminals, flat-panel antennas, and modem hardware cannot be substituted by AI. Defense SATCOM systems operate in regulated, security-cleared environments requiring physical deployment in operational theaters [3].
