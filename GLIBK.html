Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Liberty Capital Corporation (formerly GCI Liberty, Inc.) is Alaska's largest telecommunications provider, offering wireless, broadband, voice, and managed services across the state. Founded as GCI Communication in 1979 by Ron Duncan and Bob Walp, the company was spun off from Liberty Broadband and renamed Liberty Capital in May 2026. GLIBK represents the Series C shares. GCI operates Alaska's most extensive terrestrial fiber network and serves both consumer and enterprise markets. The company is backed by John Malone's Liberty empire and recently agreed to acquire Quintillion, an undersea fiber company, for $310M. Headquartered in Englewood, Colorado with operations in Anchorage, Alaska.

Investment Thesis
Liberty Capital Corporation (GLIBK) offers a unique investment thesis: Alaska's dominant telecom provider trading at just 8.82x forward earnings with $329M in TTM operating cash flow and $448M in cash, backed by John Malone's proven capital allocation expertise [1]. The $310M Quintillion undersea fiber acquisition positions GCI as the backbone of Alaska's digital infrastructure, with expected synergies and cash flow growth from expanded fiber capacity. The company's near-monopoly in Alaska (207,700 wireless lines, 150,500 cable modem subscribers) provides a durable revenue base with limited competitive threats given extreme geographic barriers to entry [1]. GCI guided to 'stable' 2026 operations with a new $60M annual dividend commencing Q4 2026, signaling management's confidence in cash flow sustainability. The GCI leverage ratio of 2.3x with $377M undrawn credit provides financial flexibility for the Quintillion integration and future investments.
The bear case centers on Alaska's structural growth limitations and governance complexity. Q1 2026 revenue declined 4% to $256M with adjusted OIBDA falling 18% to $93M, driven by the video business exit and mature consumer market headwinds — consumer cable modem subscribers declined 3% YoY [1]. The GAAP net loss of -$309M TTM (profit margin -29.4%) reflects significant non-operating charges that obscure otherwise healthy operating economics. The $290M 2026 capex commitment consumes the vast majority of operating cash flow, leaving limited free cash flow for debt reduction or shareholder returns beyond the planned $60M dividend. John Malone's consolidation of voting control and the rebrand to Liberty Capital raise questions about whether GCI's Alaska-centric mission will be maintained or diluted by broader corporate portfolio strategy [3]. Alaska's 730,000 population provides a hard ceiling on addressable subscribers, and the $310M Quintillion acquisition adds leverage to a company already managing $1.035B in debt. Average wireless and broadband ARPUs face the same pricing pressure as Lower 48 markets, but GCI's cost structure is inherently higher due to Alaska's infrastructure challenges.
