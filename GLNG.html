Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NASDAQ ·Bermuda
Golar LNG Limited designs, converts, owns, and operates marine infrastructure for LNG liquefaction. The company is the world's pioneer and market leader in Floating LNG (FLNG) technology, having developed the first FLNG conversion (Hilli, 2018) and deployed the FLNG Gimi. The fleet also includes the Mark II FLNG Esperanza under construction. Q2 2026 total operating revenue reached $130.5M (+72% YoY), driven by strong FLNG Gimi output and higher commodity-linked Hilli earnings. Adjusted EBITDA grew to $127.4M, up from $106M in Q1 2026 [1]. The company ordered a fourth FLNG unit in 2026, expanding fleet capacity 41%. Golar holds a contracted $17B EBITDA backlog (before commodity upside) through 2045 and projects annual EBITDA exceeding $1.2B by 2030 [1][2]. Founded in 1946 and headquartered in Hamilton, Bermuda.

Investment Thesis
Golar LNG is a unique pure-play FLNG operator with a contracted $17B EBITDA backlog through 2045 and management projecting annual EBITDA exceeding $1.2B by 2030 — providing extraordinary long-term revenue visibility for an energy infrastructure company [1]. Q2 2026 demonstrated strong execution with revenue of $130.5M (+72% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $127.4M, driven by FLNG Gimi's ramp and commodity-linked Hilli upside [1]. The ordering of a fourth FLNG unit, expanding fleet capacity 41%, positions Golar as the dominant global FLNG operator at a time when energy security concerns, data center power demand, and coal-to-gas switching drive growing LNG demand [1][2]. Profit margin of 31.3% reflects the contractual structure of FLNG leases with investment-grade counterparties. Golar pioneered FLNG technology and holds first-mover advantage in a capital-intensive, high-barrier niche with limited competition.
The bear case centers on execution risk, valuation distortion, and capital intensity. Forward P/E of 91.9x and PEG of 7.69x appear extremely expensive, though these metrics are distorted by heavy depreciation of FLNG assets that compress reported EPS while the underlying EBITDA grows strongly [4]. The $2.68B debt load and -$381M free cash flow reflect the capital-intensive fleet expansion phase — Golar is spending heavily to build FLNGs before they generate revenue, creating near-term cash flow stress [4]. Construction of FLNG units faces shipyard and supply-chain constraints, with cost inflation and schedule delays common in complex marine engineering projects. D/E of 122.3% is elevated and any construction overruns would further strain the balance sheet. The company's dividend was suspended from 2019-2022 and only recently resumed at $1.00/year (1.94% yield), reflecting the volatility of this capital-intensive business model. Country and counterparty risk is inherent in 20+ year contracts — political instability, resource nationalism, or counterparty default in developing-nation gas projects could impair contracted backlog [3]. LNG's long-term role faces uncertainty as renewable energy costs decline and environmental opposition to fossil infrastructure intensifies.
