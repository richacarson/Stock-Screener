Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NMS ·United States
Galaxy Digital, founded by Michael Novogratz in 2018 and headquartered in New York, operates as a diversified digital asset merchant bank spanning crypto trading, asset management, and investment banking, and has increasingly pivoted into AI/HPC data center infrastructure through its Helios campus in West Texas. The company redomiciled from the Cayman Islands to Delaware and moved its primary listing from the TSX Venture Exchange to Nasdaq in 2025. Helios Phase I (133 MW critical IT load) entered service under a 15-year lease to CoreWeave in 2026, with approved power capacity now exceeding 1.6 gigawatts and a development pipeline targeting up to 5.7 gigawatts. Q2 2026 reported a net loss of $85M driven by digital asset price depreciation, while the data center segment generated $11M adjusted EBITDA [1].

Investment Thesis
Galaxy Digital at $18.25 with a book value of $9.36/share (1.95x P/B) is executing one of the most consequential pivots in the digital asset sector — from a crypto merchant bank into an AI/HPC data center developer with a 15-year, $1B+ annual revenue lease to CoreWeave at projected ~90% EBITDA margins, backed by 1.6+ GW of ERCOT-approved power capacity and a pipeline targeting 5.7 GW [1][2][4]. Phase I of Helios (133 MW) is live and generated $11M adjusted EBITDA in Q2 2026, with Q3 2026 expected as the first full quarter of Phase I revenue — providing a contracted, AI-demand-linked cash flow stream that is largely decoupled from crypto price volatility [3]. Forbes characterized the pivot as a potential $20B opportunity for Novogratz, who controls ~80% of shares and is personally committed to the outcome [5]. The 2025 redomiciliation to Delaware and Nasdaq uplisting improved institutional capital access.
The bear case centers on persistent core-business losses, high leverage, and dual-business complexity. Q1 2026 produced a $216M net loss and Q2 2026 lost $85M as digital asset price depreciation overwhelmed data center gains, with ROE of -10.41% and profit margin of -0.42% [1][9]. D/E of 187% reflects leverage taken on to fund the capital-intensive Helios buildout, and the stock has declined 22.8% from August levels ($23.64 to $18.25) as crypto prices remained depressed. GAAP revenue of $58.4B TTM is almost entirely digital asset trading notional under gross accounting, making it a noisy and misleading indicator of underlying economics. Glassdoor reviews citing leadership making 'technical decisions without sufficient technical context' and poor work-life balance raise execution-risk questions for a company now managing two fundamentally different businesses [6]. Galaxy's investment case increasingly depends on flawless data-center execution and AI demand persistence, while the original crypto thesis contributes losses. Novogratz's ~80% ownership concentration creates key-man risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full ramp of Helios Phase I CoreWeave lease revenue beginning Q3 2026, the first full quarter of leasing income at a projected ~90% EBITDA margin on the 133 MW critical IT load, which should demonstrate the economic model for the remaining 1.5+ GW of approved capacity [3]
  2. Expansion toward 3.5 GW of contracted Helios capacity as CoreWeave exercises additional lease options, against a total site pipeline of up to 5.7 GW across Texas — each incremental GW represents a potential $1B+ annual revenue stream [4][5]
  3. Stabilization or recovery in Bitcoin and digital asset prices (Novogratz guided $60K-$80K Bitcoin range for H2 2026), which would restore profitability to the core trading and asset management segments and demonstrate the value of the dual-business model [5]
Key Risks
  1. Continued core-business losses ($216M Q1, $85M Q2 2026 net losses) tied to digital asset price depreciation outside management's control, with ROE of -10.41% and D/E of 187% creating financial stress if crypto recovery stalls [1][9]
  2. Elevated leverage funding a capital-intensive data center buildout creates execution and refinancing risk if AI data center demand plateaus, CoreWeave's own creditworthiness deteriorates (it recently went public), or power costs at Helios increase [4]
  3. Key-man concentration — Novogratz controls ~80% of shares and drives all major strategic decisions; any impairment of his involvement would leave the dual-business model without its primary architect, and Glassdoor cultural concerns raise questions about organizational depth [5][6]
