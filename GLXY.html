Company Profile
Financial Services ·Digital Assets & AI Data Center Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Galaxy Digital, founded by Michael Novogratz in 2018 and headquartered in New York, operates as a diversified digital asset merchant bank spanning crypto trading, asset management, and investment banking, and has increasingly pivoted into AI/HPC data center infrastructure through its Helios campus in West Texas. The company redomiciled from the Cayman Islands to Delaware and moved its primary listing from the TSX Venture Exchange to Nasdaq in 2025. Helios Phase I (133 MW critical IT load) entered service under a 15-year lease to CoreWeave in 2026, with approved power capacity now exceeding 1.6 gigawatts and a development pipeline targeting up to 5.7 gigawatts.

Investment Thesis
Galaxy Digital is executing one of the more consequential pivots in the digital-asset sector — from a crypto-only merchant bank into an AI/HPC data center developer with a 15-year, $1B+ annual revenue lease to CoreWeave at ~90% projected EBITDA margins, backed by 1.6+ GW of ERCOT-approved power capacity and a pipeline targeting 5.7 GW [1][2][4]. Phase I of the Helios campus (133 MW critical IT load) went fully live in 2026, and management expects Q3 2026 to be the first full quarter of Phase I leasing revenue, providing a tangible, contracted, AI-demand-linked cash flow stream that is largely decoupled from crypto price volatility [3]. The 2025 redomiciliation to Delaware and Nasdaq uplisting improved access to US institutional capital, and continued share buybacks signal management confidence.
The bear case centers on continued core-business unprofitability and leverage: Q2 2026 GAAP revenue of $8.56B declined ~1.2% YoY with an $85.3M net loss as digital-asset trading losses offset Data Center gains, and ROE (-10.41%) and profit margin (-0.42%) remain negative [8][9]. D/E of 187% reflects real leverage taken on to fund the capital-intensive Helios build-out, and the company's 'revenue' figure is heavily inflated by digital-asset trading notional volume, making it a noisy indicator of underlying earnings power. Glassdoor reviews citing leadership making 'technical decisions without sufficient technical context' and poor work-life balance raise execution-risk questions for a company now managing a complex, capital-intensive infrastructure build alongside its legacy trading business. The investment case increasingly depends on flawless data-center execution rather than the original crypto-merchant-bank thesis.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full ramp of Helios Phase I CoreWeave lease revenue beginning Q3 2026, the first full quarter of leasing income at a projected ~90% EBITDA margin on the 133 MW critical IT load already delivered [3]
  2. Expansion toward 3.5 GW of contracted Helios capacity as CoreWeave exercises additional lease options, against a total site pipeline of up to 5.7 GW across Texas [4][5]
  3. Stabilization or recovery in digital-asset prices, which would restore profitability to the core trading and asset management segments that generated the Q2 2026 net loss [8]
Key Risks
  1. Continued core-business unprofitability (ROE -10.41%, profit margin -0.42%, $85.3M Q2 2026 net loss) tied to digital-asset price volatility outside management's control [8][9]
  2. Elevated leverage (D/E 187%) funding a capital-intensive data center build-out, creating execution and refinancing risk if AI data center demand or CoreWeave's own creditworthiness deteriorates [4]
  3. Cultural and execution red flags from Glassdoor reviews describing leadership making technical decisions 'without sufficient technical context,' a risk factor as the company manages an increasingly complex dual-business model [6]
