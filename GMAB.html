Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Denmark
Genmab A/S is a Danish biotechnology company specializing in antibody-based therapeutics for cancer and other diseases. The company has eight approved antibody medicines on the market, including DARZALEX (multiple myeloma, via J&J), Kesimpta (multiple sclerosis, via Novartis), EPKINLY/TEPKINLY (lymphoma), and Tivdak (cervical cancer). Genmab's proprietary DuoBody bispecific technology platform and extensive partnership network with J&J, AbbVie, BioNTech, and Novo Nordisk drive its hybrid royalty-and-proprietary revenue model. H1 2026 revenue reached $2.05B (+25% YoY), with full-year guidance raised to $4.33-4.53B.

Investment Thesis
Genmab at 20.5x forward earnings offers compelling value for a biotech growing revenue 25% YoY with a durable royalty-and-proprietary revenue mix. H1 2026 revenue of $2.05B and raised full-year guidance to $4.33-4.53B demonstrate accelerating momentum, driven by DARZALEX royalties (~$2.5B annual from $14.3B J&J net sales) and EPKINLY proprietary sales of $312M in H1 (+48% YoY) [1][2]. The DuoBody bispecific technology platform powers four approved medicines and a deep late-stage pipeline with $5B+ potential. Infrastructure score of 10 reflects strong fundamentals across revenue growth, valuation, and margin metrics. The transition from pure royalty model to hybrid commercial company creates a higher-margin revenue mix as EPKINLY and other proprietary products scale.
The bear case centers on DARZALEX concentration risk, margin compression, and valuation premium at current levels. DARZALEX royalties represent approximately 60-65% of revenue, creating significant dependency on a single J&J-controlled product that will eventually face biosimilar competition in the late 2020s [1]. Operating expenses are guided to increase 23-32% in 2026 ($2.7-2.9B) as Genmab invests in late-stage trials and commercial launch readiness, and the stock price has risen 14% since the July screen (from $28.72 to $32.65), pushing forward P/E to 20.53x — at the threshold of the 20x target [5]. EPS growth was -9.4% YoY despite 25% revenue growth, reflecting margin pressure from the commercialization buildout. The transition from royalty-dependent to self-commercializing company carries execution risk in building sales forces across multiple geographies.
