Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Diversified ·NYSE ·United States
Global Net Lease is a publicly traded net lease real estate investment trust that owns and manages single-tenant net lease properties across the United States, United Kingdom, and Europe. The company completed a transformational period in 2025, internalizing management from former external advisor AR Global in September 2023, completing $3.4 billion in strategic asset dispositions including the sale of its entire multi-tenant retail portfolio, and reducing total debt by over $2.7 billion. GNL is now focused on its repositioned portfolio of higher-quality single-tenant assets.

Investment Thesis
GNL offers deep value potential for investors willing to bet on a successful turnaround. The stock trades at $8.94 with an 8.39% dividend yield, and the company delivered a 32% total return in 2025 — nearly triple the 12% average of net lease REIT peers — driven by the completion of its portfolio restructuring [1]. The $3.4B in strategic dispositions and $2.7B in debt reduction fundamentally improved the balance sheet, earning an S&P upgrade to BB+ with notes reaching investment-grade BBB-. The repositioned portfolio of higher-quality single-tenant assets anchored by industry-leading tenants across the US, UK, and Europe should generate more predictable rental income. New leadership under CEO Mike Weil and Chairman Robert Kauffman (co-founder of Fortress Investment Group) brings credible real estate expertise [2]. A proposed merger with Modiv Industrial could further enhance scale and portfolio quality.
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. GAAP earnings remain deeply negative (ROE -5.45%, profit margin -8.72%), and 2026 AFFO guidance of $0.80-$0.84/share represents a 15-19% decline from the $0.99 achieved in 2025, creating risk of yet another dividend cut on top of the three cuts in the past 18 months [1][4]. The five-year dividend growth CAGR of -13.83% reflects a pattern of income destruction that 8.39% current yield cannot offset for total return. Forward P/E of 26.65x on negative GAAP earnings suggests the market prices in eventual recovery but at a premium to current fundamentals. GNL's governance history under AR Global — including the Blackwells Capital lawsuit alleging $838M in self-dealing, dilutive related-party mergers, and external management conflicts of interest — has permanently damaged the company's credibility with institutional investors [3]. Revenue declined 13.08% YoY as asset dispositions reduced rental income, and the proposed Modiv Industrial merger adds integration risk and potential dilution. The sub-$10 stock price and small market cap ($2.2B) limit institutional interest and create liquidity risk during market stress.
