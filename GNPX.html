Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Genprex is a clinical-stage gene therapy company developing therapies for cancer and diabetes. Its lead oncology candidate, Reqorsa Gene Therapy (quaratusugene ozeplasmid), uses a non-viral Oncoprex Delivery System with lipid nanoparticles and is being evaluated in three clinical trials for NSCLC and SCLC, all with FDA Fast Track Designation. The diabetes program (GPX-002) uses an AAV vector to deliver Pdx1 and MafA genes to transform alpha cells into functional beta-like cells. Market cap is approximately $2.3M with 13 employees. The company has substantial going concern doubt despite $15.15M in cash at June 30, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Genprex's bull case rests entirely on clinical pipeline optionality — the Acclaim-1 trial evaluating Reqorsa in combination with AstraZeneca's Tagrisso for NSCLC could generate interim data by year-end 2026, and positive results in this large, poorly-served patient population (NSCLC is the leading cause of cancer death) could transform the company's valuation from its current $2.3M market cap [1]. The FDA has granted Fast Track Designation to all three lung cancer programs, and the diabetes gene therapy GPX-002 represents a second potential platform in a massive therapeutic category. The company has zero debt and $15.15M in cash providing runway into H2 2027.
The bear case is overwhelming for current investment. A $2.3M market cap with going concern risk, 13 employees, zero revenue after 17 years of existence, and dependence on continuous dilutive equity raises ($15.6M raised via ATM in H1 2026 alone) creates extreme risk of permanent capital loss [1]. The daily dollar volume of only approximately $654K falls below the $1M IOWN liquidity floor, making position entry and exit impractical. Clinical-stage biotech failure rates exceed 90% historically, and Genprex's programs remain in early Phase 1/2 trials with no Phase 3 visibility. The CEO transition from founder Rodney Varner (deceased May 2024) to CFO-turned-CEO Ryan Confer raises questions about scientific and strategic leadership continuity [2]. The stock trades at $2.85, well below its 50-day SMA of $4.46, reflecting deteriorating market confidence.
