Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance - Life ·NYSE ·United States
Genworth Financial is a Richmond, Virginia-based holding company that owns a majority stake in Enact Holdings (U.S. private mortgage insurance, $274B primary insurance in-force), manages a closed block of legacy long-term care, life and annuity policies, and is building CareScout, an aging-care services and insurance platform. Q2 2026 total revenues were $1.901B (+6% YoY), net income was $47M ($0.12/share) and adjusted operating income excluding the Closed Block was $112M ($0.29/share); Enact's new insurance written rose 15% and it returned $103M of capital to Genworth. Founded as a 2004 GE Capital IPO, Genworth has ~3,100 employees. On October 30, 2026 the company announced that CEO Tom McInerney will retire effective December 31, 2026, with CFO and interim CEO Jerome Upton expected to succeed him.

Investment Thesis
Genworth trades at ~8.0x forward earnings and well below the value of its Enact stake: Enact delivered $143M of adjusted operating income in Q2 2026 with 15% NIW growth and $274B of insurance in-force, and returned $103M to Genworth in the quarter [1][6]. Q2 total revenues rose 6% to $1.901B, adjusted operating income excluding the Closed Block reached $0.29/share, and the company has repurchased $918M of stock at an average $6.47 since the program began, including $128M year-to-date in 2026 [1]. CareScout offers optionality in a growing aging-care market as revenue rose 50% to $6M.
The Closed Block remains the core risk: Q2 2026 brought a $127M pre-tax actual-to-expected loss and a $110M adjusted operating loss, pulling total adjusted operating income to just $2M [1]. ROE of 3.4% and a 2.9% profit margin show how little of Enact's earnings reach shareholders, revenue has shrunk at roughly 3% a year since 2020, and LTC litigation (including the Genworth/AARP premium case) persists [3][6]. CEO McInerney's announced retirement at year-end 2026 after a health-related leave adds transition risk [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 earnings and CEO transition to Jerome Upton effective January 2027 — a clear capital allocation framework from the new CEO could narrow the sum-of-the-parts discount [2]
  2. Continued Enact capital returns ($103M in Q2 2026) funding Genworth buybacks at a discount to intrinsic value [1]
  3. CareScout scaling: services revenue up 50% YoY to $6M with 1,459 matches per quarter, and potential CareScout Insurance growth [1]
Key Risks
  1. Closed Block LTC volatility: a $127M pre-tax A/E loss in Q2 2026 shows reserve experience can swamp Enact's earnings [1]
  2. Mortgage credit cycle: Enact's loss ratio rose to 14% from 10% as favorable reserve releases shrink; a housing downturn would cut the main earnings engine [1]
  3. Litigation and regulatory risk from LTC premium increases, including the Genworth/AARP consumer protection suit, plus leadership transition at year-end 2026 [2][3]
