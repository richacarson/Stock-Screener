Investment Thesis
Grocery Outlet at $11.95 (down 45% over 52 weeks) offers deep value exposure to a 78-year-old extreme-value grocery model at a historically depressed valuation — adjusted EPS of $0.20 in Q2 2026 beat consensus by 61%, comparable store sales improved sequentially from -1.0% (Q1) to -0.3% (Q2), and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $4.71B [1]. The significant insider buying ($2.0M by Lead Director Ragatz, net $800K buying across insiders) during the stock's decline signals management conviction that the selloff has overshot fundamentals [3]. The opportunistic buying model is countercyclical — economic weakness drives more consumers to seek value and generates more surplus product from CPG companies, creating a natural hedge. Grocery Outlet's 530+ store count with a ~30 store/year expansion pace provides unit-growth optionality, and the Inspire Impact Score of 50 (ALIGNED) with 15 positive attributions and zero negatives makes this an attractive faith-alignment profile.
The bear case is substantial and explains the 45% stock decline. Grocery Outlet is GAAP unprofitable with -4.9% net margin and -38.06% ROE, likely reflecting goodwill impairments or restructuring charges that have destroyed equity value [5]. The $1.81B debt load (D/E 184%) is excessive for a grocery retailer generating $4.7B in revenue, and interest expense further compresses already-thin grocery margins. Revenue growth of approximately 1% YoY is anemic for a company still opening stores, and declining comparable store sales (-0.3% Q2, -1.0% Q1) suggest the existing store base is losing share to competitors [1]. The extreme-value model faces intensifying competition from Aldi (aggressively expanding in the US), Lidl, Dollar Tree, and Walmart's price-matching strategies. The IO model creates execution risk as Grocery Outlet doesn't directly control store operations, merchandising, or customer experience. The stock's -45% decline over 52 weeks and status as a sub-$12 stock create momentum headwinds and potential institutional investor exit risk.
