GPGI, Inc. (formerly CompoSecure, Inc.) is a multi-industry platform managed by Resolute Holdings Management under Executive Chairman David Cote (former Honeywell CEO), owning CompoSecure (premium metal payment cards for issuers such as American Express, U.S. Bank, Samsung and Klarna) and Husky Technologies (injection molding systems, aftermarket tooling and service for packaging and medical markets), combined in a $7.4B transaction completed in early 2026. Q2 2026 pro forma adjusted net sales were $473.2M (-4.1% YoY), with CompoSecure up 11.7% to $133.6M and Husky down 9.2% to $339.6M; pro forma adjusted EBITDA fell 12.5% to $113.9M. FY2026 guidance calls for $1.95-$2.1B of sales (flat organic) and $550-$610M of adjusted EBITDA, with net leverage of 3.9x targeted to fall to ~3.0x by year-end. The company is the subject of a securities class action and a derivative suit (Sullivan v. Cote) over the Husky deal and Resolute's fee of 10% of adjusted EBITDA.

Investment Thesis
CompoSecure is a high-quality franchise: Q2 2026 adjusted net sales hit a record $133.6M (+11.7%) at a 41.3% EBITDA margin, with new programs for Samsung, U.S. Bank, Amex Delta SkyMiles Reserve and Klarna and a London Design Center supporting international growth [1]. Management reiterated FY2026 guidance of $550-$610M adjusted EBITDA (+7% at the midpoint, margin up 120bps) and $275-$325M of free cash flow, targets deleveraging from 3.9x to ~3.0x by year-end, and insiders bought roughly $1.75M of stock in 2026 [1][5]. At ~8.9x forward earnings, the stock prices in significant skepticism about Husky.
Husky drives the risk: Q2 sales fell 9.2% and EBITDA 22.6% on customer delays, tariffs and resin volatility, pulling consolidated pro forma EBITDA down 12.5% [1]. The balance sheet carries $2.1B of debt, the six-month GAAP net loss was $184.7M, and governance is contentious — Resolute (controlled by the Cote family) earns 10% of adjusted EBITDA, and both a securities class action (class period November 3, 2025 to May 6, 2026) and the Sullivan v. Cote derivative suit allege the Husky deal was overvalued [1][2][4]. The stock has fallen from $19.74 before the Jehoshaphat report to ~$11 [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Husky second-half recovery: management cites order growth and a 'robust and growing pipeline' to support 2H 2026 margin expansion via ROS fixed-cost leverage [1]
  2. Deleveraging from 3.9x to ~3.0x net debt/EBITDA by year-end 2026, supported by $275-$325M of guided free cash flow [1]
  3. CompoSecure international expansion through the London Design Center and new issuer programs (Samsung, Klarna, Kast, DolarApp) [1]
Key Risks
  1. Husky cyclicality: Q2 2026 sales -9.2% and EBITDA -22.6% on customer delays, tariffs and oil/resin volatility; guided to decline low-to-mid single digits in FY2026 [1]
  2. Governance and litigation: securities fraud class action and Sullivan v. Cote derivative suit over the Husky valuation and Resolute's 10%-of-EBITDA management fee [2][4]
  3. Leverage of $2.115B of debt (3.9x net debt/EBITDA) at the operating entity limits flexibility if Husky's recovery stalls [1]
