Company Profile
Industrials ·Metal Fabrication ·NYSE ·United States
GPGI, Inc. (formerly CompoSecure, Inc., rebranded January 2026) is a diversified multi-industry platform operating two segments: CompoSecure (premium metal payment cards, security, and authentication solutions — clients include American Express, JPMorgan Chase, X Money, Robinhood) and Husky Technologies (engineered injection molding systems for food/beverage, medical devices, and packaging). The company completed its $7.4B business combination with Husky Technologies in January 2026 under Executive Chairman Dave Cote's (former Honeywell CEO) Resolute Operating System. Headquartered in Somerset, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
GPGI represents a high-risk industrial transformation led by Dave Cote's proven Honeywell playbook. CompoSecure delivered a record Q1 2026 with adjusted EBITDA of $47.6M (up 36.8%) and EBITDA margin expanding 300bps to 36.5%, driven by the Resolute Operating System and new high-profile program wins including American Express Graphite, X Money, Robinhood Platinum, and Revolut Audi F1 [1]. At 17.70x forward P/E, the stock offers reasonable valuation for a platform with ~29% pro forma EBITDA margins and $550-610M guided EBITDA. CompoSecure's dominant position in premium metal payment cards creates a durable franchise with strong organic growth, and Husky Technologies' leadership in PET injection molding provides exposure to essential packaging infrastructure.
The bear case is severe and multi-faceted. Husky Technologies' Q1 2026 performance was deeply concerning — pro forma net sales declined 5%, adjusted EBITDA plunged 40%, and orders fell 16% YoY as oil price volatility and tariffs drove customer delays and cancellations [1]. FY2026 guidance was cut by 10%+ from $2,180-2,230M to $1,950-2,100M in revenue and $620-650M to $550-610M in EBITDA. The GAAP net loss of $235M reflects massive acquisition charges. Governance concerns are escalating: Jehoshaphat Research disclosed a short position calling GPGI a 'fundamentally flawed financial scheme' with 'bizarre incentive structures,' Robbins Geller announced a securities investigation after the May 2026 earnings disclosure triggered a 26% stock decline, and the company faces a lawsuit over its Delaware-to-Nevada reincorporation that allegedly limits shareholder rights [4][5]. Three senior leadership departures in early 2026 during the critical merger integration period compound execution risk.
