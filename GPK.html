Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·United States
Graphic Packaging Holding Company is the world's largest manufacturer of fiber-based consumer packaging, producing folding cartons, coated recycled board, unbleached paperboard, and microwave packaging for hundreds of globally recognized brands. Following the $1.45B AR Packaging acquisition in 2022, GPK operates globally with a focus on replacing plastic packaging with renewable fiber-based alternatives. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.19B (-0.7% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.14; FY2026 guidance was narrowed to revenue of $8.40-$8.60B and adjusted EPS lowered to $0.65-$0.90 (from $0.75-$1.15) amid $150M in inflationary headwinds. New CEO Robbert Rietbroek (ex-PepsiCo, Kimberly-Clark) took over January 2026. Founded 1888, headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia.

Investment Thesis
GPK at 8.2x forward earnings offers deep value in the world's largest fiber-based consumer packaging franchise, trading well below book value after a ~24% decline from $10.96 to $8.38 since August [4]. The stock has fallen further than earnings, creating a compelling entry if management executes on the $85M structural cost savings program. Capital spending dropping from $935M (2025) to $450M (2026) as major CRB capacity investments complete should drive a significant free cash flow inflection supporting both deleveraging and the 5.24% dividend yield with ~8% 5-year growth CAGR [1]. Multiple insider purchases by CEO Rietbroek ($501K), director Hagemann ($154K), and director Stafeil ($182K) at higher prices signal leadership conviction [4]. The secular shift from plastic to fiber-based packaging provides a structural volume tailwind as global regulations increasingly restrict single-use plastics. The Inspire Impact Score of 57 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions is a strong positive for faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on margin deterioration, high leverage, and stagnating revenue. Profit margin of 2.25% and ROE of 6.01% reflect thin returns on significant invested capital — FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was cut from $0.75-$1.15 to $0.65-$0.90, a 22% midpoint reduction signaling worsening profitability [1][4]. D/E of 176% with substantial debt creates refinancing risk in an elevated interest rate environment, and the payout ratio has risen to 66.7% as earnings declined while dividends grew. Revenue has declined from the $9.44B peak in 2022 to $8.6B in 2025 — Q2 2026 revenue of $2.19B was down 0.7% YoY, and full-year guidance of $8.40-$8.60B suggests the decline continues [1]. The $150M inflationary headwind is only partially offset by $85M in structural cost savings. The CSO departure coinciding with the sustainability report publication undermines the ESG narrative. The PEG of 3.33 suggests the market sees limited earnings growth potential despite the cheap forward multiple.
