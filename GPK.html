Company Profile
Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·United States
Graphic Packaging Holding Company is the world's largest manufacturer of fiber-based consumer packaging, producing folding cartons, coated recycled board, unbleached paperboard, and microwave packaging for hundreds of globally recognized brands. Following the transformative $1.45B AR Packaging acquisition in 2022, GPK operates globally with a focus on replacing plastic packaging with renewable fiber-based alternatives. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.19B (-1.0% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.14; FY2026 guidance targets revenue of $8.40-$8.60B and adjusted EPS of $0.65-$0.90 amid $150M in inflationary headwinds. Founded 1888, headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia.

Investment Thesis
GPK at 12.63x forward earnings offers deep value in the world's largest fiber-based consumer packaging franchise, trading near book value after a ~46% decline over 52 weeks [5]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.14 beat estimates of $0.13, and FY2026 guidance of $8.40-$8.60B revenue with $0.65-$0.90 adjusted EPS was reaffirmed with management targeting the high end on revenue [1]. Capital spending dropping from $935M (2025) to $450M (2026) as major capacity investments complete should drive a significant free cash flow inflection, supporting both deleveraging and the 4.01% dividend yield with 7% 5-year growth CAGR. Multiple insider purchases by CEO Rietbroek ($501K), director Hagemann ($154K), and director Stafeil ($182K) at depressed prices signal leadership conviction [4]. The secular shift from plastic to fiber-based packaging provides a long-term volume tailwind as global regulations increasingly restrict single-use plastics.
The bear case centers on margin pressure, high leverage, and stagnating revenue. Profit margin of 3.0% and ROE of 6.01% reflect thin returns on significant invested capital [5]. D/E of 176% with $5.7B in debt against $207M in cash creates refinancing risk in an elevated interest rate environment. Revenue has declined from the $9.44B peak in 2022 to an estimated $8.5B run rate — Q2 2026 revenue of $2.19B was down 1.0% YoY, and full-year guidance of $8.40-$8.60B suggests the decline has not been arrested [1]. The $150M inflationary headwind is only partially offset by $85M in structural cost savings, compressing already thin margins. Management noted adjusted EBITDA is expected at the low end of guidance due to the heightened inflationary environment. New CEO Rietbroek faces the dual challenge of restoring margins while accelerating innovation — a difficult balance in a low-margin, capital-intensive manufacturing business. The Inspire Impact Score of 57 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions is a strong positive for faith-based investors [3].
