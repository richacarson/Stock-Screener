Company Profile
Technology ·Consumer Electronics ·NASDAQ ·United States
GoPro designs and sells action cameras (HERO and MAX lines), mounts and accessories, and a subscription service bundling cloud storage, camera replacement and the Quik editing app. The founder-led company has seen revenue fall from $1.16B in 2021 to $652M in 2025, and Q2 2026 revenue dropped another 31% to about $105M while subscription revenue rose 11% to $29M (28% of sales). On May 11, 2026 the board launched a strategic review including a possible sale or merger (advised by Houlihan Lokey). The stock trades below $1, and GoPro has received a Nasdaq minimum-bid-price deficiency notice.

Investment Thesis
The only constructive case is optionality: the board is running a formal sale process with Houlihan Lokey after receiving several unsolicited inbound inquiries, and GoPro still owns a globally recognized brand, a subscription business growing 11% with a 69% attach rate, and a design patent the ITC upheld against Insta360 [1][2][4]. At a ~$200M market cap, a strategic buyer could value the brand, the IP and the defense/aerospace adjacency above the current price.
Fundamentals are deteriorating fast: Q2 2026 revenue fell 31% to ~$105M, camera units fell 51.7%, the GAAP net loss widened to $51M, and adjusted EBITDA was -$29M [1]. Stockholders' equity is negative (-$32.7M) with only $27M of cash against $86M of debt, the stock faces Nasdaq delisting below $1, and any sale could come at a price that leaves little for common shareholders [3][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Outcome of the strategic review launched May 11, 2026 (sale or merger via Houlihan Lokey)
  2. Enforcement of the February 2026 ITC exclusion order on Insta360 Ace, Ace Pro and Ace Pro 2 cameras
  3. Regaining Nasdaq minimum-bid compliance within the 180-day window
Key Risks
  1. Liquidity stress: $27M cash versus $86M debt and negative stockholders' equity
  2. Continued hardware collapse (Q2 2026 hardware revenue -40%, units -51.7%) as Insta360 and DJI gain share
  3. Nasdaq delisting or a sale below the current price that wipes out common equity value
