Company Profile
Industrials ·Conglomerates ·NYSE American ·United States
Hyperscale Data, Inc. is a diversified holding company operating through multiple segments: crane rental and lifting solutions (Gresham Worldwide), data center colocation and Bitcoin mining (Sentinum), commercial lending and fintech (Ault Lending), an AI-driven analytics platform (askROI), hotel operations, and social gaming. Formerly known as Ault Alliance, Inc., the company rebranded in January 2023 to signal a strategic pivot toward data center and AI infrastructure. Headquartered in Las Vegas, Nevada.

Investment Thesis
Hyperscale Data offers aggressive revenue growth — Q1 2026 revenue jumped 76% YoY to $44M, H1 2026 grew 57% to ~$80M, with full-year guidance of $180-200M and preliminary 2027 outlook exceeding $300M [3][4]. The Sentinum data center and Michigan AI campus build-out position the company to benefit from surging AI compute demand, while Gresham crane services provides infrastructure exposure. At $0.17 per share, the $29M market cap is a deep discount to $130M+ TTM revenue, and the Inspire Impact Score of +47 (ALIGNED) is a rare positive faith-alignment signal.
The bear case is overwhelming. Hyperscale Data is a highly leveraged ($141M debt), deeply unprofitable (-71% profit margin, -$106M FCF) conglomerate with no coherent strategy [5]. The 95% decline from 52-week highs reflects market skepticism that revenue growth can convert into shareholder value. The conglomerate structure — cranes, hotels, Bitcoin mining, lending, gaming — has no strategic logic or operational synergies. D/E of 121% with ROE of -148.8% signals potential solvency risk. Management has consistently prioritized revenue growth through acquisitions while destroying value through operational losses and dilution. The Michigan AI campus faces a class-action lawsuit from Dowagiac residents over noise and land-acquisition transparency, threatening the project underpinning the growth thesis [3]. The stock's penny-stock pricing ($0.17), micro-cap status ($29M), and minimal analyst coverage create governance, dilution, and delisting risks.
