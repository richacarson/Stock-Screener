Investment Thesis
Garmin at 25.7x forward earnings delivers a rare combination of double-digit growth, best-in-class financial quality, and multi-segment diversification. Q2 2026 set a revenue record at $2.02B (+11% YoY) with operating income surging 30% and pro forma EPS up 29%, prompting raised FY2026 guidance to $8.05B revenue (+10%) and $10 EPS [1]. The fitness segment's 25% Q2 growth demonstrates Garmin's dominance in premium GPS wearables, while marine (+14%) and aviation segments provide diversified stability. The balance sheet is pristine — $2.67B cash against $227M debt, D/E of 2.5%, and $1.04B free cash flow. The Inspire-ALIGNED score of +54 with zero negative attributions reflects clean governance and responsible operations [5].
The bear case centers on valuation stretch and cyclical exposure. At 25.7x forward earnings with a PEG of 3.09 (above the 3.0 floor), Garmin is priced for sustained double-digit growth with zero margin of safety [5]. The stock's 54% rally from its 52-week low of $186.67 has compressed future return potential. The fitness wearable market faces intensifying competition from Apple Watch Ultra with AI health features, Samsung Galaxy Watch, and emerging brands like Coros that undercut Garmin on price. Marine and outdoor segments are cyclically sensitive to consumer discretionary spending — any economic slowdown could pressure these high-margin categories. Garmin's insular leadership (Glassdoor reviews noting executives who have 'only ever worked at Garmin') creates innovation risk in a rapidly evolving technology landscape. The 1.46% dividend yield — despite a 22-year payment history — falls below the 2% threshold for income-oriented investors.
