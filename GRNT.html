Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Granite Ridge Resources, Inc. is a non-operated oil and natural gas exploration and production company that owns wells and acreage across six prolific U.S. basins: Permian, Eagle Ford, Bakken, Haynesville, Denver-Julesburg (DJ), and Appalachian/Utica. The company partners with proven public and private operators rather than drilling its own wells, operating as a hybrid energy company and investment platform with only 6 direct employees. Q2 2026 revenue was $149.3M (+36.7% YoY) with net income of $30.0M ($0.23 EPS) and production of 32,044 Boe/day (51% oil). Market cap is approximately $683M.

While significant insider buying shows leadership conviction, the company lacks the organizational depth, cultural foundations, and long-term vision that characterize infinite-game players. Investment Thesis
Granite Ridge offers an asymmetric risk-reward profile as an 8.49%-yielding non-operated E&P play at 8.84x forward P/E, with Q2 2026 revenue surging 36.7% YoY to $149.3M and production reaching 32,044 Boe/day [2]. The non-operated model provides diversification across six major U.S. basins with only 6 employees, creating an ultra-lean cost structure where operating partners bear execution risk. Adjusted EBITDAX of $79.6M in Q2 2026 demonstrates significant cash generation capacity despite GAAP derivative-driven losses [2]. Insider buying exceeding $870K from CEO, CFO, and four directors in May-June 2026 — with zero sales — provides strong conviction signals [1]. The company completed 27 acquisition deals in Q2 alone, adding 21.9 net undeveloped locations to sustain the production growth trajectory.
The bear case centers on commodity dependence, negative GAAP profitability, and structural concerns. LTM GAAP net loss of $27.3M, negative ROE of -4.58%, and negative profit margin of -5.84% reflect a company that is not earning its cost of capital on a reported basis, despite positive Q2 2026 earnings of $30M [5]. The 244% payout ratio means the $0.44/year dividend is unsustainable from earnings alone — it is funded by depreciation-sheltered cash flow, and a commodity downturn could force a cut. The non-operated model means Granite Ridge has no control over drilling decisions, well performance, or operating costs — it is entirely dependent on third-party operators [3]. SPAC origin, CEO departure within three years, and 6-employee structure raise governance and key-person risk concerns [4]. Oil prices below $60/bbl would compress EBITDAX, threaten the dividend, and potentially impair well values. The Altman Z-Score of 1.03 indicates heightened bankruptcy risk [5].

  1. Q2 2026 revenue acceleration to $149.3M (+36.7% YoY) with production guidance of 34,000-36,000 Boe/day for 2026 suggests meaningful growth inflection supported by 27 acquisition deals adding 21.9 net undeveloped locations in Q2 alone [2]

Key Risks

  2. Non-operated model eliminates control over execution — Granite Ridge is entirely dependent on third-party operators for drilling decisions, well performance, and cost management across its 6-basin portfolio, with no ability to optimize operations directly [3]
  3. SPAC-origin company with CEO turnover (Brandenberg departed June 2025), only 6 direct employees, and $475M total debt against $44.1M cash creates concentration and leverage risks that smaller commodity price declines could amplify [4][5]
