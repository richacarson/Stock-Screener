Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE American ·Canada
Gold Royalty Corp. is a precious metals-focused royalty and streaming company that provides creative financing solutions to the metals and mining industry. Founded in 2020 by David Garofalo (former CEO of Goldcorp), the company has assembled a portfolio of over 240 royalties and streams primarily in low-risk jurisdictions across Canada, the United States, Mexico, and Brazil. GROY reported record H1 2026 total revenue of $17.3M (+116% YoY), its first profitable quarter in Q2 2026 with net income of $1.8M, and adjusted EBITDA of $12.6M (+212%). The company operates with zero debt, $12M cash, and a $150M fully undrawn credit facility. Market cap approximately $769M.

Investment Thesis
Gold Royalty at a $769M market cap offers leveraged exposure to gold prices through a rapidly maturing royalty portfolio that has reached operating inflection — H1 2026 total revenue of $17.3M (+116% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $12.6M (+212%) and the company's first profitable quarter in Q2 2026 with $1.8M net income [1]. The royalty model provides operating leverage with 26% profit margins and minimal operating costs, while a pristine balance sheet (zero debt, $150M undrawn credit facility) allows continued portfolio growth without dilution. The PEG ratio of 0.7 suggests the market has not fully priced in the 60%+ production growth guidance for 2026 (7,500-9,300 GEOs), and revenue is forecast to grow 31% annually over the next three years as underlying mine assets ramp toward the 2030 outlook of 28,000-34,000 GEOs from existing assets [1][4]. The Inspire Impact Score of 36 (ALIGNED) with positive ESG attributions including sector-leading carbon intensity of 0.25 tCO2e/GEO makes GROY accessible to faith-based investors [3].
The bear case centers on GROY's junior status, nascent profitability, and gold price dependency. Despite the strong H1 2026, the company has only one quarter of positive net income in its entire operating history, and trailing ROE remains negative at -1.14% [4]. The $769M market cap implies a significant premium to NAV relative to larger royalty peers like Franco-Nevada and Royal Gold, which trade at lower revenue multiples with proven decades-long track records. Gold price volatility is an existential risk — a sustained decline below $1,800/oz would compress margins across the entire portfolio, and the royalty model amplifies commodity price movements with no hedging program [1]. The company's reliance on third-party operators means GROY has no control over production decisions, permitting delays, or operational failures at underlying mines. The small 16-person team creates key-person risk around David Garofalo, and revenue growth will decelerate significantly from the 116% H1 pace as the base normalizes.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued ramp of underlying mine production driving toward 2026 GEO guidance of 7,500-9,300 (60%+ growth vs 2025), with Cote Gold, Borborema, and Pedra Branca as primary contributors [1]
  2. Transition to sustained profitability after Q2 2026's first profitable quarter, with the high-margin royalty model suggesting margins will expand further as revenue scales on a fixed cost base [1]
  3. Potential new royalty acquisitions using the $150M undrawn credit facility, which could step-function revenue growth if management deploys capital at attractive terms as mid-tier miners seek non-dilutive financing [1]
Key Risks
  1. Gold price dependency — a sustained decline below $1,800/oz would compress margins across the entire 240+ royalty portfolio, as GROY has no hedging program and the royalty model amplifies commodity price movements [1]
  2. Operator risk — production at underlying mines is entirely controlled by third parties, and permitting delays, environmental issues, or operational failures at key assets (Cote Gold, Borborema, Cozamin) could materially reduce royalty income with no recourse [1]
  3. Valuation premium relative to proven senior royalty peers — GROY trades at a significant revenue multiple premium to Franco-Nevada and Royal Gold despite lacking their multi-decade track records and diversified cash flow bases [4]
