Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·United States
The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. is a leading global financial institution providing a wide range of financial services including investment banking, securities, asset management, and consumer banking. Founded in 1869, Goldman Sachs serves corporations, financial institutions, governments, and individuals worldwide. Under CEO David Solomon, the firm has refocused on its core institutional strengths after retreating from consumer banking (Marcus). Headquartered in New York City with approximately 47,400 employees globally.

Investment Thesis
Goldman Sachs is firing on all cylinders, delivering Q1 2026 net revenues of .2B (up 14% YoY) and its biggest-ever equities trading quarter, driving annualized ROE of 19.8% [1]. Full-year 2025 EPS of .32 represented a 27% increase over 2024, demonstrating the earnings power of the refocused institutional franchise [1]. The firm's pivot away from consumer banking and toward alternatives, asset management, and transaction banking is generating measurable results. With a forward P/E of 17.2x on consensus estimates calling for continued earnings growth, Goldman is reasonably valued for a franchise business with dominant market positions in investment banking and trading. The \8/share annual dividend (1.72% yield) with a 24.4% 5-year dividend growth CAGR reflects strong capital return discipline.
The key risk is Goldman's structural leverage — debt-to-equity of 596% is inherent to the business model but creates meaningful vulnerability to credit market stress, counterparty risk, or a severe downturn in capital markets activity. Revenue remains highly cyclical, with 5-year CAGR of only 5.5% masking significant year-to-year swings [5]. The firm's Q2 2026 earnings preview suggests revenue expectations of .22B, below Q1's .2B, highlighting this volatility [2]. Goldman's net profit margin of 14.1% is below the 20% target due to the inherently expense-heavy financial services model. The SEC's 2022 \M ESG enforcement action and the firm's exit from the Net-Zero Banking Alliance signal governance and reputational risks that could impact institutional investors increasingly focused on sustainability commitments [4]. AI will reshape portions of the business but significant impact is not expected until 2027-2028 per Solomon [3].

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Goldman Sachs' core business lines — M&A advisory, capital markets underwriting, institutional trading, and asset management — require deep client relationships, regulatory expertise, and fiduciary trust that AI cannot replicate [1][3]. Physical-world relationship banking, complex negotiation, and regulatory compliance create meaningful defensive moats. However, AI poses real substitution risk to portions of the business: equity research, financial analysis, due diligence documentation, and routine trading execution could see significant automation. CEO Solomon acknowledges AI will "reshape work" at the firm, identifying three dynamics: automation of routine tasks, higher performance standards within existing roles, and emergence of new AI-governance positions [3]. Goldman's research division and back-office operations are particularly exposed. AI Opportunity (1/1): Goldman is actively deploying AI tools across the firm for code generation, document analysis, client insights, and trading optimization.
