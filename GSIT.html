Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
GSI Technology designs, develops, and markets high-performance SRAM semiconductor products and is commercializing the Gemini Associative Processing Unit (APU), a compute-in-memory architecture for AI edge computing. The Gemini platform performs massive parallel processing directly in memory, achieving GPU-class AI performance at dramatically lower energy consumption. Cornell University validated the chip's energy efficiency in 2026, and the company plans pilot shipments in calendar 2026. Headquartered in Sunnyvale, California.

Investment Thesis
GSI Technology delivered 22.4% full-year FY2026 revenue growth to $25.1M with gross margin expanding to 54.5% from 49%, driven by SRAM demand from AI chip design and simulation customers [1]. Cornell University's independent validation of the Gemini APU's energy efficiency (triggering a 133.5% stock surge) provides critical third-party credibility for the compute-in-memory technology [3]. With $67.2M cash, pilot shipments planned for calendar 2026, and government contracts providing near-term revenue visibility, GSI is positioned at the inflection point between R&D investment and commercialization. At $264M market cap with $67.2M cash ($197M enterprise value), the market is pricing in modest Gemini upside. The structural tailwind of edge AI demand and energy efficiency requirements could drive significant revaluation if pilot deployments convert to production orders.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability and execution risk. Net losses widened to $10.7M in FY2026 despite 22.4% revenue growth, as operating expenses surged 48.6% — the company is spending faster than it's growing [2]. The $25.1M annual revenue base remains a fraction of semiconductor competitors (NVIDIA alone spends more on R&D in a single day), creating vulnerability to customer concentration and supply chain disruptions in Taiwan [1]. Gemini pilot shipments have been repeatedly delayed — 'planned for calendar 2026' has been the message for multiple quarters without confirmed customer commitments or production volume targets. Competition from NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, and edge AI startups (Hailo, Syntiant, Brainchip) with vastly larger R&D budgets could marginalize Gemini before commercialization. The 52-week range of $2.82-$18.15 reflects extreme speculation-driven volatility rather than fundamental value creation.
Key Catalysts
  1. Gemini APU pilot shipments converting to production orders in H2 calendar 2026 would validate the technology commercially and establish the first meaningful AI hardware revenue stream [2]
  2. Government defense contracts (Air and Space Force edge computing) moving from proof-of-concept to production programs with specific dollar values and delivery timelines [4]
  3. SRAM revenue continuing 20%+ growth trajectory as AI chip design and simulation demand accelerates across the semiconductor industry [1]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent unprofitability — net loss widened to $10.7M despite 22.4% revenue growth, with operating expenses growing faster (48.6%) than revenue, consuming the $67.2M cash runway [1][2]
  2. Gemini commercialization delays — pilot shipments have been targeted for calendar 2026 for multiple quarters without confirmed customer commitments, volume targets, or pricing [2]
  3. Competition from NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, and edge AI startups with R&D budgets exceeding GSI's entire market cap could marginalize the Gemini platform before it achieves commercial scale [3]
