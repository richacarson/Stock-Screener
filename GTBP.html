Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
GT Biopharma, Inc. is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on developing immuno-oncology therapeutics using its proprietary TriKE (Tri-specific Killer Engager) natural killer cell platform. Lead program GTB-3650 targets CD33-expressing blood cancers (acute myeloid leukemia) in Phase 1 trials. Second candidate GTB-5550 targets B7-H3-expressing solid tumors with first patient dosed in May 2026. The company has recently deployed AI-based technologies for drug discovery acceleration. Fully remote since July 2024, facing Nasdaq minimum bid price compliance challenges. Headquartered virtually with no principal executive office.

Investment Thesis
GT Biopharma offers a speculative option on the TriKE NK cell engager platform — a differentiated immuno-oncology approach that simultaneously engages natural killer cells, delivers IL-15 signaling, and targets tumor antigens [2]. Two Phase 1 programs are active: GTB-3650 for CD33+ blood cancers (including AML) and GTB-5550 for B7-H3+ solid tumors (first patient dosed May 2026). The recent deployment of AI-based drug discovery tools has accelerated pipeline expansion, with multiple new candidates expected to reach pre-IND by 2027 [2]. At a ~$13M market cap, a Phase 1 clinical success in either program could drive significant revaluation given the large addressable markets in AML and solid tumors.
GT Biopharma is uninvestable for IOWN due to multiple existential risks. The company has zero revenue (consensus $0 for 2026), expected net losses of ~$22.5M, and cash of only ~$9M providing runway through Q4 2026 at best [1]. Nasdaq delisting is imminent unless the company completes a 1-for-10 to 1-for-30 reverse stock split by November 2026, and stockholders' equity is below the $2.5M minimum listing requirement [3]. Daily dollar volume of ~$40,000 makes position entry and exit functionally impossible for any meaningful allocation. Phase 1 clinical risk for both programs is substantial — most Phase 1 oncology candidates fail, and the company lacks the capital to fund development beyond early 2027 without significant dilution. The micro-cap, penny-stock profile with delisting risk and minimal institutional ownership make GTBP a speculative venture investment rather than a portfolio holding.

  3. Phase 1 clinical failure in either GTB-3650 or GTB-5550 would eliminate the company's primary value drivers with no capital to pivot, effectively ending the investment case
