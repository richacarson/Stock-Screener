Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Gates Industrial Corporation plc is a global manufacturer of application-specific fluid power and power transmission solutions. The company operates through two segments: Power Transmission (belts, tensioners) and Fluid Power (hoses, connectors, couplings). Gates serves automotive aftermarket, industrial, and emerging data center cooling markets across 30+ countries. Founded in 1911, incorporated in England and Wales, headquartered in Denver, Colorado, with approximately $3.4 billion in annual revenue.

Investment Thesis
Gates Industrial enters 2026 with two compelling growth vectors: data center liquid cooling (4x revenue growth in 2025, targeting $100-200M by 2028) and personal mobility (25%+ core growth expected at 25-30% annually through 2028) layered onto a resilient aftermarket base generating approximately 60% of revenue [1][2]. The balance sheet is healthy at 1.85x net leverage with $812M cash, and free cash flow conversion of 92% supports continued buybacks (>$100M in Q4 2025 alone) and the Timken belts acquisition [1][5]. Record adjusted EPS of $1.52 in 2025 (up 9%) with 2026 guidance of $1.52-$1.68 and adjusted EBITDA of $775-835M suggest continued profitability improvement. Forward P/E of 15.4x is attractive for an industrial with emerging AI infrastructure exposure [4].
The core concern is that Gates remains a low-growth industrial with only 1% organic revenue growth in 2025 and Q1 2026 revenue expected to decline 2-2.5% YoY at the midpoint, masking the fact that legacy power transmission and fluid power markets remain in contraction [1]. The data center opportunity, while exciting, remains a small fraction of the $3.4B revenue base and requires execution against established cooling competitors like Parker Hannifin and Continental. Under Blackstone ownership, talent flight on the innovation team threatens the R&D pipeline that must deliver differentiated cooling solutions [3]. GAAP profit margin of 7.23% and ROE of 7.68% are well below industrial peers, suggesting the company is not generating adequate returns on its asset base despite cost-optimization efforts [4]. ERP transition headwinds in H1 2026 (100bps EBITDA margin drag) and footprint optimization costs add near-term execution risk [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Data center liquid cooling revenue scaling toward the $100-200M target by 2028 as AI and HPC facility buildout accelerates demand for direct-to-chip cooling infrastructure, compounding on the 4x growth rate achieved in 2025
  2. Timken belts acquisition integration expanding the power transmission portfolio and creating cross-selling opportunities across Gates' global distribution network in 30+ countries
  3. Personal mobility segment sustaining 25-30% annual core growth through 2028, driven by EV and micro-mobility adoption requiring specialized belt and hose solutions engineered for electrified powertrains
Key Risks
  1. Core power transmission and fluid power markets remain in secular contraction with only 1% organic growth in 2025 and 2026 guidance of 1-4% core growth, providing limited margin of safety against macro slowdown or industrial recession
  2. Glassdoor-documented talent exodus on the innovation team and PE-driven short-termism threaten Gates' ability to develop differentiated liquid cooling products critical to the data center growth thesis against established industrial competitors
  3. Blackstone private equity ownership since 2014 creates potential for near-term exit via secondary offering that could destabilize management and strategic direction while suppressing the stock price
