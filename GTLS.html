Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Chart Industries, Inc. designs, engineers, and manufactures process technologies and equipment for gas and liquid molecule handling across what it calls the Nexus of Clean — clean power, clean water, clean food, and clean industrials. Operating through Cryo Tank Solutions, Heat Transfer Systems, Specialty Products, and Repair Service & Leasing segments, Chart serves LNG, hydrogen, carbon capture, data center cooling, and industrial gas markets globally. The company is subject to a pending acquisition by Baker Hughes for $210/share in cash ($10.1B), with EU conditional approval requiring LNG-related divestments. Headquartered in Ball Ground, Georgia.

Investment Thesis
Chart Industries is a global leader in cryogenic and process technology positioned at the intersection of LNG infrastructure, hydrogen, carbon capture, and AI data center cooling. Q2 2026 showed significant order momentum from AI/hyperscaler demand, with orders accelerating materially as electricity demand from AI infrastructure drives investment in cooling and power solutions [2]. The pending Baker Hughes acquisition at $210/share provides a price floor, with EU conditional approval (requiring LNG-related divestments) being the primary remaining hurdle [2]. The Nexus of Clean framework positions Chart's technology portfolio across four secular growth megatrends, and the estimated 25% 5-year revenue CAGR reflects the transformative Howden acquisition that created an integrated platform [1]. Inspire Insight score of 68 (ALIGNED) reflects strong ESG positioning across 21 positive attribution categories [4].
The bear case is severe: Q1 2026 revenue declined 11.7% YoY to $884.8M with gross margin contracting 550bps to 28.4% from unfavorable product mix, higher labor/material costs, and tariff impacts, resulting in a net loss of $0.36/share [1]. ROE has turned negative (-1.5%) and profit margin is -0.7%, reflecting the continued burden of Howden integration on a leveraged balance sheet (D/E 121%) [5]. If the Baker Hughes acquisition fails due to regulatory conditions around LNG divestments, GTLS faces significant downside from a $205 price point with negative earnings and heavy debt. Forward P/E of 23.1 reflects diminished near-term earnings expectations compared to the mid-teens P/E seen earlier in 2026. Employee culture issues (Glassdoor 2.9/5) and CEO departure create execution risk. The stock is essentially a merger arbitrage play — upside capped at $210 if the deal closes, with material downside risk if it breaks.
