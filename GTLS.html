Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Chart Industries, Inc. designed, engineered, and manufactured process technologies and equipment for gas and liquid molecule handling across what it called the Nexus of Clean — clean power, clean water, clean food, and clean industrials. Operating through Cryo Tank Solutions, Heat Transfer Systems, Specialty Products, and Repair Service & Leasing segments, Chart served LNG, hydrogen, carbon capture, data center cooling, and industrial gas markets globally. Baker Hughes completed its acquisition of Chart Industries on July 16, 2026 for $210 per share in cash, representing a total enterprise value of $13.6 billion. GTLS was delisted from NYSE upon completion of the acquisition. The analysis below reflects Chart's final operational state as an independent company.

Investment Thesis
Chart Industries was a global leader in cryogenic and process technology positioned at the intersection of LNG infrastructure, hydrogen, carbon capture, and AI data center cooling. The Baker Hughes acquisition closed on July 16, 2026 at $210/share ($13.6B enterprise value), validating Chart's technology portfolio and strategic positioning [2]. FY2025 demonstrated the strength of the integrated platform with $4.26B revenue, $5.68B in orders (up 13.4%), and a book-to-bill of 1.33x driven by accelerating demand in LNG, data center cooling, and energy transition end markets [1]. The Nexus of Clean framework connected Chart's engineering capabilities to four secular growth megatrends (clean power, water, food, industrials). Inspire Impact Score of 68 (ALIGNED) across 21 positive attribution categories reflected strong ESG positioning for a capital goods manufacturer [3].
The stock is no longer independently tradeable following the July 16, 2026 Baker Hughes acquisition at $210/share. Prior to the acquisition, the bear case centered on Q1 2026 operational deterioration — revenue declined 11.7% to $884.8M with gross margin contracting 550bps to 28.4% from unfavorable product mix, higher labor/material costs, and tariff impacts, resulting in a loss of $0.36/share [1]. ROE had compressed to approximately 3.7% on GAAP basis, and profit margin of approximately 3.1% reflected the continued burden of Howden integration on a leveraged balance sheet (D/E 104.4%). Employee culture issues (Glassdoor 2.9/5) and CEO departure created execution risk. The acquisition resolved equity risk but former shareholders no longer have exposure to Chart's growth trajectory, which is now embedded within Baker Hughes' $30B+ energy technology platform. The EU conditional approval required LNG-related divestments that may reshape Chart's business mix within Baker Hughes [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Baker Hughes integration provides Chart's technologies with broader global distribution, customer access, and financial resources than achievable independently — expected to deliver $325M in annual cost synergies by year three of integration [2]
  2. AI data center cooling demand creating structural growth for heat exchanger and process technology within Baker Hughes' Industrial & Energy Technology segment, as hyperscaler electricity and cooling investment accelerates globally [1]
  3. Energy transition tailwinds across LNG infrastructure, hydrogen production, and carbon capture driving order backlog growth — FY2025 orders of $5.68B (+13.4%) with book-to-bill of 1.33x demonstrate strengthening demand [1]
Key Risks
  1. Post-acquisition integration within Baker Hughes could dilute Chart's focused 'Nexus of Clean' identity and engineering culture, with $325M synergy targets requiring headcount and operational restructuring across 50+ country operations [2]
  2. EU regulatory conditions requiring LNG-related divestments may force disposal of some of Chart's highest-growth business lines, reducing the strategic value of the combined entity [2]
  3. Former GTLS shareholders no longer have direct exposure to Chart's growth trajectory — the equity story is now subsumed within Baker Hughes (BKR), and Chart's individual segment performance may not be separately reported
