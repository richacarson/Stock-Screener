Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
ZoomInfo Technologies Inc. (ticker: GTM) provides a go-to-market intelligence and engagement platform for sales, marketing, operations, and recruiting professionals. The platform combines proprietary B2B data, AI-powered analytics, and workflow automation to help businesses identify, connect with, and close ideal customers. Q2 2026 GAAP revenue of $310M (up 1.2% YoY) beat guidance, with adjusted operating income of $110M (35% margin). However, a $651M non-cash goodwill impairment charge was recorded in Q2, the company executed a 20% workforce reduction in May 2026, and full-year 2026 guidance of $1.207-$1.217B still implies a ~3% revenue decline.

Investment Thesis
ZoomInfo at 3.6x forward earnings represents a deep value opportunity in a company with $1.24B in trailing revenue, $107M quarterly unlevered free cash flow, 35% adjusted operating margins (up 130bps YoY), and a restructuring plan targeting $60M in annual savings [1][4]. Q2 2026 revenue of $310M beat guidance despite headwinds, and full-year guidance was modestly raised to $1.207-$1.217B from $1.185-$1.205B. The company's proprietary database of B2B contacts and intent signals remains a valuable asset, and the upmarket enterprise pivot (74% of business) provides a more defensible customer base. If the AI-powered Copilot Workspace and consumption-based pricing model successfully differentiate against AI-native competitors, the stock offers substantial re-rating potential from deeply compressed multiples. At $1.18B market cap against ~$350M trailing FCF, the implied FCF yield exceeds 30% — pricing in near-certain continued decline.
The bear case is that ZoomInfo's data moat is structurally eroding as AI disruption accelerates. The $651M non-cash goodwill impairment in Q2 2026 — triggered by market cap decline — is management's acknowledgment of permanent value destruction [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance of $1.207-$1.217B still implies a ~3% revenue decline after 2025's near-flat performance, creating a sustained negative growth trajectory. BofA analyst Matt Bullock reinstated coverage with Underperform and a $4 target, and the stock has declined 60% over the past year [2]. D/E of 183% — worsened by the impairment's equity hit — reflects $1.57B total debt against significantly reduced equity, creating balance sheet fragility. If AI-native competitors achieve data parity, ZoomInfo's subscription pricing model faces accelerating churn from its 35,000-customer base. Cumulative ~50% headcount reduction from peak (5,000+ to ~2,580) risks destroying institutional knowledge and customer relationships critical to executing the AI pivot [2].
