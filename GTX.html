Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Auto Parts ·NASDAQ ·United States
Garrett Motion Inc. is a global automotive and industrial technology company that designs, manufactures, and sells turbocharger and electric-boosting technologies for light vehicle, commercial vehicle, and industrial OEMs. Spun off from Honeywell in October 2018 and emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in April 2021. Headquartered in Rolle, Switzerland, Garrett operates 6 R&D centers and 13 manufacturing facilities across 20+ countries, with 60+ years of turbocharger technology leadership.

Investment Thesis
Garrett Motion delivered a breakout Q1 2026 with $985M net sales (up 12% YoY), adjusted EBIT margin expansion to 15.3%, and raised full-year guidance to $3.6-3.9B revenue and $300-360M net income [1]. The stock has surged 179% over 52 weeks as the market rerates the company's capital allocation discipline -- shares outstanding decreased 44% since January 2023 through the $250M annual buyback program, and a dividend was initiated in 2025 with a 33% raise in December [5]. Forward PEG of 0.90 on EPS growth of 22% YoY makes this one of the cheapest industrial stories in the market. The Capital Markets Day outlined $2B+ cumulative FCF through 2030 at 5% revenue CAGR, with the e-powertrain, fuel cell, and e-cooling pipeline targeting $1B non-turbo revenue by 2030 [1].
The primary risk is the ICE-to-EV transition structurally reducing turbocharger demand -- while management expects turbo revenue in 2035 to exceed 2025, this thesis depends on slower-than-expected BEV adoption and strong hybrid/PHEV uptake [1]. Negative equity of -$781M (bankruptcy legacy) creates accounting distortions in D/E and ROE metrics, though net leverage of 1.92x and ROIC of 42.9% demonstrate actual financial health. Heavy insider selling across all senior executives in 2026 (CEO $5.8M, CFO $3.6M, SVP $2.3M -- zero buys) raises alignment concerns despite management's bullish guidance [4]. The stock's 179% 52-week gain and removal from the Russell 2000 Defensive Index increase vulnerability to mean reversion. Revenue growth of 3.1% YoY (FY2025) is modest outside the Q1 2026 spike, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of 3.4% reflects the mature auto parts industry [5].
