Company Profile
Industrials ·Integrated Freight & Logistics ·NASDAQ ·Australia
Globavend Holdings is an emerging e-commerce logistics provider offering end-to-end cross-border services from Hong Kong to Australia and New Zealand, now diversifying into AI-powered digital entertainment through the acquisition of 70% of Loomi Group. Logistics services include parcel consolidation, air freight forwarding, customs clearance, and delivery. The Loomi division operates an AI production platform called 'Imaginary' and a short drama app. Founded in 2016, headquartered in Perth, Australia, with approximately 9 employees in the core logistics operation.

Investment Thesis
Globavend Holdings offers micro-cap exposure to cross-border e-commerce logistics (Hong Kong to Australia/New Zealand) now combined with an AI entertainment bet via the 70% Loomi Group acquisition [1][2]. H1 FY2026 revenue grew 8% to $14.8M with daily packages up 14.1%, and the all-warrants-expired capital structure simplifies the share count [1][3]. The Loomi acquisition adds optionality in AI-powered content creation with experienced leadership (CEO Sharon Tse, 18 years in AI/digital media). At $2.54M market cap, any commercial traction from Loomi or acceleration in logistics volumes could drive meaningful percentage moves.
The bear case is fundamental. Globavend has a $2.54M market cap and 9 employees in core logistics — essentially a small private business traded on NASDAQ with extreme liquidity risk and no analyst coverage [4]. H1 FY2026 swung from $450K net income to a $31K net loss despite 8% revenue growth, as general and administrative expenses surged, likely reflecting Loomi acquisition costs [1]. The Loomi AI entertainment acquisition diversifies the business but adds execution risk in an entirely unrelated vertical — a 9-employee logistics company is now also trying to build an AI-powered media production platform in competition with well-funded content companies. The stock dropped from $3.12 in April to $1.10, a 65% decline, with liquidity so thin that institutional investors cannot participate. The Nasdaq compliance history (deficiency notice in 2024, regained compliance in 2025) signals ongoing listing risk. No forward P/E, no analyst coverage, no institutional ownership data, and minimal public disclosure make this essentially uninvestable by institutional standards.

Key Risks

  3. Diversification risk — a 9-employee logistics company acquiring an AI entertainment business (Loomi) creates execution complexity in two unrelated verticals with no management bench depth [2]
