Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Leisure Products ·NASDAQ ·United States
Hasbro is a global play and entertainment company operating through three segments: Wizards of the Coast and Digital Gaming (Magic: The Gathering, Dungeons & Dragons, digital games), Consumer Products (toys, games, and licensed consumer products under brands including Monopoly, Transformers, Nerf, Play-Doh), and Entertainment (film/TV production). Under CEO Chris Cocks, the company has pivoted from toy-first to gaming/IP-first strategy, divesting eOne entertainment and cutting 3,100+ employees since 2023. Magic: The Gathering eclipsed $500M in quarterly revenue for the first time in Q2 2026. Market cap is approximately $13B.

Investment Thesis
Hasbro at 15x forward earnings offers a restructuring story with a genuine growth engine in Wizards of the Coast. Magic: The Gathering eclipsed $500M in quarterly revenue for the first time in Q2 2026, Wizards & Digital Gaming grew 27% YTD, and adjusted operating profit surged 21% on margins expanding toward the 24-25% target [1]. The gaming-first pivot under CEO Cocks is working -- high-margin IP licensing and digital gaming are replacing lower-margin toy manufacturing as the earnings driver. The 3.04% dividend yield provides income while the company completes its transformation, and forward PEG of 1.69 suggests reasonable growth-adjusted valuation. AI deployment across operations (Gemini, OpenAI, ElevenLabs partnerships targeting 1M+ hours freed) could unlock further margin expansion as the restructured cost base benefits from automation [3].
The bear case centers on cultural destruction, secular toy decline, and execution risk. The 3,100+ layoffs since 2023 have gutted institutional knowledge and created a demoralized workforce -- Glassdoor reviews describe the 'heart of Hasbro disappearing' and leadership 'out of touch with teams,' raising questions about whether the remaining organization can sustain innovation [4][5]. Consumer Products grew only 2% YTD while Entertainment declined 22%, meaning the entire growth story depends on a single segment (WotC). The 5-year revenue CAGR is negative (~-3%) reflecting structural decline from the $6.4B 2021 peak, and full-year guidance of only 3-5% growth suggests the YTD 15% pace cannot be sustained. D/E of 531% reflects a capital structure weakened by $4.7B+ in write-downs and restructuring charges. The company disclosed an 'unauthorized network access' cybersecurity incident in Q1 2026, introducing operational and legal risk. AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (5/9): Hasbro faces mixed AI exposure across its segments. The physical toy and board game business (Consumer Products) retains meaningful moats -- physical manufacturing, retail distribution, and brand recognition that AI cannot directly displace. However, the high-growth Wizards & Digital Gaming segment is exposed: AI can generate game artwork, narrative content, and ruleset designs that historically required human creative teams, potentially commoditizing the creative labor that underpins Magic: The Gathering and D&D content production [3]. AI-generated tabletop and digital games from competitors represent a credible substitution risk for portions of the portfolio. The Entertainment segment's film/TV production faces AI disruption in visual effects, scriptwriting, and voice performance. Hasbro's IP moats (trademarked brands, licensed characters) provide protection, but the creative production layer is increasingly vulnerable. AI Opportunity (0/1): Hasbro is deploying AI internally (Gemini, OpenAI, ElevenLabs partnerships) targeting 1M+ hours of operational efficiency, but no quantified AI revenue or cost savings have been disclosed.
