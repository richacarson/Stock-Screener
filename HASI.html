Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
HA Sustainable Infrastructure Capital (formerly Hannon Armstrong) is the first U.S. public company solely dedicated to investments in climate solutions, managing over $16.4B in assets across energy efficiency, renewable energy, and sustainable infrastructure markets. Founded in 1981, the company invests in behind-the-meter distributed energy, grid-connected renewables (solar, solar-plus-storage, wind), and fuels/transport/nature projects including renewable natural gas and ecological restoration. The company operates as a specialty REIT and changed its name from Hannon Armstrong in June 2024. CEO Jeffrey Lipson leads since March 2023, with founder Jeff Eckel serving as Executive Chair.

Investment Thesis
HASI offers a compelling combination of climate-positive mission and attractive valuation — at 10.68x forward earnings with a 4.78% dividend yield, the stock is priced below its historical average despite accelerating fundamentals [1][3]. Q1 2026 delivered adjusted earnings up 31% YoY to $102M with managed assets growing 13% to $16.4B and portfolio yield of 9.2%. The company projects 8-10% annual adjusted EPS CAGR through 2026, and AI-driven data center growth is creating secular tailwinds for renewable energy demand. The Inspire Impact Score of 74 (ALIGNED) with 16 positive attributions across environmental and governance categories makes HASI one of the strongest faith-aligned holdings in the universe.
The bear case centers on financial complexity, leverage risk, and political uncertainty. HASI's 222.66% D/E ratio reflects REIT-typical leverage but creates significant interest rate sensitivity — rising rates compress both the value of existing fixed-rate assets and the spread on new investments [3]. The 2022 Muddy Waters short report alleged that HASI inflates GAAP earnings through non-cash income recognition from renewable energy tax credits, manipulated discount rates on residual assets, and PIK interest from stressed borrowers — while the company survived the attack, these accounting concerns have not been fully resolved [3]. Trailing GAAP ROE of only 3.37% and a 260% GAAP payout ratio suggest the dividend may be less covered than headline metrics imply. Political risk remains elevated as anti-ESG sentiment and potential subsidy rollbacks (IRA clean energy tax credits) could reduce demand for HASI's financing products. The trailing P/E of 54.71x vs forward P/E of 10.68x highlights the extreme gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings that makes valuation assessment difficult.

Key Risks
  1. Accounting complexity and Muddy Waters allegations — GAAP earnings include non-cash income from tax credit accounting, fair value gains, and PIK interest that may not represent sustainable economic earnings, creating risk that distributable cash flow is lower than reported [3]
  2. High leverage (222.66% D/E, $5.9B total debt) creates significant interest rate and refinancing risk — each 100bp rate increase compresses portfolio spreads and reduces the net present value of existing fixed-rate receivables [3]
  3. Political and regulatory risk from potential rollback of IRA clean energy tax credits and anti-ESG legislative pressure could reduce demand for HASI's financing products and compress investment yields [3]
