Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products ·NYSE ·United States
Hayward Holdings, Inc. is a global designer, manufacturer, and marketer of a broad portfolio of pool and outdoor living equipment. Products include energy-efficient pumps, filters, heaters, cleaners, sanitizers, LED lighting, and water features, all digitally connected through Hayward's IoT-enabled SmartPad platform. The company serves residential and commercial swimming pool aftermarket and new construction markets. Founded in 1925 and headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina, Hayward went public in January 2021.

Investment Thesis
Hayward Holdings is a steady recovery story in the pool equipment aftermarket. Q1 2026 delivered 12% revenue growth to $255.2M with gross margin expanding to 46.5% and net income up 63% to $23.4M [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to approximately 5% revenue growth with adjusted EPS of $0.84-$0.87 (9-13% growth from 2025). The company benefits from a durable installed base of ~5.5M in-ground residential pools in the U.S., which generates recurring aftermarket replacement demand. D/E of 68% is manageable, and 50.99% gross margin demonstrates strong product pricing power. Forward P/E of 19.74 is reasonable for a recovering industrial business, and the stock's decline to $14.36 (near the 52-week low of $12.93) may create a value entry point if the recovery accelerates.
The bear case centers on Hayward's modest growth profile and the hangover from the COVID pool boom. Revenue declined from an estimated $1.4B peak (2021) to $1.05B (2024) as pandemic-driven pool construction demand collapsed, leaving a 4-year revenue CAGR of approximately -5%. While recovery is underway (6.71% growth in 2025, 12% in Q1 2026), full-year guidance of only 4-5% growth suggests management expects normalization, not acceleration. ROE of 9.13% is below the 15% target, and net profit margin of 12% is modest relative to the 50%+ gross margin, indicating significant SG&A and interest cost overhead. Forward PEG of 2.08 suggests the stock is not cheap relative to growth expectations. CEO Holleran's $12M+ net insider selling in the past year raises questions about leadership conviction. The pool industry is cyclical, tied to housing market health and consumer discretionary spending, making Hayward vulnerable to any economic downturn. Tariff exposure on manufactured components is an additional risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. Pool equipment replacement cycle acceleration as aging pools from the 2000s-2010s construction boom require major equipment upgrades, driving aftermarket demand growth beyond current 5% guidance levels
  2. Energy efficiency regulations and consumer preference for IoT-connected pool management driving upgrade cycles and premium product mix shift, expanding the 50.99% gross margin further
  3. Q1 2026 showing 12% revenue growth and 63% net income growth suggests the recovery may be accelerating faster than the conservative 4-5% full-year guidance implies, creating potential for positive guidance revisions
Key Risks
  1. Cyclical housing market exposure — pool equipment demand correlates with housing starts and consumer discretionary spending, and any economic downturn would compress both new construction and aftermarket replacement volumes
  2. CEO Kevin Holleran's consistent net selling ($12M+ in past 12 months) and modest growth guidance of 4-5% suggest limited management enthusiasm for the stock at current levels and temper expectations for upside surprises
  3. Forward PEG of 2.08 and trailing P/E of 25.3x on a single-digit revenue growth business create valuation risk — any miss on the 5% revenue growth or $0.84-$0.87 EPS guidance could trigger meaningful multiple compression

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Pool equipment manufacturing is a physical-world business that AI structurally cannot displace [1]. Hayward's core products — pumps, filters, heaters, cleaners, sanitizers, lighting — require physical manufacturing, installation by licensed contractors, and ongoing maintenance of real swimming pools. The industry depends on physical infrastructure (swimming pools), regulatory compliance (electrical and plumbing codes), and skilled labor for installation and repair. AI cannot replace the need for clean, heated, filtered pool water. The installed base of approximately 5.5 million in-ground residential pools in the U.S. creates durable aftermarket demand regardless of AI's impact on other industries. However, some competitive dynamics could shift if AI enables smarter competitive pricing or supply chain optimization by rivals.
