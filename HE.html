Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Hawaiian Electric Industries is a holding company whose subsidiaries include Hawaiian Electric Company, a regulated electric utility serving approximately 95% of Hawaii's population across five islands (Oahu, Maui, Molokai, Lanai, and Hawaii Island), and American Savings Bank, one of Hawaii's largest banks. The company is navigating recovery from the devastating August 2023 Maui wildfire that killed 102 people, with a $4 billion settlement largely resolved. In June 2026, HEI consolidated leadership with Scott Seu as CEO and Shelee Kimura as President of both HEI and Hawaiian Electric. Q2 2026 revenue rose 21.5% to $939.7M with net income of $123.2M, though core net income (excluding wildfire items) fell to $22.5M. Dividend remains suspended since August 2023. Market cap approximately $2.2B.

Investment Thesis
Hawaiian Electric trades at 16.72x forward earnings as a monopoly utility serving 95% of Hawaii's population, with the Maui wildfire settlement largely resolved at $4 billion and Q2 2026 showing strong recovery — revenue of $939.7M (+21.5%) and reported net income of $123.2M ($0.71 EPS) [1]. The $2.2B market cap represents a heavily discounted valuation relative to regulated utility peers, and resolution of the wildfire overhang could unlock significant re-rating toward pre-wildfire multiples. The June 2026 leadership consolidation under CEO Scott Seu and President Shelee Kimura brings engineering expertise and organizational stability [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 46 (ALIGNED) with 18 positive attributions makes HE accessible to faith-based investors despite operational controversies.
The bear case is substantial and dominates. Core net income (excluding wildfire items) fell to $22.5M ($0.13 EPS) from $35.4M ($0.20 EPS) a year earlier, revealing that the underlying utility earnings are declining even as reported numbers recover [1]. D/E of 187% reflects elevated leverage from settlement financing, the dividend remains suspended with no reinstatement timeline, and O&M expenses are expected to significantly outpace inflation in 2026 due to higher insurance premiums, vegetation management, and labor costs. Revenue growth is inflated by wildfire-related items and rate adjustments rather than organic demand. Profit margin of 6.82% offers minimal buffer against cost escalation. The wildfire killed 102 people and destroyed Lahaina — reputational damage that may result in unfavorable rate case outcomes and persistent valuation discount [3]. Regulatory risk remains elevated as Hawaii utility oversight tightens, and the company faces continued litigation beyond the primary settlement.
