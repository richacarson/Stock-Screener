Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Hawaiian Electric Industries (HEI) is now a pure-play regulated electric utility holding company after divesting substantially all of its non-utility businesses; its subsidiary Hawaiian Electric serves roughly 95% of Hawaii's population across Oahu, Maui, Molokai, Lanai and Hawaii Island. FY2025 revenue was $3.09B with net income of $123M, following a $1.4B loss in 2024 driven by the Maui wildfire settlement. Q2 2026 net income was $123M ($0.71/share) including a $101M after-tax non-cash gain from remeasuring the remaining wildfire settlement liability; core net income fell to $33M from $42M. Scott Seu became CEO and Shelee Kimura President of both HEI and the utility on June 1, 2026. The common dividend has been suspended since August 2023. The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $17.38 to $8.70 (market cap ~$1.5B); Q3 results are due November 6, 2026.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Seen through Sinek's lens, Hawaiian Electric has a potentially worthy cause — powering an isolated island state with its own clean energy — but the last three years have been about survival: settling the Maui wildfire liability, suspending the dividend, selling non-utility businesses and consolidating leadership. Those were necessary decisions, but they were forced by crisis rather than chosen in service of a long-term vision, and a regulated monopoly faces no worthy rivals to sharpen it.
Just Cause
4
Just Cause (1/3): Decarbonizing Hawaii and reducing imported-oil dependence is a real and service-oriented aim that leadership references [8]. But it is largely driven by the state's 100% renewable mandate, and the Maui wildfire, with its allegations of deferred vegetation management, shows the cause did not consistently shape operating decisions [1][7].

Investment Thesis
At $8.70, HEI trades at 6.7x trailing and 8.5x forward earnings and roughly 0.85x book value of ~$10.21/share, with a simplified pure-play utility structure now that non-utility businesses have been divested [3][4][2]. Q2 2026 net income of $123M included a $101M non-cash gain as the remaining wildfire settlement liability was remeasured downward, and a successful rate rebasing or 2027 rate case could restore earned returns and eventually allow the dividend to return [1][6]. As a physical monopoly utility, the business carries essentially no AI disruption risk.
The bear case is that core earnings are shrinking: core net income fell to $33M in Q2 2026 from $42M on higher interest expense and O&M, operating cash flow is negative on a TTM basis, and free cash flow per share is about -$3.28 as capex continues [1][4]. D/E of 159% and a current ratio below 1.0 leave limited flexibility, wildfire insurance disputes and environmental matters (PCB reserve of $9.3M at Waiau, endangered seabird litigation) remain open, and Sustainalytics rates its ESG risk High at 30.8 [7]. The stock has lost roughly half its value from the $17.38 52-week high, and the dividend remains suspended.
