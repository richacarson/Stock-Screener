Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NGM ·Canada
Helus Pharma (formerly Cybin Inc.) is a clinical-stage pharmaceutical company developing therapies for psychiatric and neurological conditions using deuterated psychedelic analogs. Lead candidate HLP003, a deuterated psilocybin analog, is in a Phase 3 pivotal trial (APPROACH study) for adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder, with topline data expected Q4 2026. HLP004 (deuterated DMT) is in Phase 2 for generalized anxiety disorder. The company rebranded from Cybin to Helus Pharma in October 2026 and is headquartered in Toronto, Canada.

Investment Thesis
Helus Pharma at $11.80 with an $862M market cap represents a high-risk, high-reward bet on the emerging psychedelic therapeutics space. The company's lead candidate HLP003 (deuterated psilocybin) is in a Phase 3 pivotal trial (APPROACH study) for adjunctive treatment of MDD, with topline data expected Q4 2026 — a potential de-risking catalyst that could transform the company's valuation [1][3]. The deuterated molecule offers potential advantages over traditional psilocybin including improved metabolic stability and shorter treatment sessions. Cash of $166M with zero debt provides runway into mid-2027, and exceptional insider buying in September 2026 — CEO Halstead ($1.35M) and co-founder Glavine ($1.25M) purchasing shares in the open market — signals strong management conviction in the upcoming data readout [4].
The bear case is substantial for this pre-revenue clinical-stage biotech. Quarterly net losses have expanded from $24.6M to $47.8M over the past year, with no revenue and an estimated 18-month cash runway that will require additional dilutive financing before any potential commercialization [1]. Three CEO changes in under a year (Drysdale → Cola → interim So → Halstead) raise serious governance and execution concerns — the board removed Cola at its request, suggesting undisclosed operational issues [2]. Phase 3 clinical risk is binary: failure of the APPROACH study would be catastrophic for the stock, and psychedelic therapeutics face unique regulatory hurdles including DEA scheduling, restricted distribution channels, and the need for supervised administration that limits market size. Competitor Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA therapy was rejected by the FDA in 2024, demonstrating the regulatory gauntlet facing this therapeutic class. The stock trades at 3.0x book value despite generating zero revenue, pricing in clinical success that remains unproven.
Key Catalysts
  1. Phase 3 APPROACH study topline data expected Q4 2026 — positive results for HLP003 in adjunctive MDD treatment would be a transformative de-risking event, potentially supporting an NDA filing and establishing Helus as the leader in deuterated psychedelic therapeutics [1][3]
  2. New CEO Michael Halstead brings directly relevant experience from Intra-Cellular Therapies, where he helped scale operations through the $14.6B acquisition by Johnson & Johnson in 2025, providing a commercial-stage playbook for Helus's potential transition from clinical to commercial [2]
  3. HLP004 Phase 2 readout for generalized anxiety disorder provides pipeline optionality beyond the lead MDD program, addressing a large patient population with limited effective treatments and diversifying clinical risk [3]
Key Risks
  1. Binary Phase 3 clinical risk — failure of the APPROACH study for HLP003 in MDD would eliminate the primary value driver, and the FDA's 2024 rejection of Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA therapy demonstrates the regulatory challenges facing novel psychedelic-derived treatments [1]
  2. Cash runway of approximately 18 months with quarterly burn rate accelerating to $47.8M requires additional dilutive financing before any potential NDA filing or commercialization, likely at depressed valuations if clinical results disappoint [1]
  3. Leadership instability with three CEO changes in under a year raises execution risk at the most critical juncture in the company's history, as Phase 3 data readout and potential NDA preparation require sustained operational focus and regulatory expertise [2]
