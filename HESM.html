Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Hess Midstream LP is a fee-based, growth-oriented midstream company that owns, operates, and develops midstream assets providing gathering, processing, storage, terminaling, and water handling services primarily in the Bakken and Three Forks Shale plays in North Dakota. Following the Hess-Chevron merger, Chevron beneficially owns approximately 37.8%. Q2 2026 revenue was $399M with net income of $174M and 85% EBITDA margin. 2026 guidance: Adjusted EBITDA $1,225-1,275M, Adjusted FCF $910-960M, net income $650-700M. Distribution of $0.7888/unit in Q2, with at least 5% annual DPS growth committed through 2028.

Investment Thesis
Hess Midstream is a well-executed midstream MLP transitioning from infrastructure buildout to free cash flow harvesting, with Q2 2026 delivering $174M net income on $399M revenue at an 85% EBITDA margin [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance of $1,225-1,275M Adjusted EBITDA and $910-960M Adjusted FCF provides earnings visibility, with management committing to at least 5% annual distribution growth through 2028 fully funded from FCF [3]. The 7.85% yield with 8 consecutive years of distribution growth (historically ~10% CAGR) is well-supported by fee-based, minimum volume commitment contracts with Chevron. At forward P/E of 12.98 with 23.2% profit margins, the valuation is attractive for a stable infrastructure asset [6]. AI data center electricity demand provides a secular tailwind for natural gas production and midstream throughput volumes.
The bear case centers on structural leverage, revenue decline, single-basin concentration, and sponsor dependence. D/E of 948.5% reflects the MLP capital structure with $3.8B+ debt against minimal equity — while typical for MLPs, any sustained throughput decline would stress distribution coverage [6]. Q2 2026 revenue of $399M declined from $414.2M a year earlier (-3.7% YoY) as lower Bakken throughput volumes partly offset tariff increases and third-party growth, raising questions about the production trajectory [1]. Customer concentration risk is acute: Chevron represents the overwhelming majority of throughput, making HESM essentially a single-customer business dependent on Chevron's Bakken production decisions post-merger [4]. Distribution growth is moderating from historical ~10% to guided 5%, and the GAAP payout ratio exceeding 100% (a function of MLP accounting) limits financial flexibility [3][6]. The Innovation score of 2, Inspiration score of 1, and Infinite Game score of 2 reflect that HESM is a cash distribution vehicle, not a purpose-driven enterprise — the IOWN framework structurally penalizes MLPs that lack innovation, mission, and strategic vision.
Key Catalysts
  1. FCF generation phase with Adjusted FCF of $910-960M in 2026 enabling at least 5% annual distribution growth through 2028, fully self-funded without equity issuance [3]
  2. AI data center electricity demand boom driving increased Bakken natural gas production and midstream throughput volumes, potentially exceeding minimum volume commitments and generating upside revenue [5]
  3. Third-party customer expansion beyond Chevron diversifying revenue and reducing single-customer concentration risk, as evidenced by Q2 2026 growth in third-party services offsetting Chevron volume declines [1]
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration: Chevron controls ~37.8% of HESM and represents the dominant share of throughput — any reduction in Chevron's Bakken production plans would directly impair volumes and cash flow [4]
  2. Revenue declining 3.7% YoY in Q2 2026 as Bakken throughput volumes fell, with D/E of 948.5% and GAAP payout ratio exceeding 100% leaving limited margin of safety if volumes continue declining [1][6]
  3. Distribution growth moderating from historical ~10% annual rate to guided 'at least 5%' through 2028, which may fail to keep pace with investor expectations or competing income alternatives [3]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Hess Midstream's core business of gathering, processing, storing, and transporting oil, gas, and produced water is entirely physical infrastructure that AI structurally cannot substitute [1]. FCC-style regulatory barriers do not apply, but long-term minimum volume commitment contracts with Chevron and the physical nature of the assets create equivalent protection. Midstream energy has been identified as an 'overlooked beneficiary of the AI boom' — AI data center buildouts are driving massive increases in electricity and natural gas demand, directly increasing throughput for midstream operators [5]. The company's SEC filings mention generic AI risks (biased outputs, cybersecurity) as standard disclosures but no analysts flag AI as a disruption threat to midstream operations.
