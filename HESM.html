Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Hess Midstream LP is a fee-based, growth-oriented midstream company that owns, operates, and develops midstream assets to provide services to Hess (now Chevron) and third-party customers. Assets include oil gathering, gas gathering and compression, gas processing, water handling, and storage and terminaling facilities primarily located in the Bakken and Three Forks Shale plays in North Dakota. Following the Hess-Chevron merger, Chevron beneficially owns approximately 37.8% of Hess Midstream.

Investment Thesis
Hess Midstream is transitioning from a capital-intensive buildout phase to a free cash flow harvesting model, with the completion of its multiyear Bakken infrastructure project enabling substantially lower future capex and growing distributions [1][2]. Q1 2026 revenue of $390.1M with full-year 2026 guidance of $1.225-$1.275B Adjusted EBITDA and $650-$700M net income provides earnings visibility. The distribution yield of 7.93% is well-supported by fee-based, minimum volume commitment contracts with Chevron, and the company has increased distributions for 8 consecutive years at approximately 10% annual growth [5]. At a forward P/E of 13.43 with profit margins of 22.64%, the valuation is attractive for a stable infrastructure asset converting nearly all EBITDA to distributable cash flow as capital needs diminish.
The bear case centers on structural leverage, single-basin concentration, and sponsor dependence. D/E of 1006% reflects the MLP capital structure with $3.77B debt against minimal equity, amplifying both returns and risk, while the 104.8% GAAP payout ratio indicates distributions slightly exceed reported earnings [5]. Customer concentration risk is acute: Chevron represents the overwhelming majority of throughput, making HESM essentially a single-customer business dependent on Chevron's Bakken production decisions. Q1 2026 revenue growth of just 2.1% represents meaningful deceleration from 8.3% full-year 2025 growth, and the company's 2.3% forward revenue growth rate lags the broader energy sector [1]. Net insider selling of $1.43B in the last 12 months signals limited leadership conviction. The Innovation score of 2 and Inspiration score of 1 reflect that HESM is a cash distribution vehicle, not a purpose-driven enterprise — IOWN's framework heavily penalizes the absence of innovation and mission even when infrastructure metrics are strong.
Key Catalysts
  1. Completion of infrastructure buildout enabling shift to FCF generation with capex declining to substantially lower levels, supporting continued 10%+ annual distribution growth through 2028
  2. AI data center electricity demand boom driving increased Bakken natural gas production and midstream throughput volumes above minimum volume commitments
  3. Potential third-party customer expansion beyond Chevron, diversifying the revenue base and reducing single-customer concentration risk in the Bakken
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration: Chevron controls ~37.8% of HESM and represents the dominant share of throughput — any reduction in Chevron's Bakken production plans would directly impair volumes and cash flow
  2. Debt-to-equity of 1006% and GAAP payout ratio of 104.8% leave minimal margin of safety if commodity prices decline and Bakken activity slows below minimum volume commitments
  3. Revenue growth decelerating to 2.1% YoY in Q1 2026 from 8.3% in 2025, with forward estimates of only 2.3% annual growth, risking distribution growth stall if throughput volumes plateau

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Hess Midstream's core business of gathering, processing, storing, and transporting oil, gas, and produced water is entirely physical infrastructure that AI cannot substitute [1]. Pipelines, compressor stations, gas processing plants, and storage terminals require real-world engineering and operations that are structurally immune to AI disruption. Midstream energy has been identified as an 'overlooked beneficiary of the AI boom' as AI data center buildouts are driving massive increases in electricity and natural gas demand, directly increasing throughput volumes for midstream operators [5].
