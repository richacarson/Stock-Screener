Company Profile
Industrials ·Metal Fabrication ·NasdaqCM ·Hong Kong
Highway Holdings Limited manufactures and sells metal, plastic, electric, and electronic parts and components, subassemblies, and finished products. The company operates manufacturing facilities in Myanmar and China, serving customers in Europe, North America, and Asia. Founded in 1990 by Roland Kohl, headquartered in Hong Kong. In March 2026, acquired a 51% stake in Regent-Feinbau Adermann GmbH, a German precision sheet metal fabrication company serving the automotive sector.

Investment Thesis
Highway Holdings is a micro-cap metal fabrication company navigating a significant revenue downturn following the loss of a major electric motor customer, which drove a 40.5% revenue decline in Q3 FY2026 and a nine-month net loss of $427,000 compared to $421,000 profit in the prior year period [1][2]. The company has pivoted strategically by completing the acquisition of 51% of Regent-Feinbau Adermann GmbH in March 2026 for EUR 0.66 million, a German precision sheet metal company serving the Chinese automotive market [3]. This marks an attempt to diversify revenue away from its shrinking Hong Kong/Myanmar manufacturing base. However, the company trades at a sub-$1M daily liquidity level ($215K average daily dollar volume), making it essentially uninvestable for institutional capital. With negative ROE (-12.31%), negative profit margins (-13.84%), and a PEG ratio of 20.56, the financial profile signals severe distress [4].
The Regent-Feinbau acquisition, while strategically logical, carries significant execution risk for a company of Highway Holdings' size. Integrating a German precision manufacturer into a Hong Kong-listed company with Myanmar and China operations adds complexity disproportionate to the firm's resources. CEO Roland Kohl's 36-year tenure provides stability but also raises succession risk — as the sole founder and driving force since 1990, there is no visible leadership pipeline [5]. Cash reserves of approximately $5.1 million ($1.10 per diluted share) provide limited runway for additional acquisitions or sustained operating losses [2]. The newly reconstituted board of directors with only 1.1 years average tenure raises governance concerns. Until the Regent-Feinbau acquisition generates meaningful revenue diversification and the company returns to profitability, there is insufficient evidence of a turnaround to justify an investment position.
