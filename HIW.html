Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Highwoods Properties is a fully integrated, self-administered REIT that owns, develops, acquires, leases, and manages office properties primarily in the best business districts (BBDs) of fast-growing Sun Belt markets including Raleigh, Nashville, Tampa, Charlotte, Atlanta, Dallas, Orlando, and Richmond. The company owns approximately 27 million square feet of office space and is known for its BBD-focused strategy and strong sustainability practices.

Investment Thesis
Highwoods Properties offers a 5.95% dividend yield on a 32-year payment streak, backed by a differentiated Sun Belt BBD strategy that has partially shielded the portfolio from the worst of the post-pandemic office downturn [1][4]. Q1 2026 revenue of $214.0M grew 6.1% YoY, and FFO guidance of $3.46-$3.70 per share suggests the portfolio is stabilizing with same-property NOI growth resuming. The company's sustainability-linked debt instruments and nearly 100 ENERGY STAR certified properties enhance tenant appeal in an increasingly ESG-conscious corporate real estate market [4]. At a dividend yield approaching 6% with decades of payment consistency, HIW offers income investors a stable cash flow stream from physical assets in some of America's fastest-growing metropolitan areas.
The bear case centers on secular office demand decline, valuation distortion, and growth stagnation. The post-pandemic shift to hybrid and remote work has structurally reduced office demand, and occupancy rates remain below pre-pandemic norms despite improving leasing activity [1]. Jefferies downgraded HIW specifically because its Sun Belt BBD portfolio lacks exposure to AI tenant demand — the fastest-growing source of office leasing — unlike West Coast peers capturing AI company expansion [2]. D/E of 156.32% creates interest rate sensitivity, and the GAAP payout ratio of 238.10% (though FFO-covered) signals that the dividend consumes more than all reported earnings. Most critically, the $2.00/share annual dividend has been flat with a 5-year growth CAGR of just 0.82%, offering zero inflation protection and suggesting limited financial flexibility for growth investment. Glassdoor reviews citing management's resistance to cultural adaptation raise concerns about the company's ability to evolve as workplace needs change [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sun Belt population and business migration trends continuing to drive office demand in Raleigh, Nashville, Tampa, Charlotte, and Atlanta — markets where Highwoods holds BBD-dominant positions [1]
  2. Interest rate reductions in 2026 could lower financing costs on the leveraged capital structure, improve cap rates, and drive REIT multiple expansion across the office sector
  3. Leasing momentum recovery with Q1 2026 revenue up 6.1% YoY and same-property NOI growth resuming, potentially driving FFO above guidance midpoint [1]
Key Risks
  1. Secular office demand decline from remote/hybrid work may permanently impair demand for traditional office space, even in growing Sun Belt markets [1]
  2. Zero dividend growth — flat $2.00/share annual dividend with 0.82% 5-year CAGR provides no inflation protection and signals limited financial flexibility for reinvestment or growth [5]
  3. Limited AI market exposure flagged by Jefferies as competitive weakness — unlike coastal peers capturing AI tenant demand, HIW's Sun Belt BBDs have attracted minimal AI-related leasing activity [2]
