Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Highwoods Properties is a fully integrated, self-administered REIT that owns, develops, acquires, leases, and manages office properties in the best business districts (BBDs) of fast-growing Sun Belt markets including Raleigh, Nashville, Tampa, Charlotte, Atlanta, Dallas, Orlando, and Richmond. The company owns approximately 27 million square feet of office space. Q2 2026 FFO was $0.90/share with full-year guidance raised to midpoint $3.58/share. Year-end occupancy targeted at 86.5-88.5%. Quarterly dividend of $0.50/share yields approximately 6.6%. Market cap approximately $3.4B.

Investment Thesis
Highwoods Properties offers a 6.6% dividend yield on a 32-year consecutive payment streak, backed by a differentiated Sun Belt BBD strategy that is showing operational recovery — Q2 2026 FFO of $0.90/share led management to raise full-year guidance to a $3.58 midpoint, with year-end occupancy targeted at 86.5-88.5% [1]. Revenue of $216.4M in Q2 grew 7.5% YoY, and profit margin expanded to 20.1% reflecting improving fundamentals [6]. The company's ~27M square foot portfolio in fast-growing Sun Belt markets (Raleigh, Nashville, Tampa, Charlotte, Atlanta) benefits from continued population and business migration trends. At P/FFO of approximately 8.5x and with sustainability-linked debt instruments and nearly 100 ENERGY STAR certified properties, HIW combines income generation with ESG credibility [4]. The Inspire ALIGNED rating of 52 with zero negative attributions and 14 positive categories supports faith-based allocation.
The bear case centers on secular office demand decline, growth stagnation, and AI market exclusion. The post-pandemic shift to hybrid and remote work has structurally reduced office space demand, and occupancy remains below pre-pandemic norms despite improving leasing activity [1]. Jefferies downgraded HIW specifically because its Sun Belt BBD portfolio lacks exposure to AI tenant demand — the fastest-growing source of office leasing — unlike West Coast peers capturing AI company expansion [2]. The GAAP forward P/E of 43.46x (inflated by REIT depreciation) overstates valuation risk, but D/E of 132.6% creates meaningful interest rate sensitivity [6]. Most critically, the $2.00/share annual dividend has been flat with a 5-year growth CAGR of just 0.82%, offering zero inflation protection and signaling limited financial flexibility. The GAAP payout ratio of 132.5% indicates distributions exceed reported earnings, though FFO coverage of 1.79x is adequate [6]. Glassdoor reviews citing management resistance to cultural adaptation raise concerns about the organization's ability to evolve as workplace needs change [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. FFO guidance raised to $3.46-$3.70 (midpoint $3.58) on stronger Q2 2026 leasing momentum, with occupancy trajectory improving toward 86.5-88.5% year-end, signaling portfolio stabilization [1]
  2. Sun Belt population and business migration continuing to drive office demand in Highwoods' BBD markets — Raleigh, Nashville, Tampa, Charlotte, and Atlanta are among the fastest-growing U.S. metros [4]
  3. Interest rate reductions in 2026-2027 could lower financing costs on the leveraged capital structure, improve cap rates, and drive REIT sector multiple expansion benefiting the 6.6% yield [6]
Key Risks
  1. Secular office demand decline from remote/hybrid work may permanently impair traditional office space demand, even in growing Sun Belt markets where occupancy remains below pre-pandemic levels [1]
  2. Zero dividend growth — flat $2.00/share annual dividend with 0.82% 5-year CAGR provides no inflation protection and signals limited financial flexibility for reinvestment, growth, or capital return enhancement [6]
  3. Limited AI market exposure flagged by Jefferies as competitive weakness — unlike coastal peers capturing AI tenant demand, HIW's Sun Belt BBDs have attracted only one AI-related tenant (Dallas), missing the fastest-growing leasing segment [2]
