Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Precious Metals & Mining ·NYSE ·United States
Hecla Mining is the largest primary silver producer in the United States and Canada, operating mines in Alaska (Greens Creek), Idaho (Lucky Friday), and the Yukon (Keno Hill). Founded in 1891, the company completed the sale of its Casa Berardi gold mine in March 2026 and retired all long-term debt in April 2026, transforming into a pure-play silver company with an unencumbered balance sheet. Q2 2026 revenue was $333.9M with record free cash flow and net income of $117.7M.

Investment Thesis
Hecla delivered record Q2 2026 results with revenue of $333.9M (up 52% YoY), net income of $117.7M (up 104%), and record free cash flow [1]. H1 2026 sales reached $745.3M, up 76% YTD, driven by elevated gold and silver prices. The balance sheet transformation is complete — Hecla retired all long-term debt in April 2026, achieving D/E of just 10% with substantial cash reserves [4]. As the largest primary silver producer in North America, Hecla benefits from structural silver demand growth driven by solar panel manufacturing, electronics, and AI infrastructure buildout. Forward P/E of 16.8 is reasonable for a commodity producer with 32% profit margins and 20.7% ROE. Medium-term target of 20M oz annual silver production provides a clear growth trajectory from the current 15-16M oz base [1].
The bear case centers on commodity price dependence and valuation. Silver at elevated levels is the primary driver of Hecla's record financials — any meaningful price correction would compress margins rapidly given the fixed-cost mining structure [1]. The PEG ratio of 5.64 suggests the market has already priced in the silver boom, and the stock's significant volatility ($4.71 to $34.17 52-week range) reflects commodity sensitivity [4]. 2026 silver production guidance of 15.1-16.5M oz is a step down from 2025's 17M oz as the portfolio adjusts post-Casa Berardi sale. Innovation and inspiration scores are structurally constrained for a commodity miner — Hecla does not have a distinctive competitive moat beyond its geological assets. Environmental liabilities from the Bunker Hill Superfund, Greens Creek, and Lucky Friday operations create ongoing regulatory and reputational risk [5]. The 0.08% dividend yield provides essentially no income support.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained elevated silver prices driven by industrial demand from solar, electronics, and AI data center infrastructure, plus safe-haven demand from geopolitical uncertainty supporting record-level revenue and free cash flow
  2. Keno Hill mine ramp-up in Yukon reaching nameplate capacity and medium-term production target of 20M oz annually, adding incremental silver output to North America's largest primary silver portfolio
  3. Zero long-term debt and strong cash position enabling opportunistic M&A or organic growth investments to consolidate North American silver production leadership
Key Risks
  1. Silver price correction from current elevated levels would compress margins rapidly — while AISC of $15-16/oz provides a floor, revenue and free cash flow are highly leveraged to commodity prices with limited hedging in place
  2. 2026 silver production guidance of 15.1-16.5M oz represents a temporary decline from 2025's 17M oz during portfolio transition following the Casa Berardi sale and Keno Hill ramp
  3. Environmental liability risk from historical contamination at Bunker Hill (Superfund), Greens Creek (hazardous waste), and Lucky Friday (Clean Water Act), plus potential for new regulatory enforcement disrupting production
