Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Harmonic Inc. is now a pure-play broadband infrastructure company following the June 2026 completion of its Video business divestiture to MediaKind for $145 million cash. The company's cOS cloud-native broadband platform and network-edge devices (Oyster, Ripple, SeaStar) enable cable and fiber operators to deliver hybrid fiber/coax and fiber-to-the-premises broadband service. Q1 2026 broadband revenue grew 43% YoY to $121.7 million, with full-year 2026 broadband revenue guided to $475-495 million and backlog plus deferred revenue up 87% YoY to $582.1 million.

Investment Thesis
Harmonic completed its transformation into a pure-play broadband infrastructure company in June 2026, divesting the Video business to MediaKind for $145 million cash and concentrating entirely on its cOS virtualized broadband platform [2]. The continuing business is accelerating: Q1 2026 broadband revenue grew 43% YoY to $121.7 million (beating consensus by ~17%), rest-of-market revenue (customer diversification beyond top accounts) grew 78% YoY to 42% of total revenue, and management raised FY2026 broadband revenue guidance to $475-495 million on the back of backlog plus deferred revenue up 87% YoY to $582.1 million [1]. AI-driven compression and network analytics are already shipping to customers including DIRECTV, positioning Harmonic to benefit from — rather than be threatened by — rising video and data traffic [1]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E of 36.9%) and Forward P/E of 18.26x is reasonable for a business inflecting toward growth.
The bear case is that the reported financials still look like a company in decline: TTM revenue fell 45.6% and FY2025 revenue fell 26.2%, with a -$41.9 million TTM net loss and -12.0% profit margin, even though most of this reflects the deliberate Video divestiture rather than organic deterioration [6]. Employee sentiment is mixed — headcount down 5.4% since 2023, job postings down 90.6% YoY, and Glassdoor reviews citing management 'out of touch with reality' — and insiders have been net sellers of roughly $977K over the trailing 12 months [4]. The broadband business itself carries customer-concentration risk, with 'underwhelming rollouts by its largest customers' cited as an ongoing uncertainty even as rest-of-market diversification improves [1]. Until a full post-divestiture reporting period establishes a clean baseline, the stock's mechanical Infrastructure metrics will continue to look weaker than the underlying broadband business's actual trajectory.
Key Catalysts
  1. First full clean reporting quarters as a pure-play broadband company, which should reveal the underlying growth trajectory (43% YoY in Q1 2026) without Video-segment noise dragging down consolidated figures
  2. Conversion of the $582.1 million backlog and deferred revenue balance (+87% YoY) into recognized revenue, supporting the raised $475-495 million FY2026 broadband guidance
  3. Continued rest-of-market customer diversification (already 42% of revenue, +78% YoY) reducing dependence on a small number of large North American cable operators
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration remains real — 'underwhelming rollouts by its largest customers' was flagged as an ongoing uncertainty even amid the broader broadband recovery
  2. Reported consolidated financials (TTM revenue -45.6%, net loss -$41.9M) will look weak for several more quarters until the Video divestiture fully rolls off comparisons, creating headline risk even if the underlying broadband business is healthy
  3. Mixed employee sentiment and net insider selling (~$977K trailing 12 months, though partly tied to a long-tenured executive's retirement) warrant monitoring for deeper leadership or execution issues
