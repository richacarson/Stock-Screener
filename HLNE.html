Investment Thesis
Hamilton Lane is a best-in-class private markets platform with exceptional financial characteristics — 31.7% ROE, 32.8% profit margin, 28% D/E, and a PEG ratio of 0.83 at Forward P/E 16.64x represent a rare combination of quality and growth at a reasonable price [1][4]. Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended June 2026) delivered $275.3M revenue (+56.5% YoY) and EPS of $1.94, beating consensus by $0.42, demonstrating the platform's accelerating earnings power [1]. FY2026 management fees grew 14% to $584.2M, Fee Related Earnings surged 25% to $344.5M, and the 11% dividend increase to $2.40/share annual target reflects management confidence. The Inspire Insight score of 62 (ALIGNED) with 16 positive ESG attributions is among the highest in the screening universe, consistent with the firm's responsible investment leadership since 2008 and Novata co-founding [3]. The 2.39% dividend yield with 11.7% 5yr CAGR provides an attractive and growing income stream.
The bear case centers on valuation expansion risk, CEO transition friction, and cyclical exposure. The stock has declined 34.5% over the past 52 weeks from peak levels despite strong fundamentals, suggesting the market is repricing private markets exposure amid uncertainty about PE/VC valuations [4]. Glassdoor reviews post-CEO transition cite mixed sentiment, with concerns about culture changes under the Co-CEO structure and inadequate hiring — a risk factor in a relationship-intensive business where talent retention is the product [2]. Revenue growth is lumpy due to performance fees: FY2026 total revenue grew only 6% despite 14% management fee growth, and the Q1 FY2027 spike of 56.5% may not be sustainable. Competition from Blackstone, KKR, and Apollo in the Evergreen/retail alternatives space is intensifying, potentially compressing fee rates. Payout ratio increased to 41.6%, slightly above the 40% dividend target threshold. Any sustained private markets downturn would compress both management fees (via lower AUM) and performance fees, and NAV-based fee calculations face mark-to-market risk in falling markets.

  2. PEG ratio of 0.83 with 20% 5-year EPS CAGR and 31.7% ROE represents a classic GARP (Growth at Reasonable Price) opportunity — the stock's 34.5% decline from 52-week highs creates an attractive entry point for a high-quality compounder [4]
