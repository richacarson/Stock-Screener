Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NYSE ·United States
Hinge Health, Inc. builds an AI-powered virtual musculoskeletal (MSK) care platform combining computer-vision motion tracking, wearable sensors, and clinical care teams to treat acute injury, chronic pain, and post-surgical rehabilitation for self-insured employers. Founded in 2014 by CEO Daniel Perez and public since its 2025 IPO, the company delivered 51% revenue growth to $588M in FY2025 and has raised its FY2026 revenue guidance three times, most recently to $818-824M (+40% YoY) [1].

Investment Thesis
Hinge Health is a rare combination of hypergrowth and profitability in digital health: FY2025 revenue grew 51% to $588M, and FY2026 guidance has been raised three times over the course of the year to a current range of $818-824M (+40% YoY), with a targeted 27% non-GAAP operating margin [1]. The business generated $299.6M of trailing free cash flow, carries negligible debt (D/E of 1.7%), and posts a 30.38% return on equity — financial characteristics far stronger than the typical high-growth digital health peer. Independent analyst research specifically frames the company as 'more immune to AI than most' given healthcare regulatory barriers, proprietary motion-tracking data, and hardware integration, while Hinge's own AI (TrueMotion computer vision) is the core enabler of its expansion into new clinical conditions like fall prevention [3].
The bear case centers on valuation and durability of the moat rather than current execution. At a forward P/E of 31.26x, the stock prices in continued 40%+ growth, and any deceleration toward the industry's more typical 15-25% digital-health growth rates could compress the multiple meaningfully. The same analyst research flagging AI immunity today also warns that advancing AI/computer-vision technology could eventually enable cheap, direct-to-consumer virtual PT apps distributed through app stores, and named competitors (Kaia Health, Omada Health, Sword Health) are becoming more AI-sophisticated [3]. As a company public only since 2025, Hinge Health has not been tested through a full economic downturn, a period of self-insured-employer benefits budget cuts (its core customer channel), or a leadership transition, and specific Inspiration evidence (employee retention data, insider buying, community investment) remains thin despite Perez's genuine founder-mission narrative.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued execution against the raised FY2026 guidance of $818-824M revenue (+40% YoY) and 27% non-GAAP operating margin, following three consecutive upward guidance revisions during 2026 [1]
  2. Successful commercial ramp of the new fall-prevention program and other adjacent conditions built on the TrueMotion computer-vision platform, expanding total addressable market beyond core MSK [1][2]
  3. Employer benefits budgets holding up or expanding in a softer labor market, since self-insured employers are Hinge's primary distribution channel
Key Risks
  1. A forward P/E of 31.26x assumes sustained 40%+ growth; any deceleration toward more typical digital-health growth rates could trigger meaningful multiple compression
  2. Advancing AI and computer-vision technology could eventually lower the barrier to cheap, direct-to-consumer virtual PT apps, and increasingly AI-sophisticated competitors (Kaia Health, Omada Health, Sword Health) could erode Hinge's differentiation over time [3]
  3. As a company public only since 2025, Hinge Health has not been tested by a genuine economic downturn or employer benefits budget cuts, which represent its primary customer acquisition channel
