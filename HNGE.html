Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NYSE ·United States
Hinge Health is a digital health company building a technology-driven health system to scale and automate the delivery of musculoskeletal (MSK) care. Its platform addresses acute injury, chronic pain, and post-surgical rehabilitation through AI-powered motion tracking technology and a proprietary electrical nerve stimulation wearable device (Enso), primarily serving self-insured employers. The company is expanding into migraine and gastrointestinal care via the planned Cylinder Health acquisition, with FY2026 revenue guided at $856-860M.

Investment Thesis
Hinge Health is executing at an exceptional level: Q2 2026 delivered $212.8M revenue (+53% YoY), operating margin of 29% (up from 19%), and EPS of $0.59 (beating estimates by 354%) [1]. FY2026 guidance was raised to $856-860M from $732-742M, with LTM calculated billings of $862M (+52% YoY) and free cash flow of $100M (up from $33M) [1]. The company is the dominant leader in digital MSK care with 2,000+ enterprise clients, and the expansion into migraine and GI care via Cylinder Health acquisition significantly expands the total addressable market. At forward P/E of 26.7x on forward EPS of ~$2.97, the valuation is reasonable for a healthcare platform growing 50%+ with rapidly expanding margins and strong analyst support (16 Buy ratings, $90.87 target) [2].
The bear case centers on three concerns: GAAP profitability, culture, and competition. Trailing EPS of -$12.31 and profit margin of -79% reflect massive stock-based compensation costs post-IPO — while non-GAAP margins are expanding to 29%, the GAAP/non-GAAP gap creates risk as lockup periods expire and insiders may sell [2]. The cultural red flags are significant: Glassdoor reviews describe a CEO who 'has no respect for employees,' AI being deployed to replace support teams, recent R&D layoffs, and a 'revolving door' of management [3]. These cultural issues could impair the company's ability to retain talent, innovate, and execute on its ambitious growth targets. Competitively, Big Tech health platforms (Apple, Google) and AI-native MSK startups (Sword Health) could commoditize motion tracking technology, compressing pricing power. The $5.8B market cap prices in significant execution and leaves limited room for disappointment.
