Company Profile

Hallador Energy operates as an independent power producer and coal-mining company through two segments: Electric Operations (the 1,080 MW coal-fired Merom power plant in Indiana) and Coal Operations (the Oaktown mining complex). FY2025 revenue grew 16% YoY to $469.5M with adjusted EBITDA up 3x to $56.0M, but management has flagged 2026 operational challenges at Merom that will cap performance near prior-year levels. The company is pursuing a non-binding, multi-year power supply agreement with an unnamed data center developer and has announced a 515MW gas expansion at Merom.

Investment Thesis
Hallador owns and operates the 1,080 MW Merom coal-fired power plant in Indiana and is positioning itself as a power supplier to the AI data center buildout, having signed a Conversion Transaction Commitment Agreement and non-binding term sheet for a 10+ year power delivery arrangement with an unnamed developer [2]. FY2025 revenue grew 16% YoY to $469.5M with adjusted EBITDA up 3x to $56.0M and operating cash flow up 23% to $81.1M, and the company announced a 515MW gas expansion at Merom to support future capacity commitments [1]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E of 25%) and the Forward P/E of 22.4x and PEG of 1.26 are reasonable relative to targets [5]. An Inspire Impact Score of 29 (ALIGNED) reflects positive attributions across business resilience, employee wellbeing, and low ecological impact.
The bear case centers on near-term operational deterioration and an unproven growth catalyst: management has explicitly guided that operational challenges at Merom will cap 2026 financial performance near 2025 levels, and six-month 2026 revenue of $206.0M already trailed the $220.5M reported in the same period of 2025 [1]. Trailing ROE (-0.58%) and profit margin (-0.20%) are both negative, and the trailing P/E of 736x signals a business barely profitable on a GAAP basis. The data center power agreement — the primary re-rating catalyst — remains a non-binding term sheet with no signed contract, meaning the AI upside is entirely speculative pending definitive documentation. As a coal-fired generator, Hallador also carries structural ESG and regulatory tail risk, with its own 10-K acknowledging heightened litigation exposure around ESG and 'greenwashing' claims [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Conversion of the non-binding data center power term sheet into a definitive, binding 10+ year supply agreement, which would provide multi-year revenue visibility well beyond current coal/merchant power economics [2]
  2. 515MW gas expansion at Merom progressing on schedule to add dispatchable capacity ahead of anticipated data center demand [1]
  3. Stabilization of Merom operational performance in H2 2026 after management flagged issues capping near-term results at prior-year levels [1]
Key Risks
  1. The data center power deal remains non-binding; failure to convert the term sheet into a signed contract would remove the primary catalyst supporting the current valuation [2]
  2. Six-month 2026 revenue already declined versus the prior-year period, and management has guided full-year 2026 results to be capped near 2025 levels due to Merom operational issues [1]
  3. Structural exposure to coal-sector ESG and regulatory risk, including litigation risk the company itself flags around environmental disclosures and 'greenwashing' allegations [3]
