Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Recreational Vehicles ·NYSE ·United States
Harley-Davidson is an iconic American motorcycle manufacturer founded in 1903, producing heavyweight cruiser and touring motorcycles. The company operates through three segments: Harley-Davidson Motor Company (HDMC), Harley-Davidson Financial Services (HDFS), and LiveWire (electric motorcycles). Under new CEO Artie Starrs (ex-Topgolf), the company unveiled its 'Back to the Bricks' strategic plan, relaunching the Sportster and Sprint lines and targeting mid-single-digit retail CAGR with $150M in cost reductions through 2028. Q1 2026 delivered $1.17B revenue with 14% North American retail growth and 8.1% global retail growth. Operates the Harley Owners Group (H.O.G.) with 1M+ members worldwide.

Just Cause

Investment Thesis
Harley-Davidson at $27.60 ($2.87B market cap) with a 2.72% dividend yield offers a value entry into one of America's most iconic brands during a strategic reset. Q1 2026 delivered 14% North American retail growth and 8.1% global growth to 33,500 units, validating early traction of the 'Back to the Bricks' strategy under new CEO Artie Starrs [1]. The Sportster and Sprint relaunch targeting mid-weight riders, $150M cost reduction program (2026-2028), and $225M medium-term profitability benefits provide a credible path to margin recovery from the current 4.78% profit margin [1][5]. D/E of 74% is manageable, the payout ratio of 42.21% supports dividend sustainability, and the company's aggressive $128M Q1 buyback at depressed prices (9.96% buyback yield annualized) enhances per-share value [5]. The Inspire Impact Score of 34 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions makes HOG a rare faith-compatible consumer cyclical with strong brand heritage.
The bear case centers on structural demand decline and execution risk. Revenue contracted from $5.84B in 2023 to $4.24B TTM (-27%), reflecting not just cyclical softness but aging demographics -- the average Harley buyer is 50+ and the company has no proven formula for attracting younger riders at scale [1][5]. LiveWire's electric motorcycle losses of $70-80M annually drain capital with only $5M quarterly revenue, representing a strategic bet with no clear path to profitability. The 17.98% short interest -- among the highest in the consumer space -- reflects institutional skepticism about the turnaround [5]. Forward P/E of 20.52 with PEG of 7.07 implies the market expects minimal growth relative to the multiple. Near-total C-suite turnover under Starrs creates execution risk during a critical transformation, and the $150M cost reduction program may prove insufficient if motorcycle demand doesn't stabilize. Volatile tariffs and weakening consumer discretionary spending create additional macro headwinds for a $30K+ average transaction price product.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Harley-Davidson's core business -- manufacturing, selling, and servicing physical motorcycles -- is structurally immune to AI disruption. The motorcycle riding experience is inherently physical and emotional; AI cannot replace the sensation of riding a Harley or the community bonds formed through H.O.G. events and rallies [3]. Manufacturing involves heavy industrial operations, proprietary engine designs (Milwaukee-Eight, Revolution Max), and a global dealer network of physical relationships that AI cannot displace. The HDFS financial services segment faces some AI exposure in credit underwriting and customer service, but these are enhancement opportunities not substitution risks. Tariffs, consumer discretionary spending, and demographic shifts pose far greater threats than AI [1]. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of meaningful AI revenue generation.
