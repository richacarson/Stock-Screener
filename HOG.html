Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Recreational Vehicles ·NYSE ·United States
Harley-Davidson designs, manufactures and sells heavyweight motorcycles, parts, accessories and apparel through its Harley-Davidson Motor Company (HDMC) segment. It also provides retail and wholesale financing through Harley-Davidson Financial Services (HDFS), in which it sold a $5B loan-book stake to KKR and PIMCO in 2025 while retaining control, and holds a majority stake in the electric-motorcycle company LiveWire Group (LVWR). CEO Artie Starrs, formerly CEO of Topgolf, took over in 2025 and launched the 'Back to the Bricks' dealer-health strategy. In Q3 2026 North American retail rose 9%, global dealer inventory fell 26%, and the company raised HDMC and HDFS guidance for the second time this year.

Investment Thesis
Harley's turnaround is gaining traction: Q3 2026 North American retail rose 9%, the fourth straight quarter of growth, while global dealer inventory fell 26%, and management raised HDMC and HDFS guidance for the second time in 2026 [1]. The $5B HDFS loan-book sale to KKR and PIMCO de-risked the balance sheet (D/E now 76.7%) and funded debt reduction and returns, and the stock offers a 2.4% yield with a 33-year payment record at 15.6x forward earnings [4][5].
The bear case is structural: revenue has shrunk from $5.84B in 2023 to $4.47B in 2025 and fell another 19.2% YoY, TTM margins are negative, and July guidance called for only $10-50M of HDMC operating income with $75-90M of tariff costs [2][5]. LiveWire continues to lose $70-80M a year, the core rider base is aging, and the 2024 DEI boycott and leadership churn show brand and governance vulnerability [2][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued North American retail growth (+9% in Q3 2026) from lower-priced Super Glide and Deadwood models
  2. Dealer inventory normalization (-26% YoY) allowing shipments to re-align with retail in 2027
  3. Strategic options for loss-making LiveWire ($70-80M 2026 operating loss guide)
Key Risks
  1. Tariff costs of $75-90M in 2026 compressing an already thin HDMC operating income guide of $10-50M
  2. Aging core customer demographic and weak younger-rider adoption driving secular volume decline
  3. Consumer credit deterioration affecting retained HDFS economics despite the KKR/PIMCO loan-book sale
