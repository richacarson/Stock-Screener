Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Recreational Vehicles ·NYSE ·United States
Harley-Davidson is an iconic American motorcycle manufacturer founded in 1903, producing heavyweight cruiser and touring motorcycles. The company operates through three segments: Harley-Davidson Motor Company (HDMC), Harley-Davidson Financial Services (HDFS), and LiveWire (electric motorcycles). HOG also operates the Harley Owners Group (H.O.G.), one of the world's largest brand communities with over 1 million members.

However, the near-total C-suite turnover under CEO Artie Starrs, the 2024 DEI policy reversal under social media pressure, a 'transition year' focused on cost-cutting, and HDMC guidance of near-breakeven operating income suggest a company playing to survive rather than playing to endure. Just Cause
7
Just Cause (2/3): Harley-Davidson's pursuit of 'freedom for the soul' through motorcycling is a genuine Just Cause that has endured for over 120 years — surviving wars, recessions, near-bankruptcy in the 1980s, and cultural shifts [3]. The brand represents more than transportation; it embodies personal freedom. H.O.G.'s 1M+ members are testament to people joining a cause, not buying a product. However, the aging core demographic (average buyer 50+), declining unit sales, and rotating leadership create doubt about whether the cause still inspires the next generation or shapes strategic decisions.

Investment Thesis
Harley-Davidson trades at $24.96 ($2.63B market cap) — a modestly recovered price from the $21 level earlier in 2026. Q1 2026 delivered $1.17B revenue with 14% North American retail sales growth and 22% dealer inventory reduction, signaling improving sell-through dynamics [1]. The HDMC segment's -2% revenue decline was relatively stable versus the -54% consolidated HDFS decline driven by the retail loan book sale. The brand's 120+ year heritage, dominant U.S. heavyweight motorcycle position, H.O.G. community of 1M+ members, and 3.0% dividend yield with a conservative 37.87% payout ratio provide a value floor [4]. Management's $150M annual cost savings program and new leadership team under CEO Artie Starrs could restore HDMC profitability, while LiveWire's 87% revenue growth (albeit from a tiny $5M base) shows early electric traction [1].
The bear case remains substantial. Consolidated revenue declined 12% YoY, net income fell 81% to $25M, and operating margin compressed to 2.0% from 12.1% in Q1 2026 [1]. FY2026 HDMC operating income guidance of -$40M to +$10M signals the motorcycle business may lose money this year, while LiveWire's $70-80M annual loss continues to drain capital without a path to scale. Forward P/E of 27.61 reflects analyst expectations of steep earnings decline, and the PEG ratio of 6.51 implies extremely poor growth-adjusted valuation [4]. The core demographic is aging (average buyer 50+) with no proven formula for attracting younger riders. Near-total C-suite turnover — new CEO, CFO, COO, CMO, and Chief Brand Officer — creates execution risk during a critical transition. The 2024 DEI controversy alienated both progressive and conservative customers, and revenue has contracted from $5.84B in 2023 to $4.32B TTM, suggesting structural decline rather than cyclical softness [1][4].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Harley-Davidson's core business — manufacturing, selling, and servicing physical motorcycles — is structurally immune to AI disruption. The motorcycle riding experience is inherently physical and emotional; AI cannot replace the sensation of riding a Harley or the community bonds formed through H.O.G. events and rallies [3]. Manufacturing involves heavy industrial operations, proprietary engine designs (Milwaukee-Eight, Revolution Max), and a global dealer network of physical relationships that AI cannot displace. The HDFS financial services segment faces some AI exposure in credit underwriting and customer service, but these are enhancement opportunities, not substitution risks. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of meaningful AI revenue generation. The company has appointed a Chief Marketing and Technology Officer to integrate digital capabilities but no AI products or quantified AI-driven revenue have been disclosed [2].
