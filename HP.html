Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYSE ·United States
Helmerich & Payne is a global drilling solutions provider for oil and gas exploration and production companies. Operating through North America Solutions, Offshore Solutions, and International Solutions segments, the company fields the largest active onshore rig count globally following its April 2025 acquisition of KCA Deutag. Q3 FY2026 showed a strong rebound with revenue exceeding $1B (+11% sequentially), 147 Lower 48 rigs working with super-spec utilization at 95%, and 10 rigs reactivated. Trey Adams became President and CEO in March 2026, succeeding long-time CEO John Lindsay. Founded in 1920, headquartered in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

Investment Thesis
Helmerich & Payne is the world's largest onshore drilling company by active rig count, and Q3 FY2026 marked a decisive inflection — revenue exceeded $1B (+11% sequentially), 10 rigs were reactivated, daily margins grew $1,000+ sequentially, and management raised full-year rig-count guidance [1]. Super-spec utilization reached 95%, and strong demand from private operators drove the reactivation. The early repayment of the $400M term loan and $40M annual cost savings target for FY2027 demonstrate financial discipline under new CEO Trey Adams [1]. FlexFusion automation technology differentiates H&P from commodity drillers, commanding premium day rates. International growth (Saudi Arabia, Argentina, offshore) provides diversification beyond the U.S. shale cycle. At $43.08 per share (up from ~$21 at prior screen), the stock has re-rated on the operational recovery with a 2.3% dividend yield.
The bear case centers on cyclical risk and unresolved profitability challenges. Despite the Q3 operational improvement, trailing ROE of -4.6% and profit margin of -3.4% confirm H&P remains unprofitable on a trailing basis, with forward EPS of only $1.48 implying thin margins even in recovery [3]. The PEG ratio of 6.81 suggests the stock is expensive relative to growth expectations. The dividend has been cut from $2.84 to $1.00 over five years (-12.2% CAGR), destroying income reliability and limiting appeal for dividend investors — the 2.3% yield is barely above the threshold. Revenue growth of -0.6% reflects ongoing post-acquisition normalization. An oil price decline below $60/bbl would compress U.S. rig counts and reduce North America Solutions revenue, the largest earnings contributor [1]. The stock's 112% rally from 52-week lows prices in a full recovery, leaving limited margin of safety if Q4 or FY2027 disappoints. International execution risk remains — the Saudi Arabia ramp-up and Argentina operations face geopolitical and operational complexity [2].

AI Risk Protection (7/9): H&P's core business — deploying and operating physical drilling rigs in oil fields, offshore platforms, and international locations — is structurally immune to AI substitution [2]. Drilling requires massive capital equipment ($25M+ per super-spec rig), 15,700 skilled field personnel, and real-world physical operations in remote locations that AI structurally cannot replace. No analysts have flagged AI displacement as a risk for contract drilling.
