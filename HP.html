Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYSE ·United States
Helmerich & Payne is a global drilling solutions provider for oil and gas exploration and production companies. Operating through North America Solutions, Offshore Solutions, and International Solutions segments, the company fields the largest active onshore rig count globally following its April 2025 acquisition of KCA Deutag. Trey Adams became President and CEO in March 2026, succeeding long-time CEO John Lindsay. Founded in 1920, headquartered in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

Investment Thesis
Helmerich & Payne is the world's largest onshore drilling company by active rig count following the April 2025 KCA Deutag acquisition, with a global platform spanning North America, the Middle East (24 rigs targeted in Saudi Arabia), offshore, and South America [3]. FlexFusion automation technology differentiates H&P from commodity drillers, and the orderly CEO succession to Trey Adams — who led technology integration programs and 'embodies the H&P Way' — provides leadership continuity [2]. At ~$21.22 per share (down ~42% from the prior screen), the stock trades at a significant discount to book value with a 4.71% dividend yield, offering meaningful upside if the KCA integration delivers synergies and drilling markets recover in H2 FY2026 as management guides.
The bear case centers on cyclical deterioration and integration risk. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $932M declined 8.2% YoY with an adjusted net loss of -$0.38/share, marking the second consecutive quarterly loss [1]. The U.S. drilling market remains soft with rig count pressure, and full year FY2026 revenue is estimated at $3.96B (-3% YoY), indicating post-acquisition revenue is contracting. The dividend has been cut from $2.84 to $1.00 over five years (-12.2% CAGR), destroying income reliability, and the forward payout ratio of 45.66% is stretched given negative trailing earnings [5]. D/E of 81% with $2.33B in debt requires servicing during a downturn when the company is unprofitable. Management's 'back-half recovery' narrative for H2 FY2026 depends on commodity prices and international contract awards that are not guaranteed. CSRHub flags environmental underperformance, a growing concern for an oil services company facing ESG-related capital constraints [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. International expansion ramp — 24 rigs targeted in Saudi Arabia by mid-2026 and growing presence in Middle East, Argentina, and offshore markets providing non-US growth diversification [3]
  2. KCA Deutag integration synergy realization — management expects cost savings and operational efficiencies to improve margins in H2 FY2026, with the new CEO Adams focused on disciplined execution [1][2]
  3. FlexFusion automation technology adoption driving premium pricing and market share gains in North America super-spec rig market, differentiating from commodity drillers [3]
Key Risks
  1. Oil price decline below $60/bbl would further compress U.S. rig counts, reducing North America Solutions revenue which remains the largest earnings contributor even post-KCA [1]
  2. KCA Deutag integration execution risk — two consecutive quarterly losses and impairment charges signal the integration is costlier and slower than anticipated [1]
  3. Dividend sustainability — the $1.00 annual dividend requires a return to positive earnings; another cut would further erode investor confidence and eliminate the stock's income appeal [5]
