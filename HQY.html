Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
HealthEquity is the largest independent health savings account (HSA) administrator in the United States, providing technology-enabled services platforms for consumers and employers. The company manages 17.8 million total accounts with $36.5 billion in HSA assets, offering HSAs, FSAs, HRAs, COBRA administration, commuter benefits, and an investment advisory platform. Founded in 2002, HealthEquity reported record FY2026 revenue of $1.31B (+9% YoY) with net income up 123% to $215.2M. New CEO Scott Cutler (former StockX CEO) succeeded 15-year CEO Jon Kessler in January 2025, bringing digital transformation expertise.
2,814 Employees ·www.healthequity.com

Investment Thesis
HealthEquity is a high-quality compounder with a dominant market position, strong balance sheet, and accelerating profitability. As the largest independent HSA administrator with 17.8M accounts and $36.5B in assets, the company benefits from secular tailwinds — HSA adoption continues growing as healthcare costs rise and employers shift toward high-deductible health plans [1]. FY2026 delivered record results with revenue of $1.31B (+9% YoY), net income surging 123% to $215.2M, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanding from 39% to 43% [1]. At 15.86x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.27, the valuation is reasonable for a category leader generating $397.5M in free cash flow (29% FCF margin) with conservative leverage (D/E 48.79%) [3]. AI deployment is already delivering measurable results (90% workflow reduction, 90% lower fraud), creating an efficiency flywheel that should continue expanding margins. The Inspire Impact Score of 51 (ALIGNED) with 8 positive attributions supports faith-based allocation.
The bear case centers on growth deceleration, CEO transition risk, and competitive threats. Revenue growth has moderated from double-digit levels to 7.6% YoY, with HSA organic growth dependent on employer adoption cycles and high-deductible health plan enrollment trends that may be plateauing [1]. The CEO transition from 15-year founding CEO Jon Kessler to Scott Cutler (StockX background) introduces execution risk — Cutler's digital marketplace experience may not translate directly to the regulated benefits administration market, and cultural integration of a non-healthcare leader is unproven [2]. ROE of 11.43% remains below the 15% target despite the 123% earnings surge, suggesting the company's capital efficiency has room for improvement [3]. Competition from Fidelity, Optum Bank, and AI-native fintech entrants could pressure pricing and margins in the HSA custodian market. The trailing P/E of 31.5x prices in significant continued earnings growth — any miss on the profit expansion trajectory could trigger multiple compression from these levels.
Key Catalysts
  1. HSA adoption secular growth — total HSA assets industry-wide continue growing as employers shift to high-deductible health plans, with HealthEquity opening 1M+ new accounts from sales for the second consecutive year and growing total HSA assets 14% to $36.5B [1]
  2. Margin expansion trajectory — adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 39% to 43% in FY2026 with net income surging 123%, driven by AI-powered operational efficiencies and scale leverage that should continue as the platform matures [1][2]
  3. New CEO Scott Cutler's digital transformation expertise from StockX and eBay could accelerate marketplace development, member engagement, and technology modernization, potentially unlocking a higher growth trajectory [2]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration from 9% FY2026 to 7.6% trailing YoY — if HSA adoption trends plateau or employer purchasing cycles slow, the company may struggle to sustain the earnings growth trajectory priced into the 31.5x trailing P/E [1][3]
  2. CEO transition execution risk — Scott Cutler's digital marketplace background (StockX, eBay, NYSE) has limited overlap with regulated healthcare benefits administration, and the cultural transition from founding CEO Jon Kessler's 15-year tenure is ongoing and unproven [2]
  3. Competitive pressure from Fidelity, Optum Bank, and emerging AI-native fintech platforms that could offer lower-cost, more automated benefits administration, compressing HealthEquity's margins and threatening its market share leadership position [2]
