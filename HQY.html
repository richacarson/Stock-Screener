Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
HealthEquity, Inc. is the largest independent health savings account (HSA) custodian in the United States, administering over 9 million HSAs with $37.1 billion in total assets. The company provides a full suite of tax-advantaged benefit accounts including HSAs, FSAs, HRAs, COBRA, and commuter benefits, connecting health and wealth for employers and individuals. Founded in 2002, headquartered in Draper, Utah.
4,800 Employees ·www.healthequity.com

Investment Thesis
HealthEquity delivered Q1 FY2027 revenue of $354.6M (+7% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 46% and EPS of $0.82 (+34% YoY), prompting raised FY2027 guidance to $1.41-1.42B in revenue [1]. The infrastructure profile is strong: D/E of 48%, $487.6M in TTM free cash flow, and a dominant market position as the largest independent HSA custodian with $37.1B in assets growing 19% YoY. The secular tailwind of HSA adoption — management targets making HSAs as common as 401(k)s — provides a long runway for growth, and the company's custodial model generates durable, fee-based revenue with high switching costs [1][2].
The primary risk is valuation compression: Forward P/E of 21.45 and PEG of 1.41 price in consistent execution, but revenue growth has decelerated from double-digits to 7-9.5% YoY, and the trailing P/E of 39x leaves limited margin of safety [2]. ROE of 11.06% is below the 15% target, suggesting the company's return profile does not yet justify the premium multiple. Heavy insider selling ($26M net in the last 12 months) raises questions about management's conviction at current prices [6]. Additionally, competitive pressure from Fidelity and Optum Bank — both with vast distribution networks and deeper technology budgets — could intensify, particularly as AI lowers barriers to building competitive benefits platforms [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Secular HSA adoption growth with $37.1B in total HSA assets expanding 19% YoY and management's vision to make HSAs as common as 401(k)s by 2030, providing multi-year compounding runway
  2. Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion to 46% with AI-powered claims automation driving further operating leverage as the custodial revenue base scales
  3. Raised FY2027 guidance to $1.41-1.42B revenue and $1B share buyback authorization signaling management confidence in sustained cash flow generation
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration from double-digits to 7-9.5% YoY creates valuation risk at a trailing P/E of 39x — any guidance miss could trigger multiple compression given the premium pricing
  2. Competitive intensification from Fidelity Investments and Optum Bank (UnitedHealth), both with deeper technology budgets and broader distribution networks, could erode market share in the HSA custodial space
  3. Net insider selling of $26M over the last 12 months, including multiple executive sales in 2026, signals potential misalignment between management actions and shareholder interests
