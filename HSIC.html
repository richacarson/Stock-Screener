Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Distribution ·NASDAQ ·United States
Henry Schein is the world's largest provider of health care products and services to office-based dental and medical practitioners, operating through Global Distribution and Value-Added Services, Global Specialty Products (dental implants, endodontics, orthodontics), and Global Technology (Henry Schein One practice management software). Founded in 1932 and headquartered in Melville, New York. New CEO Fred Lowery (former Thermo Fisher EVP) joined March 2026 and is reshaping the company around AI, leaner leadership, and faster execution. Q2 2026 revenue rose 6.7% YoY to $3.46B with non-GAAP EPS of $1.27 beating estimates, and management raised FY2026 guidance to 4.5-5.5% sales growth.
25,000 Employees ·www.henryschein.com

Investment Thesis
Henry Schein at 14.2x forward earnings offers value exposure to the stable, recurring demand of dental and medical supply distribution — Q2 2026 revenue rose 6.7% YoY to $3.46B with non-GAAP EPS of $1.27 beating estimates, and management raised FY2026 guidance to 4.5-5.5% sales growth [1]. The $200M+ value-creation program targeting operating income improvements by end-2026 provides a catalyst for margin expansion in a historically thin-margin business. CEO Lowery's restructuring — AI integration, management layer elimination, and supply chain consolidation — could unlock efficiency gains. The company's co-dominant position in dental/medical distribution with 1 million+ practitioner relationships and the KKR $250M strategic investment create a franchise with high switching costs and external accountability for transformation.
The bear case centers on structural growth limitations and execution risk. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately 2% reflects a mature distribution business in a slow-growth end market — dental and medical office visits grow roughly in line with population and inflation [1]. Net profit margin of 2.96% is characteristic of distribution economics with limited upside absent fundamental business model transformation. ROE of 8.9% is below cost of capital for many investors. CEO Lowery is executing a classic restructuring playbook, but Glassdoor reviews at Henry Schein One describe 'outdated products, outdated management' and 'toxic, political, and exhausting' culture — raising questions about execution capability on the AI and technology pivot [2]. The Amazon Business threat to medical/dental supply distribution remains a long-term overhang. The company lost Bergman's 35-year leadership continuity and the new CEO has not yet proven he can maintain the ethical capitalism culture while driving financial optimization. The Inspire Impact Score of -75 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors [3].

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Henry Schein's core business — physical distribution of dental and medical supplies, equipment, and pharmaceuticals to 1 million+ office-based practitioners — is structurally protected from AI disruption [1]. Logistics infrastructure, pharmaceutical distribution licenses, cold chain requirements, established supplier relationships, and high switching costs for dental practices create moats that AI cannot replicate. Analysts have not flagged AI as a material risk to the core distribution business.
