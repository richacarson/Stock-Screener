Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Distribution ·NASDAQ ·United States
Henry Schein is a global distributor of healthcare products and services to office-based dental, medical, and animal health practitioners. The company operates through two segments: Health Care Distribution (dental, medical, and animal health products) and Technology and Value-Added Services (practice management software via Henry Schein One, electronic health records, and financial services). CEO Stan Bergman retired from the CEO role after 35 years, with the company transitioning leadership while he remains Chairman.

Investment Thesis
Henry Schein offers defensive healthcare distribution exposure at an attractive 15.0x forward P/E, with Q1 2026 delivering $3.37B revenue (up 6.3% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.32 (up 14.8%), demonstrating accelerating earnings growth [1]. The KKR $250M strategic investment provides a catalyst for operational improvement, while the AWS GenAI partnership positions Henry Schein One as the first major dental platform with embedded AI capabilities [2]. At $9.63B market cap, the company co-dominates dental distribution with ~30%+ market share alongside Patterson, creating a duopoly with significant switching costs. Management targets >$200M in annual operating income improvement and $125M run-rate by end of 2026 [1]. 2026 guidance of $5.23-$5.37 non-GAAP EPS implies continued double-digit earnings growth. ROE has improved from 8.7% to 12.0%, reflecting operational progress.
The bear case centers on structural margin compression and CEO transition risk. Henry Schein's 3.1% profit margin is razor-thin for a $13B+ revenue company, and distribution businesses face persistent pressure from manufacturer direct-to-practitioner channels and AI-powered procurement platforms that could disintermediate traditional distributors [1]. D/E increased from 71.7% to 104.4%, suggesting growing reliance on leverage. The CEO transition from 35-year veteran Bergman creates execution risk at a critical juncture when the company must simultaneously deploy AI technology, execute KKR's strategic agenda, and defend market share against digital disruptors [2]. Glassdoor reviews of the Henry Schein One subsidiary describe 'outdated products, outdated management' — a vulnerability in the company's technology growth story [4]. Revenue growth guidance of only 3-5% for 2026 is modest, and the PEG ratio of 1.81 reflects fairly priced growth rather than compelling value. The stock's rise from $77.20 to $84.53 has reduced the margin of safety.

  3. Razor-thin margins — 3.1% profit margin, D/E up to 104.4%, and 12.0% ROE leave minimal buffer for execution missteps, competitive pricing pressure, or macroeconomic softness [5]
