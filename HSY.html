Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Confectioners ·NYSE ·United States
The Hershey Company manufactures and markets chocolate, confectionery, and salty snack products including Hershey's, Reese's, Kit Kat, Jolly Rancher, and SkinnyPop, sold across North America and internationally. Founded by Milton Hershey in 1894 and headquartered in Hershey, Pennsylvania, the company is majority-controlled by the Milton Hershey School Trust, whose returns fund a residential school for underprivileged children.

Investment Thesis
Hershey enters the back half of 2026 with cocoa cost relief finally showing up in results: Q1 2026 net sales rose 10.6% YoY to $3.10B and net income nearly doubled to $435.1M as pricing actions in North America Confectionery and the LesserEvil acquisition drove growth, and management raised full-year 2026 EPS guidance to $8.20-$8.52 (well above the prior $7.15 consensus) [1]. As a Dividend King with roughly 96 consecutive years of dividend payments, 50+ consecutive years of increases, and a 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 11.8%, Hershey combines category-leading brand equity (Hershey's, Reese's, Kit Kat) with a structurally embedded Just Cause via the Milton Hershey School Trust's controlling ownership [3][5].
The main financial concern is the elevated trailing payout ratio of 105% — a legacy of the 2024-2025 cocoa cost spike that squeezed TTM earnings below the current dividend rate — though forward EPS guidance suggests this should normalize toward the high-60s% as cocoa costs ease [1][5]. Structurally, Hershey carries D/E of 120% and a modest 6.9% five-year revenue CAGR, reflecting a mature, largely single-category business, and it remains exposed to renewed cocoa volatility, ongoing child-labor litigation risk in its West African supply chain (Hershey itself has acknowledged it cannot realistically eliminate child labor from that supply chain by its own 2025 target), and a leadership transition following new CEO Kirk Tanner's August 2025 appointment [1][4][7].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued cocoa cost deflation and pricing realization driving the payout ratio down from its currently elevated 105% level toward a more sustainable ~65-70%
  2. LesserEvil and other 'better-for-you' snack acquisitions scaling growth outside the core, slow-growing confectionery category
  3. AI-enabled supply chain platform (Aera Technology partnership) improving margin resilience against future commodity shocks
Key Risks
  1. Trailing payout ratio of 105% signals the dividend is not yet fully covered by trailing earnings, leaving limited room for a hiccup in the cocoa cost recovery
  2. Ongoing child-labor litigation risk and Hershey's own acknowledgment that full eradication of child labor from its West African cocoa supply chain by 2025 was not achievable creates reputational and regulatory exposure
  3. New CEO Kirk Tanner (since August 2025) represents a leadership transition risk after a period of Glassdoor-reported cultural decline tied to retail leadership changes
