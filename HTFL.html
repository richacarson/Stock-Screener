Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
HeartFlow is a medical technology company using AI and computational fluid dynamics to create personalized 3D models of patients' coronary arteries from standard CT angiography scans. Its platform provides non-invasive diagnostic solutions for coronary artery disease (CAD) including RoadMap Analysis, FFRCT Analysis, and the recently launched Plaque Analysis. Over 1,800 institutions globally use HeartFlow's platform, with nearly 650,000 patients managed and a proprietary database of 200+ million annotated cardiac images. Q2 2026 revenue of $64.1M (+48% YoY), raised full-year guidance to $246-250M. Market cap approximately $4.4B.

Investment Thesis
HeartFlow delivered exceptional Q2 2026 results — $64.1M revenue (+48% YoY) with U.S. revenue up 51% — and raised full-year guidance for the second consecutive quarter to $246-250M (40-42% growth) [1]. Plaque Analysis is the standout, with its revenue outlook nearly doubled to $29-31M from $19-21M at Q1, demonstrating rapid clinical adoption and product-market fit [5]. The company's non-invasive cardiac diagnostic platform is a genuine category creator with 1,800+ institutions globally, 650,000+ patients, and a proprietary database of 200M+ annotated cardiac images creating a formidable data moat [3]. At $50.75 per share with a $4.4B market cap (~18x forward revenue), the stock has doubled since May 2026, reflecting accelerating growth momentum and improving gross margins. Cash of $162.6M with only $27.1M debt provides adequate runway [4].
The bear case centers on valuation, persistent unprofitability, and cultural concerns. HeartFlow's stock has doubled to $50.75, pushing the market cap to $4.4B (~18x guided 2026 revenue of $248M) — a premium that requires sustained 40%+ growth and a credible profitability timeline that does not yet exist [4]. GAAP profit margin of -55.8% and ROE of -154.2% mean the company is still deeply loss-making after 19 years, though improving unit economics (gross margin ~80%) suggest operating leverage could emerge as revenue scales [1]. Employee satisfaction data reveals concerning cultural dysfunction — management criticism, leadership turnover, and concerns about technology scalability could impair execution at a critical growth stage [2]. Competition from AI-enabled cardiac imaging entrants (Siemens Healthineers, Cleerly) and dependence on favorable Medicare reimbursement decisions create ongoing risk.
