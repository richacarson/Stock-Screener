Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Lodging ·NASDAQ ·China
H World Group Limited develops and operates leased, owned, manachised, and franchised hotels in China and internationally. The company operates 13,417 hotels across brands spanning budget to luxury including HanTing Hotel, JI Hotel, Crystal Orange, Steigenberger, and others, with a pipeline of 3,054 additional hotels. Q2 2026 revenue rose 10.8% YoY to RMB7.12B beating consensus, and the company raised full-year guidance to 4-8% growth with manachised/franchise revenue growth guidance lifted to 16-20%. H World announced a new $2.5B three-year shareholder return plan. Founded in 2005 by Ji Qi, headquartered in Shanghai.

Investment Thesis
H World Group is China's largest hotel operator by hotel count with 13,417 properties and a proven franchise model generating adjusted net income growth of 26.9% YoY in Q2 2026 [1]. Q2 revenue of RMB7.12B (+10.8%) beat consensus by 5%, and management raised full-year revenue guidance to 4-8% growth with manachised/franchise revenue guidance lifted to 16-20% [1]. At a forward P/E of 13.5x and PEG of 0.27, H World is among the most attractively valued growth stories globally — ROE of 38.1%, profit margin of 18.9%, and operating cash flow of RMB8.8B demonstrate exceptional economics from the asset-light franchise model [3]. The new $2.5B three-year shareholder return plan including a $275M ordinary dividend provides immediate capital return while the 3,054-hotel pipeline and 20,000-hotel 2030 target sustain the growth narrative. The 5.01% dividend yield offers income while waiting for the growth story to compound.
The bear case centers on China macro risk, leverage, and structural ADR concerns. D/E of 240.5% reflects the capital-intensive hotel model and remains elevated despite improvement from 322% at the prior screen [3]. The payout ratio of 156% implies the new dividend level ($2.17/ADS) exceeds trailing earnings, though the forward payout ratio is more sustainable at ~68% on forward EPS of $3.19. China consumer confidence remains fragile — RevPAR and occupancy could compress in a macro slowdown, and the raised guidance to only 4-8% growth (vs 10.8% Q2 pace) suggests management expects H2 moderation [1]. The Legacy-DH international segment grew slower than China operations, suggesting international expansion has yet to achieve scale economics. As a China-domiciled company trading as ADRs on NASDAQ through a VIE structure, HTHT carries inherent regulatory, geopolitical, and potential delisting risk that limits institutional appetite and compresses the valuation multiple [3]. Hotel room supply additions of 2,200+ per year could eventually saturate China's hospitality market, particularly in lower-tier cities.

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Hotel operations involve physical-world services — cleaning rooms, managing properties, providing hospitality — that AI cannot replace.
