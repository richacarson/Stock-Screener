Company Profile
Industrials ·Rental & Leasing Services ·NMS ·United States
Hertz Global Holdings, Inc. is a vehicle rental company operating under the Hertz, Dollar, and Thrifty brands from approximately 12,000 locations worldwide. The company emerged from pandemic-era bankruptcy in 2021 and has since undergone a turbulent recovery, including a catastrophic $2.9B loss on its EV fleet strategy. Q2 2026 revenue grew approximately 10% to ~$2.4B with improving EBITDA of $81M, but the company remains GAAP unprofitable with a -3.1% profit margin and massive $21.1B debt load against a $617M market cap. CEO Gil West (ex-GM Cruise) is targeting $1B adjusted EBITDA and full-year profitability by 2027.

Investment Thesis
Hertz at $1.73 with a $617M market cap is a deep distress turnaround play under CEO Gil West (ex-GM Cruise). Q2 2026 showed encouraging operational improvement — revenue grew ~10% to approximately $2.4B, adjusted corporate EBITDA reached $81M (above the top of revised guidance), and the fleet was refreshed to 94% model year 2025-2026 vehicles [1][2]. Revenue per day improved 9% and utilization reached 81% excluding recalls. Full-year 2026 guidance of $225-$275M adjusted EBITDA represents a meaningful step toward the $1B EBITDA and profitability targets for 2027. If management executes on the turnaround — normalizing fleet costs, improving utilization, and achieving sustained profitability — the current market cap implies significant upside potential given $8.9B in annual revenue [1][2].
The bear case is overwhelming: Hertz carries $21.1B in debt against a $617M market cap (34x debt-to-equity on market value), remains GAAP unprofitable with -3.1% profit margin, and has negative equity on its balance sheet [1]. The $2.9B EV fleet loss destroyed shareholder value and management credibility, and the current $1.73 stock price (down from the $2.03 SMA50) reflects the market's assessment of significant bankruptcy risk. The 2027 target of $1B EBITDA and full-year profitability is aspirational given that Q2 2026 EBITDA was only $81M, and any macroeconomic downturn (recession reducing travel demand) would make these targets unreachable. The debt-to-revenue ratio of ~2.4x is manageable only with sustained revenue growth and margin improvement — neither is guaranteed. Long-term, autonomous ride-sharing poses an existential threat to the car rental model, and Hertz's competitive position is the weakest among the three major rental companies (Enterprise, Avis are both in stronger financial positions) [1][3][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Achieving full-year 2027 profitability and $1B EBITDA target would represent a fundamental inflection — management credibility restoration and debt-service capacity demonstration could trigger significant equity re-rating from the current distressed valuation [2]
  2. Fleet refresh to 94% MY2025-2026 vehicles should reduce depreciation headwinds and maintenance costs, improving unit economics — Q3 2026 EBITDA guidance of $275-$325M implies sequential acceleration [1][2]
  3. Travel demand resilience with revenue per day up 9% and revenue per unit above $1,500 guidance demonstrates pricing power that could accelerate margin recovery if volume growth materializes [1]
Key Risks
  1. Massive $21.1B debt load against $617M market cap creates severe financial fragility — any revenue or EBITDA shortfall could trigger covenant violations or force restructuring, with equity holders facing near-total loss in a distressed scenario [1]
  2. Macroeconomic recession risk — car rental demand is cyclical and discretionary (especially leisure travel), and a recession would collapse revenue and EBITDA at precisely the moment Hertz needs to demonstrate turnaround viability [1][2]
  3. Autonomous vehicle disruption — robotaxis from Waymo, Tesla, and others could structurally reduce car rental demand over the medium term, and Hertz's financial weakness leaves no buffer to invest in defensive positioning against this existential technology shift [3]
