Company Profile
Healthcare ·Healthcare Plans ·NYSE ·United States
Humana Inc. is a health and well-being company operating as one of the largest Medicare Advantage insurers in the United States. The company operates through Insurance (Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, commercial group, specialty benefits) and CenterWell (primary care clinics, home health, pharmacy) segments. Humana serves approximately 17 million medical members and is on track to become the largest MA carrier in the U.S. with 25%+ membership growth in 2026. Q2 2026 revenue reached $40.87B with benefit ratio tracking guidance. Founded in 1961, headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky.
67,060 Employees ·www.humana.com

Investment Thesis
Humana is executing the most aggressive Medicare Advantage growth strategy in the industry, with 25%+ membership growth in 2026 positioning it to become the largest MA carrier in the U.S., potentially surpassing UnitedHealthcare [1]. Q2 2026 revenue of $40.87B reflects this scale expansion, and the benefit ratio is tracking guidance, suggesting medical cost trends are stabilizing. The CenterWell vertical integration strategy (primary care, home health, pharmacy) provides a structural advantage in managing total cost of care and could enable the 3% MA margin target by 2028 [2]. Revenue has compounded at approximately 11% annually over five years, PEG of 1.60 reflects favorable growth-to-valuation, and the ~$50B market cap is supported by $1.3B+ in free cash flow. NTM consensus estimates of ~$13 in earnings reflect expected FY2027 recovery as Star Ratings headwinds roll off [7].
The bear case remains substantial. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $9.00 represents year-over-year earnings decline despite 23% revenue growth, demonstrating Humana is growing membership faster than its ability to profitably manage care costs [1][2]. Profit margin of 0.88% on ~$146B in TTM revenue provides virtually no cushion — a 100bps increase in the benefit ratio would erode nearly all net income. The 3% MA margin target by 2028 is aspirational and far from guaranteed given current margin compression, elevated medical cost trends, and regulatory pressure on Medicare Advantage reimbursement. Legal risks are escalating: a $90M False Claims Act settlement, a class action lawsuit over AI-driven denial of post-acute care, and DOJ marketing violations create cumulative financial and reputational exposure [5]. CEO Rechtin's public acknowledgment of culture challenges underscores the depth of the turnaround. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Healthcare insurance is a heavily regulated industry where AI cannot substitute for the core underwriting, actuarial, provider network management, and regulatory compliance functions that define managed care [3]. Humana's CenterWell physical healthcare delivery (clinics, home health) adds a second layer of AI immunity — AI cannot perform a physical exam or provide in-person care. However, AI creates meaningful disruption risk in claims processing, prior authorization, and administrative functions that employ thousands. Competitors deploying AI more effectively could gain cost advantages in administrative efficiency and member engagement. The class action lawsuit alleging AI-driven denial of post-acute care demonstrates that AI deployment in healthcare carries unique regulatory and reputational risks [5]. AI Opportunity (0/1): Despite hiring Santosh Bardwaj as SVP of Enterprise AI and deploying AI for claims and engagement, Humana has not reported quantifiable AI-generated revenue or cost savings [3]. The AI-in-claims initiative has generated legal liability rather than measurable financial benefit.
