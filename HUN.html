Global specialty chemicals company operating through three segments: Polyurethanes (MDI-based products), Performance Products (amines, surfactants), and Advanced Materials (epoxy resins, adhesives). Led by family patriarch Peter R. Huntsman as Chairman, President, and CEO since 2000, the company generated $5.78B in TTM revenue but posted net losses in both Q1 ($53M loss) and Q2 ($6M loss) 2026 amid sharp rises in benzene and European natural gas feedstock costs driven by Middle East conflict volatility.

Investment Thesis
Huntsman offers a deep value proposition at a trailing P/E of -11.96x (reflecting current losses) with a $2.49B market cap representing just 0.43x TTM revenue of $5.78B [4]. Q2 2026 results showed meaningful sequential improvement — revenue of $1.663B vs Q1's $1.42B with losses narrowing from $53M to $6M — suggesting the worst of the raw material headwind may be passing as benzene and natural gas costs stabilize [1]. The company's 19-year dividend history and Inspire Impact Score of 54 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions make it attractive for values-based income investors, and the 3.05% yield at current prices provides income while waiting for a cyclical recovery. The Advanced Materials and Polyurethanes segments delivered higher volumes in Q2, indicating end-market demand is recovering [1].
The bear case centers on structural decline and unprofitability. Huntsman has posted net losses in four of the last six quarters, with TTM revenue declining 3.46% and a 5yr revenue CAGR of approximately -5% from the 2021 commodity supercycle peak [2][4]. ROE of -8.01% and profit margin of -2.63% reflect a company that cannot generate positive returns in the current environment. The dividend consumes 130% of free cash flow and is being funded from the balance sheet — unsustainable if losses persist [4]. ESG governance has been criticized by ISS for lagging peers on environmental disclosure, human capital management, and shareholder responsiveness, creating reputational risk [5]. The chemicals sector faces secular headwinds from sustainability regulations, raw material volatility driven by geopolitical conflict, and potential demand erosion in construction and automotive end markets.
