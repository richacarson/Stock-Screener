Company Profile
Communication Services ·Interactive Media & Entertainment ·NYSE ·China
HUYA Inc. operates a game livestreaming and interactive entertainment platform in China, offering live game streaming, esports event broadcasting, and game-related services. The company is transitioning from a pure livestreaming platform to a 'content-driven integrated game services provider,' with game-related services, advertising, and other revenues growing to 37% of total revenue. Tencent holds a 67.3% controlling stake. HUYA reported Q2 2026 revenue of RMB 1.74B (+11% YoY), marking six consecutive quarters of year-over-year growth, with net income of RMB 1.6M representing a return to profitability after prior losses. The company doubled its share buyback program to $100M. Market cap is approximately $480M with $529M in net cash, meaning the stock trades below its cash value.

Investment Thesis
HUYA at $2.10 trades at a 11% discount to its net cash of $2.35 per share, creating a floor valuation that limits downside — investors are essentially getting the operating business for free [4]. Revenue momentum is genuine with six consecutive quarters of YoY growth, Q2 2026 revenue up 11% to RMB 1.74B, and the game services pivot accelerating (54% growth, now 37% of total) [1]. The return to profitability in Q2 2026 (net income RMB 1.6M vs. loss of RMB 5.5M a year ago) suggests the business model transition is reaching an inflection point. The doubled $100M buyback program at current prices would retire meaningful share count, and Tencent's 67.3% ownership provides strategic stability and potential for increased platform traffic from Tencent's gaming ecosystem. Analyst consensus is Buy with a $3.93 price target, implying 87% upside.
The bear case is substantial. HUYA's core livestreaming business — still 63% of revenue — continues to decline as Chinese consumers shift to short-form video platforms (Douyin, Kuaishouai, Bilibili), and this structural trend shows no signs of reversing [1]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately -11% demonstrates severe business erosion: revenue has fallen from ~RMB 11B in 2020 to ~RMB 6.2B in 2025, and the current growth rate of +11% barely scratches the surface of recovery [4]. Trailing ROE of -2.64% and profit margin of -2.67% show the company remains structurally unprofitable despite cost cutting. The Tencent controlling stake creates governance risk — strategic decisions are made by a parent company that may prioritize its own portfolio over minority shareholders, and the blocked Huya-DouYu merger demonstrated HUYA's limited strategic independence [3]. China regulatory risk remains elevated for internet entertainment platforms, with content restrictions and compliance costs adding structural overhead. AI Risk Protection (4/9): HUYA's core livestreaming business, which still accounts for approximately 63% of revenue, faces indirect but meaningful AI disruption risk [1]. AI-generated content, AI-powered short-form video creation (driving viewership to Douyin/TikTok), and AI game companions could reduce time spent watching human game streamers. The business is not directly substitutable by AI but faces competitive pressure from AI-enhanced rival platforms.
