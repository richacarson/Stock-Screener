Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Hexcel Corporation develops, manufactures, and markets advanced lightweight composite materials and engineered products for commercial aerospace, space and defense, and industrial applications. The company operates through Composite Materials and Engineered Products segments, supplying carbon fiber, prepregs, honeycomb structures, and engineered core products to Airbus, Boeing, and defense primes. Founded in 1948, headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut. CEO Tom Gentile leads since May 2024. Q2 2026 revenue of $529.3M grew 8% YoY with Commercial Aerospace surging 18.3%, and raised FY2026 guidance to $2.025-$2.125B revenue.

Investment Thesis
Hexcel is a duopoly leader in aerospace-grade carbon fiber composites now trading at a significantly more attractive valuation after pulling back 18% from its $104 all-time high to $85.45. At forward PEG of 0.62 and 27.3x forward earnings with FY2027 EPS projected at $3.06, the stock offers compelling growth-adjusted value during a multi-year aerospace recovery [1][5]. Q2 2026 revenue of $529.3M (+8% YoY) with Commercial Aerospace surging 18.3% demonstrates accelerating content growth driven by Airbus A350 and Boeing 787 production ramp-ups. Management raised FY2026 guidance to $2.025-$2.125B revenue with adjusted operating margins improving to 13.9% from 11.1% a year ago and a credible path to 18% margins by end of decade [1]. Boeing and Airbus combined backlogs of ~15,000 aircraft provide multi-year revenue visibility through long-term supply agreements, and strong insider buying signals management conviction [2].
The bear case centers on aerospace cycle dependency, margin recovery execution, and balance sheet leverage. Revenue growth of 8% YoY is almost entirely driven by OEM build rate increases — a Boeing production disruption, supply chain bottleneck, or defense budget cut would directly impact volumes given ~65% revenue concentration in commercial aerospace [1]. Profit margin of 7.76% and gross margin of 25.0% remain well below pre-COVID levels despite improving operating leverage, and raw material cost inflation or energy price spikes could delay the path to 18% target margins. D/E of 73.9% with $959M debt against only $62.5M cash limits balance sheet flexibility if aerospace demand stalls — Hexcel is a leveraged single-industry play [5]. The stock at $85.45 trades below its 50-day SMA of $97.97, reflecting broader market concern about aerospace supply chain execution and potential tariff impacts. Glassdoor culture ratings of 3.1/5 and ongoing leadership transitions add organizational uncertainty during a critical scaling phase.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Hexcel's core business — manufacturing physical carbon fiber composites, honeycomb structures, and engineered materials — is structurally immune to AI disruption [3]. AI cannot replace the physical production of advanced aerospace materials, nor can it substitute for decades of materials science expertise and the multi-year FAA/EASA certification process for aerospace composites. The certification barrier alone creates a regulatory moat that no AI system can bypass — each new composite material requires years of testing and qualification before aircraft manufacturers can use it. AI may actually increase demand as next-generation autonomous aircraft, AI-powered defense systems, and advanced air mobility vehicles all require more composite content per platform. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI revenue products — Hexcel's value proposition is physical materials science and manufacturing, not software or data. The company uses AI in manufacturing processes but does not generate revenue from AI products.
