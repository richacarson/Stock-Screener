BlackRock Corporate High Yield Fund is a closed-end fixed income fund, launched in 2003 and managed by BlackRock Advisors, LLC, that invests at least 80% of assets in domestic and foreign below-investment-grade securities — high yield bonds, corporate loans, convertible debt, and preferred securities — with a primary objective of current income and a secondary objective of capital appreciation. The fund pays a consistent monthly distribution (current yield approximately 11.9%) and received a Morningstar Bronze medal against 26 peer high-yield bond funds as of August 31, 2026. Investment Thesis
HYT is a 20+ year old BlackRock-managed closed-end high-yield bond fund offering a current yield of approximately 11.9% through consistent monthly distributions, drawing on BlackRock's roughly $100 billion leveraged finance platform for credit selection [2]. The fund earned a Morningstar Bronze medal against 26 peer high-yield bond funds as of August 2026, and 2025 investment income grew 14.7% YoY with a low D/E of 37.8% relative to typical CEF leverage levels [3][4]. For income-focused allocators, the fund offers professionally managed, diversified below-investment-grade credit exposure with a long operating history and no interruption to its monthly distribution.
The bear case is structural rather than fundamental: a payout ratio of 155.8% indicates the current distribution is not fully covered by net investment income and likely includes return of capital, eroding NAV over time if unaddressed [4]. No evidence was found of consecutive distribution increases or a positive 5-year distribution growth trend, meaning income has not kept pace with inflation. Because HYT is a passive income vehicle rather than an operating business, it scores structurally low across Innovation, Inspiration, and Infinite Game — dimensions this IOWN framework weights at 75% of the composite score — meaning the overall score understates its narrower merits as a diversified high-yield income instrument. Closed-end fund discount/premium volatility and credit-cycle sensitivity in a below-investment-grade portfolio remain additional structural risks.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued Morningstar Bronze-tier risk-adjusted performance versus the peer high-yield CEF universe supporting demand for the fund's shares [3]
  2. Stable or tightening high-yield credit spreads supporting NAV and coverage of the current distribution rate [1]

Key Risks
  1. Payout ratio of 155.8% suggests the monthly distribution is not fully covered by net investment income, implying ongoing return of capital and potential NAV erosion [4]
  2. No confirmed history of distribution increases; income has likely not kept pace with inflation over a 5-year horizon
  3. Below-investment-grade credit exposure leaves the fund vulnerable to a high-yield credit cycle downturn or spread-widening event, and use of leverage magnifies both gains and losses
