Company Profile
Financial Services ·Closed-End Fund - Fixed Income ·NYSE ·United States
BlackRock Corporate High Yield Fund is a closed-end fixed income fund managed by BlackRock Advisors, LLC. The fund invests at least 80% of assets in domestic and foreign high yield securities including bonds, corporate loans, convertible debt, and preferred securities rated below investment grade. Formed in May 2003, the fund uses approximately 22% leverage to enhance income and targets current income as its primary objective with capital appreciation as secondary. Total assets are approximately $1.8B with a distribution rate of approximately 10.9% on market price.

Investment Thesis
HYT offers a 10.93% distribution yield backed by BlackRock's institutional-grade credit management and a $1.8B diversified high-yield portfolio with 22% leverage [1]. The fund has delivered income continuously for 23 years since its 2003 inception, and the January 2026 oversubscribed rights offering signals strong investor demand for high-yield CEF income. P/E of 9.36 on TTM EPS of $0.91, D/E of just 28.2%, and a Morningstar Bronze rating position the fund competitively among high-yield CEFs. The closed-end structure allows managers to invest in less liquid high-yield opportunities that open-end funds cannot access, potentially generating alpha through credit selection.
The bear case centers on structural NAV erosion and fee drag. The payout ratio exceeding 100% at 102.7% means HYT distributes more than it earns — in 2025, approximately 25% of distributions were classified as return of capital rather than net investment income [3]. NAV declined from $9.65 at year-end 2025 to $9.19 by mid-2026, a 4.8% erosion that partially offsets the high yield. Zero dividend growth over the past five years provides no inflation protection, and the 2.33% expense ratio is nearly 5x the cost of ETF alternatives like iShares HYG (0.49%). Revenue growth of just 0.73% YoY confirms the fund's income is stagnant. Rising defaults in high-yield credit markets during any economic slowdown would directly impair portfolio value and could force distribution cuts, with the 22% leverage amplifying losses.
Key Catalysts
  1. Federal Reserve rate cuts would compress high-yield credit spreads and drive NAV appreciation, potentially narrowing the fund's persistent ~7% discount to NAV and boosting total returns [1]
  2. BlackRock's Aladdin platform providing institutional-grade credit analytics may help the fund navigate credit cycles more effectively than smaller competitors, supporting risk-adjusted returns [2]
  3. The January 2026 oversubscribed rights offering provides fresh capital to deploy in potentially attractive high-yield credits at wider spreads, enhancing forward income generation [1]
Key Risks
  1. Payout ratio above 100% structurally erodes NAV over time — the 25% return-of-capital component means shareholders receive income partly from their own principal, creating a slow NAV erosion that accelerates in credit downturns [3]
  2. Economic recession would spike high-yield default rates above the historical 3-4% average, directly impairing portfolio holdings and forcing potential distribution cuts, with 22% leverage amplifying downside [1]
  3. The 2.33% expense ratio significantly erodes net returns versus low-cost high-yield ETF alternatives (HYG 0.49%, JNK 0.40%), making HYT's income advantage largely illusory after fees for investors who do not require the closed-end structure [3]
