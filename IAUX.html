Company Profile

i-80 Gold is a mining company focused on the exploration and advancement of gold and silver mineral deposits in Nevada, operating the Granite Creek, Ruby Hill, Lone Tree, McCoy-Cove, and FAD properties. The company completed a $787.5M recapitalization in Q1 2026 including a $250M Franco-Nevada royalty, a $250M gold prepayment facility, and $287.5M in convertible notes. Q1 2026 revenue reached $52.4M (+274% YoY) with record gross profit of $16.1M as Granite Creek production ramped to 10,825 oz, though Q2 revenue declined to $24.3M due to third-party processing delays. Production guidance is 30,000-40,000 oz for 2026, scaling to 150,000-200,000 oz by 2028.

Investment Thesis
i-80 Gold at $1.49 with a $1.29B market cap is a development-stage Nevada gold miner positioned for a dramatic production ramp-up. Q1 2026 revenue surged 274% YoY to $52.4M with record gross profit of $16.1M as Granite Creek production doubled to 10,825 oz, and the company's $787.5M recapitalization (Franco-Nevada royalty, gold prepay, convertible notes) fully funds the development plan through first production at Lone Tree and McCoy-Cove [1][3]. Production is guided to 30,000-40,000 oz in 2026, scaling to 150,000-200,000 oz by 2028 and 600,000+ oz by the early 2030s — a 15-20x production increase that would transform the company's revenue and profitability profile at current gold prices above $2,500/oz. Cash of $465M provides substantial development runway, and CEO Richard Young's track record at Teranga Gold ($2.4B exit to Endeavour Mining) adds operational credibility [3].
The bear case centers on execution risk and current financial weakness. The -198.8% profit margin and -71.7% ROE reflect a company burning significant cash during the development phase, with D/E of 174% from the recapitalization that loaded the balance sheet with $449M in debt including $287.5M in convertible notes dilutive at conversion [1][3]. Q2 2026 revenue dropped to $24.3M (-13% YoY) because third-party processing delays prevented conversion of 5,300+ recoverable ounces into sales — highlighting dependence on a single toll processing facility as a key bottleneck [2]. Revenue growth YoY on a TTM basis is -12.5%, masking extreme quarterly volatility driven by processing throughput and gold price fluctuations. The pathway from 40,000 oz to 600,000 oz requires flawless execution on Lone Tree autoclave restart (complex metallurgy), McCoy-Cove underground development, and Ruby Hill expansion — each subject to permitting, construction, and geological risk. The stock has declined 35% from its 50-day moving average, and the COO departure during a critical development phase adds leadership risk [2][3].
