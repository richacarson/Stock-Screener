Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NASDAQ ·United States
Interactive Brokers is an automated electronic broker providing execution, clearance, and settlement across stocks, options, futures, forex, bonds, precious metals, and cryptocurrencies globally, built on the low-cost, technology-first model founder Thomas Peterffy pioneered since 1977 [2]. Q2 2026 revenue reached a record $1.9 billion (+28% YoY), with net income of $1.338 billion, a 77% pretax margin for a seventh consecutive quarter above 70%, client equity up 40% YoY to $930 billion, and Daily Average Revenue Trades up 36% YoY to 4.8 million [1]. Just 3,265 employees support this scale, reflecting the automation-first operating model that has defined the company for decades.

Investment Thesis
Interactive Brokers is compounding at scale: Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $1.9 billion (+28% YoY) with a 77% pretax margin for a seventh consecutive quarter above 70%, client equity up 40% YoY to $930 billion, and DARTs up 36% YoY to 4.8 million — growth metrics that materially outpace most brokerage peers [1]. A mechanically-derived infrastructure score of 8 reflects genuinely strong fundamentals: 24% ROE, 26% revenue growth, and a roughly 19% five-year revenue CAGR, all achieved with just 3,265 employees supporting nearly a trillion dollars in client equity — an extraordinary efficiency ratio that underscores the durability of the automation-first model founder Thomas Peterffy built over five decades.
The bear case centers on valuation and governance texture rather than fundamentals: a forward P/E of 30.8x and forward PEG of 1.8x are not cheap for a business with 26% revenue growth, and persistent net insider selling — Peterffy alone recorded 152 sales and zero buys over five years, with other executives also net sellers — removes a potential conviction signal even as the business performs well [2]. A $38 million SEC fine for anti-money-laundering and suspicious-activity-report failures is a governance blemish worth monitoring, and D/E of 205.75% (largely customer margin-loan liabilities, typical for broker-dealers) still requires investors to be comfortable with the balance sheet structure inherent to the brokerage model [1][3]. IBKR's revenue is also meaningfully levered to net interest income, meaning a sustained rate-cutting cycle could pressure the ~$1 billion quarterly net interest income line even as trading volumes stay strong.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued client equity and account growth (already +40% and +34% YoY respectively) as global retail and professional traders consolidate onto IBKR's low-cost automated platform [1]
  2. Further international exchange access expansion following the B3 Brazil access extension to non-Brazil clients, broadening the addressable market for cross-border trading [1]
  3. Sustained elevated trading volumes (DARTs +36% YoY) driven by continued market volatility and growing algorithmic/AI-assisted trading flows routing through IBKR's execution infrastructure [1]
Key Risks
  1. A sustained decline in interest rates would pressure net interest income, which grew 23% YoY to over $1 billion in Q2 2026 and represents a meaningful share of total revenue, independent of trading activity trends [1]
  2. Persistent net insider selling across the founder and multiple executives removes a positive signal that management sees the current valuation (30.8x forward P/E) as attractive, and could pressure sentiment if selling accelerates [2]
  3. The $38 million SEC fine for AML/SAR compliance failures highlights regulatory risk inherent to operating a large, automated global brokerage, with potential for further scrutiny or penalties [3]
