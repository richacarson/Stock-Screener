Company Profile
Financial Services ·Financial Data & Stock Exchanges ·NYSE ·United States
Intercontinental Exchange operates regulated exchanges and clearing houses (including the New York Stock Exchange and ICE Futures, home of the Brent crude benchmark), fixed income and data services, and an end-to-end mortgage technology platform built from the Ellie Mae (2020) and Black Knight (2023) acquisitions. Founded in 2000 by chairman and CEO Jeffrey Sprecher, ICE reported its best third quarter ever in Q3 2026 with $2.8B of net revenue and adjusted EPS of $2.02.

Investment Thesis
ICE is a high-margin compounder that just posted the best third quarter in its history: $2.8B of net revenue, net income of $940M, GAAP EPS of $1.67, and adjusted EPS of $2.02, with financial futures and options revenue up 37% and total Q3 volume up 31% YoY [1][5]. Revenue has grown from $8.24B (2020) to $12.64B (2025) with diluted EPS rising from $3.77 to $5.77, and the business earns a 38% net margin with a forward P/E of 18.9 [2][3]. Volatility in rates and energy drives exchange volumes, while data and mortgage technology add recurring revenue that smooths the cycle.
The bear case centers on leverage and mortgage exposure. Total debt of ~$24B against $936M cash (D/E 80%) reflects the Black Knight purchase, and ROE of 14.4% remains below the 15% hurdle [2]. Mortgage Technology depends on a housing-origination recovery that has repeatedly been pushed out, and AI-native loan software could compress pricing. Exchange revenue is also cyclical: the 31% volume surge driven by interest-rate contracts could normalize, and the 1.93 PEG means the stock is not cheap relative to expected growth [2][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained rate and energy volatility — Q3 2026 volume up 31% YoY and financial futures and options revenue up 37%, with interest-rate contracts driving the gain [1][5]
  2. Mortgage origination recovery — a lower-rate refinancing cycle would lift transaction revenue on the Encompass and Black Knight platforms built from the Ellie Mae and Black Knight acquisitions [4]
  3. Deleveraging and capital return — strong free cash flow from a 38% net-margin business can pay down Black Knight debt and grow the dividend ($2.08 annual rate) [2]
Key Risks
  1. Mortgage technology stagnation — prolonged high mortgage rates keep origination volumes depressed and delay returns on the Ellie Mae and Black Knight investments [4]
  2. Volume normalization — the 31% Q3 volume spike was rate-driven and could reverse if macro volatility fades, pressuring exchange revenue [5]
  3. Leverage — ~$24B of total debt (D/E 80%) limits flexibility for further acquisitions and exposes earnings to refinancing costs [2]
