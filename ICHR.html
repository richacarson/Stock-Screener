Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment & Materials ·NASDAQ ·United States
Ichor Holdings designs, engineers, and manufactures critical fluid delivery subsystems and components for semiconductor capital equipment. The company's gas delivery subsystems deliver, monitor, and control gases used in semiconductor manufacturing processes such as etch and deposition, while chemical delivery subsystems blend and dispense reactive liquid chemistries for CMP, electroplating, and cleaning. Ichor also provides precision-machined components, weldments, and proprietary valve/fitting products. CEO Phil Barros (promoted from CTO in November 2025, 20+ year tenure) leads 1,891 employees. Q2 2026 revenue reached $294.8M (+24% YoY), with unconstrained demand exceeding $300M driven by AI hyperscaling.

Investment Thesis
Ichor is positioned at the epicenter of the AI semiconductor buildout with its steepest revenue ramp in company history — Q2 2026 revenue of $294.8M (+24% YoY) with unconstrained demand exceeding $300M, driven by AI hyperscaling in etch and deposition tools [1]. The company offers an exceptionally attractive entry point at 18.28x forward P/E and 0.25x forward PEG, reflecting rapid earnings recovery from a cyclical trough, with customer demand visibility extending well into 2027 [3]. The balance sheet is pristine (D/E 17.81%, net cash position with $256M cash against $154M debt), and the CTO-to-CEO promotion of 20-year veteran Phil Barros ensures technical leadership continuity through the next generation of semiconductor manufacturing complexity [2]. Gross margins are expanding as volumes ramp, and non-GAAP EPS hit a three-year high in Q2 2026. As a critical subsystem supplier to the world's largest semiconductor equipment OEMs with proprietary products and high switching costs, Ichor benefits from the multi-year AI capex supercycle without the direct customer concentration risk of chip designers.
The bear case centers on semiconductor cyclicality, low profitability, and customer concentration. Despite the current boom, semiconductor equipment spending is notoriously cyclical — ICHR's revenue collapsed from approximately $1.2B peak to approximately $600M trough in 2023-2024, demonstrating extreme demand volatility that could recur if AI capex cools [3]. Trailing profit margin of -3.97% and ROE of -5.17% reflect that the company was operating at a loss until recently, and operating margins (2.76%) and gross margins (12.67%) are thin even at current revenue levels, suggesting limited pricing power relative to its OEM customers [3]. Ichor's business is concentrated among a small number of semiconductor equipment OEMs — loss of a major customer or an OEM decision to in-source fluid delivery subsystems would be devastating. The stock at $59.76 (2.58x price-to-book) prices in substantial earnings recovery that has not yet been demonstrated on a trailing basis, creating downside risk if the ramp decelerates. Geopolitical risks from U.S.-China semiconductor restrictions could also disrupt demand patterns for equipment subsystems.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI hyperscaling demand cycle driving the steepest revenue ramp in company history — unconstrained Q2 2026 demand exceeded $300M (30%+ growth in two quarters) with customer visibility extending into 2027, supporting continued sequential revenue and margin expansion [1]
  2. Margin recovery from cyclical trough — non-GAAP EPS hit a three-year high in Q2 2026 as gross margin expanded on higher volumes, with the path to double-digit operating margins visible as revenue scales toward prior peak levels [1]
  3. Proprietary product portfolio expansion under CEO Phil Barros (multiple patents in valves, fittings, and substrate components) increases content per tool and pricing power as semiconductor manufacturing complexity grows with next-generation nodes [2]
Key Risks
  1. Semiconductor equipment cyclicality — revenue collapsed approximately 50% from peak to trough in the 2023-2024 downturn, and a cooling of AI capex spending or demand normalization could trigger a similar decline from current elevated levels [3]
  2. Customer concentration risk — Ichor sells primarily to a small number of semiconductor equipment OEMs, and the loss of a major customer or OEM vertical integration into fluid delivery subsystems would materially impact revenue [1]
  3. Thin operating margins (2.76% trailing, 12.67% gross margin) despite strong revenue growth suggest limited pricing power relative to OEM customers, creating profitability risk if input costs rise or volume growth decelerates [3]
