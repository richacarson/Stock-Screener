Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·Israel
ICL Group Ltd. is a global specialty minerals and chemicals company operating through four segments: Potash, Phosphate Solutions, Growing Solutions, and Industrial Products. The company extracts minerals from the Dead Sea in Israel and mines potash in Spain, producing fertilizers, phosphoric acid, specialty chemicals, and food additives. ICL reported Q2 2026 sales of $2.1B (+17% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $448M (+28%) and raised 2026 EBITDA guidance by $100M to $1.5B-$1.7B. The company is expanding into specialty fertilizers with a new manufacturing facility in India and invests in agricultural innovation through its BIG corporate innovation program. Market cap approximately $7.1B.
12,500 Employees ·www.icl-group.com

Investment Thesis
ICL Group at 12.56x forward earnings offers value exposure to the global food security megatrend through a diversified specialty minerals platform. Q2 2026 revenue of $2.1B (+17% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $448M (+28%) demonstrate accelerating earnings power as potash prices recover and specialty segments grow, prompting management to raise 2026 EBITDA guidance by $100M to $1.5B-$1.7B [1]. The Dead Sea concession provides an irreplaceable mineral asset with structural competitive advantage, and the expansion into specialty fertilizers (India facility) positions ICL to capture the precision agriculture opportunity. The 3.26% dividend yield with 19+ years of consecutive payments provides income while investors wait for commodity cycle tailwinds, and the Inspire Impact Score of 64 (ALIGNED) makes ICL eligible for faith-based investment mandates [4].
The bear case centers on commodity price volatility, weak profitability metrics, and geopolitical risk. Net profit margin of 3.52% and ROE of 5.04% are well below specialty chemicals peers, reflecting the capital-intensive mining operations and volatile potash/phosphate pricing that caused a revenue roller-coaster from $5.3B (2020) to $10B (2022 peak) back to $7.15B (2025) [5]. The payout ratio of 75% is elevated — if potash prices decline again (as they did from 2022 highs), dividend sustainability becomes questionable and the company may be forced to cut payouts as it did post-2022. Israel-based operations introduce geopolitical risk premium given ongoing regional security concerns, and the Dead Sea concession renewal terms remain a long-term uncertainty. EPS growth of 33% YoY is impressive but largely reflects recovering from a cyclical trough rather than structural earnings improvement. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 6% masks extreme annual volatility and suggests the company's growth trajectory is commodity-cycle-driven rather than secular.
Key Catalysts
  1. Recovering potash and phosphate prices driving Q2 2026 revenue +17% YoY and EBITDA +28%, with raised full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance of $1.5B-$1.7B (up $100M) suggesting continued commodity tailwinds [1]
  2. India specialty fertilizer manufacturing facility expansion positioning ICL to capture growing demand for precision agriculture inputs in the world's largest agricultural market by smallholder farmer count [2][3]
  3. Strategic shift toward higher-margin specialty chemicals and growing solutions reducing commodity price sensitivity over time — specialty segments carry structurally higher margins than commodity potash [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Potash and phosphate price volatility drove revenue from $5.3B (2020) to $10B (2022) back to $7.15B (2025) — another commodity downcycle could compress EBITDA well below the $1.5B-$1.7B guidance range and force dividend cuts as occurred post-2022 [5]
  2. Net profit margin of 3.52% and ROE of 5.04% indicate weak capital efficiency relative to specialty chemicals peers, and the 75% payout ratio leaves minimal cushion for earnings decline before dividend sustainability is threatened [5]
  3. Geopolitical risk from Israel-based operations including Dead Sea mineral extraction facilities, with regional security concerns and long-term Dead Sea concession renewal uncertainty creating structural risk premium [4]
