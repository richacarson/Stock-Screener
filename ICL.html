Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Agricultural Inputs ·NYSE ·Israel
ICL Group is a global specialty minerals and chemicals company operating across Industrial Products, Potash, Phosphate Solutions, and Growing Solutions segments. The company extracts bromine and potash from the Dead Sea under a government concession expiring in 2030, produces specialty phosphates for food and industrial applications, and develops precision agriculture fertilizer solutions. Headquartered in Tel Aviv, serving pharmaceutical, food, agriculture, and industrial markets worldwide.
12,165 Employees ·www.icl-group.com

Investment Thesis
ICL Group at 12.4x forward earnings offers a low-leverage specialty minerals company (D/E 50%) positioned at the intersection of food security and sustainability, with a recovering earnings trajectory. Q1 2026 delivered $2.0B revenue (up 14% YoY) and $412M adjusted EBITDA (up 15%), with potash sales surging 25% on higher prices and improved production, prompting management to raise full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance by $100M to $1.5-1.7B [1]. The Dead Sea concession provides irreplaceable bromine and potash resources, while the specialty pivot into food-grade phosphates, battery materials (LFP), and precision agriculture positions ICL for higher-margin growth. The Inspire Impact Score of 64 (ALIGNED) with 19 positive attributions and zero negative flags reflects exceptional faith-based governance alignment.
The bear case centers on the approaching Dead Sea concession expiration in 2030 — ICL's irreplaceable resource advantage depends on renewal terms that may impose stricter environmental requirements or higher royalties [3]. Profitability remains severely depressed with profit margin of just 3.52% and ROE of 7.44%, far below the 15% target and well below the 2022 commodity boom levels when margins reached 20%+. The dividend is strained with a ~90% trailing payout ratio, zero consecutive years of growth, and a negative 5yr CAGR of -1.7% — leaving minimal buffer if earnings disappoint. The CEO transition from Raviv Zoller to Elad Aharonson introduces execution uncertainty at a critical time. Geopolitical risk from Israeli operations and regional conflicts creates a structural discount, and potash prices remain well below 2022 peaks despite the recent recovery.
Key Catalysts
  1. Raised 2026 EBITDA guidance to $1.5-1.7B validates the earnings recovery trajectory, with potash volumes targeting 4.5-4.7M metric tons and higher bromine prices providing tailwinds [1]

  3. Battery materials (LFP) commercialization could open a high-margin revenue stream as EV and energy storage demand accelerates, leveraging ICL's phosphate expertise into clean energy supply chains
Key Risks
  1. Dead Sea concession expiring in 2030 creates existential uncertainty — renewal terms may impose stricter environmental mandates, higher royalties, or operational restrictions that compress margins [3]
  2. Profit margin of 3.52% and trailing payout ratio of ~90% create dividend sustainability risk — any earnings shortfall would pressure the $0.18 annual dividend, which already shows a -1.7% 5yr CAGR
  3. Geopolitical risk from Israeli operations including regional conflicts, potential sanctions impacts on trade routes, and the structural market discount applied to Israel-domiciled companies [1]
