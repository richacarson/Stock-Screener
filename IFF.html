Investment Thesis
IFF is a 137-year-old specialty ingredients company undergoing aggressive portfolio reshaping that could unlock significant value. The Food Ingredients divestiture to Givaudan refocuses the company on higher-margin Taste, Scent, and Health & Biosciences segments, with continuing operations revenue guided to $7.4-7.6B and EBITDA of $1.53-1.6B (4-8% growth) for 2026 [1]. Q2 2026 showed profitability with $50M net income and Scent revenue growing 8% on double-digit fragrance ingredients strength [1]. D/E of 44.8% reflects disciplined balance sheet management. The Inspire Impact Score of 58 (ALIGNED) with 14 positive ESG attributions is attractive for faith-based investors. At ~21x forward earnings with a 2.0% dividend yield and 53 consecutive years of payment, IFF offers a restructuring story with income support and secular tailwinds in natural, bio-based, and AI-enhanced ingredients.
The bear case centers on execution risk from serial restructuring and deteriorating financial metrics. ROE has deteriorated to -5.55% (from 6.05% in the prior screen), reflecting ongoing write-downs and restructuring charges despite returning to quarterly profitability [5]. TTM profit margin of 2.57% is far below the 20% target for a specialty chemicals company. The 5-year post-merger period has produced revenue contraction rather than the promised synergies, and the Food Ingredients divestiture effectively unwinding a portion of the $26.2B DuPont deal suggests capital misallocation. CEO Fyrwald is IFF's third CEO in four years — frequent executive turnover undermines strategic continuity. The EU antitrust investigation (employee deleting WhatsApp messages) and Frutarom bribery allegations create governance concerns [3]. The dividend cut in 2024 (51%) broke a multi-decade growth streak, and the current payout ratio is unsustainable against negative TTM earnings. Forward P/E has expanded to 20.96x from 17.26x, reducing the valuation margin of safety.
