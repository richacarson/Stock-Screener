Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management (Closed-End Fund - Global Real Estate) ·NYSE ·United States
CBRE Global Real Estate Income Fund is a diversified, closed-end fund launched February 24, 2004 and managed by CBRE Investment Management. It invests globally in income-producing common and preferred equity of real estate companies and REITs across North America, Europe, Australia, and Asia, spanning office, retail, industrial, residential, healthcare, data center, and storage property types. The Fund uses effective leverage (~32% of assets) to pursue a high current income objective, paying monthly distributions, with capital appreciation as a secondary goal.

Investment Thesis
IGR offers a 15.4% distribution yield paid monthly, backed by a globally diversified portfolio of real estate equities across North America, Europe, Australia, and Asia (top positions include Goodman Group, Aedifica, Klepierre, and Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield) [4]. The fund has paid distributions for 22 consecutive years without a cut since the 2008 financial crisis, including holding steady through the 2020 pandemic, and currently trades near NAV (a 1.5% premium versus a 5-year average discount of -4.14%) [2][3]. Year-to-date through July 2026, the fund delivered a 13.62% NAV return, ahead of its category average of 12.03% [2]. Effective leverage of 31.9% is moderate for a leveraged CEF, and CBRE Investment Management brings institutional-quality real estate research to portfolio construction.
The bear case is structural and severe: trailing EPS of just $0.11 supports only a fraction of the $0.72 annual distribution, implying a payout ratio near 655% and confirming the fund is substantially returning shareholders' own capital rather than generating true investment income [6]. The distribution has been completely flat since March 2022, meaning income-seeking holders have seen no growth against inflation for over four years, and the ~2.35% annual interest-expense drag from the fund's leverage (part of a 3.84% total expense ratio) compounds the erosion [2]. TTM investment income declined 2.8% YoY, and the fund's structural reliance on covered-call and leverage income to sustain a mechanically unsupportable payout ratio means any sustained real estate downturn or rate spike would likely force a distribution cut. There is no Just Cause, no innovation, and no strategic evolution — this is a pure yield vehicle whose main achievement is not having cut its distribution since 2008.
Key Catalysts
  1. Global real estate recovery, particularly in office and retail REITs, which would support NAV growth and narrow the gap between the mechanically unsustainable distribution and actual investment income
  2. Interest rate cuts reducing the fund's leverage cost (part of its 3.84% expense ratio), directly improving net investment income available for distribution
  3. Continued exposure to data center and industrial REIT holdings that could benefit from AI-driven infrastructure demand
Key Risks
  1. A payout ratio near 655% against $0.11 trailing EPS means distributions are substantially return of capital, an unsustainable structure that risks a distribution cut if real estate income does not improve materially
  2. Effective leverage of ~32% amplifies losses in a real estate downturn and increases interest-expense drag (part of a 3.84% total expense ratio) if rates rise
  3. The distribution has been flat since March 2022, meaning income has not kept pace with inflation, eroding real purchasing power for income-dependent holders
