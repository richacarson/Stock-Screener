Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Infrastructure ·NYSE ·Cayman Islands
IHS Holding Limited is one of the largest independent owners, operators, and developers of shared telecommunications infrastructure in the world, operating approximately 37,000 towers across Africa. The company provides colocation, build-to-suit, and managed services to mobile network operators including MTN, Airtel, and 9mobile. IHS has agreed to be acquired by MTN Group for $8.50/share in an all-cash transaction valuing the company at approximately $6.2B enterprise value, expected to close in 2026. Founded in 2001 by Sam Darwish, headquartered in London.
3,500 Employees ·www.ihstowers.com

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)

Investment Thesis
IHS Towers has agreed to be acquired by MTN Group for $8.50/share in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately $6.2B enterprise value, expected to close in 2026 with shareholder and regulatory approvals pending [4]. The stock trades at $8.22, representing a 3.4% spread to the deal price and making this primarily a merger arbitrage situation. The underlying business is solid: Q1 2026 revenue of $415.4M (up 6%), adjusted EBITDA of $268.7M (up 6.4%), and ALFCF of $173.5M (up 15.8%), with net leverage improving to 2.9x [1]. With approximately 37,000 towers serving 600 million people across Africa and $45M in community investments since 2017, IHS provides essential digital infrastructure in one of the world's fastest-growing connectivity markets [5].
The bear case centers entirely on deal risk and the path to close. At $8.22 vs. the $8.50 deal price, the remaining upside is capped at 3.4%, while deal failure could send the stock back toward pre-announcement levels near $2.50 — a 70% downside [4]. The transaction requires shareholder approval (MTN and Wendel control 40%+ of votes, providing a floor) and regulatory approval across multiple African jurisdictions, each carrying execution risk. On a standalone basis, IHS faces structural headwinds: negative stockholders' equity, currency volatility in Nigerian Naira-denominated revenue, organic revenue decline of 1.7% in Q1 2026, and customer concentration risk with MTN as the largest tenant becoming the owner. The 60% discount to the $21 IPO price reflects five years of value destruction for public shareholders despite operationally sound tower economics [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. MTN buyout closing at $8.50/share expected in 2026, providing 3.4% upside from current price with 40%+ shareholder voting support already secured
  2. Adjusted levered free cash flow growth of 15.8% YoY to $173.5M and net leverage reduction to 2.9x demonstrate improving underlying economics regardless of deal outcome
  3. African mobile data consumption growth driven by smartphone penetration and AI-powered services increasing demand for tower infrastructure across IHS's 37,000-tower portfolio
Key Risks
  1. Deal failure would remove the $8.50 floor and could return the stock toward $2.50 pre-strategic-review levels — a 70% downside from current price
  2. Nigerian Naira currency volatility creates revenue unpredictability: Q1 2026 benefited from 11.4% Naira appreciation but organic revenue declined 1.7% excluding FX and dispositions
  3. Customer concentration with MTN (also the acquirer) as the largest tenant creates dependency risk if the deal fails and MTN reduces its IHS commitment

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (9/9): Telecommunications towers are physical infrastructure that AI structurally cannot substitute — cell signals require physical antennas mounted on physical towers, and no software innovation can replace this fundamental requirement [3]. IHS's 37,000 towers across Africa represent essential infrastructure for mobile connectivity across a continent with rapidly growing smartphone penetration and data consumption. AI actually increases demand for tower infrastructure: AI-driven services require more data, more data requires more cell sites, and more cell sites require more towers. The proliferation of AI applications, edge computing, and agentic AI systems will dramatically increase data traffic across African mobile networks, directly benefiting tower operators. IHS is the infrastructure that AI depends on, not a business that AI threatens. AI Opportunity (0/1): IHS has no AI-related revenue streams or AI-powered products. The company provides passive physical infrastructure (towers and fiber) rather than technology services.
