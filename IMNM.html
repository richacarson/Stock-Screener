Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Immunome is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company led by Chairman, President and CEO Dr. Clay Siegall (founder of Seagen, which was acquired by Pfizer for $43B), focused on developing first-in-class and best-in-class targeted oncology therapeutics. Its lead candidate, varegacestat, is a gamma-secretase inhibitor for progressing desmoid tumors that met the primary endpoint in the pivotal Phase 3 RINGSIDE trial with an 84% reduction in risk of disease progression or death and a 56% objective response rate versus 9% for placebo [1]. The FDA accepted Immunome's NDA for varegacestat with a PDUFA target action date of April 28, 2027. The company reported FY2025 revenue of $6.94M (down 23.2% YoY) and a widening net loss, but held $520M in cash and marketable securities as of June 30, 2026, funding operations into 2028 [1].

Investment Thesis
Immunome offers an asymmetric, binary-catalyst setup: the pivotal RINGSIDE Phase 3 trial for varegacestat in desmoid tumors hit its primary endpoint with an 84% reduction in progression risk and a 56% objective response rate, the FDA has accepted the NDA, and a PDUFA decision is dated for April 28, 2027 [1]. CEO Clay Siegall's track record building and selling Seagen for $43B, combined with over 333,000 shares of personal stock purchased in the past year with zero sales, signals strong leadership conviction in the asset [2]. The company's $520M cash position funds operations into 2028 regardless of the FDA outcome, meaning Immunome is not financing-dependent through the key catalyst, and the stock clears IOWN's liquidity floor comfortably.
The bear case is straightforward: Immunome has zero approved products, revenue of just $6.94M (declining 23% YoY), and a widening annual net loss of $212.4M, producing an ROE of -64% and a profit margin near -3,000% that mechanically crater the Infrastructure score to 2/10 [1]. The entire investment thesis rests on a single binary FDA decision 18+ months out; a Complete Response Letter or approval delay would remove the primary catalyst with no other late-stage asset to fall back on. As a young, single-catalyst clinical-stage biotech, Immunome also lacks any demonstrated Worthy Rivals framing or crisis-tested Just Cause, and its Infinite Game score of 5/10 reflects a company still building its long-term institutional track record rather than one that has proven it.
