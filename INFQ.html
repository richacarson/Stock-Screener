Company Profile
Technology ·Quantum Computing & Sensing ·NYSE ·United States
Infleqtion is a global leader in quantum sensing and quantum computing powered by neutral-atom technology, and the first publicly listed neutral-atom quantum technology company (NYSE: INFQ, listed February 2026 via SPAC merger with Churchill Capital Corp X). Founded in 2007 as ColdQuanta by physicist Dana Anderson at the University of Colorado Boulder, the company designs, builds, and sells quantum computers, precision sensors (quantum optical clocks, RF receivers, inertial sensors), and software to governments, enterprises, and research institutions. Customers include NVIDIA, the U.S. Department of Defense, NASA, and the U.K. government. The team includes 130+ PhD physicists and engineers.

Investment Thesis
Infleqtion is one of the few quantum technology companies with genuine commercial traction — $32.5M in 2025 revenue, 2026 guidance raised to at least $40M, record Q1 2026 revenue of $9.5M (+14% YoY), and a blue-chip government/enterprise customer base including NVIDIA, U.S. DoD, NASA, and the U.K. government [1][2]. Its neutral-atom approach offers inherently lower error rates and higher projected qubit counts at scale versus trapped-ion and superconducting alternatives, and the multi-platform strategy spanning quantum computing, sensing, clocks, and networking creates durable switching costs [2]. With $569M in cash, zero debt, and the NVIDIA partnership explicitly targeting quantum-classical hybrid AI workloads, Infleqtion occupies a unique position at the intersection of the two most transformative technology platforms (quantum and AI) [3][4].
The primary risk is the path to profitability. With a $35.3M operating loss in 2025, quarterly cash burn of $19M in Q1 2026 (up from $7M a year ago), and a -166.7% profit margin, Infleqtion must execute on revenue growth while managing capital consumption [1][3]. At a $2.7B market cap against $40M in 2026 revenue guidance, the stock trades at approximately 68x forward revenue — a premium that requires sustained execution on the qubit roadmap and customer pipeline. The quantum computing industry has a history of timeline slippage, and competition from IBM, Google, IonQ, and Quantinuum — all with substantially larger R&D budgets — could compress Infleqtion's differentiation before it achieves the scale needed to sustain its lead. For IOWN investors with a 5-10 year horizon, INFQ offers asymmetric exposure to quantum technology commercialization backed by proven government revenue, but the stock will remain volatile.
