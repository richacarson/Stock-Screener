Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·India
Infosys Limited is a Bengaluru-headquartered global provider of consulting, technology, outsourcing, and digital services, generating $20.16 billion in FY2026 revenue. Founded in 1999 on a 'powered by intellect, driven by values' governance ethos, the company is repositioning around its AI-first Topaz platform, with AI-related revenue reaching 8.2% of total revenue in Q1 FY2027. CEO Salil Parekh's second term ends March 31, 2027, with succession planning underway as the broader Indian IT sector confronts agentic AI-driven disruption to the traditional labor-arbitrage outsourcing model.

Investment Thesis
Infosys pairs a fortress balance sheet (D/E of just 10%, ROE of 32%, Forward P/E of 14.89) with a well-covered 3.69% dividend yield and a 27-year uninterrupted payment history, delivering a 9/10 mechanical infrastructure score [4]. The company's Topaz AI platform is generating disclosed, quantified results — AI-related revenue reached 8.2% of total revenue in Q1 FY2027 — and the firm continues to win large transformation deals ($3.6B TCV in the quarter, 61% net new), evidence that Infosys can capture some AI-driven demand even as its legacy model faces pressure [1]. At current valuation, the stock offers meaningful income and balance-sheet safety for investors willing to look past near-term growth deceleration.
The bear case is structural, not cyclical. Analysts estimate 30-40% of IT-services revenue — concentrated in exactly the application development, maintenance, and testing work Infosys built its scale on — is at risk from agentic AI-driven cost deflation, and the marginal cost of AI coding agents has collapsed toward near-zero, undermining the labor-arbitrage pricing model at its foundation [6]. Infosys's own results already show the warning signs: FY2027 constant-currency growth guidance was narrowed to just 1.5%-3.0%, and headcount fell ~15,000 YoY in FY2025 even as revenue grew — the exact 'output/employment decoupling' pattern analysts warn compresses future pricing power [1][6]. Governance and culture questions compound the risk: three separate regulatory complaints over unlawful trainee layoffs in under seven months, a historical $34M U.S. visa-fraud settlement, and a CEO succession decision due by March 2027 all land at a moment when the company most needs stable, values-driven leadership to navigate an existential business-model shift [2][3][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued scaling of AI-related revenue beyond the current 8.2% of total revenue as Topaz-driven deals convert from pipeline to billings [1]
  2. Large-deal momentum ($3.6B TCV in Q1 FY2027, 61% net new) converting into accelerated revenue growth above the narrowed 1.5%-3.0% FY2027 guidance range [1]
  3. A smooth CEO succession by March 2027 that preserves strategic continuity through the AI transition rather than introducing execution risk at a critical juncture [2]
Key Risks
  1. Agentic AI and coding automation directly threaten the core application development/maintenance/testing business, with analysts estimating 30-40% of IT-services revenue at risk from AI-led deflation and a 60-80% potential headcount compression for equivalent output [6]
  2. FY2027 constant-currency revenue growth guidance was narrowed to just 1.5%-3.0%, and headcount already fell ~15,000 YoY in FY2025 even as revenue grew — early evidence of pricing power erosion [1][6]
  3. Repeated regulatory and reputational controversy — three NITES complaints over unlawful trainee layoffs in under seven months plus a historical $34M U.S. visa-fraud settlement — signals workforce-relations and governance risk during a critical leadership transition period [2][3][5]
