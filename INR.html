Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYQ ·United States
Infinity Natural Resources is an independent oil and gas exploration and production company focused on the Appalachian Basin. The company holds interests in the Utica Shale Oil (~64,000 net surface acres in Ohio) and the Marcellus/Utica Deep Dry Gas (~34,000 net acres in Pennsylvania). INR dramatically scaled production through the Antero Acquisition (241 producing wells), growing net daily production 75% to 348.5 MMcfe/d in Q2 2026. Founded in 2017 and headquartered in Morgantown, West Virginia.

Investment Thesis
INR at $13.34 trades at an exceptionally low valuation — 4.0x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.13 — reflecting the market's deep skepticism toward small-cap E&P companies despite genuine production growth. Q2 2026 net daily production grew 75% to 348.5 MMcfe/d driven by the Antero Acquisition's 241 producing wells, Q2 revenue rose ~130% YoY to $171M, and the balance sheet is manageable at D/E of 37% with $900.9M in total liquidity against $524.1M net debt [1]. The Utica Shale Oil and Marcellus/Utica Dry Gas positions provide multi-decade reserve life in proven formations, and the structural demand for natural gas from AI data center power generation and LNG exports creates a supportive commodity backdrop.
The bear case is straightforward: production growth has come entirely through debt-funded acquisitions, not organic innovation, and EPS declined 25.7% YoY to $0.88 in Q2 2026 despite 130% revenue growth, indicating that volume gains are being offset by commodity price volatility, higher operating costs from integrating acquired assets, and increased share dilution [1]. CFO David Sproule's resignation in December 2025 creates financial leadership uncertainty at a critical growth period, and CEO compensation rising while earnings fell raises governance concerns [2]. The stock is thinly traded at $3.8M daily dollar volume, barely above the $1M liquidity floor, which limits institutional participation and creates exit risk for any concentrated position. Commodity price exposure to natural gas remains unhedged and uncontrollable.
