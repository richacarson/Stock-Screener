Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Inspire Medical Systems develops and commercializes a minimally invasive neurostimulation platform for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). The Inspire system is an implantable device that stimulates the hypoglossal nerve to keep the airway open during sleep, offering an alternative to CPAP therapy for patients who cannot tolerate it. Founder and CEO Tim Herbert has led the company since 2007.

Investment Thesis
Inspire Medical is a monopoly medical device company with 85.5% gross margins, $276M net cash, and a founder-CEO with 18 years of tenure, now trading at a significant discount after multiple headwinds compressed the stock from $200+ to ~$57 [1][4]. Q2 2026 was a positive inflection — non-GAAP EPS of $0.14 beat consensus, management raised full-year guidance to $835-$875M revenue and $1.05-$1.45 EPS, and the stock jumped 13.6% [1]. Gross margins improved 150bps to 85.5%, and Project Horizon creates $30M of annualized growth investment capacity. At ~46x forward earnings, the market still prices significant risk, but the underlying franchise — the only dominant FDA-approved implantable solution for CPAP-intolerant OSA patients — remains intact with 1,200+ implanting centers and 100,000+ cumulative patients.
The bear case is multi-dimensional and significant. FY2026 revenue guidance of $835-$875M represents a -6% decline from FY2025's $912M, and EPS has collapsed from $4.89 to a guided $1.05-$1.45 — a ~75% earnings decline driven by Medicare coding disruption (removal of CPT code 64568), the federal WISeR prior authorization program, and the troubled Inspire V launch [1][5]. The DOJ civil investigation into marketing and reimbursement practices remains unresolved, creating regulatory overhang that could result in fines or consent decrees [5]. Class II voluntary recalls for the stimulation lead and respiratory sensing lead due to incorrect labeling raise device safety concerns [4]. GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy) are reducing obesity-driven OSA severity in a meaningful patient subset, potentially structurally shrinking the addressable market. Net insider selling of $5.96M over 12 months signals weak management conviction at current prices [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Resolution of Medicare coding and reimbursement uncertainty for the Inspire system could restore revenue growth trajectory — Q2 guidance raise suggests the worst of the disruption may be passing [1]
  2. DOJ investigation resolution could remove the primary legal/regulatory overhang — manageable settlement or dismissal would unlock re-rating potential from the depressed ~46x forward P/E [5]
  3. Project Horizon strategic growth plan creating $30M in annualized growth investment capacity signals long-term confidence in expanding the implantable sleep apnea franchise [1]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue declining -7.6% YoY in Q2 2026 with FY2026 guided 6% below FY2025, and EPS collapsing ~75% from $4.89 to guided $1.05-$1.45, driven by Medicare coding changes and WISeR prior authorization disruption [1]
  2. DOJ civil investigation into marketing and reimbursement practices could result in significant fines, corporate integrity agreements, or restrictions that impair the commercial model, with securities class-action lawsuits adding legal uncertainty [5]
  3. GLP-1 drug competition structurally shrinking the addressable market for surgical OSA treatment as obesity pharmacotherapy reduces OSA severity in a growing patient subset, combined with Class II device recalls for stimulation and sensing leads raising safety concerns [4]
