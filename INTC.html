Investment Thesis
Intel's turnaround under CEO Lip-Bu Tan is gaining real, measurable traction: Q2 2026 revenue of $16.1B grew 25.4% YoY — the fastest growth rate in more than 15 years — with non-GAAP gross margin up 12.1 points to 41.8% and non-GAAP operating margin swinging from -3.9% to +17.2% [1]. Intel 18A is executing ahead of plan, with output ~25% above internal targets and Panther Lake now in high-volume manufacturing on ASML's High-NA EUV tools, while DCAI revenue grew 59% YoY to $6.3B, evidence that Intel is capturing real AI-cycle demand rather than being left behind [1][2]. Tan's operational discipline — flattening the org chart, cutting 34% of headcount, and putting engineering departments directly under his oversight — has translated into six consecutive quarters of revenue beats, and his personal share purchases signal genuine conviction in the plan [2].
The bear case is that Intel remains a profitability story still in progress, not yet delivered. ROE of -10.72% and profit margin of -19.79% both sit at their scoring floors, the 5-year revenue CAGR is roughly -9.6% reflecting the deep decline Tan inherited, and Forward P/E of 61.69x is elevated for a company that is not yet consistently profitable on a GAAP basis [4]. Intel Foundry's external customer revenue was just $293M against a $2.1B quarterly loss in that segment — the make-or-break bet that Intel can win outside customers at the leading edge against TSMC is still unproven [1]. The 34% workforce reduction, while strategically defensible, has created real near-term organizational strain, and Intel continues to cede AI-training silicon share to Nvidia and AMD even as it grows DCAI revenue from a small base.
