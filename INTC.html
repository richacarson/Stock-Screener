Investment Thesis
Intel's turnaround under CEO Lip-Bu Tan is gaining measurable traction: Q2 2026 revenue of $16.1B grew 25% YoY — the fastest growth rate in over 15 years — with DCAI revenue surging 59% to $6.3B and Foundry revenue up 31% to $5.8B [1]. Intel 18A is executing ahead of plan with output ~25% above internal targets, Panther Lake is in high-volume manufacturing on ASML High-NA EUV tools, and Q3 guidance of $15.8-16.8B suggests sustained momentum [1]. Tan's operational discipline — flattening the org, cutting 34% of headcount, putting engineering departments directly under his oversight — has delivered six consecutive revenue beats, and his personal share purchases signal genuine conviction [2]. At a forward PEG of 1.36 and $631B market cap, the stock prices in significant recovery but not yet full foundry success.
The bear case centers on execution risk in an unproven pivot. ROE of -10.7% and profit margin of -19.8% reflect ongoing GAAP losses, the 5-year revenue CAGR is -9.6% from the deep decline Tan inherited, and Forward P/E of 57.9x is elevated for a company not yet consistently GAAP-profitable [5]. Intel Foundry's external customer revenue remains small against billions in quarterly segment losses — the make-or-break bet that Intel can win outside customers against TSMC at 18A/14A is still unproven [1]. The 34% workforce reduction created near-term organizational strain during Intel's most technically demanding period, and the company continues to cede AI-training silicon share to NVIDIA and AMD even as DCAI grows [4]. Government CHIPS Act funding ($10.86B) comes with workforce and environmental commitments that create political risk amid ongoing layoffs.
