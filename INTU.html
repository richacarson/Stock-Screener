Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Intuit Inc. provides AI-powered financial management, compliance, and marketing products through four segments: Global Business Solutions (QuickBooks), Consumer (TurboTax), Credit Karma, and ProTax. The company serves consumers, small businesses, and accounting professionals, generating ~$21B in FY2026 revenue. In May 2026, Intuit announced a 17% workforce reduction (~3,100 employees) to restructure around AI, including winding down Mailchimp operations and establishing partnerships with Anthropic and OpenAI.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 5 /10
Intuit demonstrates a paradoxical infinite game profile. The company has repeatedly reinvented itself over 40+ years — desktop to cloud, standalone to platform, SaaS to AI-native — which reflects genuine existential flexibility. CEO Goodarzi's willingness to bet the company on AI despite divided leadership shows courage. However, two rounds of massive layoffs (27% of workforce in two years) severely undermine the trust foundation that Sinek considers essential. You cannot play the infinite game while treating employees as expendable inputs.

Existential Flexibility

Winding down Mailchimp and cutting 17% of workforce represent dramatic operational changes. However, Sinek distinguishes between flexibility driven by Just Cause and flexibility driven by competitive fear. Courage to Lead

Courage to Lead (1 pt -> 6/10 display): Goodarzi pushed AI transformation when his leadership team was 'absolutely divided,' demonstrating conviction over consensus [3]. The decision to cut 17% of workforce, close offices, wind down Mailchimp, and partner with Anthropic/OpenAI represents a dramatic bet on AI that risks massive near-term disruption for long-term capability [4]. The $300-340M restructuring charge is a real financial cost accepted in service of transformation. However, the courage is directed at survival (avoiding disruption) rather than vision (advancing the cause), which Sinek would note as a finite game motivation.

Investment Thesis
Intuit at $262 represents a rare opportunity to own a triple-category leader (TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma) at a forward P/E of 10.3x and PEG of 0.67 — valuations not seen for this franchise in over a decade [5]. FY2026 guidance of $21.34B revenue (13-14% growth) with non-GAAP EPS of $23.80-23.85 reflects sustained profitable growth despite the AI transition headwinds [1]. QuickBooks Online Accounting grew 22% and Online Ecosystem revenue increased 19% in Q3, driven by AI-powered features and higher effective prices [1]. The aggressive restructuring — 17% workforce reduction, Anthropic/OpenAI partnerships, AI-native product transformation — positions Intuit to emerge as an AI winner rather than AI victim. D/E of 36%, ROE of 22.5%, profit margin of 21.9%, and $5B+ free cash flow demonstrate a financial fortress that can fund the transition [5]. The 15% dividend increase to $1.20/quarter signals management confidence in the business trajectory.
The bear case centers on AI disruption risk and cultural destruction. Microsoft's Satya Nadella warned that 'SaaS applications will collapse in the agent era,' and Intuit's core products — tax preparation and bookkeeping — are precisely the well-structured, rule-based tasks that AI agents excel at automating [3]. The stock's ~37% decline from 2025 highs reflects genuine market concern, not mere sentiment. Two rounds of massive layoffs (27% of workforce in two years) and the Mailchimp wind-down ($12B acquisition value destroyed) signal a company in crisis mode, not confident transformation [3][4]. The $300-340M restructuring charge will pressure Q4 FY2026 earnings. Revenue growth decelerated from 17.4% in Q2 to 10% in Q3 FY2026, and Consumer segment growth of 8% lags the company average, suggesting TurboTax — the highest-profile brand — is already feeling AI pressure [1].
