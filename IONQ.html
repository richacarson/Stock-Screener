Investment Thesis
IonQ delivered its fifth consecutive record revenue quarter with Q2 2026 revenue of $80.1M, up 287% YoY and roughly 20% above the company's own internal expectations, driven by global deployment of its fifth-generation quantum systems across all four business segments [1]. Full-year guidance was raised to $280-290M (roughly 100% organic YoY growth at the midpoint, excluding SkyWater), and remaining performance obligations nearly quadrupled to $485M from $122M a year earlier, providing genuine forward revenue visibility rather than a single-quarter spike [1]. IonQ's AI Resilience score of 8/10 reflects a structural advantage: quantum computing hardware is largely immune to AI substitution and is increasingly framed as complementary infrastructure for AI-era optimization workloads, with government backing (DARPA, Space Development Agency) adding durability that few growth-stage tech companies can claim.
The bear case centers on governance and cash burn rather than the technology roadmap. IonQ posted a GAAP net loss of $1.87B in Q2 2026 (substantially driven by a $1.65B non-cash warrant fair-value charge, with Adjusted EBITDA loss of $120.3M reflecting the underlying operating burn), and TTM net profit margin of -392.55% and ROE of -60.48% both sit at their scoring floors [1][4]. More concerning is the pattern of insider behavior: former CEO Peter Chapman resigned and two weeks later made his first-ever discretionary stock sale of $37.5M, a short seller alleges eight insiders sold or authorized $396.6M in stock via newly created 10b5-1 plans, and a securities-fraud investigation into IonQ's revenue practices remains open — insiders have sold roughly $988M in stock over five years against just $3.35M in purchases [3]. Management itself acknowledges 'no commercial killer app running at scale on quantum hardware' yet, and any delay to the 256-qubit roadmap or SkyWater integration could extend the timeline to durable profitability. The Inspire Impact Score of +44 (ALIGNED) is a distinguishing positive for faith-based portfolios relative to most large-cap tech peers.
