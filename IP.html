Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·United States
International Paper is one of the world's largest packaging and paper companies, producing corrugated packaging, containerboard, and pulp. The company completed its $9.9B acquisition of DS Smith in January 2025, significantly expanding its European operations. IP announced plans to separate into two independent public companies — North America and EMEA — within 12-15 months. Q2 2026 net sales were $6.0B with EPS of ($0.02), reflecting DS Smith integration costs, though North America adjusted EBITDA grew 37% YoY.

Investment Thesis
International Paper at $33 offers deep value with a 5.67% dividend yield backed by the world's largest corrugated packaging operation following the $9.9B DS Smith acquisition [1]. North America adjusted EBITDA grew 37% YoY in Q2 2026 on $710M in executed cost-out actions, demonstrating operational improvement beneath the GAAP loss headline. The planned separation into North America and EMEA public companies should unlock conglomerate discount value and allow each entity to optimize for its regional market dynamics. Forward P/E of 23.89x reflects reasonable expectations, and D/E of 51% indicates manageable leverage. The Inspire Impact Score of 49 (ALIGNED) with 20 positive attributions and zero negatives reflects strong ESG positioning in a business fundamentally tied to sustainable, renewable packaging materials.
The bear case centers on near-term profitability, integration risk, and dividend sustainability. GAAP EPS of ($0.02) in Q2 2026 with profit margin of -13.77% and ROE of -16.04% indicate the DS Smith integration is destroying shareholder value in the near term, with restructuring charges, purchase accounting amortization, and operational disruption weighing heavily on results [1]. The $1.85 annual dividend is being paid from negative earnings — a clearly unsustainable payout ratio that depends on operating cash flow and cost savings materializing as projected. The planned company separation introduces execution risk and could distract management from the core integration. Corrugated packaging is a cyclical commodity business with limited pricing power, and any economic slowdown would pressure both volumes and margins. The 'old-school' culture (Glassdoor 3.3/5) and average employee sentiment raise questions about whether the organization can execute a complex integration and separation simultaneously [3]. Only 4 consecutive years of dividend growth and recent insider selling by a top executive add to uncertainty.
Key Catalysts
  1. DS Smith integration cost synergies — $710M in cost-out actions already executed with further savings expected as the integration matures, driving margin expansion from current negative levels toward historical mid-single-digit profitability [1]
  2. Planned separation into North America and EMEA public companies within 12-15 months could unlock 15-25% conglomerate discount value and attract pure-play investors to each entity [1]
  3. E-commerce secular growth driving sustained demand for corrugated packaging — the shift from brick-and-mortar to online retail increases packaging intensity per unit of GDP, providing a structural tailwind for the world's largest corrugated producer [2]
Key Risks
  1. Dividend sustainability at risk — $1.85 annual dividend paid from negative GAAP earnings with payout ratio undefined, dependent on operating cash flow and cost synergy realization; a dividend cut would eliminate the primary investment thesis for income investors [1]
  2. DS Smith integration and company separation execution risk — simultaneously integrating a $9.9B acquisition and preparing to split into two public companies creates organizational complexity that could delay synergy capture and distract management [1]
  3. Cyclical commodity exposure — corrugated packaging volumes and pricing are correlated with industrial production and GDP growth; an economic slowdown would pressure revenues at a time when the balance sheet carries $9.76B in acquisition-related debt [2]
