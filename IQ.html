Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·China
iQIYI, Inc. provides online entertainment video services in China, often called the 'Netflix of China.' The company operates a platform with professionally produced and self-produced content including long-form videos, micro dramas, and interactive entertainment. It provides membership, online advertising, and content distribution services. At its World Conference 2026 (April), iQIYI unveiled Nadou Pro — a platform integrating 70 AI agents for professional long-form video generation — and announced a strategic shift toward a decentralized content model granting creators greater autonomy. CEO Yu Gong's controversial AI Actor Library initiative and comments that 'live-action filming could become an intangible cultural heritage' triggered significant backlash from over 100 actors. A subsidiary of Baidu Holdings Limited. Market cap approximately $700 million.

iQIYI offers a deep value play at $1.27/share (12.3x forward earnings, ~0.5x book value) on China's #2-3 long-form streaming platform with ~100M paid subscribers and the most advanced AI content strategy in Chinese entertainment [1][2]. Nadou Pro — integrating 70 AI agents for professional video generation launched at World Conference 2026 — could reduce content production costs 30-50%, fundamentally improving the path to profitability [2]. International membership revenue grew 40%+ YoY in Q1 2026, demonstrating the global cultural export thesis is gaining traction. The stock has rallied 25% from $1.02 to $1.27 since the last screen, and Q2 results (due August 18) could catalyze further re-rating if domestic trends stabilize. Analysts maintain consensus Buy with targets of $1.85-$2.36 (46-86% upside) [4].
The bear case is substantial and multi-dimensional. Q1 2026 revenue fell 13% YoY to RMB 6.23B with a net loss of RMB 294.6M ($42.7M), and the 5-year revenue CAGR of -1.7% reflects structural decline in China's long-form streaming market [1]. The AI Actor Library controversy — where CEO Gong Yu claimed actor participation that was publicly denied and suggested live-action filming could become 'cultural heritage' — damaged the company's reputation with the creative community it depends on for content [2]. D/E of 105.67%, negative ROE of -5.11%, and negative profit margins demonstrate a fundamentally unprofitable business. Workforce reductions (from 7,500+ to 4,603), CFO resignation, and Baidu's tightening governance control signal organizational stress. As a Chinese ADR with VIE structure, investors face governance risks, HFCAA delisting concerns, and Baidu's controlling ownership potentially prioritizing parent interests over minority shareholders [3]. Competition from Tencent Video, Bilibili, and short-form platforms (Douyin/Kuaishou) continues to intensify as AI lowers content creation barriers for all competitors.
