Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Ingersoll Rand Inc. is a diversified industrial company providing mission-critical flow creation and industrial solutions. Products include compressors, blowers, pumps, vacuum systems, and power tools across diverse end markets including data centers, aerospace and defense, food and beverage, water management, and life sciences. The company was formed from the 2020 merger of Gardner Denver and Ingersoll-Rand's Industrial segment. Under CEO Vicente Reynal, IR has executed a disciplined M&A-driven growth strategy with 16 acquisitions in 2025.

Investment Thesis
Ingersoll Rand is a premier industrial compounder delivering disciplined M&A-driven growth through 16 acquisitions in 2025 ($525M invested), Q1 2026 revenue of $1.85B (+8% YoY), and adjusted EBITDA of $469M at 25.4% margin [1]. The company is an indirect beneficiary of AI infrastructure buildout — data centers require compressors, cooling systems, and fluid handling equipment that IR manufactures — providing a multi-year secular tailwind. D/E of 46% with $3.9B total liquidity supports continued deal execution, while aftermarket revenue at 37% of total provides recurring visibility [1]. The Inspire score of 68 (ALIGNED) with 18 positive attributions and zero negatives represents one of the cleanest ESG/faith profiles in the industrial sector [3]. Revenue growth 5yr CAGR of 11.64%, Dow Jones Sustainability World Index inclusion for 4 consecutive years, and Ownership Works equity program differentiate IR as a quality industrial franchise.
The bear case focuses on valuation stretch, acquisition dependence, and margin weakness. Forward P/E of 25.7x and PEG of 2.0 are expensive for a company guiding only 2.5-4.5% revenue growth and 3-7% EPS growth in FY2026 [1][4]. Organic growth at just 1% midpoint means the growth narrative depends almost entirely on M&A — if the acquisition pipeline dries up or target multiples become uneconomic, growth stalls. ROE of 8.11% and profit margin of 9.19% are weak for a company of this scale, suggesting that serial acquisitions are creating goodwill bloat and integration costs that depress returns on capital [4]. At $81.70 (trailing P/E 43.7x), the stock is priced for flawless execution, leaving minimal margin of safety. CEO decision centralization ('every decision goes to the CEO') creates organizational bottleneck risk as the company scales through M&A [2]. Free cash flow declined to $163M in Q1 2026 from $223M in Q1 2025, a concerning trend for an acquisition-dependent company [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center infrastructure buildout driving demand for IR's compressors, cooling systems, and fluid handling equipment — a multi-year secular tailwind as hyperscalers invest hundreds of billions in compute capacity [1]
  2. Continued M&A execution in 2026 with management targeting 400-500 bps of inorganic revenue growth, backed by $3.9B total liquidity and a proven integration playbook from 16 deals in 2025 [1]
  3. Margin expansion as recent acquisitions integrate and synergies realize, potentially lifting profit margin from the current 9.19% toward the industrial peer average of 12-15% [4]
Key Risks
  1. Acquisition-dependent growth — organic revenue guidance of flat to 2% means the growth narrative collapses if the M&A pipeline dries up or target multiples become uneconomic in a potentially rising rate environment [1]
  2. Valuation risk — forward P/E of 25.7x and PEG of 2.0 leave minimal margin of safety; any disappointment in acquisition integration, organic growth, or margin expansion could trigger meaningful multiple compression [4]
  3. Margin compression and capital efficiency — ROE of 8.11% and profit margin of 9.19% are below industrial peers, and the 16-acquisition pace creates goodwill accumulation and integration execution risk that may continue to dilute returns on capital [4]
