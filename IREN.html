Investment Thesis
IREN's AI cloud pivot has scaled materially since early 2026 — TTM revenue reached $757M (+104.8% YoY) and management now guides to annualized revenue from operations approaching $1.25B, anchored by the $9.7B Microsoft cloud contract and a new 60,000-unit NVIDIA GB300 GPU deployment [1][2][3]. Profit margin of 20.88% has held up through the scaling phase, and the company has materially deepened its leadership bench with senior hires from Oracle, Google, and CyrusOne, addressing prior concerns about execution capacity with only a few hundred employees. Liquidity is excellent at roughly $2.1B in average daily dollar volume, and the 100% renewable energy positioning provides a durable ESG differentiator versus other neoclouds.
The bear case centers on leverage and relative scale. D/E has risen to 149% as GPU and data-center financing accelerates, and ROE of 7.73% remains well below the 15% target, indicating the AI cloud ramp has not yet translated into strong returns on equity [3]. IREN also remains sub-scale next to CoreWeave (~$2.6B quarterly revenue) and Nebius (454% YoY growth to $582M quarterly), meaning it must keep out-executing much larger-balance-sheet rivals to defend its position [2]. Consensus estimates for FY2026 actually project a swing to an adjusted EPS loss, and the stock trades without a meaningful forward P/E anchor, leaving valuation dependent almost entirely on execution against the GB300 deployment timeline and continued customer contract wins beyond Microsoft [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Completion of the 60,000 NVIDIA GB300 GPU deployment would push annualized revenue from operations toward the $1.25B target and materially improve ROE as new capacity comes online [2]
  2. Additional hyperscaler or AI-lab contracts beyond Microsoft would diversify customer concentration and validate the vertically integrated AI Cloud platform being built by the newly hired Oracle/Google/CyrusOne leadership team [2]
  3. Continued 5 GW power portfolio buildout and 100% renewable positioning could attract ESG-constrained AI customers as sustainability requirements tighten across the industry [4]
Key Risks
  1. Rising leverage (D/E of 149%) funding the GPU and data-center buildout creates balance-sheet risk if AI cloud revenue growth decelerates before returns on invested capital improve [3]
  2. Scale disadvantage versus CoreWeave and Nebius, both of which posted substantially larger quarterly revenue and/or faster growth in the same period, pressuring IREN's ability to win the largest hyperscaler contracts [2]
  3. Consensus estimates project a swing to an adjusted EPS loss in FY2026, and the absence of a meaningful forward P/E anchor leaves the stock highly sensitive to any execution shortfall on the GB300 deployment timeline [3]
