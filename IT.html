Investment Thesis
Gartner is the undisputed global leader in IT research and advisory, generating approximately $1.3B in annual free cash flow on ~$6.5B revenue with a subscription model that delivers high contribution margins [1]. At 11.28x forward earnings with 33.1% earnings growth, the stock offers deep value rarely seen for a franchise business with dominant market share and recurring revenue [4]. Q2 2026 adjusted revenue grew 2.8% with net income up 14% to $275.5M and adjusted EBITDA up 6.4% to $466M, demonstrating that the business continues to compound despite AI disruption fears [1]. Contract value of approximately $5.3B provides multi-year revenue visibility, and Gartner's institutional relationships with Fortune 500 CIOs represent a distribution moat. Aggressive buybacks drive per-share value creation even on modest organic growth.
The existential question remains whether generative AI structurally erodes demand for Gartner's subscription research. While the stock has recovered 31% from its July trough, AI-powered analysis tools increasingly compete with the technology synthesis and vendor evaluation that Gartner sells to CIOs [2]. Reported revenue declined 0.6% YoY (Q2 adjusted +2.8% excluding Digital Markets divestiture), and contract value growth remains in low single digits — the weakest trajectory on record. D/E of 1047% reflects an equity base depleted by aggressive buybacks, leaving limited margin of safety if subscription revenue declines. Profit margin of 12.0% is well below the 20% target, and insiders including CEO Hall have been persistent net sellers exceeding $100M. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) restricts faith-based investor allocation. If AI substitution accelerates, the subscription flywheel that generates Gartner's premium free cash flow could reverse.
Key Catalysts
  1. Contract value growth reaccelerating above 5% FX-neutral as enterprises recognize LLMs cannot replace Gartner's proprietary benchmarking data, peer-validated insights, and institutional credibility embedded in procurement processes [1]
  2. AI advisory demand expanding as a growth driver — enterprise spending on AI strategy guidance, vendor evaluation (Magic Quadrant for AI platforms), and deployment best practices creates a direct revenue tailwind from the disruption narrative [2]

Key Risks

  2. CEO Gene Hall succession risk after 22 years with no visible succession plan, combined with persistent insider selling exceeding $100M over 12-18 months signaling management's own assessment of long-term value [2]
  3. D/E of 1047% from buyback-depleted equity creates financial fragility — net debt of ~$2.5B against ~$1.3B FCF is manageable, but a material revenue decline would stress debt covenants and force buyback suspension, removing the primary EPS growth driver [4]
