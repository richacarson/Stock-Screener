Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
Gartner, Inc. is the world's leading research and advisory company, serving C-suite leaders and their teams across enterprise functions including IT, finance, HR, legal, marketing, and supply chain. Operates through three segments: Research (subscription-based insights and Magic Quadrant rankings), Conferences (industry events), and Consulting (strategic advisory). Acquired CEB in 2017 to expand beyond IT into all business functions. Founded in 1979, headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut.

Investment Thesis
Gartner is the undisputed global leader in IT research and advisory, generating $1.26B in TTM free cash flow on $6.47B revenue with a subscription model that delivers 78.2% Insights contribution margins [1]. At 9.98x forward earnings and 0.67 PEG, the stock trades at deep-value levels rarely seen for a high-quality franchise, with aggressive capital returns — $535M in Q1 buybacks alone — driving 17.3% EPS growth despite flat revenue [1][2]. Contract value of $5.3B provides multi-year revenue visibility, and the company's institutional relationships with Fortune 500 CIOs represent a difficult-to-replicate distribution advantage. The Magic Quadrant remains the most influential technology evaluation framework in enterprise IT.
The existential question is whether generative AI structurally erodes demand for Gartner's subscription research. The stock's 60.75% decline over the past year reflects market consensus that LLM-powered analysis tools will substitute for Gartner's core offering of technology synthesis and vendor evaluation [2]. Contract value growth of just 1.0% FX-neutral is the weakest on record, and Global Technology Sales CV grew only 0.4%, suggesting early-stage demand erosion in the core IT segment [1]. Insiders including CEO Hall have been net sellers of $111M in 12 months, undermining confidence in the long-term story [5]. The Consulting segment declined 14.7%, signaling that discretionary advisory spend is already being redirected. If contract value growth turns negative, the subscription flywheel that generates Gartner's premium free cash flow will reverse rapidly.
Key Catalysts
  1. Contract value growth reaccelerating above 5% FX-neutral as enterprises realize LLMs cannot replace Gartner's proprietary benchmarking data and peer-validated insights
  2. Successful monetization of AI advisory demand through dedicated AI-leader subscription products, converting the disruption threat into a revenue growth driver
  3. Continued aggressive buybacks ($600M new authorization in April 2026) driving 15%+ annual EPS growth even on flat revenue, supported by a 13.4% free cash flow yield
Key Risks
  1. Generative AI tools delivering technology synthesis that directly substitutes for Gartner's subscription research, accelerating contract value growth deceleration below 1% and potentially turning negative
  2. CEO Gene Hall's $111M in insider sales over 12 months signals potential leadership transition risk after 22 years, with no visible succession plan articulated publicly
  3. 17.85% short interest reflects deep institutional skepticism about the AI disruption thesis, creating amplified selling pressure if contract value or guidance deteriorates further
