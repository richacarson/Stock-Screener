Investment Thesis
INVO Fertility at $0.58 with a $2.7M market cap shows encouraging operational improvement — Q2 2026 revenue up 17% YoY to $2.18M, Q1 up 23% to $2.02M, with clinic-level adjusted EBITDA reaching $333K demonstrating the owned-clinic model can generate positive contribution margin [1]. The INVOcell device offers a genuinely differentiated lower-cost approach to fertility treatment, addressing the large underserved market of couples who cannot afford conventional IVF. The fertility services market is growing as delayed childbearing and growing acceptance of ART expand the addressable population. The bear case remains severe. Cash of $3.73M provides limited runway at current burn rates, requiring further dilutive capital raises. The fertility clinic business requires significant capital for scaling (facilities, equipment, physician recruitment), and INVO lacks the balance sheet for meaningful acquisitions without further dilution. Profit margin of -92.3% and ROE of -72.7% reflect a company years away from self-sustaining profitability [1].

AI Risk Protection (7/9): INVO Fertility's core business — providing hands-on fertility treatment at owned clinics using the INVOcell device — is a deeply physical, regulated medical service that AI structurally cannot replace [2]. Fertility procedures require licensed physicians, laboratory facilities, physical medical devices, and direct patient care. The regulatory framework for assisted reproductive technology provides additional protection against AI disruption. AI may enhance certain aspects of fertility treatment (embryo selection, hormone protocol optimization, diagnostic imaging), but these augment rather than replace clinic-based care. However, larger clinic networks could adopt AI-enhanced protocols more effectively, and the INVOcell's simplicity advantage could narrow if AI makes traditional IVF more accessible.
