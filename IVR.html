Company Profile
Real Estate ·Mortgage REITs ·NYSE ·United States
Invesco Mortgage Capital Inc. is a real estate investment trust (REIT) that primarily invests in, finances, and manages residential and commercial mortgage-backed securities and mortgage loans. Externally managed by Invesco Advisers, Inc., the company focuses on agency RMBS and is structured to qualify as a REIT for federal income tax purposes. Founded in 2008, IPO in 2009, headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia.

Investment Thesis
Invesco Mortgage Capital offers an 18% dividend yield at a price-to-book of 0.97 ($7.87 stock price vs. $8.08 book value), with a very low forward P/E of 3.97 suggesting significant earnings improvement ahead if interest rate conditions stabilize [4]. The 17-year dividend payment history and leadership continuity (Collins and Lyle since inception) provide some institutional credibility, and the transition to a new CEO with fintech expertise could modernize operations [3]. Earnings available for distribution remained stable at $0.55/share in Q1 2026 despite GAAP volatility [1].
The bear case centers on structural fragility. The 610% D/E ratio (6.1x leverage) means small changes in MBS spreads or interest rates produce outsized swings in book value and earnings — Q1 2026's GAAP loss of $0.28/share vs. Q4 2025's $0.68 income illustrates this volatility [1]. The trailing payout ratio of 105% means the company is distributing more than it earns on a GAAP basis, which is unsustainable without spread improvement. The dividend has declined 27% over five years, and the parent company Invesco was fined $17.5M by the SEC in 2024 for misleading ESG claims, creating reputational overhang [2]. The externally managed structure creates permanent agency costs — management fees are asset-based, misaligning incentives with shareholder returns during drawdowns.
Key Catalysts
  1. Federal Reserve rate cuts improving net interest margins on leveraged agency MBS positions, potentially restoring book value growth and supporting the 18% dividend yield
  2. New CEO Kevin Collins executing on stated AI and automation initiatives to reduce operating costs and improve portfolio risk management efficiency
  3. Narrowing of mortgage-backed security spreads that would simultaneously increase book value and reduce unrealized losses that drove the Q1 2026 GAAP loss
Key Risks
  1. Interest rate volatility directly impacts book value and earnings — a 100bps rate move can produce 10-15% swings in NAV at 6.1x leverage, creating risk of forced portfolio liquidation [1]
  2. Trailing payout ratio of 105% and 5-year dividend decline of 27% suggest the current $1.44 annual dividend is not sustainably covered, creating risk of further cuts [5]
  3. Externally managed structure creates permanent agency costs and potential conflicts of interest, as Invesco earns fees on assets regardless of shareholder returns — SEC fined the parent $17.5M for misleading ESG claims in 2024 [2]
