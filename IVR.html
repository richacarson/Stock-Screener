Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Mortgage ·NYSE ·United States
Invesco Mortgage Capital is an externally managed mortgage REIT that invests in, finances, and manages residential and commercial mortgage-backed securities and other mortgage-related assets. Managed by Invesco Advisers, the company operates a primarily agency RMBS portfolio funded through repurchase agreements. IVR reported Q2 2026 net income of $0.34 per share, reversing a Q1 loss, and delivered a 3.8% economic return for the quarter. CEO Kevin Collins was appointed in May 2026 following John Anzalone's retirement. Market cap is approximately $450M.

Investment Thesis
IVR at 3.93x forward earnings and a 19.28% dividend yield offers one of the cheapest income streams in public equities — a compelling setup if the Federal Reserve continues easing, which would expand agency MBS book values and widen net interest margins. Q2 2026 marked a return to profitability with $0.34 EPS after a Q1 loss of -$0.28, and the 3.8% economic return demonstrates positive portfolio performance in the current rate environment [1]. Revenue grew 9.7% YoY in Q2 to $50.9M, and new CEO Kevin Collins — a 17-year company veteran since IVR's formation — provides deep institutional knowledge and strategic continuity [2]. The agency RMBS-focused portfolio benefits from an implicit U.S. government guarantee, reducing credit risk to near zero.
The bear case is severe. IVR's 105% payout ratio means the company is distributing more than it earns — a mathematically unsustainable practice that erodes book value and will eventually force a dividend cut unless earnings improve materially [4]. Debt-to-equity of 609% creates existential leverage risk; the 2020 COVID crisis saw IVR's stock drop over 80% and the dividend slashed as leveraged MBS positions faced margin calls. The externally managed structure embeds a permanent conflict of interest — Invesco collects management fees regardless of IVR shareholder returns, and parent Invesco was fined $17.5M by the SEC in 2024 for misleading ESG claims, creating reputational overhang [3]. IVR has destroyed massive shareholder value since IPO, with the stock declining from split-adjusted levels well above $100 to ~$7.58. The 2026 revenue forecast was already cut from $223.1M to $214.3M. For IOWN's framework, IVR scores poorly across Innovation (0), Inspiration (1), and Infinite Game (1) — it is a pure financial instrument, not a purposeful enterprise.
