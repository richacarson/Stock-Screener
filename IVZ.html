Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Invesco Ltd. is a global independent investment manager offering equity, fixed income, multi-asset, and alternative strategies through mutual funds, ETFs (including the flagship QQQ Trust), and private funds to retail, institutional, and high-net-worth clients worldwide. Under CEO Andrew Schlossberg (since 2023), Invesco reported record Q2 2026 assets under management of $2.47 trillion (+23.4% YoY) and record net long-term inflows of $45.1B, with adjusted EPS up 97.2% YoY to $0.71. The company continues to work through the aftermath of a November 2024 SEC enforcement action and $17.5M penalty for misleading ESG-integration disclosures.

Investment Thesis
Invesco's Q2 2026 results were genuinely strong on the surface — record AUM of $2.47T (+23.4% YoY), record net long-term inflows of $45.1B, adjusted EPS up 97.2% YoY to $0.71, and adjusted net revenues up 20.3% to $1.33B — and the stock trades at a modest Forward P/E of 10.03x with a PEG of 0.90 [1][4]. The QQQ Trust franchise continues to be a differentiated, durable flow generator that few competitors can replicate, and the balance sheet carries very light leverage (D/E of 12.06%).
The bear case is substantial. Trailing GAAP EPS is negative (-$0.69) and both ROE (-0.57%) and profit margin (-0.93%) score at the floor, reflecting a persistent gap between adjusted and GAAP profitability tied to intangible amortization from the 2019 OppenheimerFunds deal — a structural drag, not a one-quarter anomaly [4]. Active asset management faces genuine secular AI disruption risk: fee compression has run for 15 consecutive years and agentic AI is beginning to compress the research and analysis workflows that justify active management fees, while Invesco's own CEO has downplayed near-term AI application to the investment process itself [2][3]. The November 2024 SEC settlement ($17.5M penalty) for misleading ESG-integration claims — and the subsequent decision to stop publishing firmwide ESG integration data since late 2022 — is a governance red flag that predates but continues to shadow the current story. Dividend growth has been inconsistent, with the payout flat for several years after a 50% cut in 2020 before a modest recent increase, undermining the income-durability case despite the current 2.65% yield.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued record net long-term inflows (Q2 2026: $45.1B) sustaining the AUM growth trajectory toward $2.47T and beyond, driven disproportionately by QQQ Trust-related flows [1]
  2. A sustained recovery in GAAP profitability as intangible amortization from the 2019 OppenheimerFunds acquisition rolls off, closing the gap between adjusted EPS growth (+97.2% YoY) and the currently negative trailing GAAP EPS [1][4]
  3. Resumption of consistent annual dividend increases (two consecutive small raises in 2025-2026 after years flat) that would begin to rebuild the income-durability case for the stock [4]
Key Risks
  1. Structural fee compression accelerated by AI-enabled passive and algorithmic strategies, which industry commentary flags as compressing active-management economics that still anchor a meaningful share of Invesco's revenue base [3]
  2. Negative trailing GAAP ROE and profit margin reflect a persistent adjusted-vs-GAAP earnings gap tied to acquisition-related intangible amortization, a structural rather than transitory drag on reported profitability [4]
  3. The November 2024 SEC settlement over misleading ESG-integration disclosures, and the halt in firmwide ESG integration reporting since late 2022, represent an unresolved governance overhang for a company marketed partly on ESG capability [3]
