Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Janus Living is a pure-play senior housing REIT spun off from Healthpeak Properties in March 2026, operating 34 senior housing communities comprising 10,422 units across 10 states, with Florida and Texas representing approximately 69% of holdings. Led by CEO Scott Brinker (former Healthpeak CEO), all properties operate under RIDEA structures with revenue flowing directly from residents. Q1 2026 consolidated revenue was $200M (+35% YoY) with Adjusted EBITDAre of $65M (+42%). The company completed approximately $1B in acquisitions post-Q2 2026 and has $1.5B in liquidity with no debt.

Investment Thesis
Janus Living offers pure-play exposure to the senior housing demographic wave — the only publicly traded U.S. REIT focused exclusively on senior housing, with 10,422 units across 34 communities generating Q1 2026 revenue of $200M (+35% YoY) and Adjusted EBITDAre of $65M (+42%) [1]. The company's balance sheet is pristine — zero debt with $1.5B in liquidity — providing substantial firepower for acquisitions, as demonstrated by the $1B deployed post-Q1 [1][3]. Same-store NOI growth of 13.8% with 230bps occupancy improvement and 4.7% revenue per occupied room growth indicates strong operational execution. The aging baby boomer cohort (10,000 turning 65 daily) creates structural demand growth for senior housing that is AI-immune and largely recession-resistant. RIDEA structure provides direct exposure to operating upside rather than capped rental yields.
The bear case centers on execution risk, premium valuation, and the company's unproven independence. The Forward P/AFFO of 33.77x values Janus Living at a significant premium to healthcare REIT peers, pricing in substantial acquisition-driven growth that must be executed flawlessly [3]. As a 5-month-old public company, Janus Living has no track record of independent capital allocation, operator management, or navigating senior housing cyclicality. The $1B in post-Q1 acquisitions must be successfully integrated while maintaining same-store NOI growth. Senior housing faces structural labor challenges — caregiver recruitment and retention are industry-wide problems that constrain capacity and compress margins. GAAP metrics appear distorted by REIT depreciation (PE ~80x, profit margin ~11%), but even on an FFO basis, the valuation assumes significant growth execution. The limited trading history (5 months) means the stock's true risk profile during market stress is unknown. The Inspire score is N/A, providing no faith alignment signal for values-based investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Baby boomer demographic wave — approximately 10,000 Americans turn 65 daily, creating structurally growing demand for senior housing that is AI-immune, recession-resistant, and accelerating through the 2030s as the 78M-member boomer cohort ages into senior living [2]
