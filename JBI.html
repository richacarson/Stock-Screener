Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NYSE ·United States
Janus International Group is the leading global manufacturer and supplier of turn-key self-storage building solutions, commercial and industrial doors, hallway systems, relocatable storage units, and facility automation technologies. The company also provides the Noka Smart Entry access control platform. Products serve the self-storage industry across North America and internationally through new construction, R3 (retrofit/replace/repair), and commercial segments. Founded in 2002 and headquartered in Temple, Georgia.
1,817 Employees ·www.janusintl.com

Investment Thesis
Janus International offers a deep-value opportunity at 8.85x forward earnings with dominant market share in self-storage building solutions, trading at $5.37 versus a 52-week high of $10.80 — a 50% discount from peak [4]. Q1 2026 revenue of $222.7M (up 5.8% YoY) marks the first positive growth quarter after a two-year contraction, with international revenue surging 28.8% and Noka Smart Entry units growing 24.2% [1]. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $940-980M revenue and $165-185M EBITDA, with the Kiwi II acquisition contributing $90-100M inorganic. Free cash flow of $33.4M in Q1 with 155% TTM conversion demonstrates strong cash generation even at cyclical lows. CEO Ramey Jackson's tenure since Janus's 2002 founding provides strategic continuity and deep industry relationships [2].
The bear case centers on severe margin compression and cyclical risk. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin contracted 340bps to 14.8%, and net income collapsed to near zero ($0.2M) despite revenue growth — suggesting the Kiwi II acquisition may be dilutive to margins [1]. Net profit margin has compressed from 12.7% in 2023 to ~4.8% TTM, demonstrating significant operating deleverage. D/E of 113% with $546.7M in total debt against a $732M market cap creates financial risk if the downturn extends [4]. Cash declined from $194M to $112M in Q1, partly from the $15.7M buyback and operational needs. Glassdoor reviews at 2.9/5 with culture at 2.8/5 suggest retention and execution risks during a critical integration and recovery period [2]. Self-storage new construction ($96.4M in Q1, 43% of revenue) remains the company's largest segment and has not yet shown organic recovery — the 5.8% total growth is primarily Kiwi II-driven [1]. No dividend provides no downside support.

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Janus International manufactures physical building products — steel roll-up doors, hallway systems, modular storage structures, and relocatable units — that AI structurally cannot replace [2]. The self-storage construction and R3 (retrofit/replace/repair) segments require physical fabrication, installation, and on-site assembly that is entirely immune to AI substitution. Customer relationships are project-specific with high switching costs once a supplier is specified into architectural plans. AI may increase demand for data centers requiring physical infrastructure adjacent to Janus's capabilities. The Noka Smart Entry access control platform, while technology-enabled, is hardware-based IoT rather than software that AI could displace. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of AI-generated revenue. The Noka platform provides smart access control but is IoT/hardware-based; CEO Jackson describes AI as a future opportunity rather than a current revenue contributor [2].
