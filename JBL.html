Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYSE ·United States
Jabil Inc. is a global manufacturing solutions provider operating across more than 100 facilities in 30 countries. Following the 2024 divestiture of its Mobility segment, Jabil focuses on two segments: Intelligent Infrastructure (cloud/data center, capital equipment, networking — $17B FY2026E) and Connected Living & Digital Commerce (healthcare, automotive, consumer). The company manufactures server racks, liquid-cooling systems, and power management products for AI data centers, with AI-related revenue of ~$13.6B in FY2026.

Investment Thesis
Jabil at 21.6x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.76 offers attractively valued exposure to the AI infrastructure buildout. AI-related revenue of $13.6B (growing 50% YoY) now represents ~39% of total company revenue, and the Intelligent Infrastructure segment reached $17B in FY2026 [3]. ROE of 65.88% demonstrates exceptional capital efficiency, and the FY2026 outlook was raised to $35B with Core EPS guidance lifted to $12.25 from $11.55 [1]. The Mobility divestiture removed a lower-margin, customer-concentrated business and freed capital for the $500M U.S. AI manufacturing investment. With hyperscaler capex continuing to accelerate and AI data center demand described as 'extremely strong' by CEO Dastoor, Jabil is well-positioned as a picks-and-shovels play on the AI infrastructure cycle.
The bear case centers on razor-thin margins, extreme leverage, and cyclical exposure. Profit margin of 2.57% and operating margin of ~5.7% leave minimal room for error in a capital-intensive, labor-intensive business — any demand softening or pricing pressure from hyperscaler customers could rapidly erode earnings [1]. D/E of 297% approaches the 300% floor and constrains financial flexibility. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~3.6% (distorted by the Mobility divestiture) masks the reality that Jabil's revenue base is heavily concentrated in a single secular trend — AI infrastructure — and any slowdown in hyperscaler capex spending would hit disproportionately hard. Additionally, net insider selling of ~$6.4M by CEO Dastoor in H1 2026 raises alignment questions, and the trailing P/E of 42x prices in significant growth that must be sustained [5][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued acceleration of hyperscaler AI capex driving Intelligent Infrastructure segment above $17B in FY2026, with Cloud & Data Center revenue growing 47% YoY to $10.9B [3]
  2. $500M U.S. manufacturing investment for AI data center infrastructure coming online, expanding domestic capacity and potentially capturing reshoring tailwinds [3]
  3. FY2026 Core EPS guidance raised to $12.25 (from $11.55), with revenue outlook increased to $35B — further upward revisions possible if AI demand exceeds current forecasts [1]
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 297% near the 300% floor constrains balance sheet flexibility in a capital-intensive business with 2.57% profit margins — any demand downturn could strain debt coverage and force dilutive actions [6]
  2. Revenue concentration risk: ~39% of revenue ($13.6B) tied to AI infrastructure spending by a small number of hyperscaler customers, creating binary exposure to capex cycle timing and customer consolidation [3]
  3. CEO Dastoor's net insider selling of ~$6.4M in H1 2026 (26 sales vs. 1 purchase across all insiders) conflicts with the bullish public narrative and signals potential concerns about near-term valuation sustainability [5]
