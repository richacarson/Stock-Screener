Company Profile
Industrials ·Airlines ·NASDAQ ·United States
JetBlue Airways is a U.S. low-cost carrier operating primarily on the East Coast, Caribbean, and select transcontinental routes. Led by CEO Joanna Geraghty since February 2024, the airline is executing the JetForward transformation strategy targeting $1.2B in incremental EBIT by 2028 and $1/share EPS. Q2 2026 operating revenue was $2.7B (+14.5% YoY) with RASM growth of 10.9%, though the company reported an adjusted loss of $0.66/share as fuel costs surged 81%. JetBlue operates approximately 1,000 daily flights with a fleet of ~280 Airbus aircraft serving ~100 destinations. TTM revenue is approximately $9.2B.

Investment Thesis
JetBlue at $5.10 per share ($1.55B market cap) is a speculative turnaround play on the JetForward transformation strategy — Q2 2026 revenue of $2.7B (+14.5% YoY) with RASM growth of 10.9% and premium RASM up 13% demonstrates the commercial initiatives are working [1]. JetForward targets $310M incremental EBIT in 2026, $850-950M by 2027, and $1.2B by 2028, with a $1/share EPS target by 2028 representing 100%+ upside from the current price if achieved. JetBlue's 'inspiring humanity' culture (Glassdoor 13th best workplace), strong Inspire alignment score of 43, and values-driven leadership under CEO Geraghty differentiate it from airline peers. The AI-immune core business (physical air travel) and strong revenue growth trajectory provide a foundation for recovery if cost management and the premium product expansion deliver.
The bear case is severe: JetBlue is an unprofitable airline with $9.33B in debt against $1.81B equity (516% D/E), generating a -7.8% profit margin and -33.51% ROE on $9.16B TTM revenue [4]. The Q2 2026 adjusted loss of $0.66/share came despite 14.5% revenue growth, as fuel costs surged 81% — demonstrating the airline's vulnerability to input cost volatility that management cannot control [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance for -2.0% to -5.0% adjusted operating margin means continued losses with no near-term path to profitability. The $1/share EPS target is two years out (2028) and requires flawless execution of JetForward while managing a heavily leveraged balance sheet. The failed $3.8B Spirit Airlines merger (blocked by DOJ in 2024) consumed management attention and resources. JetBlue competes with much larger, profitable airlines (Delta, United, Southwest) with greater scale, route networks, and financial resilience. At $5.10/share, the stock trades near its all-time low, reflecting the market's skepticism about the turnaround timeline and the real risk of continued dilution or recapitalization if losses persist.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Air travel requires physical aircraft, licensed pilots, trained cabin crew, ground operations, airport infrastructure, and FAA-regulated safety systems that AI structurally cannot displace [1]. The fundamental need to transport people physically through the air is immune to AI substitution — no AI system can replace the experience of boarding a flight from New York to Los Angeles. FAA regulations mandate human pilots and specific staffing levels. AI enhances airline operations (crew scheduling, pricing optimization, customer service chatbots, predictive maintenance) but these are efficiency tools, not substitution threats. JetBlue's deployment of AI across four business lines demonstrates AI as an enabler, not a disruptor [2]. The only meaningful AI risk is customer service automation reducing call center roles, which is a minor component of the cost structure. AI Opportunity (0/1): JetBlue deploys AI internally for operational efficiency (Databricks platform, ML across four business lines) but does not generate revenue from AI products or services [2]. AI is used to reduce costs and improve service quality, not to create new revenue streams.
