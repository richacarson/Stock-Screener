Company Profile
Financials ·Closed-End Fund - Debt ·NYSE ·United States
Nuveen Floating Rate Income Fund is a closed-end management investment company that seeks to achieve a high level of current income by investing at least 80% of assets in senior floating rate loans, including both secured and unsecured senior loans. The fund uses structural leverage of approximately 37% through bank borrowings and term preferred shares to amplify income. Co-managed by Nuveen Fund Advisors LLC and Symphony Asset Management LLC. Launched in 2004, approximately $1.34B in total assets.

Investment Thesis
JFR offers a 12% distribution yield backed by a $1.34B portfolio of senior secured floating rate corporate loans, providing income that adjusts with interest rates — a structural advantage in volatile rate environments [1]. The 22-year track record since 2004 inception demonstrates durability through multiple credit cycles including the 2008 financial crisis and COVID-19 pandemic. Nuveen's institutional management infrastructure and TIAA parentage provide operational stability and access to deal flow in the syndicated loan market [2]. The fund's 0.38 beta offers portfolio diversification benefits with limited correlation to equity markets.
JFR scores poorly in the IOWN framework because it is a passive investment vehicle, not an operating company — it lacks innovation, purpose, and infinite game qualities by structural design. Beyond framework limitations, fundamental concerns persist: the 192% payout ratio indicates distributions exceed net investment income, meaning the fund is returning capital to shareholders and eroding NAV over time [4]. The 37% leverage amplifies credit losses in economic downturns and increases sensitivity to rising funding costs [3]. Revenue (interest income) declined 7.2% in FY2025 and a further 1.8% TTM as rate expectations moderate [4]. The 1.99% expense ratio is high relative to competing floating rate ETFs (BKLN at 0.65%, SRLN at 0.70%) that offer similar exposure at a fraction of the cost. Closed-end fund structures face secular pressure from ETF proliferation, and persistent NAV discounts may never close.
Key Catalysts
  1. Federal Reserve maintaining higher-for-longer interest rate policy would sustain floating rate loan income and support the 12% distribution yield
  2. Credit market stability and low default rates in the leveraged loan market would protect NAV and support distribution coverage
  3. Potential narrowing of the CEF discount to NAV if interest rate volatility drives demand for actively managed floating rate exposure
Key Risks
  1. Distribution payout ratio of 192% means the fund is returning capital and eroding NAV — if net investment income continues declining with rates, distribution cuts become likely
  2. 37% structural leverage amplifies losses in credit market downturns — a recession driving leveraged loan defaults above 5% could trigger significant NAV destruction
  3. Secular shift from actively managed CEFs to low-cost ETFs (BKLN: 0.65% expense ratio vs JFR: 1.99%) threatens long-term relevance of the high-fee closed-end fund structure
