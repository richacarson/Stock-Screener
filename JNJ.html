Investment Thesis
Johnson & Johnson is executing a historic growth trajectory — Q2 2026 revenue of $25.3B (+6.6% YoY) with full-year guidance raised to $101.1B, on track to surpass $100B in annual revenue for the first time in its 140-year history [1]. At a forward P/E of 23.18x, the stock has re-rated significantly (52-week performance +49.27%), reflecting the market's recognition of J&J's post-Kenvue transformation into a pure-play healthcare growth company. ROE of 25.74%, profit margin of 21.48%, $22.2B in FCF, and 64 consecutive years of dividend increases underscore financial durability. DARZALEX ($14B+), CARVYKTI, and the OTTAVA surgical robot pipeline provide multi-year growth visibility, while the July 2026 $5.5B talc settlement resolves a decade-long litigation overhang covering 99.75% of claims [1][3].
The bear case centers on valuation expansion, patent cliffs, and decelerating growth. The stock's 49% rally over 52 weeks has pushed the trailing P/E to 30.18x and forward P/E to 23.18x — meaningfully above the prior 17.96x — and the PEG ratio of 2.85 suggests the market has priced in substantial growth expectations [4]. Revenue growth decelerated from 9.9% in Q1 to 6.6% in Q2 2026, and Stelara biosimilar competition continues to pressure the pharmaceutical portfolio with an estimated $3-4B annual revenue headwind. The 62.13% payout ratio and 5% 5-year dividend growth CAGR are below ideal targets for a Dividend King. The $5.5B talc settlement, while resolving 76,000 lawsuits, requires 95% claimant acceptance to finalize, and future talc-related claims remain a tail risk [3]. At $260 per share, any OTTAVA FDA delay or pharmaceutical pipeline setback could trigger multiple compression from elevated levels.
