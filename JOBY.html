Company Profile
Industrials ·Airports & Air Services ·NYSE ·United States
Joby Aviation, Inc. is an air mobility company developing electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft for passenger transportation, now on the fifth and final stage of FAA type certification with its first conforming aircraft already flying. The Blade Air Mobility subsidiary generates near-term revenue from helicopter and fixed-wing charter services, with FY2026 revenue guidance raised to $115-125M. Founded in 2009 and led by founder JoeBen Bevirt, Joby is headquartered in Santa Cruz, California.

Investment Thesis
Joby Aviation is entering the commercialization endgame — the fifth and final stage of FAA type certification is showing record progress with the first conforming aircraft already flying, manufacturing non-conformance rates down nearly 40% in the first half, and two aircraft targeted for delivery this year [1][2]. The Blade Air Mobility integration is delivering real, growing revenue (Q2 2026 revenue of $39M, up $14M sequentially, on 50%+ Blade seat sales growth), and management raised full-year revenue guidance to $115-125M [1][2]. The White House-backed eIPP program offers a parallel, non-type-certificate path to begin carrying passengers this year, and the $2.47B cash position funds continued execution through the critical certification-to-commercialization window.
The bear case centers on cash burn, dilution, and reputational risk. Operating expenses rose to $300M in Q2 2026 against just $39M in revenue, and management guided to $385-415M in second-half cash use, prompting fresh equity raises and Wall Street sell ratings even after a record revenue quarter [1][2]. ROE of -65.9% and a profit margin of -755% reflect a company still years from sustainable profitability. Archer Aviation's 2026 fraud countersuit alleging Joby concealed Chinese supply-chain ties (via its Shenzhen subsidiary, Joby Metal Shenzhen Co Ltd) and misclassified aircraft parts to evade tariffs introduces legal and reputational overhang, even though Joby maintains the Shenzhen entity has been disclosed in SEC filings for years [4]. Unproven commercial demand at scale for eVTOL air taxi services remains the central long-term uncertainty.
Key Catalysts
  1. Stage 5 FAA type certification completion and first paying-passenger flights under the eIPP program in Texas would be the first proof of commercial-scale eVTOL operations, a binary catalyst for re-rating the stock [1][2]
  2. Continued Blade revenue growth (50%+ seat sales growth, aircraft availability now the binding constraint over demand) provides a growing, de-risked revenue bridge while certification completes [1]
  3. Resolution of the Archer litigation in Joby's favor would remove reputational overhang and reinforce the company's positioning as the certification frontrunner [4]
Key Risks
  1. Continued heavy cash burn ($385-415M guided for second-half 2026) against a still-small revenue base raises ongoing dilution risk, evidenced by the market's negative reaction and fresh equity raise despite a record revenue quarter [1][2]
  2. Archer's fraud countersuit alleging concealed Chinese supply-chain ties and tariff misclassification creates legal, regulatory, and reputational risk regardless of ultimate merit, particularly given foreign-influence sensitivities in aerospace [4]
  3. FAA certification delays or any safety incident during the final certification stage remain an existential binary risk that could push full commercial launch beyond current targets
