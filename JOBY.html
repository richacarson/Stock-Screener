Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense / eVTOL ·NYSE ·United States
Joby Aviation is developing all-electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft for commercial passenger air taxi service. Founded in 2009 by CEO JoeBen Bevirt and headquartered in Santa Cruz, California, the company has approximately 2,200 employees. Joby acquired Uber Elevate (2021) and Blade Air Mobility (2025), integrating helicopter charter operations while pursuing FAA type certification for its five-seat eVTOL aircraft. In Q2 2026, Joby reported revenue of $38.6M (beating estimates of $31M) primarily from Blade operations, and raised full-year 2026 guidance to $115-125M. The company holds $2.26B in cash to fund development through commercialization. Strategic investors include Toyota ($894M invested) and Delta Air Lines. Joby targets first commercial passenger service in 2026 across markets in Texas, Florida, and New York. Market cap is approximately $8.7B.

Investment Thesis
Joby at $8.81 is a high-conviction bet on the creation of an entirely new transportation category — urban air mobility via eVTOL aircraft. The Q2 2026 results showed meaningful acceleration with revenue of $38.6M beating estimates by 25%, full-year guidance raised to $115-125M, and Blade seat sales growing 50%+ YoY demonstrating that passenger demand for air taxi service exists and is growing [1]. The company's $2.26B cash position (against $747.7M debt) provides years of runway through FAA certification and commercial launch. Toyota's $894M investment provides manufacturing partnership credibility, Delta's partnership provides distribution, and the 40% reduction in manufacturing nonconformance rates signals readiness for production scaling. If FAA type certification is achieved and commercial service launches successfully, the addressable market (estimated at $500B+ globally for urban air mobility) could justify a substantial re-rating from the current $8.7B market cap.
The bear case is severe: Joby has burned through $3B+ in capital over 15+ years without operating a single commercial eVTOL flight. The $8.7B market cap for a company with -$878M in TTM net losses, no FAA type certificate in hand, and revenue entirely dependent on the acquired Blade helicopter business represents extreme speculation [4]. FAA certification timelines have historically stretched beyond initial estimates for novel aircraft designs — any delay pushes commercial revenue further out while cash burn continues at ~$250M per quarter in operating losses. The eVTOL competitive landscape is crowded (Archer, Wisk, Lilium), and it remains unproven whether urban air taxis can achieve sufficient utilization rates, pricing, and passenger adoption to generate sustainable unit economics. The P/S ratio of 75x on current revenue prices in a future that may not materialize. Insider selling by the CFO and a major director suggests insiders may view current valuations as stretched [5]. The Inspire Impact Score of 57 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors, but the business risk profile is extreme for any portfolio allocation.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Joby's core business — designing, manufacturing, certifying, and operating eVTOL aircraft — is structurally immune to AI disruption. Physical aircraft manufacturing, FAA type certification processes (requiring years of testing and regulatory engagement), pilot training, vertiport infrastructure, and flight operations cannot be replicated or substituted by AI [2]. The regulatory moat alone (FAA Part 135 certification, type certification) creates barriers that AI cannot circumvent. eVTOL transportation addresses a physical-world problem (urban congestion, travel time) that requires physical-world solutions. AI could marginally improve flight path optimization or maintenance scheduling, but these are incremental enhancements, not substitution risks. The only vulnerability is if autonomous AI piloting advances faster than expected, potentially commoditizing the flight operations layer — but this is a distant risk given current FAA certification requirements for human pilots. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of AI-generated revenue. Joby's technology stack focuses on electric propulsion, composite manufacturing, and flight control systems rather than AI-powered products or services.
