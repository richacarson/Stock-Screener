Investment Thesis
JPMorgan Chase at 15.03x forward earnings is the highest-quality bank franchise globally, delivering record Q2 2026 net income of $21.2B ($7.70/share) on revenue of $57.3B (+28% YoY) with ROTCE of 23% [1]. The bank dominates across every major business line — equities revenue surged 86%, investment banking fees jumped 30%, and NII guidance was raised to $105.5B for full-year 2026. The $2B+ annual AI investment with 450+ use cases deployed across 60% of the workforce is generating measurable results: 6% more accounts per employee, 11% lower fraud costs, 10% higher engineering productivity [2]. ROE of 17.79%, profit margin of 34.92%, and a conservative payout ratio of 25.75% with dividend raised to $1.65/share provide sustainable capital returns. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 8.57% and 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 10.73% demonstrate compounding power. Jamie Dimon's 21-year track record of navigating crises while investing through cycles is unmatched in banking.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, regulatory risk, and CEO succession uncertainty. JPMorgan's record results are heavily driven by elevated trading activity (equities +86%) and investment banking volumes (+30%) that are inherently cyclical — any market downturn would compress these revenue lines materially [1]. The bank faces persistent regulatory friction: $348M in trade surveillance fines and a $12.18M ECB penalty for risk-weighted asset misreporting in 2026 alone suggest systemic compliance gaps despite massive compliance spend [3]. Forward P/E of 15.03x and PEG of 1.52 price in continued growth that may not materialize in a recession. The card net charge-off rate of ~3.2% signals consumer credit stress. The elephant in the room is CEO succession — Jamie Dimon at 70 has not named a successor, and his personal leadership IS the franchise premium; post-Dimon JPM is uncharted territory.
