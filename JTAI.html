Investment Thesis
Jet.AI's bull case rests entirely on optionality and a steeply discounted valuation — the company holds cash with no debt, has secured power and permitting milestones for its Convergence Compute JV data center sites (Manitoba, Maritimes, Midwest), and the July 2026 merger LOI would value the combined entity at roughly $320M enterprise value with approximately $20M allocated to existing Jet.AI shareholders [1][2]. H1 2026 revenue of $7.0M represents 22.8% growth over H1 2025, and Q2 2026 revenue of $5.32M significantly beat the $1.12M estimate, suggesting improving near-term execution [1]. If the merger closes and any data center project reaches operational status, current shareholders could see meaningful value creation relative to the company's ~$5.5M market cap.
The bear case is overwhelming and multi-dimensional. The stock has fallen 99%+ over the past 52 weeks, forcing two reverse stock splits (1-for-225 in 2024, 1-for-200 in April 2026) just to maintain Nasdaq compliance, while operating losses reached -$9.97M on a trailing basis and Q2 2026 EPS was -$1.72 despite the revenue beat [1][4][5]. Actual daily trading volume of approximately 55,000 shares ($85K daily dollar volume) renders the stock essentially untradeable for any institutional investor — the yfinance average volume of 1.33M shares is distorted by reverse-split-era calculation artifacts. The company has only 9 employees, an executive team that has operated under interim titles, and has pivoted its core strategy multiple times in three years. The July 2026 merger/spin-off LOI introduces further uncertainty about whether current shareholders will retain meaningful economic exposure to the AI data center assets they have funded, and no data center has reached operational or revenue-generating status [2]. This is a speculative micro-cap unsuitable for IOWN allocation.
