Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
KB Home is one of the largest homebuilders in the United States, operating across major metropolitan markets in California, Texas, Arizona, Colorado, Florida, and other states. Founded in 1957, the company differentiates through its Built to Order (BTO) model — 73% of net orders are customer-configured homes rather than spec builds. KB Home is the national leader in ENERGY STAR certified homes and offers ZeroHouse 3.0 net-zero energy-ready homes. FY2026 guidance projects $4.9-5.3B in revenue with 10,500-11,000 deliveries.

Investment Thesis
KB Home at 15x forward earnings and 0.95x book value offers deep-cycle value in a homebuilder with distinctive competitive positioning. The Built to Order model (73% of orders) reduces spec inventory risk and commands customer loyalty, while ENERGY STAR leadership and ZeroHouse 3.0 differentiate on sustainability [1][3]. D/E of 53% provides balance sheet flexibility through the downturn, and $359M in trailing free cash flow supports both the $1.00 annual dividend and share repurchases. Community count expansion of 11% to 280 seeds recovery — when mortgage rates normalize, KB Home is positioned with more selling capacity and a differentiated product offering. The Inspire score of 66 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions reflects clean ESG positioning.
The bear case is the severity and duration of the housing downturn. Revenue is declining -18% YoY in FY2026 with full-year guidance of $4.9-5.3B, Q2 2026 revenue fell 27% with gross margins compressing to 15.2% (vs. historically 20%+), and EPS dropped from $1.53 to $0.44 in Q2 [1]. ROE of 7.0% and profit margin of 4.94% reflect a business operating well below mid-cycle profitability. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -2.3% means the company has not grown over the cycle. Average selling price declined 8% to $457,000, suggesting pricing pressure. Insider selling of $16.3M by Executive Chairman Mezger and CEO McGibney in July 2026 — with zero buys and seven sells over the past year — signals that leadership expects extended pressure [4]. Recovery depends entirely on mortgage rate relief, which remains uncertain with the Fed's timeline unpredictable.
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage rate decline below 6% would unlock pent-up housing demand across KB Home's 280 communities, with the BTO model uniquely positioned to convert interest into personalized orders [1]
  2. ZeroHouse 3.0 adoption accelerating as energy costs rise and DOE zero-energy-ready certification becomes a competitive advantage — average $1,800 annual utility savings per home drives buyer preference [3]
  3. Build time reduction to 100 days (lowest in a decade) improves capital turns and positions KB Home for margin expansion when volumes recover [1]
Key Risks
  1. Extended high-rate environment keeps housing affordability constrained — Q2 2026 deliveries fell 19% YoY and average selling price declined 8% to $457,000, with no visibility on when rate relief materializes [1]
  2. Gross margin compression from 20%+ historically to 15.2% in Q2 2026, combined with SG&A deleverage to 12.7%, risks prolonged profitability erosion if revenue remains below $5B [1]
  3. Heavy insider selling ($16.3M by Mezger and McGibney in July 2026 alone, zero insider purchases in the past year) contradicts the deep-value narrative and may signal leadership expects further deterioration [4]

KB Home's Built to Order model involves complex customer interactions, design customization, and local market expertise that resist automation.
