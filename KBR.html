Company Profile
Industrials ·Engineering & Construction ·NYSE ·United States
KBR is a global engineering, technology, and services company providing science, technology, and engineering solutions to governments and commercial customers. It operates through Mission Technology Solutions (MTS) focused on defense, intelligence, space, and the Golden Dome missile defense initiative, and Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS) providing proprietary process technologies for ammonia, petrochemicals, and decarbonization. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.0B (+2% YoY) with net income of $96M (+32%). The company plans to spin off MTS as an independent entity (Trinzic) on January 4, 2027. Full-year 2026 guidance reaffirmed: $7.90-8.36B revenue, $3.87-4.22 adjusted EPS. Founded in 1901, headquartered in Houston, Texas.
37,000 Employees ·www.kbr.com

Investment Thesis
KBR trades at a compelling 8.74x forward P/E with PEG of 0.54 and massive backlog providing exceptional revenue visibility, while management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $7.90-8.36B revenue with adjusted EPS of $3.87-4.22 [1]. Q2 2026 demonstrated improving fundamentals — revenue of $2.0B (+2% YoY, up from Q1's -5%), net income of $96M (+32%), and adjusted EBITDA of $258M at 13% margin [1]. STS revenue surged 10% to $676M driven by project ramp-ups in the Middle East and Latin America, validating the high-margin proprietary technology business [1]. The planned Trinzic spin-off (January 4, 2027) should unlock the conglomerate discount, creating two focused entities. KBR's strategic positioning on the Golden Dome missile defense initiative with increased IRAD investment positions the company at the center of a generational defense modernization cycle [2]. The Inspire score of 48 (ALIGNED) with 11 positive ESG attributions is constructive for faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on leverage, thin margins, and spin-off execution risk. D/E of 170.4% with $2.8B total debt against $323M cash creates financial risk, with interest expense consuming a meaningful portion of operating income [4]. Profit margin of 5.49% is thin for an engineering services firm, leaving minimal room for contract cost overruns or macro shocks. MTS revenue declined 2% in Q2 due to EUCOM contingency runoff, and excluding that headwind, growth was positive but modest — the defense backlog needs new large awards to sustain momentum [1]. The Trinzic spin-off creates near-term uncertainty about organizational structure, stranded costs, talent retention, and contract allocation across two entities involving 37,000 employees. Revenue growth of 1.6% YoY is below the 10% target, and the 5-year CAGR of ~5.9% reflects steady but unspectacular long-term growth. The stock has declined approximately 32% from its 52-week high, reflecting market uncertainty about the spin-off and government spending trajectory [4].
