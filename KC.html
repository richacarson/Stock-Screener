Company Profile
Technology ·Cloud Computing ·NASDAQ ·China
Kingsoft Cloud Holdings provides cloud computing services in China, including public cloud (compute, storage, delivery), enterprise cloud, and AI cloud services. A subsidiary of Kingsoft Corporation with strategic backing from Xiaomi founder Lei Jun, the company serves gaming, video, public services, healthcare, and financial services industries. AI cloud has become the primary revenue driver, accounting for over half of public cloud revenue as of Q1 2026.

Investment Thesis
Kingsoft Cloud is executing a rapid pivot to AI cloud infrastructure, with AI revenue of RMB 998M in Q1 2026 growing 91% YoY and surpassing 50% of public cloud revenue for the first time [1]. Total revenue grew 37.2% YoY to RMB 2.7B, while adjusted EBITDA surged 134.7% to RMB 748M (27.6% margin), demonstrating improving unit economics as AI scale builds. The captive Xiaomi/Kingsoft ecosystem (68.9% ecosystem revenue growth) provides a built-in customer base that reduces go-to-market friction [1]. At ~$3.5B market cap with 37%+ growth, the valuation is modest for a Chinese AI infrastructure play if profitability materializes.
The bear case is substantial: TTM net losses of ~$134M and negative ROE of -12.7% indicate KC has not achieved sustained GAAP profitability [3]. Adjusted gross margin compressed from 70% to 30% in Q1 2026 due to GPU server costs, raising questions about long-term margin sustainability as AI cloud scales [1]. Competition from Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, and Huawei Cloud — each with 10-50x greater scale, GPU procurement capacity, and financial resources — creates persistent pricing pressure. As a Chinese ADR on NASDAQ, KC faces VIE structure risk, potential delisting concerns under HFCAA, and US semiconductor export controls that could constrain GPU access critical for AI cloud expansion. The stock has declined from $16+ to ~$10 in 2026, suggesting the market is discounting execution risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI cloud revenue trajectory toward RMB 4-5B annualized as AI workloads scale across Xiaomi ecosystem (EVs, smart devices) and enterprise customers, with 91% YoY growth momentum
  2. Path to GAAP profitability as AI cloud margins normalize — adjusted EBITDA margin improvement of 11.4pp YoY to 27.6% suggests operating leverage is building
  3. China's AI sovereignty policies favoring domestic cloud providers over foreign alternatives, creating a protected addressable market for KC's AI infrastructure services
Key Risks
  1. Persistent net losses with ~$134M TTM deficit and negative ROE; the company has never achieved sustained GAAP profitability, and AI infrastructure capex may delay breakeven further
  2. Gross margin compression from 70% to 30% in Q1 2026 reflects the capital-intensive reality of AI cloud infrastructure, with uncertain long-term margin structure as GPU costs remain elevated
  3. Chinese ADR delisting risk under HFCAA, VIE structure vulnerability, and US semiconductor export controls could constrain GPU access critical for AI cloud expansion
