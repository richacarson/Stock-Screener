Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Non-Alcoholic ·NMS ·United States
Keurig Dr Pepper Inc. is a leading beverage company in North America operating across U.S. Refreshment Beverages, U.S. Coffee, and International segments. The company owns Dr Pepper (the #2 U.S. soda brand), Keurig single-serve coffee systems (~80% U.S. market share), and a portfolio including 7UP, Snapple, and Canada Dry. KDP closed its transformative $10B+ acquisition of JDE Peet's on April 1, 2026, adding global coffee operations and guiding FY2026 revenue of $25.9-26.4B. Q2 CY2026 revenue surged 75.6% YoY to $7.31B, driven primarily by the acquisition. Founded in 2018 via merger and headquartered in Burlington, Massachusetts.

Investment Thesis
Keurig Dr Pepper at $31 with a $42.5B market cap is executing a transformative pivot to become a global coffee-and-beverages platform — the JDE Peet's acquisition (closed April 2026) adds ~$11B in annual revenue, guiding FY2026 to $25.9-26.4B (up from $16.6B in FY2025) [1]. The core U.S. Refreshment Beverages segment grew 10% organically in Q2 CY2026, anchored by Dr Pepper's ascent to #2 U.S. soda brand, while Keurig maintains ~80% U.S. single-serve coffee market share. Forward P/E of 12.3x and PEG of 0.94 suggest the market is pricing in integration risk but not giving credit for normalized earnings power — forward EPS of $2.53 implies a normalized payout ratio of just 36%, supporting the 2.94% dividend yield with ample coverage. Management targets low-double-digit constant-currency adjusted EPS growth and leverage reduction to 4.1x by year-end [1].
The bear case centers on integration execution risk and structural weakness in the coffee segment. The JDE Peet's deal adds $10B+ in debt (pro forma leverage 4.1x), and synergy realization from integrating global coffee operations across different markets, cultures, and supply chains is far from guaranteed [1]. Trailing ROE of 5.1% and profit margin of 7.1% reflect acquisition charges but also reveal how thin the underlying profitability is before synergies materialize. The U.S. Coffee segment has been flat since 2022, with the K-Cup platform facing secular pressure from competition (Nespresso, Starbucks at-home) and consumer preference shifts toward fresh-brewed coffee [2]. The SEC's $1.5M settlement over misleading K-Cup recyclability claims raises questions about management credibility on sustainability commitments [4]. The planned 2027 separation adds uncertainty about the company's long-term identity, and JAB Holdings' controlling stake means governance is concentrated rather than independent. At 4.1x leverage, any integration misstep or consumer spending slowdown could pressure the dividend [1][3].

Key Risks
  1. Integration execution risk from the $10B+ JDE Peet's acquisition — combining global coffee operations across different markets, supply chains, and cultures while maintaining 4.1x leverage leaves minimal margin for error [1]
  2. Secular pressure on the U.S. Coffee segment — K-Cup sales have been flat since 2022, facing competition from Nespresso, Starbucks at-home, and consumer preference shifts toward fresh-brewed alternatives, threatening the company's highest-margin business [2]
  3. Concentrated governance risk from JAB Holdings' controlling stake and the planned 2027 separation — minority shareholders have limited influence over strategic direction, and the separation could dilute value or create subscale standalone entities [1][3]
