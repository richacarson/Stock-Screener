Company Profile
Technology ·Data Center Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Keel Infrastructure (formerly Bitfarms) develops large-scale powered land sites in North America into high-performance computing campuses leased to hyperscaler, neocloud, enterprise, and government customers for AI workloads. The company completed a transformative pivot from Bitcoin mining to AI data center infrastructure over the past 18 months, rebranding from Bitfarms, exiting Latin American operations, and decommissioning all U.S. Bitcoin mining facilities. Keel maintains a 2.2 GW development pipeline with established grid interconnections across Pennsylvania, Washington State, and Quebec, Canada, with $819M total liquidity including $698M unrestricted cash and $121M in unencumbered Bitcoin (1,861 BTC). Headquartered in New York City. Market cap approximately $2.0B.

Investment Thesis
Keel is a pure-play bet on AI infrastructure demand with a 2.2 GW development pipeline across three established grid interconnections (Pennsylvania, Washington, Quebec) and $819M total liquidity to execute — $698M unrestricted cash plus $121M in unencumbered Bitcoin [2]. The company completed a transformative pivot from Bitfarms (Bitcoin mining) to AI data center development, positioning its power capacity for the most capital-constrained bottleneck in the AI supply chain: physical data center capacity with grid-connected power. The 153.9% one-year stock return and $2.0B market cap demonstrate market conviction in the AI infrastructure thesis. Hyperscaler capex budgets exceeding $200B annually in 2026 create massive demand for the exact infrastructure Keel is building. The hiring of Ganesh Aiyer (25 years at Digital Realty) as president brings credible data center execution expertise [3].
The bear case is execution risk compounded by financial fragility. Keel has zero signed tenant leases — only 'active negotiations at three sites with deepening commercial engagement' — meaning the entire $2.0B market cap is pricing a data center business that generates no data center revenue [2]. Q2 2026 revenue of $30.4M declined 50% YoY with a $65M net loss and -$23.7M adjusted EBITDA as Bitcoin mining revenue evaporated faster than AI revenue materialized. D/E of 315.88% after the $458M convertible note raise creates significant financial leverage risk. Profit margin of -230.59% and ROE of -75.64% reflect a company burning cash at an alarming rate while waiting for tenants to sign. Environmental permitting delays (Panther Creek) and regulatory scrutiny of data center expansion create timeline risk [3]. The company competes against Digital Realty ($45B market cap), Equinix ($88B), and other massive incumbents with established tenant relationships and proven execution track records. If AI capex cycles slow or hyperscalers build their own facilities, Keel's speculative pipeline could become stranded assets. The stock declined 12.37% on Q2 2026 results.
