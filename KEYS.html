Company Profile
Technology ·Scientific & Technical Instruments ·NYQ ·United States
Keysight Technologies is the world's leading provider of electronic design and test solutions, operating through Communications Solutions Group and Electronic Industrial Solutions Group segments. Spun off from Agilent Technologies in 2014 (with heritage tracing to Hewlett-Packard), the company provides oscilloscopes, network analyzers, signal generators, and software platforms serving wireless, data center, aerospace/defense, and automotive markets. Q3 FY2026 delivered revenue of $1.85B with orders exceeding $2B for the second consecutive quarter, and full-year FY2026 revenue is guided to approximately $7.1B, representing ~37% growth.

Investment Thesis
Keysight at $341 with a $58B market cap is executing a powerful cyclical recovery with structural growth tailwinds — Q3 FY2026 delivered $1.85B revenue with orders exceeding $2B for the second consecutive quarter, and Q4 guidance of $1.93-1.95B (+37% YoY) implies full-year FY2026 revenue of ~$7.1B [1][2]. The PEG ratio of 0.66 is exceptionally attractive given forward EPS consensus of $13.93 (vs trailing $7.39), reflecting massive earnings leverage as the cyclical recovery compounds with secular demand for 5G/6G, AI infrastructure, and data center test equipment [2][3]. The balance sheet is clean at 42% D/E, ROE of 20.9% demonstrates capital efficiency, and 15% R&D investment sustains the technology moat across measurement platforms. Five consecutive years on the Dow Jones Sustainability Index and a Sustainalytics controversy score of 1.0 make KEYS one of the cleanest ESG profiles in technology [4].
The key risk is valuation sustainability after the cyclical bounce. Trailing P/E of 46x is elevated, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of only 7.5% reveals that FY2026's spectacular growth follows two years of stagnation (FY2024 revenue actually declined to $4.98B from $5.46B in FY2023), raising sustainability questions once the order recovery normalizes [3]. Forward P/E of 24.5x and non-GAAP EPS guidance of $3.34-3.40 for Q4 alone suggest the market is pricing in continued acceleration, but semiconductor and communications capex cycles are inherently volatile — a slowdown in 5G or data center buildout could recreate the FY2024 trough [2]. Keysight's profit margin of 19.1% is below the 20% target and leaves room for margin expansion but also indicates the growth is partly volume-driven rather than purely mix-driven. Competition from Rohde & Schwarz in high-frequency measurement and emerging AI-native test platforms could compress software margins over time. The company's heavy acquisition strategy introduces integration risk, and culture quality varies significantly across teams per employee reviews [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI infrastructure test demand — Orders exceeding $2B for two consecutive quarters are driven partly by 800G/1.6T Ethernet validation, PCIe Gen 6 testing, and high-speed optical interconnect measurement for AI data centers, with this spending cycle expected to persist through 2028+ [2]
  2. 5G-to-6G transition — Keysight's Communications Solutions Group grew 27% in Q1 FY2026 with commercial communications up 33%, and early 6G research spending by operators and equipment makers will sustain multi-year demand for next-generation wireless test solutions [1][3]
  3. Earnings leverage at PEG 0.66 — Forward EPS of $13.93 represents 88% growth over trailing $7.39, with operating leverage from fixed R&D costs and software mix shift providing significant margin expansion runway as revenue scales above $7B [2]
Key Risks
  1. Cyclical vulnerability — The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 7.5% masks the FY2024 trough ($4.98B), and test & measurement demand is inherently tied to semiconductor and communications capex cycles that could reverse if AI infrastructure spending cools or 5G deployment decelerates [3]
  2. Valuation stretch at 46x trailing earnings — While the PEG of 0.66 is attractive on forward estimates, any miss in the $13.93 forward EPS consensus would compress the multiple sharply, as happened in FY2024 when the stock declined from $170 to $130 during the downturn [2]
  3. Software platform disruption — AI-native test automation tools could erode Keysight's growing software margins (PathWave, Eggplant), and competitors like Rohde & Schwarz are investing heavily in next-generation measurement platforms [5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Keysight's physical test & measurement instruments — oscilloscopes, network analyzers, signal generators — cannot be replaced by software or AI models; engineers will always need to measure real-world electronic signals [1]. The hardware business represents the majority of revenue and is structurally immune to AI substitution. However, Keysight's growing software business (PathWave platform, Eggplant test automation) faces moderate risk as AI-native testing tools emerge — automated test generation and AI-driven quality assurance could erode some software-based revenue streams over time. The company's exposure to the AI infrastructure buildout is a significant offset, as semiconductor companies, data center operators, and network equipment makers all require Keysight test equipment for AI chip validation and high-speed interconnect testing. AI Opportunity (1/1): Keysight is a direct beneficiary of the AI infrastructure supercycle — Q3 FY2026 orders exceeded $2B for the second consecutive quarter, driven partly by demand for testing AI-related networking equipment, high-speed optical interconnects, and next-generation data center infrastructure [2]. The company's test solutions for 800G/1.6T Ethernet and PCIe Gen 6 are essential for AI hardware validation.
