Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Equipment & Instruments ·NYSE ·United States
Keysight Technologies, Inc. provides electronic design and test solutions for communications, networking, semiconductor, aerospace/defense, and automotive industries worldwide. Products include oscilloscopes, signal analyzers, network analyzers, photonics test equipment, and AI data center validation solutions. Spun off from Agilent Technologies (itself from Hewlett-Packard) in 2014. Q3 FY2026 delivered record revenue of $1.846B (+36% YoY) driven by explosive AI wireline demand, with operating margin expanding 820bps to 33.2% and EPS of $3.07. FY2026 revenue growth guided at 32% with ~60% EPS growth.

FY2026 guidance calls for 32% revenue growth and ~60% EPS growth. The balance sheet is fortress-grade with D/E of only 45%, ROE of 22.27%, and the company's Hewlett-Packard heritage provides 87 years of technology innovation DNA. Q4 FY2026 guidance of $1.93-1.95B (+37% YoY) implies continued acceleration [1]. STEM education reaching 798,000+ students and A+ executive team ratings from Comparably confirm best-in-class organizational quality [2][3]. The Electronic Industrial Solutions Group delivered a record $501M (+21%) in Q3, providing diversified growth beyond communications [1].
The primary risk is valuation. At 49x trailing P/E and 30.5x forward P/E, Keysight is priced for continued hyper-growth that depends on sustained AI infrastructure spending [4]. If AI data center capex moderates — whether from hyperscaler budget discipline, technology changes, or economic slowdown — orders could decelerate sharply from the current 36% revenue growth rate, compressing the premium multiple. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 7.05% reflects the FY2024 cyclical downturn and reminds that test equipment is historically cyclical. PEG of 1.75 suggests the stock is no longer cheap relative to growth expectations. The stock has surged 78% over the past year, creating vulnerability to mean reversion if AI momentum fades [4]. 800G/1.6T optical interconnect standardization could eventually commoditize some test capabilities.
