Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Canada
Kinross Gold Corporation is a senior gold mining company that acquires, explores, develops, and operates gold properties in the United States, Brazil, Chile, Canada, and Mauritania. Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Toronto, Kinross produces approximately 2.0 million gold equivalent ounces annually across mines including Fort Knox (Alaska), Paracatu (Brazil), Tasiast (Mauritania), and Round Mountain (Nevada). Q2 2026 revenue reached $2.24B (+29% YoY) with net earnings of $844M ($0.71/share), driven by realized gold prices of $4,487/oz. The company holds a net cash position of $1.9B with total liquidity of ~$4.4B and is on track to return 40% of free cash flow to shareholders.

Investment Thesis
Kinross Gold is delivering exceptional financial performance in a historic gold bull market. Q2 2026 revenue of $2.24B (+29% YoY) with net earnings of $844M (37.5% margin) and attributable free cash flow of $727M continue a streak of record results [1]. The balance sheet is pristine: D/E of 8%, $2.66B cash, net cash position of $1.9B, and total liquidity of ~$4.4B [4]. At 8.79x forward earnings with PEG of 0.65, KGC offers compelling value for a company growing earnings ~100% YoY. The shareholder return program — 40% of FCF through buybacks ($230M in Q2) and a recently increased $0.04/share quarterly dividend — signals capital discipline. Production guidance of 2.0M oz through 2028 at AISC of $1,730/oz provides visibility, while the Lobo-Marte project (~350,000 oz/year potential) offers upside optionality [1].
The primary risk is extreme gold price dependency — Kinross's record performance is overwhelmingly driven by realized gold prices of $4,487/oz (Q2 2026) versus AISC of $1,821/oz, creating $2,666/oz margins that would collapse in a gold correction [1]. At AISC of $1,730/oz, a gold price decline to $2,500/oz would cut margins by 53%, and a decline to $2,000/oz would compress margins by 90%. Production volumes are flat at 2.0M oz/year — there is no organic growth story independent of commodity prices. Revenue growth YoY has decelerated from 61% in Q1 2026 to 29% in Q2 2026 as YoY comps normalize, and consensus expects this deceleration to continue [1]. Geopolitical risk at Tasiast (Mauritania) exposes a significant portion of production to political instability. Rising AISC ($1,821/oz in Q2, above the $1,730/oz full-year guidance midpoint) signals industry-wide cost inflation. The 0.59% dividend yield provides minimal income relative to massive $3B+ annual FCF generation, and insider selling (net basis) contradicts the bullish capital returns narrative [4].

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Gold mining is structurally immune to AI disruption. The core business involves physical extraction, processing, and sale of a commodity whose value is determined by global supply-demand dynamics, central bank reserve policy, and macroeconomic conditions — none of which can be substituted by AI [1]. No analysts have flagged AI as a substitution risk for Kinross or the gold mining industry. The business requires massive physical infrastructure (mines, processing plants, tailings facilities), geological expertise, environmental permitting, and regulatory compliance across multiple jurisdictions that AI structurally cannot displace. AI data center buildout is increasing electricity demand globally, indirectly supporting gold demand as an inflation hedge. AI Opportunity (0/1): Kinross deploys AI internally for operational efficiency — autonomous trucks at Round Mountain, AI ore prediction at Fort Knox — but does not generate revenue from AI-related products, services, or infrastructure [2]. The efficiency gains are real but do not constitute AI revenue per the scoring criteria.
