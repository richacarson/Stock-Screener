Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYQ ·Canada
Kinross Gold Corporation is a senior gold mining company operating mines across the Americas, West Africa, and Alaska. The company produces approximately 2.0 million gold-equivalent ounces annually with an all-in sustaining cost of ~$1,730/oz, generating TTM revenue of $8.47B with a 37.5% profit margin and 37.0% ROE, driven by record gold prices averaging $4,483/oz realized in Q2 CY2026. Q2 CY2026 revenue grew 29.5% YoY to $2.24B with adjusted EPS of $0.71 (up 61.4% YoY). Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Toronto, Canada.

Investment Thesis
Kinross Gold at $23.83 with a $28.3B market cap is a mid-tier gold producer delivering exceptional financial results in the current record gold price environment — Q2 CY2026 revenue grew 29.5% YoY to $2.24B with adjusted EPS of $0.71 (up 61.4%) on a realized gold price of $4,483/oz, while the balance sheet carries minimal debt at just 7.7% D/E [1]. The financial profile is among the strongest in the gold mining universe: 37.0% ROE, 37.5% profit margin, Forward P/E of just 8.6x, and PEG of 1.12. FY2026 guidance targets 2.0M gold-equivalent ounces at AISC of $1,730/oz (+/- 5%), implying $2,750+/oz margins at current spot prices. The Great Bear project in Ontario adds significant long-term production optionality, and the near-debt-free balance sheet provides downside protection [1][3].
The bear case is straightforward: Kinross's financial performance is almost entirely a function of the gold price, which it does not control. Gold at $4,483/oz in Q2 CY2026 is up 36.5% from a year ago — but Q2 production actually declined 4% YoY to 492,326 ounces, meaning the revenue growth is price-driven, not operational [1]. Any meaningful correction in gold prices from record levels would compress margins, earnings, and the stock price proportionally — a return to $2,500/oz gold would cut margins by roughly 60% and likely push the stock well below $15. The company is a single-commodity producer with no diversification and limited control over its revenue trajectory. AISC guidance of $1,730/oz is subject to inflationary cost pressures from energy, labor, and consumables that have historically compressed margins in prior gold cycles [1][3]. The dividend (0.69% yield, $0.16/share) is minimal and has not grown consistently — annual payouts fluctuated between $0.06 and $0.16 over the past six years, reflecting the cyclicality of the business rather than a commitment to income investors [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record gold prices sustaining $2,750+/oz margins — realized price of $4,483/oz in Q2 CY2026 represents a 36.5% YoY increase, and central bank buying, geopolitical uncertainty, and de-dollarization trends support continued elevated pricing [1]
  2. Great Bear project development — the $1.8B Ontario acquisition represents a multi-million-ounce development asset that could meaningfully increase production beyond the current 2.0M oz/year run rate over the next 3-5 years [1]
  3. Near-debt-free balance sheet (7.7% D/E) provides exceptional downside protection and financial flexibility to fund growth projects, increase dividends, or pursue accretive M&A through any gold price cycle [1]
Key Risks
  1. Gold price dependence — nearly 100% of revenue comes from gold sales, and Q2 CY2026 production declined 4% YoY while revenue grew 29.5%, demonstrating that financial performance is driven by commodity price rather than operational improvement [1]
  2. Cost inflation risk — AISC of $1,730/oz is subject to energy, labor, and consumable cost pressures that have historically compressed margins in prior cycles, and the +/- 5% guidance range implies $87/oz of cost uncertainty [1][3]
  3. Jurisdictional and operational risk — Kinross operates in geopolitically complex regions (West Africa, Brazil, Chile) where permitting, regulatory changes, and community relations can disrupt production, as demonstrated by the forced Russian exit in 2022 [2]
