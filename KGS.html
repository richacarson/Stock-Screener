Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Kodiak Gas Services is the largest independent contract compression services provider in the United States, operating approximately 4.8 million horsepower of natural gas compression equipment across major U.S. basins and internationally. Following the April 2024 CSI Compressco acquisition and the 2026 Distributed Power Solutions (DPS) acquisition, Kodiak expanded into power infrastructure serving data centers and microgrids. Q2 2026 revenue of $391M grew 21% YoY with record Adjusted EBITDA of $216.8M (+21.7%). Market cap ~$5.9B, IPO'd June 2023, headquartered in The Woodlands, Texas.

Investment Thesis
Kodiak Gas Services is the dominant U.S. contract compression platform with accelerating growth and a compelling dual-engine strategy. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $391M (+21% YoY) with record Adjusted EBITDA of $216.8M (+21.7%), and management raised full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance to $830-860M with discretionary cash flow of $570-600M [1]. At 18.09x forward earnings with 21% revenue growth and 23% earnings growth, the valuation is reasonable for a market-leading infrastructure business benefiting from LNG export growth and AI data center power demand. D/E improved significantly to 130% from 238% at the prior screen, demonstrating deleveraging progress. The DPS acquisition launches a Power Infrastructure segment with $95-125M in 2026 revenue at 60-70% margins, diversifying into the AI-driven power demand theme [1]. The Inspire Impact Score of 30 (ALIGNED) makes it accessible to faith-based investors.
Risks center on the capital-intensive model and execution complexity. D/E of 130.26% remains elevated, with $2.6B in total debt creating vulnerability if natural gas prices decline or customer utilization rates fall [4]. Trailing P/E of 66.56x reflects GAAP earnings depressed by heavy depreciation and interest expense — the business generates substantial cash flow but GAAP profitability (5.77% margin, 4.59% ROE) remains thin. The 218% dividend payout ratio on GAAP earnings is unsustainable without continued EBITDA growth. A potential FCPA compliance issue related to pre-acquisition payments to Mexican officials in the CSI Compressco deal creates regulatory and reputational risk [3]. The company's young public history (IPO June 2023) means it has not been tested through a full commodity cycle, and natural gas price volatility could compress customer demand. Long-term energy transition risk from renewables could reduce natural gas demand on a 10+ year horizon, though near-term demand is structurally supported by AI and LNG trends.
Key Catalysts
  1. Power Infrastructure segment scaling beyond $125M in revenue as AI data center demand accelerates, with 60-70% margins contributing meaningfully to earnings growth and diversifying the revenue base beyond compression [1]
  2. Continued deleveraging from D/E 130% as EBITDA growth of $830-860M generates substantial discretionary cash flow of $570-600M for debt reduction and dividend growth [1]
  3. Natural gas demand growth from LNG export capacity expansion, petrochemical buildouts, and AI data center electricity demand creating a structural multi-year tailwind for compression services utilization and pricing
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 130% with $2.6B total debt creates refinancing and interest rate risk — if natural gas prices decline or customer demand softens, the capital-intensive model amplifies downside through fixed fleet costs [4]
  2. Potential FCPA compliance issue from pre-acquisition payments to Mexican officials in the CSI Compressco deal could result in regulatory penalties, management distraction, and reputational damage [3]
  3. Thin GAAP profitability (5.77% margin, 4.59% ROE) relative to 66.56x trailing PE means any deceleration in growth trajectory could trigger severe multiple compression, and the 218% payout ratio is unsustainable without continued EBITDA expansion [4]
