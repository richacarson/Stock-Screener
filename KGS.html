Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Kodiak Gas Services is the largest independent contract compression services provider in the United States, operating approximately 4.8 million horsepower of natural gas compression equipment across major U.S. basins and internationally. Following the April 2024 CSI Compressco acquisition and the 2026 Distributed Power Solutions (DPS) acquisition, Kodiak has expanded into power infrastructure serving data centers and microgrids. Headquartered in The Woodlands, Texas; IPO'd June 2023.
1,300 Employees ·www.kodiakgas.com

Investment Thesis
Kodiak Gas Services is the dominant U.S. contract compression platform with a powerful dual-growth engine. Q1 2026 delivered record Adj. EBITDA of $190.1M (up 7% YoY) with Contract Services margins of 70.6%, demonstrating exceptional operational leverage [1]. The DPS acquisition, now closed, launches a Power Infrastructure segment with $95-125M in 2026 revenue at 60-70% margins, diversifying beyond compression into AI data center and microgrid power generation [1]. The PEG of 0.23 at a ~$5.2B market cap suggests significant undervaluation relative to the 20%+ revenue CAGR trajectory, and 2026 EBITDA guidance was raised to $820-860M with discretionary cash flow of $520-570M [1][2]. Natural gas compression is essential infrastructure benefiting from both LNG export growth and AI-driven electricity demand.
Primary risks center on the capital-intensive business model. D/E of 238.24% reflects heavy equipment financing and DPS acquisition debt, creating vulnerability if natural gas prices decline or customer utilization rates fall [2]. Profit margins of 5.84% and ROE of 5.77% are thin for a company trading at 89.9x trailing earnings, meaning profitability needs significant improvement to justify the valuation without continued growth [2]. The trailing PE of 89.9x vs forward PE of 26.43x implies heavy reliance on forward earnings acceleration from the Power Infrastructure ramp. The stock declined 18.7% in July 2026 from $75.31 to $61.10, indicating market concern about execution or macro factors [2]. Long-term secular risk from energy transition could reduce natural gas demand over a 10+ year horizon, though near-term demand is supported by AI data center buildouts.
Key Catalysts
  1. Power Infrastructure segment scaling beyond $125M in revenue as AI data center demand accelerates, with 60-70% margins contributing meaningfully to earnings growth and diversifying the revenue base [1]
  2. Compression fleet expansion and utilization improvements driving organic EBITDA growth toward the $820-860M 2026 guidance range, with potential for further guidance increases as DPS synergies mature [1]
  3. Natural gas demand growth from LNG export capacity expansion, petrochemical facility buildouts, and AI data center electricity demand creating a structural multi-year tailwind for compression services
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 238.24% creates refinancing and interest rate risk — if natural gas demand softens or customer defaults increase, the heavily leveraged balance sheet amplifies downside [2]
  2. Thin profitability (5.84% margin, 5.77% ROE) relative to 89.9x trailing PE means any slowdown in growth trajectory could trigger severe multiple compression from the $61.10 level [2]
  3. DPS acquisition integration and power infrastructure capacity buildout execution risk — the Power segment must prove commercial viability at scale while the stock has already declined 18.7% in July
