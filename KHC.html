Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Packaged Foods ·NASDAQ ·United States
The Kraft Heinz Company is the third-largest food and beverage company in North America and the fifth-largest in the world, with iconic brands including Heinz, Kraft, Oscar Mayer, Philadelphia, Lunchables, Velveeta, Planters, Jell-O, and Maxwell House. Formed from the 2015 merger of Kraft Foods Group and H.J. Heinz Company, backed by 3G Capital and Berkshire Hathaway. Q2 2026 revenue of $6.26B beat consensus with adjusted EPS of $0.56 vs. $0.53 estimate. Full-year 2026 organic sales guided to -0.5% to -2.0%, adjusted EPS $2.03-$2.09. CEO Carlos Abrams-Rivera leading turnaround with $600M+ AI/technology investment. Market cap ~$26B.

Investment Thesis
Kraft Heinz reported Q2 2026 revenue of $6.26B, beating the $6.12B consensus, with adjusted EPS of $0.56 vs. $0.53 expected — extending the streak to five consecutive quarters of consensus EPS beats [1]. Full-year organic sales guidance was narrowed to -0.5% to -2.0% (from -1.5% to -3.5%), signaling stabilization, and adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $2.03-$2.09. Emerging Markets grew 10.4% organically, providing a growth offset to North America pressure. At 10.6x forward earnings, KHC trades at a significant discount to the CPG sector average of ~20x, offering deep value if the brand reinvestment program succeeds. The 7.21% dividend yield with conservative D/E of 53% and $600M+ AI/technology investment provide a floor [4]. The balance sheet remains stable with impairment charges being non-cash items masking adjusted profitability.
The bear case is substantial: organic net sales have declined for multiple consecutive quarters, driven by a fundamental consumer shift away from legacy processed food brands toward fresher, healthier, and private-label alternatives [1]. The dividend has been frozen at $1.60 annually for seven consecutive years since the 2019 cut from $2.50, offering 0% growth that erodes real income value every year despite the 7.21% current yield [4]. GAAP earnings remain deeply negative (-13.6% profit margin, -8.8% ROE) due to ongoing goodwill impairment charges reflecting the destruction of value from the overpriced 2015 merger. Three CEO transitions since 2019 suggest the turnaround strategy lacks continuity. The 73% adjusted payout ratio leaves minimal cushion if earnings deteriorate further. Competition from private-label products exceeding 20% share in many KHC categories is structural and accelerating. North America remains under pressure with pricing tailwinds of 1.3% offset by a 2.6% volume/mix drag. AI Risk Protection (7/9): Kraft Heinz's core business — manufacturing, distributing, and marketing physical food products — is structurally immune to AI substitution [1]. AI cannot make ketchup, distribute condiments, or replicate century-old brand equity. The company's value chain involves physical-world operations (food processing plants, cold chain logistics, retail distribution) that AI cannot displace. Consumer preference for trusted food brands provides an additional moat.
