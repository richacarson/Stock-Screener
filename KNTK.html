Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Kinetik Holdings is a fully integrated, pure-play, Permian-to-Gulf Coast midstream C-corporation operating in the Texas Delaware Basin. The company provides gathering, transportation, compression, processing, and treating services for natural gas, NGLs, crude oil, and water. With over 3,500 miles of steel gathering lines and approximately 2.2 Bcf/d of processing capacity, Kinetik is the largest midstream operator in the Delaware Basin. Revenue grew 19% to $1.76B in 2025, and Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was $251.2M.

Investment Thesis
Kinetik offers a 7.14% dividend yield backed by the dominant midstream infrastructure position in the Permian Basin's Delaware sub-basin — 3,500+ miles of gathering lines and 2.2 Bcf/d processing capacity with long-term producer contracts [4]. Revenue grew 19% to $1.76B in 2025, and Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $251.2M and distributable cash flow of $180.8M demonstrate strong cash generation capacity despite a GAAP net loss [1]. AI-driven data center electricity demand and LNG export growth are structural tailwinds for Permian gas infrastructure. CEO Welch's 6% ownership stake ($412M) provides significant shareholder alignment [6]. The forward P/E of 21.66x and consensus 2026 revenue of $1.97B imply continued growth from Delaware Basin development [2].
The bear case centers on GAAP earnings deterioration, leverage, and commodity sensitivity. Q1 2026 posted a $5.1M net loss, and full-year 2026 EPS estimates have been cut 33% from $1.93 to $1.29 over 90 days, inflating the trailing P/E to 52.23x [1][2]. The GAAP payout ratio now exceeds 350% on trailing earnings — while dividends are comfortably covered by distributable cash flow, GAAP earnings volatility driven by non-cash charges and commodity price swings creates headline risk. D/E of 143% limits balance sheet flexibility, and ROE has fallen to 6.57% on a TTM basis as heavy asset depreciation and acquisition amortization weigh on returns [2]. Customer concentration in the Delaware Basin creates binary risk — any producer capital discipline slowdown or sustained natural gas price weakness below $2/MMBtu could reduce throughput volumes across Kinetik's entire system.
