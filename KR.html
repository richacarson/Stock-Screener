Full-year FY2026 EPS guidance maintained at $5.10-$5.30, though same-store sales growth forecast was lowered to 0.2%-0.8%. The company also operates the 84.51° data analytics platform and a growing retail media business.

Investment Thesis
Kroger at $64.72 with a $38B market cap is America's largest pure-play grocer trading at a compelling 11.76x forward earnings with a 17-year dividend growth streak and ~12% 5-year dividend CAGR [1]. Q2 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.09 (+5% YoY) beat consensus, full-year guidance of $5.10-$5.30 EPS is maintained, and the 84.51° data analytics platform is building an alternative profit engine in retail media estimated at $1B+ annually. The company's AI strategy — Chief Data and AI Officer, associate AI assistant, shrink reduction AI, agentic shopping development — positions Kroger as a technology leader among traditional grocers [2]. The post-Albertsons balance sheet reset allows capital to flow toward buybacks, dividends, and organic investment rather than merger integration.
The bear case centers on structural growth limitations and balance sheet strain. Same-store sales growth guidance was cut to 0.2%-0.8% from 1%-2%, reflecting a challenging consumer environment and the inherently low-growth nature of grocery retail [1]. D/E of 413% reflects significant leverage, and trailing profit margin of just 0.73% (depressed by Albertsons-related charges) illustrates grocery's razor-thin economics even at $150B in revenue. Revenue growth of 2.0% YoY is well below the 10% target, and the 5-year revenue CAGR is similarly modest at approximately 2-3%. The Inspire Impact Score of -83 (MISALIGNED) with negative attributions for Abortifacients, Abortion Activism, and LGBT Activism may exclude Kroger from faith-based mandates. CEO McMullen's March 2025 departure for conduct issues, ongoing Albertsons litigation costs, and Carl Icahn's ESG criticism over animal welfare and worker wages create governance overhang [2][3]. Amazon's continued grocery expansion with AI-driven operations represents a long-term competitive threat to traditional grocery economics.
