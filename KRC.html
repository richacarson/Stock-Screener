Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Kilroy Realty Corporation is a premier West Coast real estate investment trust focused on innovation-centric office, life science, and mixed-use properties in Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego, Seattle, and Austin. Under CEO Angela Aman (appointed January 2024), Kilroy has pivoted toward disciplined capital recycling, selling $443M of non-core assets to fund the $205M Maple Plaza acquisition in supply-constrained Beverly Hills. Q2 2026 revenue was $272.4M with FFO of $0.92 per share, and occupancy stood at 77.0% (81.5% excluding KOPT). The company raised 2026 FFO guidance to $3.49-$3.63 per share and reported its highest Q1 leasing volume since 2017, driven by AI company demand in San Francisco.

Investment Thesis
Kilroy Realty offers a 5.56% dividend yield with an emerging AI-driven West Coast office recovery narrative — Q1 2026 delivered the highest leasing volume since 2017, and San Francisco AI company demand hit post-pandemic highs with Kilroy's SOMA assets seeing 170% YoY tour activity growth [1][3]. The raised FFO guidance of $3.49-$3.63 per share implies a P/FFO of approximately 11.3x, attractive relative to the REIT sector average. CEO Angela Aman's disciplined capital recycling — $443M in asset sales funding the strategic Maple Plaza acquisition in Beverly Hills — is improving portfolio quality and positioning the company for the 'flight to quality' trend in West Coast office markets. 29 consecutive years of dividend payments provide income reliability, and AI immunity (score 8/10) means the business benefits from, rather than faces risk from, AI growth.
The bear case is anchored in the structural challenges facing West Coast office REITs. Occupancy of 77.0% (81.5% excluding KOPT) remains well below pre-pandemic levels, and revenue declined 6% YoY in Q2 2026, reflecting ongoing tenant attrition and sublease competition [1]. FFO declined 20% from Q2 2025 despite raised full-year guidance, suggesting the recovery is uneven. The 79.76% payout ratio approaches unsustainable levels if earnings continue declining, and the 0.76% 5-year dividend growth CAGR barely exceeds zero — the dividend has been essentially flat for five years despite inflation eroding its real value [5]. The forward P/E of 51.36 (GAAP-based, though P/FFO is more appropriate at 11.3x) reflects market skepticism about future GAAP earnings. Remote and hybrid work adoption remains a secular headwind for office REITs, and a recession would compound tenant losses in an already weak occupancy environment. The San Francisco AI demand narrative is encouraging but concentrated — a pullback in AI capital spending could quickly reverse the tour activity growth that underpins the bull case.
