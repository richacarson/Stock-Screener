Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Kilroy Realty is a West Coast office and life science REIT with operations concentrated in San Francisco, Los Angeles, San Diego, Seattle, and Austin. The company owns and develops premier office and life science campuses, and is actively repositioning legacy office assets — including flagship 100 First Street in San Francisco — to capture demand from AI-focused tenants.

Investment Thesis
Kilroy Realty is showing genuine operational momentum: Q1 2026 leasing of 568,000 sq ft was the strongest first quarter since 2017 (more than double the prior year), management raised full-year 2026 FFO guidance to $3.49-$3.63/share, and same-property cash NOI guidance improved by 150bps at the midpoint [1]. The company is uniquely positioned to benefit from AI-driven office demand — the San Francisco Bay Area, now 44% of NOI, is seeing AI labs and tech tenants drive leasing activity and rent growth, and Kilroy is repositioning flagship assets like 100 First Street specifically to capture this demand [2]. A 5.37% dividend yield backed by 27 consecutive years of payments offers meaningful income, and new CEO Angela Aman has moved decisively on leadership restructuring and $350M+ of non-core dispositions to sharpen the portfolio [1][4].
The bear case centers on REIT-specific financial strain: the payout ratio of 118.0% breaches the mechanical floor entirely, and the quarterly dividend has been frozen at $0.54/share since Q3 2022 (5yr dividend growth CAGR of just ~1.15%), suggesting limited room for increases and latent cut risk if NOI growth stalls [1]. ROE of 4.4% is well below the 15% target and revenue has been roughly flat for three years running (~0.5% CAGR), reflecting the broader West Coast office sector's structural challenges — occupancy still sits in the high-70s to low-80s percent range even after the leasing rebound. Beyond company-specific execution, analysts continue to flag a broader 'AI disruption narrative' around remote work reducing aggregate office demand, a sentiment overhang on valuations that could persist even as Kilroy's specific AI-tenant thesis plays out [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued AI-driven leasing momentum in San Francisco (44% of NOI) as AI labs and tech tenants compete for scarce large-block space, supporting rent growth and occupancy gains
  2. 100 First Street and other flagship repositioning projects converting AI-tenant demand into signed leases and NOI growth ahead of 2026-2027 guidance
  3. Continued non-core asset disposition ($350M+ YTD) and balance sheet discipline under CEO Angela Aman, funding the 1900 Broadway development and further portfolio sharpening
Key Risks
  1. Payout ratio of 118.0% leaves no margin of safety on the dividend; a flat quarterly rate since Q3 2022 and any NOI setback could force a cut
  2. ROE of 4.4% and roughly flat multi-year revenue growth reflect a West Coast office sector still working through secular demand challenges despite the recent leasing rebound
  3. Analyst-flagged 'AI disruption narrative' around remote work reducing aggregate office space needs could offset company-specific AI-tenant demand gains and keep valuations compressed
