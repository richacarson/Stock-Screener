Karman Holdings Inc. (Karman Space & Defense) designs, tests, manufactures, and sells mission-critical systems for hypersonics, strategic missile defense, tactical missile, space, and launch vehicle programs, including payload protection and deployment systems, aerodynamic interstage systems, and propulsion systems. Founded in 2020 (as a vertically integrated roll-up of legacy aerospace suppliers backed by Trive Capital), headquartered in Huntington Beach, California, and public since a February 2025 IPO on the NYSE.

Investment Thesis
Karman is a pure-play beneficiary of generational growth in missile defense, hypersonics, and space-launch spending: Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 51% YoY to $151.2M with record adjusted EBITDA of $44.8M (29.6% margin), full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $720-735M (~54% growth), and total backlog reached a record $1.0B, up 61% YoY [1]. The active pipeline nearly tripled from ~$1B to ~$3B between March and May 2026 across hypersonics, tactical missiles, maritime, and space/launch programs, and the January 2026 Seemann Composites and MSC acquisitions extend the company into maritime defense composites — a genuine new growth vertical layered onto an already-accelerating core business [2]. The stock has already corrected sharply (down from a 52-week high of $118.38 to $46.17, near its 52-week low of $44.00), which has compressed the valuation considerably even as the fundamental growth story has strengthened [1].
The bear case centers on leverage, thin GAAP profitability, and execution risk under a brand-new leadership team. D/E of 213.7% reflects real debt taken on to fund the maritime defense acquisitions, ROE of 7.9% and profit margin of 5.7% are both well below Infrastructure targets despite the explosive top line, and free cash flow is negative (-$40.1M), meaning the growth is not yet self-funding [1]. CEO Jon Rambeau took over only in March 2026 and the company's first Chief AI/Information Officer started in June 2026 — analysts explicitly cite 'execution by a relatively new leadership team' as a key risk in converting the roughly $3B pipeline and $1B+ in contingent multi-year customer commitments into funded, delivered backlog [3]. As a still-recent, PE-backed IPO (Trive Capital), the stock also carries overhang risk from potential secondary offerings as lock-ups continue to unwind.
Key Catalysts
  1. Converting the ~$3B active pipeline and $1B+ in contingent multi-year customer commitments into funded backlog, validating the growth trajectory beyond the current $1.0B record backlog
  2. Successful integration of the Seemann Composites and MSC acquisitions, establishing Karman's maritime defense vertical alongside its core hypersonics/missile-defense/space-launch business
  3. Continued U.S. and allied defense-budget tailwinds (missile stockpile replenishment, hypersonics investment) sustaining the 50%+ revenue growth trajectory guided for FY2026
Key Risks
  1. Execution risk under a brand-new leadership team (CEO since March 2026, first Chief AI Officer since June 2026) converting a rapidly expanding pipeline and contingent commitments into delivered, funded contracts
  2. Leverage and cash-flow strain — 213.7% D/E and -$40.1M free cash flow leave limited cushion if acquisition integration costs run high or defense program timing slips
  3. Valuation remains demanding even after the sharp pullback from 52-week highs — at 200.7x trailing and 49.9x forward earnings, any guidance miss could trigger further multiple compression
