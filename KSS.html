Consumer Discretionary ·Department Stores ·NYSE ·United States

Investment Thesis
Kohl's trades at a deeply discounted 10.47x forward earnings with a 2.79% dividend yield, reflecting severe market skepticism about the department store turnaround [4]. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $3.32B (-0.9% YoY) and EPS of $1.28 showed sequential improvement from Q1's -1.7% revenue decline, but the margin surge was driven by $150M in one-time tariff refunds — gross margin excluding refunds rose only 5 basis points [1][2]. The Sephora partnership ($2B+ annual sales, expanding to 850+ locations with prestige brands) is the strongest asset, providing differentiated foot traffic and higher-margin beauty revenue that off-price competitors cannot easily replicate. Updated FY2026 guidance (sales flat to -1.5%, adjusted operating margin 3.5-4.0%, EPS $1.80-$2.40) suggests stabilization, not growth [2].
The bear case is structural and severe. Department stores face secular decline as consumers shift to off-price retailers (TJX), fast fashion, and Amazon, with AI-powered personalization accelerating this migration. Revenue has declined at a -2.35% 5yr CAGR with no inflection point visible [4]. Net profit margin of 0.75% leaves essentially no buffer against further deterioration — one bad quarter could swing to losses. The CEO crisis — three leadership changes in under two years culminating in an interim CEO — creates strategic paralysis at the worst possible time [3]. D/E of 164% limits financial flexibility as interest rates remain elevated, and the dividend has already been cut from $1.00 to $0.50 annually with further cuts possible if cash flow deteriorates. The stock's 125% rally from 52-week lows may have priced in turnaround hopes that the fundamentals do not yet support.
