Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Carriers ·NYSE ·South Korea
KT Corporation is South Korea's largest fixed-line telecommunications provider and second-largest mobile operator. Originally established as Korea Telecom in 1981, KT provides wired and wireless telecom services, internet, and IPTV across South Korea. The company is undergoing a strategic transformation into an AICT (AI, Information, and Communications Technology) platform company, investing 18 trillion won ($12B) in AI-driven services under new CEO Park Yun-young. KT Cloud, enterprise AI solutions, and the SOTA K AI system developed with Microsoft represent the company's AI pivot.
23,000 Employees ·corp.kt.com

Investment Thesis
KT Corporation offers deep value characteristics with a trailing P/E of 8.22, forward P/E of 9.79, and a 4.47% dividend yield backed by a sustainable 37.5% payout ratio and 13.2% five-year dividend growth CAGR [1][6]. The 2025 fiscal year delivered strong results with revenue up 6.86% to KRW 28.24 trillion and earnings surging 268.67%, though the latter was partly driven by one-off items. KT's strategic pivot to AICT under new CEO Park Yun-young, anchored by an 18 trillion won ($12B) AI investment and the SOTA K AI platform developed with Microsoft, positions the company at the intersection of Korea's telecom infrastructure and the rapidly growing enterprise AI market [3][4][5].
The primary concern is execution risk on the AICT transformation. KT has historically been a slow-moving former state monopoly, and the ambitious AI pivot requires capabilities (cloud computing, AI model development, enterprise software sales) that are fundamentally different from traditional telecom operations. The CEO transition creates leadership uncertainty, and the 18 trillion won investment plan must deliver tangible returns within 3-5 years to justify the capital allocation. Revenue growth is forecast at only 1.7% for 2026, suggesting the AI pivot has not yet translated into accelerating top-line growth [1]. Geopolitical risk from the Korean peninsula, competitive pressure from SK Telecom and LG U+ pursuing similar AI strategies, and the Inspire Insight MISALIGNED rating for sexually explicit content create additional headwinds for faith-aligned investors.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): KT's core business -- physical telecom infrastructure including fiber networks, cell towers, spectrum licenses, and data centers -- is structurally immune to AI substitution [1]. These are essential physical assets that AI cannot replicate. The regulatory environment in South Korea's telecom market, including spectrum licensing and interconnection requirements, creates additional moats. AI is actually an enabler for KT's business: AI data centers need more connectivity, and enterprise AI deployments require robust network infrastructure. KT's position as a national telecom provider with deep government relationships and critical infrastructure designation further insulates the business. Some peripheral services (customer support, content delivery) face AI-driven efficiency pressure, but these are not core revenue drivers. AI Opportunity (1/1): KT is generating revenue from AI-related services through KT Cloud, SOTA K enterprise deployments, and B2B AX consulting services [3][4]. The 18 trillion won AI investment plan signals management's view that AI is a revenue opportunity, not a threat.
