Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NMS ·United States
Kratos Defense & Security Solutions is a technology-focused defense company operating through Kratos Government Solutions (satellite communications, microwave electronics, hypersonic systems) and Unmanned Systems (tactical drones, target drones, autonomous systems) segments. The company differentiates through affordable, high-volume production of military systems rather than the cost-plus model of traditional defense primes. FY2026 revenue is guided to $1.75-1.81B with ~18-23% organic growth, Q2 FY2026 delivered $458.8M in revenue, and consolidated backlog stands at $2.084B with a $15B bid pipeline.
4,300 Employees ·www.kratosdefense.com

Investment Thesis
Kratos at $38.74 with a $7.3B market cap offers pure-play exposure to the two most transformative trends in defense — the shift from manned to unmanned systems and the proliferation of hypersonic weapons — with 30.5% revenue growth, $2.084B backlog, a $15B bid pipeline, and FY2026 revenue guided to $1.75-1.81B at 18-23% organic growth [1][2]. The company's affordable manufacturing model (tactical drones under $10M, jet engines at $15K) positions it to win in a budget-constrained DoD environment shifting toward attritable, mass-producible systems rather than exquisite platforms [3]. CEO DeMarco's 22-year tenure provides exceptional strategic continuity, and the TTM book-to-bill ratio of 1.3x with consecutive record order quarters signals accelerating demand. Kratos is essentially debt-free at 5.65% D/E, and the Inspire Impact score of 54 (ALIGNED) is among the strongest in the defense sector [1].
The fundamental challenge is profitability — Kratos operates at a 2% profit margin with Q2 FY2026 posting an operating loss of $1.6M, and the trailing P/E of 228x makes the stock uninvestable on current earnings [1]. The investment thesis requires faith that revenue scale will drive margin expansion, but the defense contracting model (cost-plus and firm-fixed-price) structurally limits profitability versus commercial businesses. Forward P/E of 34.35 and PEG of 36.41 are demanding even on optimistic earnings estimates, and the stock trades at $38.74 versus a 50-day SMA of $48.13, reflecting recent sentiment deterioration. Non-cash stock compensation of $16.3M in Q2 alone ($65M+ annualized) significantly dilutes reported earnings and represents real economic cost to shareholders [1]. The company's competitive moat is narrower than it appears — Anduril, Shield AI, and other venture-backed defense startups are pursuing similar affordable autonomous systems with deep funding, and traditional primes (Northrop, General Atomics) are investing aggressively in unmanned systems [3]. Kratos's key-person risk on CEO DeMarco is significant given that the company's purpose and strategy are articulated almost entirely through his personal vision.
Key Catalysts
  1. Hypersonic franchise doubling — Revenue from hypersonic systems expected to approximately double to ~$400M in FY2026, representing the fastest-growing product line and demonstrating Kratos's ability to scale production of advanced weapon systems [5]

  3. Manned-to-unmanned transition — DoD's strategic shift toward attritable, mass-producible autonomous systems (CCA program, loyal wingman concepts) directly aligns with Kratos's Valkyrie platform and manufacturing model, creating a generational growth runway [3][5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Kratos manufactures physical defense hardware — drones, rocket motors, satellite ground systems, microwave electronics, hypersonic vehicles — that cannot be replaced by software or AI models [1][5]. The defense industrial base requires ITAR compliance, security clearances, specialized manufacturing facilities, and decades-long customer relationships with the DoD that create insurmountable barriers to AI disruption. The company's vertically integrated supply chain for mission-critical components adds another structural moat. The only theoretical AI risk is that AI-enabled competitors could design superior unmanned systems faster, but Kratos is itself at the leading edge of AI-enabled autonomy in its drone platforms. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI is a direct enabler of Kratos's core growth thesis — the shift from manned to unmanned combat systems is fundamentally driven by AI autonomy capabilities, and Kratos's tactical drones, target systems, and satellite ground infrastructure all become more valuable as AI enables higher levels of autonomous operation [3][5]. The company's orbital data centers for AI-driven satellite data processing represent a direct AI revenue stream. The 'generational recapitalization' DeMarco describes is partly an AI-enabled transformation of warfare.
