Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYSE American ·United States
KULR Technology Group designs and builds advanced battery systems and thermal management solutions for autonomous platforms, digital infrastructure, e-mobility, and space applications. The company leverages a 40-year heritage in carbon fiber thermal management technology derived from NASA research, offering battery production, thermal runaway shield technology, the KULR Vibe cell screening system, and the KULR ONE battery platform. KULR also maintains a Bitcoin treasury strategy with approximately $60M in BTC holdings.

Investment Thesis
KULR Technology offers speculative upside through its battery safety and thermal management platform, which delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $4.8M (up 98% YoY) with gross margin expanding sharply from 8% to 29% [1]. FY2025 revenue grew 51% to $16.2M, and the five-year Caban Energy battery supply agreement ($30M estimated revenue), KULR ONE automated production line targeting 10,000 drone packs per month, and $100M AI Server Rack Battery Backup Unit JV provide a credible growth pipeline [1][3]. The minimal D/E of 4% means the balance sheet is not over-leveraged on traditional debt, and at $221M market cap the stock is priced for a meaningful re-rating if the company achieves profitability.
The bear case is substantial. KULR posted a net loss of $28.1M in Q1 2026 alone on $4.8M revenue, and FY2025 losses totaled $61.9M on $16.2M revenue — a -383% net margin that makes profitability a distant prospect [1][2]. The company massively missed its 2024 revenue guidance ($10.7M actual vs. $26-34M projected), destroying management credibility on forward targets [4]. The Bitcoin treasury strategy ($60M in BTC representing 90% of cash reserves) introduces speculative cryptocurrency volatility to an already fragile balance sheet. Grizzly Research alleged revenue inflation through licensing agreement pull-forwards and misrepresentation of DoD contract relationships [4]. A class action insider trading lawsuit is pending. R&D spending declined 28% YoY even as the company attempts to scale new product lines [1]. With deeply negative earnings, governance concerns, and execution risk on multiple simultaneous growth initiatives, KULR carries substantially more downside risk than the growth narrative suggests.
