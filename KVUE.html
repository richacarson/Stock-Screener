Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Household & Personal Products ·NYSE ·United States
Kenvue is the world's largest pure-play consumer health company, spun off from Johnson & Johnson in May 2023. It operates iconic brands including Tylenol, Listerine, Band-Aid, Aveeno, and Neutrogena across Self Care, Skin Health, and Essential Health segments. In 2026, the board fired CEO Thibaut Mongon and appointed Kirk Perry as interim CEO, launching a strategic review to identify a permanent CEO and redefine the company's growth strategy.

Investment Thesis
Kenvue's financial fundamentals are improving despite leadership turmoil. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.32 (up 33% YoY) and Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.31 (up 7% YoY) demonstrate margin expansion, with net margin improving to 10.6% [1]. The dividend payout ratio improved dramatically from 109% to 65%, making the $0.84 annual dividend sustainable at a 3.9% yield [5]. At 13.4x forward earnings, Kenvue offers a defensive consumer health portfolio at a discount to consumer staples peers (P&G at ~25x, Unilever at ~19x). The stock's 25% appreciation from $17 to $21 reflects market confidence in the operational improvement trajectory. Analyst projections of $1.16 EPS in FY2026 (up 7.4%) and $1.23 in FY2027 (up 6%) suggest steady earnings growth. Activist Starboard Value's $55.6M share purchase provides an external catalyst for value creation [3].
The bear case centers on leadership instability and organic growth weakness. CEO Mongon was fired in 2026, CFO departed, and a 3.5% workforce reduction was executed — all within three years of the J&J spinoff, signaling a company struggling to find its independent identity [3]. Organic growth of just 0.7-1.6% in H1 2026 is anemic for a consumer health leader, driven primarily by price increases rather than volume. The Kimberly-Clark merger status is uncertain amid the strategic review, and if it proceeds, integrating two $15B+ companies carries significant execution risk and management distraction. Talc litigation legacy from J&J continues to create legal overhang — UK legal action involving ~3,000 claimants names both J&J and Kenvue [4]. The 3-year dividend history provides no track record comfort for income investors, and a dividend cut — while less likely with the improved 65% payout ratio — cannot be ruled out if organic growth stalls.
Key Catalysts
  1. New permanent CEO appointment following strategic review — bringing experienced consumer goods leadership could catalyze strategic clarity and operational acceleration
  2. Margin expansion trajectory continuing with Q1-Q2 2026 EPS growth of 7-33% YoY, supported by cost restructuring savings and improving payout ratio from 109% to 65%
  3. Resolution of Kimberly-Clark merger status — either completion unlocking cost synergies or formal withdrawal allowing management to focus on organic brand investment
Key Risks
  1. Leadership instability — CEO firing, CFO departure, and ongoing search for permanent CEO create strategic uncertainty and potential talent flight across the 21,780-person organization
  2. Anemic organic growth of 0.7-1.6% driven by pricing rather than volume suggests weakening brand pull and competitive pressure from private label alternatives
  3. Talc litigation legacy — UK legal action by ~3,000 claimants naming Kenvue alongside J&J creates ongoing legal and reputational liability that could resurface in other jurisdictions
