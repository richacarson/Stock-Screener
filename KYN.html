Company Profile
Financial Services ·Closed-End Fund - Equity ·NYSE ·United States
Kayne Anderson Energy Infrastructure Fund is a non-diversified, closed-end fund investing primarily in midstream energy infrastructure companies. Since inception in 2004, it focuses on delivering high after-tax total returns with emphasis on monthly cash distributions. Portfolio is approximately 95% midstream energy, 3% power infrastructure, and 2% other. Net assets of $2.7 billion as of June 30, 2026. Managed by Kayne Anderson Capital Advisors, L.P.

Investment Thesis
KYN offers compelling access to midstream energy infrastructure through a leveraged closed-end fund structure trading at an approximately 17% discount to its $16.02 NAV as of June 30, 2026 [1]. The fund's $2.7 billion portfolio of predominantly midstream assets is positioned to benefit from two secular tailwinds: AI-driven data center buildouts consuming record natural gas and electricity volumes, and the energy security imperative supporting domestic infrastructure investment. With 8.1% distribution yield, recently increased to $0.09/month (5.9% raise), and management targeting 10-15% total annual returns through a combination of 4-6% dividend yields, 5-7% cash flow growth, and 0-3% excess free cash flow from portfolio holdings, KYN provides an attractive income vehicle [2]. The exceptionally strong insider buying — 18 purchases and zero sales across four executives in the past year totaling over $1 million — signals deep management conviction in the fund's undervaluation [5].
The primary risk is KYN's structural vulnerability to energy commodity cycles and midstream sector-specific headwinds — the fund's 95% concentration in midstream energy creates zero diversification, and a sustained downturn in natural gas demand or production could compress the NAV and force distribution cuts as occurred during the 2020 oil crash [2]. The 3.3% expense ratio (including leverage costs) is elevated relative to passive midstream ETFs like AMLP (0.85%), creating a meaningful performance drag that management must overcome through security selection and leverage alpha. The persistent 11-17% discount to NAV, while representing a buying opportunity, also signals structural market skepticism about the CEF format's fee structure and governance. Several core financial metrics (revenue growth, profit margin) are not calculable in traditional terms for a closed-end fund, limiting the quantitative rigor of the Infrastructure assessment and potentially inflating the score by excluding weak metrics [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center buildout driving record natural gas demand growth, directly increasing throughput volumes and distributable cash flow for KYN's midstream portfolio holdings
  2. Persistent 11-17% discount to NAV combined with 18 insider purchases and zero sells creates a compelling setup for NAV convergence through share buybacks or market recognition
  3. Distribution growth trajectory accelerating from $0.085 to $0.09 per month with potential for further increases as midstream free cash flow generation reaches record levels
Key Risks
  1. 95% concentration in midstream energy provides zero sector diversification — a sustained commodity downturn or natural gas demand contraction could compress NAV and force distribution cuts, as occurred during the 2020 oil crash when monthly distributions were reduced by more than 50%
  2. 3.3% total expense ratio (including leverage costs) creates a substantial performance drag relative to passive midstream ETFs, requiring consistent alpha generation from security selection to justify the cost structure
  3. Closed-end fund structure exposes investors to discount volatility independent of portfolio performance — the NAV discount can widen during risk-off periods, amplifying losses beyond underlying portfolio declines
