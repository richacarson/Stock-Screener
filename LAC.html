Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NYSE ·Canada
Lithium Americas Corp. is a pre-production development-stage company building Thacker Pass in northern Nevada, projected to be the largest lithium resource in the United States. Phase 1 targets 40,000 tonnes per year of battery-quality lithium carbonate, with detailed engineering over 95% complete, procurement over 80% complete, and mechanical completion targeted for late 2027 ahead of a 2028 production ramp. The project is a joint venture with General Motors (62% Lithium Americas / 38% GM) and is financed in part by a $2.23B U.S. Department of Energy loan, with a $432M second advance drawn in 2026.

Investment Thesis
Lithium Americas offers leveraged exposure to a re-accelerating domestic lithium supply chain thesis: Thacker Pass Phase 1 is over 95% engineered and over 80% procured, with $1.8B of $2.93B total capex incurred and mechanical completion targeted for late 2027 ahead of a 2028 production ramp to 40,000 tonnes per year [1][2]. The project is de-risked by a General Motors joint venture (38% GM ownership with an offtake relationship) and a $2.23B DOE loan (with a $432M second advance drawn in 2026), and the balance sheet carries ~$1.21B in cash and restricted cash against a 7.4 current ratio, providing runway through the remaining build [1][2]. As a physical mining operation, the business is structurally immune to AI disruption and stands to benefit indirectly from AI-driven electrification and EV demand trends.
The bear case is straightforward: this is a single-asset, pre-revenue company with -32.03% ROE and -$646.98M trailing free cash flow, entirely dependent on Thacker Pass reaching production on schedule and on budget [2]. Three tribes (Reno-Sparks Indian Colony, Burns Paiute, Summit Lake Paiute) maintain active litigation alleging inadequate consultation, and a federal judge already found the BLM 'could have done more' to engage affected tribes — creating ongoing legal and reputational overhang even after prior rulings favored the company [3]. Construction-stage capital projects of this scale carry meaningful cost-overrun and schedule-slip risk, and the company has no revenue diversification to cushion a delay. The stock is speculative and appropriate only for investors comfortable with binary, execution-dependent outcomes tied to a single mine reaching commercial production in 2028.
Key Catalysts
  1. Mechanical completion of Thacker Pass Phase 1 targeted for late 2027, followed by a 2028 production ramp to 40,000 tonnes per year of battery-grade lithium carbonate, would convert the company from pre-revenue developer to producer [1][2]
  2. Continued DOE loan advances (following the $432M second advance in 2026) reduce near-term equity dilution risk and confirm continued federal government support for the project [2]
  3. A sustained recovery in global lithium prices from current depressed levels would materially improve the economics of Phase 1 and any future Phase 2 expansion
Key Risks
  1. Active tribal litigation (Reno-Sparks Indian Colony, Burns Paiute, Summit Lake Paiute) challenging the adequacy of BLM consultation creates ongoing legal risk that could delay construction or increase costs [3]
  2. Single-asset concentration: the entire investment thesis depends on Thacker Pass Phase 1 reaching mechanical completion on the targeted late-2027 timeline and ramping successfully in 2028, with no revenue diversification to absorb a delay or cost overrun [1][2]
  3. -32.03% ROE and -$646.98M trailing twelve-month free cash flow reflect a company still years from self-sustaining cash generation, dependent on DOE loan draws and existing cash ($1.21B) to fund the remainder of the ~$2.93B Phase 1 build [1][2]
