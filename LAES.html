Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·Switzerland
SEALSQ Corp designs, manufactures, and sells tamper-resistant secure microcontrollers, post-quantum cryptography (PQC) chips, and digital identity infrastructure for IoT devices, critical infrastructure, and government/defense applications. The QS7001 is the first hardware-embedded post-quantum secure chip, integrating NIST-standardized ML-KEM and ML-DSA algorithms directly in silicon. Spun off from WISeKey in December 2023, the company reported H1 2026 revenue of approximately $11M (+120% YoY), reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of 50-100% growth ($27-36M), and held ~$495M in cash with a >$225M pipeline through 2029. The mid-2025 acquisition of IC'ALPS added approximately 100 ASIC engineers.
185 Employees ·www.sealsq.com

Investment Thesis
SEALSQ is a first-mover in the emerging post-quantum cryptography semiconductor market, with the QS7001 being the industry's first hardware-embedded PQC chip integrating NIST-standardized algorithms directly in silicon [2]. H1 2026 revenue surged 120% YoY to ~$11M with Q1-to-Q2 sequential acceleration ($4M to $7M), and management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of 50-100% revenue growth ($27-36M) backed by a >$225M pipeline through 2029 [1]. With ~$495M cash, minimal debt (D/E 2%), and regulatory tailwinds from France's ANSSI PQC mandate and US CNSA 2.0 requirements, SEALSQ has the financial runway and market positioning to capture the hardware PQC buildout. The appointment of a Chief Quantum Officer and Chief AI Officer, combined with the IC'ALPS acquisition adding ~100 ASIC engineers, positions the company at the convergence of quantum security and AI infrastructure.
The bear case is dominated by dilution and execution risk at extreme valuations. Shares outstanding grew 257% in one year through aggressive equity raises, massively diluting existing shareholders [3]. At ~$505M market cap on $18M TTM revenue (~28x P/S), the stock is priced for significant future growth that remains unproven at scale. Net losses of ~$34M annually with -187% profit margin show the company is far from breakeven, and the ~$495M cash pile was accumulated through shareholder dilution, not business operations. Revenue has been erratic historically — swinging from $30M (FY2023) to $11M (FY2024) to $18M (FY2025) — though H1 2026 shows accelerating traction [1]. Net insider selling (Peter Ward and others sold shares in June 2026) signals limited executive conviction at current price levels [4]. Competition from semiconductor giants NXP, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics with established manufacturing scale and customer relationships poses a serious threat once NIST PQC standards fully mature and larger players integrate PQC into existing product lines. The stock price decline of 44% year-to-date reflects market skepticism about the path to profitability.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (7/9): SEALSQ manufactures physical semiconductor chips — tamper-resistant secure microcontrollers and PQC processors — that AI software structurally cannot displace [2]. AI systems themselves need hardware security; as AI deployments scale into Physical AI, robotics, and autonomous systems, demand for hardware roots of trust increases. The defense and government partnerships (US DoD via Trusted Semiconductor Solutions, ANSSI compliance) add regulatory moats. The post-quantum security thesis is strengthened by AI: more AI-generated data and AI-connected devices means more attack surface requiring hardware-level security. The primary exposure is that AI could accelerate competitor chip design timelines, and larger competitors (NXP, Infineon, STMicroelectronics) with established manufacturing scale could deploy PQC capabilities faster. AI Opportunity (0/1): While SEALSQ has appointed a dedicated Group AI Officer and is developing AI-integrated chipsets at the SEALQuantum lab, no AI products are currently generating quantifiable revenue. The company's PQC chips secure AI infrastructure but are not themselves AI products, and the AI-powered chipsets remain in the development phase.
