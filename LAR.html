Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals — Lithium ·NYSE ·Argentina
Lithium Argentina AG operates the Cauchari-Olaroz lithium brine facility in Jujuy, Argentina, one of the lowest-cost lithium carbonate operations globally. The company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $174.1M (+173% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $110M and a 70% cash operating margin at realized prices of $19,563/t. Production guidance for 2026 is 35,000-40,000 tonnes with potential to exceed 40,000t as debottlenecking progresses. LAR holds a 44.8% interest through its JV with Ganfeng Lithium and is advancing Stage 2 expansion to add 45,000 tpa. Market cap approximately $1.1B. CEO Sam Pigott joined from Ganfeng Lithium.
60 Employees ·www.lithium-argentina.com

Investment Thesis
Lithium Argentina at $6.79 per share offers exposure to one of the lowest-cost lithium carbonate producers globally, with Q2 2026 demonstrating the operational inflection: revenue of $174.1M (+173% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $110M, 70% cash operating margins, and $141M+ free cash flow from operations at realized prices of $19,563/t [1]. The Stage 2 expansion approved under RIGI would more than double capacity to ~80,000 tpa, positioning LAR as a major global lithium supplier as EV adoption and AI-driven battery storage demand accelerate. The conservative 30% D/E ratio provides balance sheet flexibility. At approximately $1.1B market cap with H1 2026 EBITDA exceeding $200M and 2026 EBITDA outlook of $370-640M depending on lithium prices, the stock trades at a deeply discounted EV/EBITDA relative to peers [1].
The bear case centers on commodity price risk, single-asset concentration, and Argentine country risk. Lithium carbonate prices remain volatile and well below 2022-2023 peaks, with sustained prices below $15,000/t potentially eroding margins despite LAR's cost leadership [1]. Trailing ROE of 0.71% reflects the early-stage economics now inflecting, and profit margin of ~5% TTM understates the improving trajectory but remains thin. The Stage 2 expansion carries execution risk including capital cost inflation, construction delays in remote Jujuy province, and the possibility that new global lithium supply from DLE technology projects depresses prices further. The JV structure (44.8% interest with Ganfeng Lithium) limits LAR's share of economic upside. Argentine political risk (currency devaluation, regulatory changes, indigenous rights disputes over water use) is an ongoing overhang [3]. The stock price has risen 170% in 52 weeks, pricing in substantial optimism. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) is neutral for faith-based investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Stage 2 expansion construction progressing under RIGI approval, which would add 45,000 tpa capacity and transform LAR into a ~80,000 tpa producer — one of the largest lithium operations globally [1]
  2. Continued quarterly profitability improvement in H2 2026 as debottlenecking pushes production above 40,000t guidance and realized prices recover, validating sustainable economics [1]

Key Risks
  1. Lithium carbonate price volatility — sustained prices below $15,000/t would erode margins even at LAR's low-cost position, and the EBITDA outlook range of $370M-$640M demonstrates extreme commodity price sensitivity [1]
  2. Single-asset concentration risk — Cauchari-Olaroz is the sole revenue source, making LAR vulnerable to operational disruptions, Argentine regulatory changes, water access disputes, or JV partner disagreements with Ganfeng Lithium [1][3]
  3. Argentine country risk — currency devaluation, political instability, regulatory changes to RIGI or mining concessions, and indigenous community opposition to water-intensive lithium extraction in arid Puna region [3]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Lithium brine extraction is a physical-world operation involving evaporation ponds, chemical processing, and geological resources in remote Argentina. AI structurally cannot displace lithium mining — there is no digital substitute for the physical commodity [1].
