Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NASDAQ ·United States
Laser Photonics Corporation designs, develops, and manufactures industrial laser systems for surface cleaning, rust removal, de-painting, and corrosion control. The company targets disruption of the $46 billion sandblasting and abrasives market with safer, more environmentally friendly laser-based alternatives, serving defense, medical, energy, and industrial sectors. Products include the CleanTech laser cleaning line and MarkStar laser marking systems.

Investment Thesis
Laser Photonics offers high-risk exposure to the disruption of the $46B sandblasting and abrasives market with cleaner, safer laser-based surface cleaning technology. FY2025 revenue surged 144% to $8.3M driven by the CMS acquisition, and deferred revenue/contract liabilities increased to $4.2M at Q1 2026 quarter-end, providing near-term order visibility [1]. The company was selected for a LSAD decontamination project, validating its defense market positioning, and manufacturing consolidation is expected to yield $1M in annual savings [1]. At $1.09/share and $44M market cap, the stock offers significant upside if the company can scale revenue and narrow losses in a massive addressable market.
The bear case is severe and intensifying. Q1 2026 revenue collapsed 60% YoY to $915.6K with a net loss of $2.9M, erasing the growth narrative from FY2025's 144% surge [1]. The company has just $650K cash against $8.9M debt, creating a -$8.3M net cash position that makes dilutive equity raises likely [2]. TTM profit margin of approximately -390% and ROE of -674% reflect a company burning equity at an alarming rate [5]. CEO Wayne Tupuola is on health leave, Glassdoor reviews allege shadow governance with the CEO as a 'figure person,' and employee feedback about 'questionable press releases' raises credibility concerns [4]. The stock barely clears the $1M daily liquidity floor at $1.08M, NASDAQ delisting risk persists at $1.09, and no analyst coverage or institutional validation exists. The path from $8.3M revenue micro-cap to scaled industrial manufacturer requires capital the company does not have.
