Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
nLIGHT designs, manufactures, and sells high-power semiconductor and fiber lasers for directed energy weapons, optical sensing, and advanced manufacturing applications. The company is vertically integrated from semiconductor chip to fiber amplifier to beam-combined laser weapon systems, including the HADES directed energy platform. nLIGHT won a $627M Joint Laser Weapon System (JLWS) contract in July 2026 and delivered record Q2 2026 revenue of $82.6M (+33.8% YoY). Manufacturing facilities in Vancouver/Camas, WA and Longmont, CO (doubled capacity in January 2026). Market cap ~$2.3B.

Investment Thesis
nLIGHT is a vertically integrated defense laser company riding the directed energy weapons wave, with Q2 2026 record revenue of $82.6M (+33.8% YoY) and a landmark $627M JLWS contract ceiling award in July 2026 [1][2]. The HADES 70 kW laser weapon module addresses the military's urgent counter-UAS, C-RAM, and C-Missile needs as AI-enabled drone proliferation accelerates. Near-zero leverage (D/E 3.4%) and doubled manufacturing capacity in Longmont, CO provide the balance sheet and production runway to scale [4][7]. Products revenue grew 45% in Q2 to $59.4M, and the company briefly achieved GAAP profitability in Q1 ($0.6M net income), suggesting an operating leverage inflection as defense revenue scales. At $39.84 (down ~43% from highs), the valuation has compressed significantly from 149.7x forward P/E toward 63.2x, offering a more reasonable entry for a high-growth defense pure-play.
The bear case is substantial: despite 33.8% revenue growth, nLIGHT remains marginally unprofitable (-4.0% profit margin, -3.8% ROE) and Forward P/E of 63.2x still prices in profitability improvement that has not yet consistently materialized [7]. Q3 2026 guidance of $63-73M represents a meaningful sequential decline from Q2's $82.6M record due to ~$17M in supply chain-delayed product revenue, highlighting execution risk and program lumpiness [2]. CEO Scott Keeney sold $25M+ in stock over August-September 2026 and CFO Corso sold $3.9M in May — massive insider liquidation during what should be the company's strongest period raises serious questions about management conviction at current valuations [6]. The JLWS contract ceiling of $627M is aspirational — only $44M is initially funded, and conversion from ceiling to funded backlog depends on DoD appropriations, program milestones, and political priorities that nLIGHT cannot control. Glassdoor reviews describing cultural dysfunction (3.6/5 rating, reports of fear and hostility) suggest organizational challenges that could impair execution at scale [5].

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): nLIGHT's core business — designing and manufacturing physical semiconductor lasers and high-energy laser weapon systems for defense — is structurally immune to AI disruption. Directed energy weapons require physical hardware (semiconductor chips, fiber amplifiers, beam combiners) that AI cannot replicate or substitute [1]. Government defense contracts operate under strict security clearances, ITAR regulations, and long qualification cycles creating impenetrable switching costs. AI may actually increase demand: proliferation of AI-enabled autonomous drones creates greater urgency for counter-UAS laser weapon systems like the HADES 70 kW platform [3]. The company's vertically integrated manufacturing from chip to weapon module adds further physical-world defensibility that no AI system can commoditize. AI Opportunity (0/1): No quantifiable AI-derived revenue. While nLIGHT's lasers are used in semiconductor fabs that produce AI chips (microfabrication segment), the company does not sell AI-specific products or report AI as a revenue driver [2].
