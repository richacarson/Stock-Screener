Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·United States
Lazard, Inc. is a 175-year-old global financial advisory and asset management firm. The Financial Advisory segment provides M&A, capital markets, restructuring, shareholder advisory, and geopolitical advisory services. The Asset Management segment manages equity, fixed income, and alternative strategies for institutional and private clients. CEO Peter Orszag (former White House Budget Director) leads a Lazard 2030 strategy targeting doubled revenue and 10-15% annual TSR.

Investment Thesis
Lazard is a 175-year-old independent advisory franchise delivering accelerating revenue growth — H1 2026 adjusted net revenue of $1.5B with Q2 at $786M beating estimates by 8.5% [1]. At 12.9x forward P/E with 36.4% ROE, the valuation is compelling for a franchise generating record advisory revenue and $254B AUM in asset management. The Lazard 2030 strategy targets doubling revenue with $12.5M per MD (up from $8.9M in 2025), and the 4.8% dividend yield offers attractive income. CEO Orszag's aggressive talent investment and AI strategy position the firm for operating leverage as repositioning spending matures into earnings [1][2].
The bear case centers on margin compression and dividend sustainability. Q2 2026 GAAP net income collapsed 91% to $5M ($0.03 EPS vs $0.52 prior year) as the 69.9% compensation ratio and 22.0% non-compensation ratio produced just 8.1% adjusted operating margin [1]. The firm explicitly acknowledges this as repositioning investment, but there is no guaranteed timeline for margins to recover. The 79% payout ratio is elevated with the dividend flat at $2.00 since 2023 (previously cut from $2.35 in 2020), meaning income investors face real risk of another reduction if M&A activity slows [5]. D/E of 241.7% adds leverage risk, and the 3% 5-year revenue CAGR reflects the cyclical volatility of advisory fees. The company is essentially asking shareholders to accept severely compressed near-term earnings in exchange for a 2030 vision that may or may not materialize.
Key Catalysts
  1. Lazard 2030 operating leverage inflection — as repositioning spending matures and MD productivity rises from $8.9M to $12.5M target, margins should expand meaningfully from the current 8.1% adjusted operating margin [1][2]
  2. Record asset management inflows and growing AUM ($254B) provide a more stable recurring revenue base that reduces earnings cyclicality relative to pure advisory peers
  3. AI-enabled advisory tools deployment under LAM CEO Hogbin's head of AI initiative and board-level expertise from Perplexity CBO Shevelenko could differentiate Lazard's offering and improve per-capita productivity [3]
Key Risks
  1. Margin compression risk — Q2 2026 GAAP earnings collapsed 91% with 69.9% comp ratio, and there is no guaranteed timeline for repositioning investments to translate into sustained margin expansion [1]
  2. Dividend sustainability — 79% payout ratio with the dividend flat at $2.00 since 2023 and a prior cut in 2020 creates real income risk if M&A cyclicality compresses earnings further [5]
  3. M&A cyclical exposure — advisory revenue is inherently volatile, the 3% 5-year revenue CAGR reflects this cyclicality, and a recession or deal drought would directly squeeze the already-thin profit margins

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Lazard's core Financial Advisory business — M&A counsel, restructuring, capital markets, and geopolitical advisory — relies on senior relationship-driven judgment that AI cannot easily replicate. CEO Orszag explicitly notes that 'human capital, judgment, and trust — the core of its operating model — will become even more valuable' in an AI-enabled world [2][3]. AI Opportunity (1/1): Lazard is actively investing in AI capabilities — LAM CEO Hogbin appointed a head of AI, Perplexity CBO Shevelenko sits on the board, and the firm is deploying AI tools to enhance analyst productivity and advisory research [2][3]. While no specific AI revenue is quantified, the organizational investment is meaningful.
