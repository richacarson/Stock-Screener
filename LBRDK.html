Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Liberty Broadband Corporation is a holding company whose primary asset is a 28% equity stake in Charter Communications (CHTR), the second-largest U.S. cable operator. The company also owns GCI, Alaska's largest communications provider, which is being spun off prior to the pending all-stock acquisition by Charter. John Malone holds a controlling interest via supervoting shares. Stockholders approved the Charter acquisition in February 2025, and the company completed a share repurchase program retiring approximately 34% of outstanding shares.

Liberty Broadband shares have rallied to $83.31, reflecting improved Charter Communications fundamentals and narrowing NAV discount as the approved all-stock merger progresses toward closing [1]. The company's financial profile shows P/E of 15x, clean D/E of 46.9%, and a 78.1% profit margin boosted by equity method Charter income. Analyst median target of $97 implies 16% additional upside, and the merger arbitrage spread to implied Charter exchange value provides a valuation floor [4].
The bear case is that Liberty Broadband is a pure derivative of Charter Communications, and Charter faces structural headwinds from fixed wireless access (T-Mobile, Verizon), fiber overbuilders (AT&T, Frontier), and satellite (Starlink) competitors eroding its cable broadband monopoly [1]. The all-stock merger structure means Liberty Broadband shareholders become Charter shareholders — if CHTR shares decline, merger value declines proportionally. A pension fund lawsuit alleging Malone 'engineered the transaction to siphon money from Charter's public investors' creates governance risk [5].
