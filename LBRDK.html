Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Liberty Broadband Corporation is a holding company whose primary asset is an approximately 26% equity stake in Charter Communications (CHTR), the second-largest U.S. cable operator, along with direct ownership of GCI, the largest communications provider in Alaska. The company is part of John Malone's Liberty family of companies and trades at a persistent discount to the net asset value of its Charter holdings. FY2025 GCI standalone revenue was $1.0B (+3% YoY). Consolidated Q2 2026 results (including Charter proportional consolidation) showed revenue of $13.5B with a net loss of $2.1B driven by impairment charges. A pending merger with Charter Communications would simplify the corporate structure. Market cap is approximately $5.2B.

Investment Thesis
Liberty Broadband at 5.4x forward earnings trades at a deep NAV discount to its ~26% Charter Communications stake and direct GCI ownership, creating a sum-of-parts opportunity for patient investors willing to wait for structural simplification. The pending Charter merger would eliminate the holding company discount and create direct ownership value. Charter's broadband infrastructure is a beneficiary of AI-driven demand for connectivity, and GCI's Alaska telecom dominance provides stable revenue [1]. The D/E of 37.9% reflects conservative leverage at the holding company level. FY2025 GCI revenue of $1.0B grew 3% YoY with Adjusted OIBDA margins improving 12% — the subsidiary operations are profitable and stable.
The bear case is substantial and drives the low IOWN score. Liberty Broadband is a pure financial engineering vehicle with no independent operating innovation, no mission beyond shareholder value, and deeply negative financial metrics (ROE -68.4%, profit margin 0.0%) driven by Q2 2026 impairment charges of $2.1B on the Charter stake [1]. The company scores 1/10 on Innovation and 1/10 on Inspiration — it creates no products, serves no higher purpose, and has no organizational culture to evaluate at the holding company level. The persistent NAV discount may never close if the Charter merger is delayed or renegotiated. Charter itself faces secular challenges — video subscriber losses, fixed wireless competition from T-Mobile/Verizon, and Starlink satellite broadband in rural markets. Capital intensity is peaking in 2026 with heavy fiber buildout costs.
