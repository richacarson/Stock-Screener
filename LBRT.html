Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Liberty Energy provides hydraulic fracturing and complementary oilfield services across North American unconventional basins, operating next-generation electric and dual-fuel frac fleets with AI-powered automation. The company is expanding into data center power infrastructure through a 3 GW buildout partnership with SLB. Founded by Chris Wright (now U.S. Energy Secretary) and headquartered in Denver, Colorado, Liberty posted Q3 2026 revenue of $1.20B (up 26% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $142M, though a net loss of $39M reflected drilling-related delays and cost inflation headwinds.

Investment Thesis
Liberty Energy at $16.69 with a $2.7B market cap is an oilfield services company executing a bold dual-strategy: core hydraulic fracturing operations generated Q3 2026 revenue of $1.20B (up 26% YoY, beating estimates of $1.18B) while the nascent data center power business targets 3 GW of capacity by 2029 through a July 2026 SLB alliance [1][2]. Nine-month 2026 revenue of $3.41B represents 15% growth over the prior year, AI-driven fleet automation has cut sand truck needs by 35% and doubled equipment life, and the quarterly dividend was raised 11% to $0.10 per share. The stock trades near its 52-week low of $9.90, down 52% from its high of $34.48, potentially offering contrarian value if oilfield activity stabilizes and the power infrastructure pivot gains traction.
The bear case is substantial and worsening. Q3 2026 delivered a $39M net loss despite strong topline growth, with adjusted EBITDA of $142M missing consensus of $149M, as drilling-related delays and cost inflation compressed margins to just 2.9% profit [1]. Forward P/E of 59.2x reflects severely depressed earnings expectations, and $1.3B in convertible note issuances have increased leverage (D/E 82%) during a period of uncertain oilfield activity. ROE of just 6.1% demonstrates weak capital efficiency in a capital-intensive business. The data center power pivot requires execution capabilities far outside Liberty's core competency and competes against well-capitalized energy infrastructure incumbents. CEO transition from founder Chris Wright to Ron Gusek remains untested through a meaningful downturn, and the company's 52-week range of $9.90-$34.48 reflects extreme volatility tied to commodity price cycles [2][3].
