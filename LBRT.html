Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Liberty Energy is North America's second-largest hydraulic fracturing company, providing integrated oilfield services including hydraulic fracturing, wireline, and related completions services. The company operates conventional and next-generation electric (digiFrac) fracturing fleets and is expanding into distributed power generation services for AI/data centers. Founded in 2011 by Chris Wright (now U.S. Secretary of Energy), with Ron Gusek as CEO since February 2025. Q2 2026 revenue reached $1.2B (+14% YoY), driven by record utilization and power business growth, though net income declined to $43M from $71M in Q2 2025. FY2026 revenue forecast raised to $4.31B.
5,800 Employees ·www.libertyenergy.com

Investment Thesis
Liberty Energy is North America's second-largest hydraulic fracturing company executing a meaningful recovery — Q2 2026 revenue surged to $1.2B (+14% YoY, +16% sequentially) driven by record fleet utilization and growing power services demand, prompting management to raise the FY2026 revenue forecast to $4.31B [1]. The company's differentiated technology platform — digiFrac electric fleets, Cummins natural gas engine partnership, and distributed power generation — positions it at the intersection of energy services and AI infrastructure demand. As U.S. Secretary of Energy Chris Wright advocates for 'massively more energy' to power AI data centers, Liberty's expertise in natural gas-powered operations creates a tailored beneficiary of this secular trend [2]. The balance sheet is manageable (D/E 82%) and the dividend provides a 1.80% yield, while the stock trades near its 52-week low at $26.19, potentially offering contrarian value if drilling activity stabilizes.
The bear case is rooted in cyclical earnings pressure and valuation uncertainty. Despite the revenue recovery, Q2 2026 net income fell 39% YoY to $43M and adjusted EBITDA declined 16% to $151M, indicating pricing compression even as utilization improves [1]. Profit margin has compressed from 5.3% to 2.92% over the past year, and ROE of 6.12% reflects weak capital efficiency in a capital-intensive business [4]. The dividend was cut from approximately $0.17/quarter to $0.09/quarter, undermining the income thesis and signaling management concern about near-term cash flows. Founder Chris Wright's departure to become Secretary of Energy removes the company's most distinctive strategic voice, leaving successor Ron Gusek untested through a commodity downturn [2]. The oilfield services industry faces structural headwinds from upstream capital discipline, potential shifts in U.S. energy policy, and long-term demand uncertainty as the energy transition progresses.
