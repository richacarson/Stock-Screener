Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
LendingClub Corporation (rebranded to Happen, Inc. in June 2026, ticker changed to HAPN) is a digital marketplace bank offering personal loans, auto refinancing, patient financing, and high-yield savings accounts. Originally founded as a peer-to-peer lending platform in 2006, the company acquired Radius Bank in 2021 and transformed into a full-service digital bank. Headquartered in San Francisco, California.

Each pivot required abandoning core assumptions about the business. The willingness to acquire a bank charter, accept regulatory burden, and rebrand entirely demonstrates exceptional flexibility.

Investment Thesis
LendingClub delivered explosive growth in Q1 2026: originations of $2.67B (+31% YoY), total net revenue of $252.3M (+16%), and net income of $51.6M (+342% YoY) with ROTCE of 14.5% [1]. The company's AI-driven credit scoring platform creates a data moat — a decade of loan performance data trains ML models that outperform traditional underwriting. At 11.24x forward earnings with full-year 2026 EPS guidance of $1.65-$1.80 and origination guidance of $11.6-12.6B, the stock offers significant growth at a value multiple [4]. The bank charter acquired in 2021 provides deposit funding ($10.2B, +14% YoY) at lower cost than marketplace competitors, while CET1 of 17.0% provides substantial capital buffer [1]. The provision for credit losses collapsed to $0.4M in Q1 2026 (from $58.1M prior year), validating the AI model's credit accuracy.
The bear case centers on credit cycle risk and competitive intensity: LendingClub's personal loan portfolio is concentrated in unsecured consumer credit — the most cyclically sensitive lending category. A recession could rapidly deteriorate credit quality and reverse the near-zero provision environment [1]. The beta of 1.94 reflects this cyclicality. Revenue growth is decelerating (16% in Q1 2026 vs. 27% for full-year 2025), and the Happen rebrand carries execution risk — brand transitions in financial services often cause customer confusion and acquisition cost increases. Competition from SoFi (which also has a bank charter), traditional banks investing in digital lending, and Upstart's AI underwriting creates pricing pressure. The high D/E (673%) reflects bank leverage that amplifies losses in downturns, and the absence of a dividend provides no downside cushion for investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year 2026 origination guidance of $11.6-12.6B (+30%+ YoY) with continued margin expansion as the credit model proves out through economic cycles [1]
  2. Happen rebrand and product expansion beyond personal loans into broader digital banking services, increasing customer lifetime value and cross-sell opportunities
  3. Near-zero provision for credit losses in Q1 2026 ($0.4M vs $58.1M prior year) suggests potential for significant earnings upside if credit quality remains stable [1]
Key Risks
  1. Concentrated exposure to unsecured consumer credit creates severe recession vulnerability — a credit cycle downturn could rapidly reverse the near-zero provision environment and compress earnings [1]
  2. Happen rebrand execution risk — financial services brand transitions often cause customer confusion, increased acquisition costs, and temporary growth deceleration during the transition period
  3. Beta of 1.94 reflects extreme stock price volatility; intense competition from SoFi, Upstart, and traditional bank digital lending programs creates ongoing pricing pressure and customer acquisition cost inflation
