Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Furnishings, Fixtures & Appliances ·NYSE ·United States
Leggett & Platt designs and manufactures engineered components for bedding (innersprings, specialty foam, adjustable bases), furniture (mechanisms, seat suspensions), flooring (carpet cushion, underlayment), and specialty wire products. Founded in 1883 in Carthage, Missouri, the company divested its Aerospace Products Group in 2025 and is now subject to a pending all-stock merger with Somnigroup valued at approximately $2.5B, with shareholder vote scheduled for August 20, 2026. Q1 2026 revenue was $918.2M, down 10% YoY.

Investment Thesis
Leggett & Platt at 10.94x forward earnings is effectively a merger arbitrage play on the pending Somnigroup all-stock acquisition, which has cleared antitrust review (HSR waiting period expired June 3, 2026) and awaits shareholder vote on August 20, 2026 [2][5]. ROE of 27.49% demonstrates efficient capital deployment despite revenue headwinds, and the company remains the dominant U.S. supplier of bedding innerspring components with high switching costs. The Somnigroup combination would create a vertically integrated bedding platform spanning components through finished mattress retail. The Inspire Insight score of 45 (ALIGNED) and low ESG controversy rating provide faith-based investment appeal [4].
The bear case is that Leggett is a structurally declining business nearing the end of its independent existence. Revenue has fallen 28% from $5.15B in 2022 to an annualized ~$3.7B in 2026, with Q1 2026 showing a 10.2% YoY decline and adjusted EBITDA margin compressing to just 7.8% [1]. Profit margin of 5.81% and D/E of 162% with $1.66B in debt leave minimal balance sheet flexibility [5]. The 89% dividend cut destroyed the income thesis that supported the stock for decades, and shareholders will own only 9% of the combined Somnigroup entity — a deeply dilutive outcome [2]. If the merger fails, the standalone company faces continued revenue erosion, no growth strategy, no innovation pipeline, and no AI strategy. The stock has traded from $50+ to $11 in three years, and the declining trajectory shows no organic reversal catalyst.
