Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Lennar, founded in 1954 and based in Miami, is one of the two largest U.S. homebuilders. It builds single-family homes for first-time, move-up, active-adult, and luxury buyers across its East, Central, Texas, and West segments, and it also runs mortgage, title, multifamily, and strategic technology-investment businesses. In fiscal Q3 2026 (ended August 31) it delivered 20,840 homes on $8.05B of revenue (-9% YoY), with home-sale gross margin compressed to 15.8%, and it cut its FY2026 delivery target to 80,000-81,000 homes. Stuart Miller has been sole CEO since co-CEO Jon Jaffe retired at the end of 2025. Market cap is about $21.7B.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Lennar has endured for more than 70 years under the Miller family, a continuity Sinek respects. Its operating philosophy, however, is volume-driven: it keeps production steady and sells through incentives, accepting gross margin falling to 15.8%, and its competitive identity is about scale and efficiency rather than a cause. Employee reviews describing chaotic strategy shifts and technology-leadership churn suggest a weak Circle of Safety. Sinek would see a durable finite competitor rather than a cause-driven infinite player.

Investment Thesis
Lennar is a scale leader trading near book value: 1.0x P/B, about 18x FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.05, D/E of only 19.9%, and 42 years of uninterrupted dividends with a 37.6% payout [3][5]. Q4 FY2026 guidance of 22,000-23,000 deliveries and home-sale gross margin of 15.5-16.0% points to stabilization, and consensus expects EPS to grow from about $4.82 in FY2026 to $6.19 in FY2028 as rates ease [2][3]. The Inspire score of 39 (ALIGNED, including Product Access and Affordability) supports faith-based allocation.
The bear case is a housing downturn that has not bottomed. Q3 FY2026 revenue fell 9%, net earnings fell 52% to $284M, new orders fell 9%, home-sale gross margin dropped to 15.8% from 17.5%, and EPS of $1.19 GAAP ($1.23 adjusted) missed the $1.29 consensus [1][2]. With mortgage rates around 6.8% and rising, management cut FY2026 deliveries to 80,000-81,000, and the volume-over-margin strategy keeps TTM ROE at only 6.0% [2][3]. The dividend has been flat at $0.50 per quarter since 2024, and a Sustainalytics ranking of 82 of 83 homebuilders on ESG risk is a governance flag [5][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage-rate relief below 6.5% lifting orders and reducing the incentives that compress home-sale margins [2]
  2. Q4 FY2026 delivery of 22,000-23,000 homes at 15.5-16.0% gross margin, showing margin stabilization [2]
  3. Continued buybacks, with the diluted share count falling from about 256M to 238M YoY, and potential dividend growth resuming [3]
Key Risks
  1. Mortgage rates near or above 6.8% further eroding affordability and orders, which fell 9% YoY in Q3 [2]
  2. Gross-margin compression from heavy incentives, with home-sale margin at 15.8% versus 17.5% a year ago and Q4 guided to 15.5-16.0% [2]
  3. Technology-investment mark-to-market losses ($53M in Q3) and execution risk from frequent strategy and leadership changes [2][4]
