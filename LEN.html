Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Lennar Corporation is one of the largest homebuilders in the United States, constructing and selling single-family attached and detached homes, townhomes, and condominiums primarily for first-time, move-up, and active adult buyers. The company also provides mortgage financing through Lennar Mortgage, title insurance, and closing services. Founded in 1954 and headquartered in Miami, Lennar operates in markets across the U.S. and delivered over 80,000 homes in FY2025 with revenue of approximately $33.3B.

Investment Thesis
Lennar at 7.3x forward earnings with a fortress 17.2% D/E ratio and 15.3% ROE offers deep cyclical value in the nation's second-largest homebuilder [1]. The Millrose Properties spin-off has fundamentally de-risked the balance sheet, creating an asset-light model with land options rather than owned inventory — a structural advantage through housing cycles. The 2.3% dividend yield, 18% payout ratio, and 25% five-year dividend growth CAGR provide income with substantial room for continued increases. Housing affordability remains the binding constraint, but Lennar's 'Everything's Included' model and scale-driven cost advantages position it to capture demand when rates normalize [2].
The bear case centers on cyclical deterioration and margin compression. Q2 2026 net earnings fell to $305M ($1.24 EPS) from $477M ($1.81 EPS), with gross margin compressing to 15.6% from 17.8% as 12.9% incentives weighed on profitability [1]. Revenue declined 6% to $7.9B despite a 2% increase in deliveries, indicating significant average selling price erosion. New orders fell 4% to 21,749 homes, suggesting further demand softening. Management moderated FY2026 delivery guidance to 82,000-83,000 homes. The -8.1% revenue decline and -41% EPS decline reflect the severity of the current housing downturn, driven by persistent mortgage rate headwinds above 6.5%. Q1 2026 was even worse, with EPS falling 52% to $0.93 from $1.96. If the rate environment does not improve, further margin compression and volume declines are likely.
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage rate decline below 6% would unlock substantial pent-up housing demand, with Lennar's scale and 82,000+ annual delivery capacity positioned to capture disproportionate share of the recovery [1]
  2. Asset-light Millrose model generating higher ROIC through housing cycles — as the land option strategy matures, capital efficiency should drive valuation re-rating from the deeply discounted 7.3x forward P/E [1]
  3. Housing supply deficit of 3-5 million units nationally provides a structural demand floor that limits downside and supports long-term volume growth regardless of near-term rate headwinds [2]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent mortgage rates above 6.5% could extend the housing downturn beyond FY2026, with Q2 2026 new orders already declining 4% and incentives at 12.9% compressing margins to 15.6% [1]
  2. Incentive escalation spiral — competitors may match Lennar's 12.9% incentives, triggering a race to the bottom that permanently impairs industry margins without meaningfully expanding the buyer pool
  3. Revenue decline of -8.1% YoY with EPS down 41% demonstrates the cyclical vulnerability inherent in homebuilding, and a prolonged downturn could pressure the dividend despite the low 18% payout ratio
