Company Profile
Energy ·Uranium ·NYSE ·United States
Centrus Energy is the only US-owned uranium enrichment company, supplying low-enriched uranium (LEU) fuel components to nuclear power utilities worldwide. The company operates two segments: LEU (selling separative work units and uranium products) and Technical Solutions (engineering and manufacturing services). Centrus is advancing HALEU production for next-generation reactors, holds a $900M DOE HALEU enrichment award, and signed a new 2026 LEU/HALEU supply agreement with advanced-reactor developer X-energy, building a total order book of $4.5 billion extending to 2040.

Investment Thesis
Centrus Energy is the only US-owned uranium enrichment company licensed for commercial HALEU production, holding a strategically irreplaceable position as AI data center demand accelerates nuclear power investment. Q2 2026 revenue rose 14% YoY to $176.1M, and Centrus has built a $4.5B total order book extending to 2040, anchored by a $900M DOE HALEU enrichment award and a new 2026 definitive LEU/HALEU supply agreement with advanced-reactor developer X-energy [1][2]. The Palantir AI partnership has identified $300M in potential cost savings across operations, and sustained upward pressure on SWU pricing from sanctioned Russian supply gives Centrus structural pricing power [2].
The bear case centers on deteriorating near-term profitability against a rich valuation. Forward P/E of ~68x prices in years of flawless HALEU ramp execution, yet Q1 and Q2 2026 GAAP net income both fell sharply YoY ($10.0M vs $27.2M, and $16.8M vs $28.9M) on rising 'advanced technology' costs, and trailing EPS is down nearly 64% YoY, leaving ROE at just 6.02% [1][3]. D/E of 139% reflects moderate leverage. Glassdoor reviews citing 'rough culture,' 'very low morale,' and replacement of long-term employees are concerning for a company handling classified nuclear operations that depend on high-trust teams [2]. Heavy DOE funding and policy dependency creates political risk, and the $4.5B backlog will take years to convert into recognized revenue, leaving a gap between the AI-nuclear narrative and near-term financials.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full commercial ramp of the HALEU production cascade converting the $4.5B order book (through 2040) into recognized revenue, including the $900M DOE award and new X-energy supply agreement [1][2]
  2. Palantir AI partnership converting $300M in identified cost savings and operational efficiencies into realized margin expansion during the capacity buildout [2]
  3. Continued upward pressure on global SWU pricing as sanctioned Russian (Rosatom/TENEX) enrichment supply stays constrained, supporting further contract wins at improving terms [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of ~68x prices in a multi-year execution runway with little margin for delay; any HALEU cascade commissioning slip or cost overrun could trigger sharp multiple compression
  2. Near-term profitability is deteriorating — Q1 and Q2 2026 GAAP net income both fell sharply YoY on rising advanced-technology costs, with ROE at just 6.02% and trailing EPS down 64% YoY [1][3]
  3. Internal culture concerns (Glassdoor 3.4/5, 'rough culture,' 'very low morale,' long-term employees replaced) plus heavy DOE funding/policy dependency could impair execution of the multi-billion-dollar expansion program [2]
