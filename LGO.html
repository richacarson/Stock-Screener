Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Largo Inc. is a Canadian critical-minerals producer focused on vanadium pentoxide (V2O5) and ilmenite from the Maracás Menchen Mine in Bahia, Brazil — one of the world's highest-grade vanadium deposits. The company recently diversified into copper-platinum group metals (PGM) concentrate production and previously operated a vanadium redox flow battery business (Largo Clean Energy / Storion JV). In Q2 2026 Largo reported $44.0M revenue (+68.5% YoY) and positive adjusted EBITDA of $2.7M, but posted a net loss of $22.7M due to elevated operating costs and raw material input pressures. The company secured a $60.1M U.S. Defense Logistics Agency delivery order for high-purity vanadium products in July 2026. Market cap approximately $70M.

Investment Thesis
Largo offers exposure to vanadium — a critical mineral for high-strength steel alloys and vanadium redox flow batteries — at a deeply distressed valuation of 7.6x forward earnings and a $70M market cap. Q2 2026 revenue surged 68.5% YoY to $44.0M driven by 53.5% higher vanadium sales volumes and 8.9% higher realized pricing ($6.96/lb), while adjusted EBITDA turned positive at $2.7M [1]. The $60.1M DLA delivery order provides 5-year revenue visibility and validates Largo's strategic importance as a Western-hemisphere vanadium supplier amid rising U.S. critical minerals policy priorities [2]. Copper-PGM production guidance of 300–380 tonnes/month adds a new revenue stream leveraging existing Maracás mine infrastructure.
The bear case is severe: Largo reported a net loss of $22.7M in Q2 2026 despite 68.5% revenue growth, with cash operating costs rising 10.1% to $5.10/lb — well above the top of the company's own $3.50–$4.50/lb guidance range [1]. Cash on hand was just $5.1M against $114.2M in total debt, creating acute going-concern risk. The stock fails the IOWN liquidity floor ($970K average daily dollar volume vs. $1M minimum), making it effectively untradeable at institutional scale. Vanadium prices remain volatile and tied to Chinese supply dynamics that Largo cannot control. Profit margin is deeply negative at -64.6%, ROE is -52.6%, and the stock has declined from ~$10 in 2022 to $0.68, destroying over 90% of shareholder value. The co-CEO governance structure adds complexity without clear strategic accountability [5].
