Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
L3Harris Technologies is a global aerospace and defense technology company providing mission-critical solutions across three segments: Space & Mission Systems (satellite and missile defense), Communications & Spectrum Dominance (tactical radios, broadband, night vision), and Missile Solutions (propulsion, hypersonics). Formed through the 2019 merger of L3 Technologies and Harris Corporation with the 2023 Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition adding vertical propulsion integration. Headquartered in Melbourne, Florida.

Investment Thesis
L3Harris delivered a breakout Q1 2026 — revenue surged 12% YoY to $5.74B (+15% organic), GAAP EPS jumped 33% to $2.72, and orders of $7.8B drove book-to-bill to 1.4x with record backlog of $40.7B (2x annual revenue) plus $25B of pending Munitions Acceleration Council orders under negotiation [1]. Management raised full-year guidance to $23-23.5B revenue and $11.40-11.60 EPS with ~$3.0B FCF target. International book-to-bill of 2.2x reflects NATO and allied defense spending acceleration. The Palantir AI partnership and Golden Dome missile defense program position L3Harris to capture the AI-enabled defense modernization wave [2]. At ~$350/share with 44 consecutive years of dividend payments and a conservative D/E of 57.7%, the stock offers defense exposure with financial discipline.
The bear case centers on elevated valuation and defense budget cyclicality. At ~31x forward P/E (PEG ~2.0) and trailing PE of ~43x, L3Harris is priced for sustained double-digit growth that depends on continued defense spending increases — politically uncertain given deficit reduction pressures [4]. ROE of 8.2% remains well below the 15% target, weighed down by the $19.6B+ equity base inflated by acquisition goodwill from L3 and Aerojet — any future write-downs could materially impact returns. The Aerojet integration adds operational complexity, and Glassdoor reviews describing 'toxic culture' and leadership disconnection suggest margin expansion may come at the cost of engineering talent retention [2]. D/E increased from 34.6% to 57.7%, reflecting higher leverage. Dividend yield of 1.4% is below the 2% threshold for income investors despite the long payment streak, limiting income appeal.
Key Catalysts
  1. Pentagon Golden Dome missile defense program contracts and continued AI integration through Palantir and Shield AI partnerships, driving high-margin technology revenue growth [2]
  2. Record $40.7B backlog conversion into revenue through 2027-2028, with $25B of pending MAC orders providing additional upside if awarded [1]
  3. International defense spending surge — NATO 2% GDP commitments and Indo-Pacific security concerns driving 2.2x international book-to-bill, expanding addressable market beyond US DoD [1]
Key Risks
  1. Defense budget uncertainty — political shifts toward deficit reduction or spending caps could constrain the 12%+ revenue growth trajectory that supports the ~31x forward P/E valuation [1][4]
  2. Valuation compression risk — PEG of ~2.0 and trailing PE of ~43x leave minimal margin of safety; any execution miss or growth deceleration could trigger significant multiple compression
  3. Cultural and retention risk — Glassdoor reviews describing 'toxic culture' and 'zero leadership' at a company requiring high-security-clearance engineering talent could undermine long-term execution [2]
