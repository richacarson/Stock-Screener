Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto Manufacturers ·NASDAQ ·China
Li Auto Inc. designs, develops, manufactures and sells premium smart electric vehicles in China. It pioneered extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs) with its L-series family SUVs and has expanded into battery-electric vehicles (MEGA MPV and i-series). Founder and CEO Li Xiang is repositioning the company as an AI company, targeting Level 4 autonomous driving by 2028 and developing humanoid robots. After 2025 deliveries fell 18.8% to 406,300 units, the company is in a model-refresh cycle led by the all-new L9 launched in Q2 2026.

Investment Thesis
Li Auto combines a net-cash-rich balance sheet (cash of ~RMB 85/share against 65 book value per share) and a strong 5-year growth record with an aggressive bet on AI-defined vehicles and robotics, all at about 0.7x sales and 1.1x book [9]. Q2 2026 revenue rose 12% QoQ, operating margin improved from -13.0% to -9.0%, and management expects margin expansion in the second half as the new L9 mix improves [1].
The near-term picture is weak. Q2 revenue fell 15% YoY, gross margin halved to 11.0%, the company lost CN¥1.7B in the quarter, and Q3 guidance of RMB 26.6B-28.0B came in below expectations [1][2]. Execution on the AI pivot, cultural repair after leadership turnover, and China's price war will determine whether Li Auto recovers its premium franchise.
Key Catalysts
  1. Margin recovery toward the 15%-20% long-term gross margin target as the all-new L9 and refreshed lineup scale in H2 2026 [1][4]
  2. Progress toward Level 4 autonomous driving (target no later than 2028) and humanoid-robot launches validating the AI strategy [3]
  3. Q3 2026 delivery guidance of 95,000-100,000 units marking stabilization after 2025's 18.8% decline [1][6]
Key Risks
  1. Intense Chinese EV price war and AI-native competitors eroding share; 2025 deliveries fell 18.8% [6]
  2. Continued losses (Q2 2026 net loss of CN¥1.7B) and negative operating cash flow [2][9]
  3. Quality and safety risk after the 11,411-unit MEGA battery-fire recall, plus talent attrition in autonomous driving [3][6]
