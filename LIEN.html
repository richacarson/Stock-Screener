Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
Chicago Atlantic BDC, Inc. (formerly Silver Spike Investment Corp.) is a specialty finance Business Development Company (BDC) focused on providing direct loans and equity financing to privately held cannabis companies. It is the only publicly traded BDC primarily focused on the U.S. cannabis industry, filling a critical financing gap created by federal prohibition that prevents traditional banks from serving cannabis operators. The company secured a $100M senior secured revolving credit facility to fund new cannabis and non-cannabis loan originations. Q1 2026 revenue was $16.7M (+91.6% YoY) with record net investment income of $10M ($0.44/share). CEO Peter Sack also co-manages Chicago Atlantic Real Estate Finance (REFI), and a pending merger between LIEN and REFI is subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. Headquartered in New York.

Investment Thesis
Chicago Atlantic BDC at $9.15 with a 14.86% dividend yield, forward P/E of 5.58, and 52.8% profit margin is the only publicly traded BDC focused on U.S. cannabis lending, occupying a niche that traditional banks cannot enter due to federal prohibition [2]. Q1 2026 revenue surged 91.6% YoY to $16.7M with record net investment income of $0.44/share, driven by growing loan originations as the cannabis industry matures and consolidates [1]. The $100M revolving credit facility provides capacity to scale the loan portfolio, and cannabis rescheduling from Schedule I to Schedule III (recreational rescheduling pending DEA review) is a structural tailwind that could attract more institutional capital to the sector. The pending merger with REFI would create a larger, more liquid entity with a combined cannabis real estate and direct lending platform.
The bear case is dominated by the liquidity constraint and regulatory risk. Daily dollar volume of only $545K makes LIEN impractical for institutional positioning — the stock fails IOWN's liquidity floor, and any attempt to build or exit a meaningful position would move the price materially [1]. The 97.8% payout ratio leaves virtually no retained earnings for balance sheet growth, making the company dependent on external capital markets to grow its loan book. Cannabis remains federally illegal under the Controlled Substances Act, and a change in DOJ enforcement posture could impair the entire loan portfolio overnight [3]. The externally managed BDC structure creates inherent conflicts of interest between the Chicago Atlantic management team and LIEN shareholders. EPS declined 29.4% YoY despite revenue growth, suggesting margin compression or portfolio quality issues. The pending REFI merger introduces integration risk and potential shareholder dilution. If cannabis rescheduling stalls or fails, the regulatory arbitrage that drives premium yields could persist but the industry growth that drives portfolio expansion would slow.
