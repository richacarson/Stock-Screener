Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Life360 is the leading family safety and location-sharing platform, surpassing 100 million monthly active users in Q2 2026 with 3.2 million paying circles. The app provides real-time location sharing, driving safety alerts, crash detection with emergency dispatch, and family coordination tools. Revenue is generated through subscriptions (~$475M guided for FY2026), a growing advertising platform (Place Ads, Uplift by Life360), and hardware (Tile trackers). Founded in 2008, headquartered in San Francisco. CEO Lauren Antonoff succeeded founder Chris Hulls in 2025. Dual-listed on NASDAQ (LIF) and ASX (360). Market cap ~$3.2B.

Investment Thesis
Life360 surpassed 100M MAU in Q2 2026 while delivering $159.0M record revenue (+38% YoY) with 3.2M paying circles (+27%) and record Q2 net additions of 185,000 [2]. The company reiterated FY2026 revenue guidance of $650-685M and raised subscription guidance to $475-480M, demonstrating accelerating monetization. At $39.62 (down ~19% from recent highs), the stock now trades at just 22.2x forward earnings — a compelling valuation for a company growing revenue 38% with 25.7% profit margins and 30.1% ROE [8]. D/E of 50.8% reflects balance sheet strength. The advertising platform (Place Ads, Uplift) creates a high-margin second growth vector leveraging 100M+ users of proprietary location data. The network effect (entire families must be on the platform) creates switching costs that deepen with scale, and no competitor approaches Life360's user base in family safety.
The bear case centers on platform dependency, insider selling, and cultural execution risks. Apple and Google control iOS and Android — native family safety features (Find My, Family Link) could marginalize Life360's core offering without warning, and App Store policy changes could restrict the data collection essential to the advertising business [3]. Founder Chris Hulls sold 250K+ shares in a single August 2026 transaction plus multiple other sales throughout the year — a concerning pattern for the largest individual shareholder [7]. EPS missed Q2 2026 forecasts despite the revenue beat, and earnings growth of -25.1% raises questions about the path from revenue growth to sustained earnings power [8]. Glassdoor reviews describe constant restructuring, fear-based AI transformation, and employees racing to automate to avoid being fired — cultural dynamics that could drive talent flight during a critical scaling period [6]. The 2021 controversy over selling 33M users' precise location data (including children's data) to data brokers remains a reputational overhang for a company built on family trust [5].
