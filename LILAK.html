Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·Bermuda
Liberty Latin America is a leading telecommunications company operating in over 20 countries across Latin America and the Caribbean. The company provides residential and business services including broadband internet, video, telephony, and mobile services through brands including Flow, Liberty, and BTC. Formed in 2018 as a spin-off from Liberty Global, and acquired AT&T's Latin American wireless operations in 2020. Headquartered in Denver, Colorado with operational headquarters in Miami.

Investment Thesis
Liberty Latin America provides essential telecommunications infrastructure across 20+ Caribbean and Latin American markets, generating $4.43B TTM revenue with growing subscriber momentum — 45,000 postpaid and broadband net adds in Q2 2026 and 50,000 in Q1 2026 [1][2]. At $8.39 per share with a forward P/E of 12.19, the stock is valued at a discount to developed-market telecom peers, reflecting the risk premium on Caribbean/LatAm operations. Q2 2026 operating income of $181M versus -$333M in Q2 2025 demonstrates meaningful operational improvement, and the company's leading positions in Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Panama, and other markets provide recurring revenue stability.
The bear case centers on Liberty Latin America's extreme leverage (D/E 469%), persistent negative earnings (-$734M net loss on $4.43B revenue), and limited organic growth (-0.8% YoY revenue) in mature telecom markets [5]. The debt-funded acquisition strategy has failed to generate positive returns, with ROE of -34.9% indicating the company is destroying shareholder value. Revenue growth has stagnated at a ~3.2% 5-year CAGR despite the AT&T Latin America acquisition, suggesting limited pricing power in competitive Caribbean/LatAm markets. The $500M preferred stock issuance in Q1 2026 signals ongoing capital needs, and FX exposure across 20+ countries creates additional earnings volatility [1]. The analyst consensus price target of $6.70 implies 20% downside from current levels, reflecting skepticism about the path to profitability.
Key Catalysts
  1. Operational leverage from subscriber growth momentum — 95,000 combined postpaid and broadband net adds in H1 2026 could drive margin expansion as fixed-cost network infrastructure is better utilized
  2. Fiber-to-the-home expansion across Caribbean markets enabling higher ARPU broadband tiers and reducing churn, supported by increasing demand for bandwidth from AI applications and streaming
  3. Potential strategic value in Caribbean/LatAm telecom consolidation as larger operators (T-Mobile, America Movil) seek regional scale, making LILAK an acquisition target at a premium to current valuation
Key Risks
  1. Extreme leverage with D/E of 469% and persistent net losses create refinancing risk — rising interest rates would further pressure the already negative earnings profile and could threaten debt covenant compliance
  2. Revenue growth has stagnated at -0.8% YoY in mature Caribbean/LatAm markets where population growth is modest and competitive intensity from Claro and Digicel limits pricing power
  3. Currency exposure across 20+ Latin American and Caribbean markets introduces significant FX volatility to USD-reported earnings, amplifying the impact of any regional economic downturns
