Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NYSE ·United States
Lionsgate Studios Corp. is a global entertainment company engaged in motion picture production and distribution, television programming, and digital content. The company operates a library of approximately 20,000 titles and owns iconic franchises including John Wick, The Hunger Games, SAW, and Twilight. Following the May 2025 separation from the Starz premium cable network, Lionsgate Studios operates as an independent, publicly traded pure-play content studio. Headquartered in Santa Monica, California.

Investment Thesis
Lionsgate Studios is positioned at an inflection point following the May 2025 separation from Starz, now operating as a pure-play content studio focused on its 20,000-title library and franchise IP [3]. Q4 FY2026 delivered $906.5M revenue with $117.5M operating income, adjusted OIBDA reached a 12-year high of $165M (up 17%), and free cash flow was a positive $190M [1]. 'Michael' is tracking to become Lionsgate's first $1 billion worldwide box office film, demonstrating the franchise engine's capacity for breakout success [1]. The company's AI strategy is the most aggressive among major studios — the expanded Runway partnership with equity stake, proprietary AI model trained on 20,000 titles, and first Chief AI Officer position Lionsgate to lead the industry's AI transformation with projected production timeline reductions of 15-20% and savings of 'tens and tens of millions' annually [2]. The Inspire score of +21 (MIXED) is neutral for faith-based investors.
However, significant risks temper the thesis. Full-year FY2026 GAAP EPS was -$0.70, meaning the company lost money on a reported basis despite strong adjusted metrics, raising questions about whether adjusted profitability translates to real economic value [1]. Revenue growth of ~2% ($2.63B vs $2.58B) is anemic for a company trading at 33.7x forward earnings — the multiple implies significant growth acceleration that the top line has not yet delivered. The entertainment industry is inherently hit-driven, with a single box office disappointment capable of destroying quarterly results. AI-generated content poses a dual threat: while Lionsgate is proactively embracing AI, the same technology could enable smaller competitors and independent creators to produce comparable content at much lower cost, potentially commoditizing the mid-budget content where Lionsgate has traditionally excelled. The post-Starz separation creates an entity without the recurring subscription revenue that provided earnings stability, increasing cyclicality. D/E data is unavailable, creating a blind spot on leverage risk typical for studios that finance individual productions through debt [4].
