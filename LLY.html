Eli Lilly and Company discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets human pharmaceuticals worldwide, anchored by its incretin franchise — Mounjaro (tirzepatide for type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (tirzepatide for obesity). Q3 2026 revenue rose 39% to $24.4B on 53% volume growth, with worldwide Mounjaro revenue up 65% to $10.7B, and management raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $91-92B from $85-87B. FY2025 revenue was $65.2B with $20.6B of net income. Headquartered in Indianapolis and led by CEO David Ricks; market cap roughly $1.26T.

Investment Thesis
Eli Lilly at $1,410.99 is compounding at a pace without precedent for a trillion-dollar pharmaceutical company: Q3 2026 revenue grew 39% to $24.4B on 53% volume growth, Mounjaro reached $10.7B in a single quarter (+65%), and full-year 2026 revenue guidance was lifted by $5.5B at the midpoint to $91-92B [1]. Revenue has compounded at a 21.6% CAGR from $24.5B in 2020 to $65.2B in 2025, while net income more than tripled to $20.6B [3]. Profitability is elite — 31.9% net margin, 86% ROE, 53.7% operating margin — and the stock trades at 28.1x forward earnings with a 1.36 PEG, cheaper on a forward basis than many lower-growth peers [4]. R&D of $13.3B (20.5% of revenue) and continued pipeline buying (AtaiBeckley, Curevo) keep the next wave of medicines funded [1][3].
The bear case is concentration and price. Mounjaro and Zepbound accounted for more than half of 2025 revenue, and Lilly's own filings flag manufacturing difficulties, third-party performance and supply disruptions as risks to product supply [2]. Realized prices fell 15% in Q3 2026 — volume is carrying growth while pricing erodes — and Q3 revenue included roughly $900M of one-time benefits (Jardiance milestones and rebate adjustments) that will not recur [1]. Acquired IPR&D charges of $4.2B in Q3 more than offset the underlying EPS raise, leaving adjusted 2026 EPS guidance at $35-36 [1]. D/E of 123% ($49.5B total debt vs $4.9B cash) reflects debt-funded capacity expansion [4]. S&P Global has placed Lilly's ESG score under review pending a Media & Stakeholder Analysis controversy case, and the Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED, LGBT Activism) may limit allocation for faith-based mandates [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to $91-92B (from $85-87B) after Q3 revenue of $24.4B (+39%), with 53% volume growth signaling GLP-1 demand still outrunning supply additions [1]
  2. Pipeline conversion beyond tirzepatide — newly acquired AtaiBeckley and Curevo assets ($4.2B of Q3 IPR&D) plus next-generation incretins broaden the revenue base beyond Mounjaro/Zepbound [1]
  3. AI-accelerated drug discovery through the TuneLab model platform for biotechs and the 2026 co-innovation AI lab could compress development timelines that CEO Ricks has said AI might cut in half [2]
Key Risks
  1. Pricing erosion: realized prices fell 15% in Q3 2026; continued government, payer and competitive pressure on GLP-1 pricing could turn volume growth into flat revenue growth [1]
  2. Franchise concentration: Mounjaro and Zepbound exceeded half of 2025 revenue, so any safety signal, supply disruption, or manufacturing failure (risks flagged in Lilly's own annual report) would hit the $1.26T valuation disproportionately [2]
  3. Earnings quality: ~$900M of one-time Q3 revenue benefits and $4.2B of acquired IPR&D charges make GAAP results lumpy, and serial pipeline acquisitions financed alongside $49.5B of debt raise leverage risk [1][4]
