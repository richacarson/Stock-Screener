Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Lockheed Martin Corporation is the world's largest defense contractor, operating across four segments: Aeronautics (F-35, F-16, C-130J), Missiles and Fire Control (THAAD, PAC-3 MSE, Javelin, HIMARS), Rotary and Mission Systems (Sikorsky, Aegis), and Space (Orion, GPS satellites). Q3 2026 sales reached approximately $20.7B (up 11% YoY) with net earnings exceeding $1.8B (up 14% YoY) and GAAP EPS of $7.97. Backlog hit a record $292B, up from $230.4B at Q2 end, driven by a transformative multi-year PAC-3 MSE interceptor contract. FY2026 guidance was raised for the second consecutive quarter.

Investment Thesis
Lockheed Martin is the world's largest defense contractor executing at peak form — Q3 2026 sales grew approximately 11% YoY to $20.7B with net earnings up 14% and GAAP EPS of $7.97, while backlog surged to a record $292B (from $230.4B just a quarter earlier) driven by a transformative multi-year PAC-3 MSE interceptor contract [1]. This backlog provides multi-decade government-backed cash flow visibility, underpinning the 22-year consecutive dividend growth streak (2.50% yield, $13.80 annual, 6.4% 5-year CAGR). The F-35 remains the world's only operational 5th-gen fighter available to allies, MFC delivers the highest growth with PAC-3 and THAAD production scaling, and the AI Center (350 engineers, Sanctum counter-drone AI, Lamprey autonomous systems) positions the company at the center of AI-enabled defense modernization [2]. At 16.89x forward earnings, LMT offers reasonable valuation for the highest-quality franchise in defense.
The bear case centers on margin constraints, leverage, and defense budget dependence. Profit margin of 8.16% reflects the cost-plus contract model that structurally caps upside even as revenue accelerates, and D/E of 234% remains elevated despite improvement [1]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of just 3.8% demonstrates how slowly defense spending translates to top-line growth relative to commercial sectors. F-35 Lot 20 costs running past contractual authorization signal execution risk on the flagship program, and the Q3 backlog surge from $230.4B to $292B includes multi-year commitments whose margin profiles may compress over time. The stock has pulled back 8% from $598 to $551 but the price still embeds significant execution expectations. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits faith-based investor participation despite strong positive attributions across 18 ESG categories [3].
