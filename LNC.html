Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance - Life ·NYSE ·United States
Lincoln National Corporation, operating as Lincoln Financial Group, is a diversified insurance and financial services company offering annuities, life insurance, retirement plan services, and group protection products. The company serves approximately 17 million customers through four business segments: Annuities, Retirement Plan Services, Life Insurance, and Group Protection. Headquartered in Radnor, Pennsylvania, founded in 1905.

Investment Thesis
Lincoln National trades at a deep value multiple of 5.28x forward earnings with a 4.40% dividend yield and 24.6% payout ratio, offering meaningful income with substantial dividend growth potential as earnings recover [4]. The company reported Q4 2025 adjusted operating income of $434M (+31% YoY) with record Q1 2026 earnings in Group Protection, demonstrating profitability momentum after the 2023 crisis year [1]. At $40.96 with a $7.83B market cap, the stock prices in significant skepticism despite ROE of 20% and improving fundamentals. The conservative D/E of 62% and strong capital position support the dividend, while the newly appointed Chief AI Officer (January 2026) positions Lincoln for operational efficiency gains ahead of insurance peers [2].
The bear case centers on structural challenges and turnaround uncertainty. Revenue has stagnated — declining 1.25% in 2025 with a 5-year CAGR of only 0.7% — suggesting the core business is not growing [1]. The 2023 net loss of -$752M revealed significant risk management failures in the life insurance segment that may recur during market dislocations. GAAP net income fell 65.9% in 2025 vs. 2024, complicating the profitability narrative (2024 included one-time gains) [4]. The dividend was cut from $1.80/quarter to $1.00/quarter in 2023 and only partially restored to $1.80/year ($0.45/quarter), leaving a damaged income track record. Insurance industry faces secular headwinds: AI-powered underwriting disruptors, declining whole life demand among younger consumers, and low interest rate environments compressing spread income. Below-average ESG scores and 'Moderate Controversy' Sustainalytics rating add governance risk [3].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience DEVELOPING
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Insurance is a mixed AI exposure. AI significantly threatens traditional underwriting, claims processing, and distribution — all core functions for Lincoln [2]. Automated underwriting platforms (Lemonade, Bestow) can process life insurance applications in minutes versus weeks, potentially commoditizing Lincoln's underwriting advantage. AI-powered robo-advisors threaten the annuity distribution channel. However, complex life insurance products (variable annuities, whole life) require human advisory relationships and regulatory compliance that provide partial protection. Group protection benefits from employer relationships and HR system integrations that create switching costs. The regulatory environment (state-by-state insurance regulation) creates barriers to AI-native disruptors. Lincoln's appointment of a Chief AI Officer in December 2025 shows proactive response [2]. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI enables significant efficiency gains — automated claims adjudication, predictive policyholder behavior modeling, personalized product recommendations, and fraud detection. Lincoln's new Chief AI Officer role is positioned to capture these opportunities at enterprise scale [2].
