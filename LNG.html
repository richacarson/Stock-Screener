Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Cheniere Energy is the largest producer and exporter of LNG in the United States and the second-largest LNG operator globally. The company operates the Sabine Pass (Louisiana) and Corpus Christi (Texas) liquefaction facilities, exporting approximately 10% of global LNG supply under long-term take-or-pay contracts with investment-grade counterparties. Q1 2026 revenue was $5.87B with Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $2.33B. CEO Jack Fusco has led the company since 2016.
1,700 Employees ·www.cheniere.com

Investment Thesis
Cheniere Energy is the dominant US LNG exporter at a structural inflection — AI data center construction is driving historic increases in US electricity demand powered by natural gas, European allies need non-Russian supply for decades, and Asia continues its coal-to-gas transition [3]. Q1 2026 delivered revenue of $5.87B (up 8% YoY) and Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $2.33B (up 25% YoY), prompting management to raise full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance to $7.25-7.75B and Distributable Cash Flow guidance to $4.75-5.25B [1]. At 17.95x forward earnings with contracted cash flows, CC Stage 3 trains completing in 2026, and Midscale Trains 8 and 9 advancing for 2028, Cheniere offers visible multi-year production growth underpinned by take-or-pay contracts. The Inspire Impact Score of 42 (ALIGNED) with 14 positive attributions including Energy Management, Business Resilience, and Regulatory Adherence reflects strong ESG positioning within the energy sector.
The bear case centers on commodity oversupply, regulatory risk, and GAAP distortions. The Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $3.5B — driven by $5.4B in non-cash unfavorable derivative fair value changes from IPM contracts — distorts trailing metrics (PE 38.45x, profit margin 7.10%) and may spook investors unfamiliar with the accounting treatment [1]. D/E of 321% reflects $27.85B in project finance debt, a level above the IOWN 300% floor and a structural consequence of the capital-intensive LNG terminal model [4]. US LNG capacity is scaling rapidly — Venture Global's Plaquemines, Sempra's Port Arthur, and NextDecade's Rio Grande create potential spot margin pressure as new supply enters the market. Methane emissions regulation in the EU and US could raise compliance costs despite Cheniere's OGMP 2.0 Gold Standard recognition [3]. The combined CEO/Chairman role under Fusco concentrates governance power, and employee culture reviews suggest organizational stress below the C-suite.
Key Catalysts
  1. Corpus Christi Stage 3 remaining trains completing in 2026 and Midscale Trains 8 and 9 advancing toward 2028, supporting the raised EBITDA guidance of $7.25-7.75B and multi-year production growth to 51-53 million tons [1]
  2. AI data center electricity demand driving structural growth in US natural gas consumption — each 1GW data center requires 6 Bcf/year of natural gas equivalent, creating a multi-decade demand tailwind for LNG [3]

Key Risks
  1. US LNG oversupply from Venture Global Plaquemines, Sempra Port Arthur, and NextDecade Rio Grande coming online could compress spot margins beyond contracted volumes, reducing optimization upside [1]
  2. D/E of 321% (exceeding the 300% IOWN floor) reflects $27.85B in project finance debt — while secured by take-or-pay contracts, rising interest rates elevate refinancing costs and constrain financial flexibility [4]
  3. EU methane regulations and carbon border adjustments could raise compliance costs and reduce the emissions advantage of US LNG over pipeline gas, despite Cheniere's QMRV monitoring and Cargo Emissions Tag programs [3]
