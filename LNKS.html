Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NASDAQ ·Malaysia
Linkers Industries Limited is a manufacturer and supplier of wire and cable harnesses with over 20 years of experience. Based in Sungai Petani, Malaysia, the company serves global brand name manufacturers and OEMs in the home appliances, industrial products, and automotive industries, primarily in the Asia Pacific region. Linkers works closely with customers across the product lifecycle from design and prototyping through production. The company recently increased its stake in Thai wire-harness maker LPW Electronics Co., Ltd. from 20% to 49%.

Investment Thesis
Linkers Industries trades at a price-to-book ratio of 0.01 with a net cash position of $2.91M that exceeds the $83K market cap by 35x, creating a theoretical deep value scenario if the company can stabilize operations and return to profitability [2]. The 20+ year operating history, 190-person workforce, established OEM customer relationships in home appliances and automotive sectors, and Thailand expansion through the LPW Electronics JV (now 49% owned) provide some foundation for a potential turnaround. The wire harness market benefits from secular electrification and EV trends that increase wiring content per vehicle.
The overwhelming bear case makes this essentially uninvestable for IOWN: the $83K market cap represents a nano-cap stock trading at the threshold of Nasdaq delisting (received minimum bid price deficiency notice in March 2025) [4]. Losses are deepening — FY2025 loss per share of RM0.51 vs RM0.18 in FY2024, with TTM net loss of $1.59M on just $5.48M revenue [1][2]. The -29.1% net margin and -16.56% ROE indicate fundamental profitability problems, not a temporary downturn. Revenue has been essentially flat while losses have widened, suggesting the business model is structurally challenged. The LPW Electronics stake increase is a related-party transaction that may not serve minority shareholders. With approximately 54K shares outstanding and 4.3M daily volume, the trading dynamics are distorted and potentially manipulated. No analyst coverage, no institutional ownership of note, and no path to profitability articulated by management.
