Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities—Regulated Electric ·NASDAQ ·United States
Alliant Energy Corporation is a regulated utility holding company serving approximately one million electric and 430,000 natural gas customers in Iowa and Wisconsin through its subsidiaries Interstate Power and Light (IPL) and Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL). The company is transitioning from coal to renewables with 1,000+ MW of wind capacity installed and a commitment to eliminate coal from its generation mix by 2040. Alliant is actively pursuing AI data center load growth with a $1B equity raise earmarked for infrastructure expansion.

Investment Thesis
Alliant Energy delivered Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.87 (up from $0.83 YoY) on revenue of $1.184B (+5% YoY), reaffirming full-year ongoing EPS guidance of $3.36-$3.46 and continuing its decade-plus track record of 6%+ compound earnings growth [1]. The 2026 dividend target of $2.14/share represents a 5.4% increase and extends 21 consecutive years of growth on a base of 80 continuous years of payments since 1946. The AI data center demand thesis is compelling — Alliant's $1B equity raise specifically targets data center infrastructure in its Iowa/Wisconsin territory, positioning the company to benefit from hyperscaler buildout that requires physical grid infrastructure only regulated utilities can provide. TTM revenue of $4.36B, net profit margin improving to 18.6%, and regulated earnings visibility make LNT a defensive dividend compounder with an AI-driven growth kicker.
The bear case centers on utility-sector headwinds and valuation. At 22x forward P/E, Alliant trades at a premium to the utility sector average, pricing in data center growth optionality that may face execution risk — regulatory delays, community opposition to new power plants, and transmission constraints could slow data center customer additions [2]. D/E of 132% and the $1B equity raise dilute existing shareholders while the 63% payout ratio leaves less reinvestment flexibility than lower-payout peers. Revenue growth has been modest at 3.5% 5-year CAGR, and the 10% ROE is below cost of equity for many investors. Rising interest rates compress regulated utility valuations, and if 10-year Treasury yields remain elevated, the 2.8% dividend yield faces competition from risk-free alternatives.
