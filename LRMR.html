Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Larimar Therapeutics is a clinical-stage biotechnology company based in Bala Cynwyd, Pennsylvania, developing treatments for rare diseases on a cell-penetrating peptide platform. Its lead candidate, nomlabofusp (CTI-1601), is a recombinant fusion protein designed to deliver frataxin into the mitochondria of patients with Friedreich's ataxia. In June 2026 it began a rolling BLA for accelerated approval, with completion expected in 2H 2026 and a target launch in mid-2027 if approved. The company has no product revenue and held $156.3M in cash at June 30, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Larimar is a binary but tangible regulatory catalyst story. It began a rolling BLA for accelerated approval of nomlabofusp in June 2026, after FDA confirmed the open-label data package appears capable of supporting the submission; completion is expected in 2H 2026, with a target launch in mid-2027 [1]. Friedreich's ataxia is a fatal rare disease, and nomlabofusp is designed to address the root cause by delivering frataxin to the mitochondria. FMP consensus has revenue rising from about $41M (2027) to about $484M (2030), and the stock trades at a $277M market cap with $156.3M of cash — an enterprise value of about $190M [1][2].
The bear case centers on safety and funding. Anaphylaxis in 10 of 43 open-label participants (including one of 11 treatment-naive patients) led to discontinuations, and the stock fell 33.7% and 12.6% on the two safety-related updates; a Pomerantz securities investigation is ongoing [1][5]. That safety profile could restrict labeling, slow uptake or prompt the FDA to demand more data. TTM net loss was about $172M, cash runway reaches only Q3 2027 (less than a year from filing dates), and consensus shows losses through 2028, so further dilution is close to certain — weighted shares already rose about 33% in three quarters [1][2]. Infrastructure scores just 3/10.
Key Catalysts
  1. Completion of the rolling BLA for accelerated approval in 2H 2026, followed by FDA acceptance and a PDUFA date that supports the targeted mid-2027 launch [1]
  2. First patient dosed in the global confirmatory Phase 3 study (expected Q3 2026), which is needed to convert accelerated approval to full approval [1]
  3. A partnership or non-dilutive financing that extends cash runway beyond Q3 2027 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Safety: anaphylaxis in 10 of 43 open-label participants could lead to a complete response letter, a restrictive label or a boxed warning that limits commercial uptake [1][5]
  2. Financing: $156.3M of cash against a quarterly burn of roughly $30M funds operations only into Q3 2027, so heavy dilution is likely before any launch revenue [1][2]
  3. Litigation and credibility: the Pomerantz LLP securities-fraud investigation into disclosures around the September 2025 and June 2026 data updates [5]
