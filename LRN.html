Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Education & Training Services ·NYSE ·United States
Stride, Inc. (formerly K12 Inc.) is the largest provider of virtual K-12 education in the United States, serving students across 31 states through proprietary online curriculum, software systems, and educational services, alongside Career Learning programs under the Galvanize, Tech Elevator, and MedCerts brands. FY2026 (ended June 2026) revenue reached $2.52B (+4.7% YoY) with net income of $338.2M, but the stock fell over 51% intraday on October 29, 2025 after weak forward guidance citing platform-upgrade-driven customer experience problems and enrollment declines. Robert E. Knowling was appointed CEO on July 29, 2026 following James Rhyu's departure, and the company faces an active securities fraud class action alleging inflated 'ghost student' enrollment counts and compliance failures.
8,600 Employees ·stridelearning.com

Investment Thesis
Stride trades at a deeply discounted forward P/E of 8.47 and PEG of ~0.6 against FY2028 targets of $3.3-3.5B revenue and $700-800M adjusted operating income (10% revenue CAGR), with FY2026 results ($2.52B revenue, $338.2M net income) still showing the diversification into Career Learning is working (enrollments +13.9%, revenue +15.0%) [1][6]. New CEO Robert Knowling brings 30+ years of education and technology leadership, and if he can restore trust following the governance crisis, the valuation gap versus historical multiples could close meaningfully.
The bear case is serious. A securities fraud class action alleges Stride inflated enrollment with 'ghost students,' violated teacher caseload and special-education compliance requirements, and suppressed whistleblowers who documented directives to delay hiring and deny services to preserve margins — allegations that, if substantiated, describe systemic governance failure rather than an isolated incident [5]. The October 2025 51% single-day stock collapse followed weak guidance tied to a botched platform upgrade, and trailing revenue growth has turned negative (-2.7% YoY) even as FY2026 full-year results showed modest growth [1][2]. The CEO transition adds execution uncertainty during litigation discovery, and free AI tutoring tools from Khan Academy, Google, and OpenAI could further pressure curriculum pricing power. Regulatory bodies in multiple states could tighten oversight or curtail enrollment agreements if the allegations are substantiated.
Key Catalysts
  1. Resolution or dismissal of the securities fraud class action removing the litigation overhang that has depressed the stock well below its FY2028 target-implied valuation [5]
  2. New CEO Robert Knowling's education and technology background (NYC Leadership Academy) could restore governance credibility and re-accelerate enrollment growth toward FY2028 targets [1][2]
  3. Career Learning's 13.9% enrollment growth and 15.0% revenue growth continuing to diversify Stride away from General Education, which is now under the most regulatory scrutiny [1]
Key Risks
  1. Securities fraud class action alleging 'ghost student' enrollment inflation, special-education non-compliance, and whistleblower suppression could result in material litigation costs, regulatory sanctions, or loss of state operating agreements [5]
  2. Trailing revenue growth has turned negative (-2.7% YoY) following the October 2025 platform-upgrade failure, raising doubt about whether the FY2028 targets requiring ~10% annual growth remain achievable [1][2]
  3. CEO transition during active litigation discovery adds execution risk at the exact moment the company needs to rebuild regulator and investor trust [2]
