Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Leisure ·NYSE ·United States
Life Time Group Holdings operates large-format premium athletic country clubs across the United States and Canada, offering luxury fitness, wellness, sports, and social programming. Founded in 1992 by Bahram Akradi, the company operates 175+ centers with over 849,000 center memberships, generating revenue through membership dues, in-center services (personal training, spa, cafe), and digital offerings. Life Time distinguishes itself through resort-quality facilities averaging nearly 100,000 square feet, comprehensive wellness programming including ARORA (55+ community), MIORA (performance and longevity health), and the AI-powered L.AI.C health companion in the Life Time app.

Investment Thesis
Life Time Group Holdings is the dominant operator in premium athletic country clubs, delivering FY2025 revenue of $2.99B (up 14.3%), net income of $373.7M (up 139.2%), and adjusted EBITDA of $825.2M (up 21.9%) while achieving record membership retention and revenue per membership — both at the highest levels in the company's 32-year history [1][2]. Founder Bahram Akradi's 'Healthy Way of Life' mission has proven remarkably durable, and the company's differentiation through resort-quality facilities averaging nearly 100,000 square feet creates a structural moat against both mass-market gyms and boutique fitness concepts. Growth catalysts include 12-14 new club openings planned for 2026 under an asset-light model, MIORA longevity health expanding to 8 locations, Life Time Digital reaching 2.3M accounts (up 216% YoY) with the L.AI.C companion, and a $500M share repurchase program [1][2]. The company projects 2026 revenue of approximately $3.32B, maintaining double-digit growth momentum.
The primary concern is that valuation has expanded significantly — Forward P/E of 21.47 (up from 14.7 at the prior screen three months ago) and PEG of approximately 1.55 (up from 1.19) reflect a stock that has appreciated roughly 50% while fundamentals, though strong, have not changed commensurately. Revenue growth of 11.7% faces tougher comparisons as the post-COVID membership recovery normalizes, and EPS growth has decelerated from 124.3% to 15.7% as the base-effect tailwind fades. Debt-to-equity of 129.1% is elevated for a cyclical consumer business, creating financial vulnerability in a recession scenario where premium discretionary spending is the first budget item consumers cut. The aggressive 2026 expansion with 12-14 new clubs creates execution risk, particularly with the back-half-loaded opening schedule. Prior screening identified significant insider selling by multiple executives ($7M+ in three months), which remains a concerning signal despite the corporate-level $500M buyback authorization. Consumer cyclical risk is real: Life Time targets affluent members paying premium monthly dues, and any prolonged economic downturn would pressure both membership counts and in-center spending.
Key Catalysts
  1. Accelerated club opening cadence of 12-14 new locations in 2026 under an asset-light model, adding nearly as much new square footage as opened in the prior two years combined, driving membership and revenue growth toward $3.32B guidance
  2. MIORA performance and longevity health scaling from pilot to 8+ locations with premium pricing potential that could significantly increase revenue per member and expand Life Time's addressable market into the longevity wellness category
  3. Continued record membership retention rates combined with the $500M share repurchase program and deleveraging trajectory, creating a path toward multiple expansion as the balance sheet strengthens
Key Risks
  1. Valuation has expanded significantly with Forward P/E of 21.47 and PEG of ~1.55 after a ~50% stock price appreciation, leaving less margin of safety if revenue growth continues decelerating from 14.3% toward high-single-digit rates
  2. Debt-to-equity of 129.1% creates financial vulnerability in a consumer recession scenario where premium fitness membership is among the first discretionary expenses consumers reduce, and the aggressive expansion cadence constrains free cash flow
  3. Consumer cyclical exposure means Life Time's premium-priced membership model is sensitive to economic downturns, while back-half-loaded 2026 club openings and prior insider selling activity by multiple executives introduce execution and conviction concerns

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Life Time's core value proposition — luxury physical fitness facilities, in-person wellness experiences, social community, and personal training — is fundamentally resistant to AI disruption. Physical fitness requires physical effort in physical spaces; no AI model can substitute for the experience of a 100,000-square-foot athletic country club with pools, courts, spas, and group fitness studios [2]. The social and community dimensions of club membership address human needs for connection and belonging that are inherently analog. Personal training, spa services, childcare, and food and beverage operations all require human presence. The physical infrastructure, real estate moat, and experiential nature of the service create near-complete insulation from AI substitution risk. Some peripheral exposure exists in personal training (AI coaching apps), nutrition counseling, and digital content delivery, but these represent a small fraction of total revenue and the premium in-person model commands significant pricing power over digital alternatives. AI Opportunity (1/1): Life Time has proactively deployed AI through L.AI.C, its AI-powered health companion in the Life Time app, which now reaches 2.3M digital accounts (up 216% YoY) [2][3]. AI can enhance personalization of fitness programming, predict member churn, optimize club operations, and improve the digital experience, making the technology a net enabler of the membership model.
