Company Profile
Industrials ·Airlines ·NYSE ·Chile
LATAM Airlines Group is South America's largest airline, operating passenger and cargo services to 160 destinations across 27 countries. Founded in 1929 and headquartered in Santiago, Chile, the company operates a fleet of 371 aircraft and emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2022. In 2025, LATAM achieved record net income of $1.5B and transported 87 million passengers.

Investment Thesis
LATAM Airlines is delivering exceptional post-bankruptcy revenue growth — Q2 2026 revenue surged 27.9% to $4.2B, prompting management to raise the full-year EBITDA forecast to $4.1-4.4B [1]. The 2025 record of $1.5B net income (up 49.4%) and 87M passengers transported demonstrates the structural efficiency gains from Chapter 11 restructuring [1]. At 7.99x forward earnings with ROE of 108%, LATAM offers attractive valuation for South America's dominant airline franchise with 160 destinations and 371 aircraft. AI deployment is industry-leading with 40+ services via the Cosmos platform, including schedule optimization generating 1,440 more revenue service days [3]. 2026 guidance projects 8-10% capacity growth with EBITDA of $4.1-4.4B.
The bear case centers on balance sheet leverage, earnings volatility, and cyclical risk. D/E of 454.5% with $9.0B total debt remains dangerously elevated for a cyclical business only 3 years removed from Chapter 11 [2]. Q2 2026 revealed a critical disconnect: revenue grew 27.9% but earnings declined 46.2%, with profit margin compressing from 11.2% to 9.85%, suggesting cost pressures (fuel, labor, fleet expansion) are outpacing top-line gains [2]. The revenue CAGR of ~15% is inflated by post-COVID recovery and will normalize to mid-single digits. Latin American macro exposure creates persistent headwinds: currency devaluations, political instability across five operating countries, and fuel price volatility represent structural risks. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
LATAM's core business — physically transporting 87M passengers annually across Latin America using a 371-aircraft fleet — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. Aircraft maintenance, crew operations, airport logistics, and passenger movement require physical capabilities beyond AI's reach. Regulatory barriers (bilateral air service agreements, safety certifications, slot allocations) create additional structural moats. LATAM is proactively deploying AI rather than being disrupted: 40+ AI services via the 'Cosmos' platform on Google Cloud Vertex AI, including schedule optimization yielding 1,440 more revenue service days, predictive maintenance, and AI-managed customer interactions [3]. The dual CDO/CIO leadership structure and 'tech company that flies planes' mentality position LATAM as an AI beneficiary within the airline industry.
