Company Profile
Industrials ·Airlines ·NYSE ·Chile
LATAM Airlines Group is South America's largest airline, operating passenger and cargo services to 160 destinations across 27 countries. Founded in 1929 and headquartered in Santiago, Chile, the company operates a fleet of 371 aircraft and emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2022. In 2025, LATAM achieved record net income of $1.5B and transported 87 million passengers.

Investment Thesis
LATAM Airlines is delivering exceptional post-bankruptcy performance — record Q1 2026 revenue of $4.15B (beating by 4.88%), EPS of $2.01 (beating by 74.78%), and TTM revenue of $15.07B with 16.7% YoY growth [1][5]. The 2025 record of $1.5B net income (up 49.4%) and 87M passengers transported demonstrates the structural efficiency gains from Chapter 11 restructuring [2]. At 10.98x forward earnings with profit margin of 11.21% and ROE of 117.56%, LATAM offers attractive valuation for South America's dominant airline franchise with 160 destinations and 371 aircraft. AI-driven operational improvements (1,440 more revenue service days, AI-managed customer interactions) and the digital transformation vision position LATAM to sustain efficiency gains [3]. 2026 guidance of $15.5-16.0B revenue with 8-10% capacity growth provides clear visibility.
The bear case centers on balance sheet leverage and cyclicality. D/E of 435.97% with $8.40B gross debt against $2.54B cash, while improved from 604.88% in April, remains dangerously elevated for a cyclical business that was in Chapter 11 as recently as 2022 [5]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~29% entirely reflects COVID recovery from a $3.9B 2020 base and is misleading — underlying growth normalizes to mid-single digits. Latin American macro exposure creates persistent headwinds: currency devaluations (Brazilian real, Colombian peso), political instability across the five-country operation, and fuel price volatility represent structural risks to the 11.21% profit margin. The nascent dividend program (only 2 years post-emergence) and the airline industry's historical boom-bust patterns suggest current record profitability may be a cyclical peak. The Inspiration score of 4 reflects limited purpose-driven leadership and community investment beyond ESG compliance [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. 2026 revenue guidance of $15.5-16.0B with 8-10% capacity growth, fleet expansion, and adjusted operating margin of 15-17% targeting continued record profitability [2]
  2. AI-driven operational efficiencies scaling across the fleet — schedule optimization (1,440 more revenue days), predictive maintenance, customer interaction automation — with CDO targeting further digitization [3]
  3. D/E improvement from 604.88% to 435.97% demonstrates active deleveraging trajectory; continued free cash flow generation ($2.27B TTM) supports balance sheet repair and dividend sustainability [5]
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 435.97% with $8.40B gross debt in a cyclical, capital-intensive industry — only 3 years removed from Chapter 11, any demand shock or fuel price spike could pressure the 11.21% margin [5]
  2. Latin American macro exposure including currency volatility across five operating countries, political instability, and cargo revenue weakness signaling potential trade slowdown [2]
  3. Post-pandemic revenue CAGR of ~29% is unsustainable — underlying growth normalizes to mid-single digits as capacity recovery matures, creating risk of multiple compression from the current 10.98x forward P/E [5]

AI Risk Protection (7/9): LATAM's core business — physically transporting 87M passengers annually across Latin America using a 371-aircraft fleet — is structurally immune to AI disruption [2].
