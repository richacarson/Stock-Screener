Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Leisure Facilities / Entertainment ·NYSE ·United States
Lucky Strike Entertainment Corporation (formerly Bowlero Corp., rebranded December 2024) is the world's largest operator of ten-pin bowling centers with over 360 locations across North America. The company operates traditional bowling under the AMF brand, upscale entertainment venues under the Lucky Strike and Bowlero brands, and has expanded into water parks and family entertainment centers. It also owns the Professional Bowlers Association (PBA). FY2026 revenue was $1.245B (+4% YoY) with a net loss of $35.8M. The company carries $3.24B in debt against negative shareholders' equity of -$228M and faces a federal antitrust class action alleging monopoly power in bowling center acquisitions.

Investment Thesis
Lucky Strike Entertainment offers exposure to the only national-scale bowling and location-based entertainment platform with 360+ venues, a 67.81% gross margin business, and structural immunity to AI disruption [6]. The company's FY2027 guidance of $340-360M adjusted EBITDA and ~$50M free cash flow suggests cash flow generation capability despite net losses, and the 3.5% dividend yield provides income while waiting for the turnaround [1]. Multiple insiders — including founder-CEO Thomas Shannon — have been actively purchasing shares at higher prices, with 22 insider buys in the past year signaling management conviction [5]. The revenue 5-year CAGR of 17.87% demonstrates the roll-up model's historical growth trajectory, and further acquisition opportunities remain as the U.S. bowling center industry continues to fragment.
The bear case is severe: Lucky Strike fails the IOWN liquidity floor with average daily dollar volume of approximately $614K, making the stock too illiquid for institutional-scale trading [2]. The balance sheet is deeply distressed — $3.24B in debt against negative shareholders' equity of -$228M, with net losses widening from $10M in FY2025 to $35.8M in FY2026 despite a 4% revenue increase [1][2]. The company's debt service burden is consuming operating profits, turning a 67.81% gross margin business into a net money-loser. Revenue growth has decelerated dramatically to 4% YoY as the acquisition pipeline slows and same-store sales stagnate (essentially flat FY2026). The federal antitrust class action alleging monopoly power in bowling center acquisitions creates meaningful litigation risk and could constrain future M&A — the company's primary growth engine [4]. The ISS Governance QualityScore of 9 (poor, with Board and Shareholder Rights scores of 9 and 10) raises governance concerns. CEO approval of just 51% on Glassdoor and employee descriptions of EBITDA-obsessed culture suggest organizational vulnerabilities.
