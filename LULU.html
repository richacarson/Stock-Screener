Investment Thesis
lululemon trades at a compelling 10.46x forward earnings and 0.87 PEG, reflecting market concerns about Americas weakness and tariff headwinds. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 4% to $2.5B with China surging 30% YoY, but EPS declined 35% to $1.69 due to margin pressure [1]. ROE of 32% and D/E of 44% demonstrate a high-quality balance sheet. The brand's premium positioning, community-driven model, and fabric technology innovation create durable competitive advantages. At current valuation, the stock prices in significant pessimism, creating potential upside if Americas trends stabilize.
The primary risk is the Americas deceleration — comparable sales declined 5% in Q1 FY2026, suggesting the core North American market may be saturating or facing competitive pressure from Vuori, Alo Yoga, and Nike's direct-to-consumer push. EPS declined 35% due to tariff-related cost increases and markdowns, raising questions about margin sustainability. Growing China dependence (now ~19% of revenue) creates geopolitical risk. The FY2026 guidance reflects only modest growth expectations, suggesting management doesn't see a near-term catalyst for Americas reacceleration.
Key Catalysts
  1. China revenue growing 30% YoY could offset Americas weakness and drive overall revenue reacceleration as international mix increases
  2. Valuation reset — at 10.5x forward PE and 0.87 PEG, any positive Americas comp inflection could drive significant multiple expansion
  3. Footwear expansion and men's category growth providing new revenue vectors beyond core women's athletic apparel
Key Risks
  1. Americas comparable sales declining 5% signals potential market saturation or brand fatigue in the core North American market
  2. EPS declined 35% due to tariff headwinds and markdowns, with limited visibility on margin recovery timeline
  3. Growing China dependence creates geopolitical risk — trade tensions, regulatory changes, or consumer sentiment shifts could impact the fastest-growing geography
