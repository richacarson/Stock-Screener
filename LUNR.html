Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NGM ·United States
Intuitive Machines, Inc. is a space infrastructure and services company providing lunar access, data transmission, and infrastructure-as-a-service solutions. The company operates Nova-C and Nova-D lunar landers, the Micro Nova Hopper propulsive drone, and a lunar data network serving NASA (civil and national security), DoD, commercial, and international customers. LUNR achieved the first U.S. commercial lunar landing (IM-1, February 2024) and has won six NASA CLPS missions. Q2 CY2026 delivered record revenue of $206.2M (4x YoY), FY2026 guidance of $900M-$1B, and a record backlog of $1.8B. Founded in 2013 and headquartered in Houston, Texas.
525 Employees ·www.intuitivemachines.com

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 7 /10
Intuitive Machines demonstrates many hallmarks of infinite game thinking under CEO Steve Altemus — a bold Just Cause ('begin a new economy in cislunar space'), the courage to attempt the first commercial lunar landing knowing failure was likely, and a 'forced innovation' philosophy that embraces impossible challenges. The company has shown existential flexibility in pivoting from a pure lunar lander company to a diversified space infrastructure provider. However, the 13-year-old company has not yet proven its cause through decades of consistent execution or survived major crises, and the transition to sustained profitability remains unproven.
Just Cause

Just Cause (2/3): 'Beginning a new economy in cislunar space' is a genuine Just Cause by Sinek's criteria — it is resilient (endures beyond any single mission), inclusive (invites nations, companies, and explorers to participate), service-oriented (benefits humanity's multi-planetary future), idealistic (describes a state that does not yet exist), and big (can never be fully achieved) [2]. The cause drives strategic decisions: pursuing lunar landings despite high failure risk, building infrastructure for sustained presence rather than one-off missions, and diversifying into data services and orbital capabilities. Investment Thesis
Intuitive Machines at $13.50 with a $2.2B market cap is the leading commercial lunar infrastructure company executing a rapid scale-up — Q2 CY2026 delivered record revenue of $206.2M (4x YoY), FY2026 guidance of $900M-$1B with positive adjusted EBITDA, and a record $1.8B backlog with $920M booked in Q2 alone [1]. The company is defining the commercial lunar economy with six NASA CLPS mission awards (the most of any provider), the first U.S. commercial lunar landing (IM-1), and a rapidly diversifying revenue mix that grew national security from 3% to 30% of revenue in one year. The Lanteris acquisition transforms LUNR into a full-spectrum space infrastructure provider, and the $1.8B backlog (7+ quarters of revenue visibility) provides a strong foundation for continued hypergrowth. Management expects 25-30% of backlog to convert in 2026, 35-40% in 2027, creating a multi-year revenue ramp [1].
The bear case centers on profitability risk, execution complexity, and government contract dependency. Despite $490M TTM revenue, the company remains unprofitable with -26.6% profit margin, -30.8% ROE, and trailing EPS of -$0.95 [1]. Forward EPS of -$0.155 implies only marginal improvement, and the path to sustained profitability depends on mission execution, contract margins, and integration synergies from the Lanteris acquisition that are far from guaranteed. The IM-1 mission's partial failure (lander tipped over) raises execution risk questions as the company scales to more complex missions (IM-2, IM-3 with Micro Nova Hopper). NASA civil space represents 38% of revenue and is subject to federal budget politics — any change in Artemis program funding or CLPS contract structure could materially impact the backlog's conversion to revenue [1]. The $2.2B market cap on a company with no GAAP profits prices in significant future growth that is dependent on flawless execution of multiple simultaneous lunar missions, a successful Lanteris integration, and continued government contract wins [1][2].

  3. Government contract dependency — NASA and DoD contracts represent ~68% of revenue, subject to federal budget appropriations, political priorities, and potential Artemis program restructuring that could delay or cancel missions in the $1.8B backlog [1]
