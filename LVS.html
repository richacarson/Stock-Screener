Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Resorts & Casinos ·NYQ ·United States
Las Vegas Sands owns and operates integrated resorts in Macao (The Venetian Macao, The Londoner Macao, The Parisian Macao, The Plaza Macao/Four Seasons, The Sands Macao) and Singapore (Marina Bay Sands), combining gaming, hotel, convention, dining, and retail. Patrick Dumont became Chairman and CEO effective March 1, 2026, succeeding Robert Goldstein who transitioned to a senior advisor role [2]. Q1 2026 net revenue rose 25.3% to $3.59B with net income up 57.1% to $641M; Q2 2026 revenue was $3.15B (-0.7% YoY) with net income of $373M. H1 2026 revenue was $6.74B (+11.6%). The company is developing an $8B ultra-luxury expansion in Singapore that broke ground July 2025.

Investment Thesis
LVS at $38 with a $24.6B market cap is executing a strong Asia-focused strategy under new Chairman/CEO Patrick Dumont, with Q1 2026 net revenue up 25.3% to $3.59B and net income surging 57.1% to $641M, driven by Marina Bay Sands EBITDA of $788M (+30% YoY) and Sands China mass gaming revenue growing 8% versus the broader Macao market's 4% [1]. Forward P/E of 11.2x and PEG of 0.81 are attractive, with the $8B ultra-luxury Singapore expansion (broke ground July 2025) positioning LVS for the next decade of premium hospitality demand in Asia's fastest-growing gaming market [2]. The company generates $3.7B in operating cash flow, returned $984M to shareholders in Q2 alone ($787M buybacks + $197M dividends), and has expanded its buyback authorization to $6B. Integrated resorts combining gaming, conventions, luxury hotels, and retail are structurally immune to AI disruption.
The bear case centers on extreme leverage, cyclical exposure, and governance concerns. D/E of 1704% reflects $15.3B in debt against a razor-thin equity base ($0.90 book value) from the capital-intensive integrated resort model, leaving minimal balance sheet flexibility if Macao or Singapore demand softens [4]. Revenue growth has stalled — Q2 2026 revenue declined 0.7% YoY and net income fell 28.1% to $373M, with the consensus estimate of $3.40B missed by $250M, suggesting the post-COVID recovery momentum is fading [1]. The concentration of revenue in two geographies (Macao and Singapore) exposes LVS to regulatory risk from China's gaming supervision policies and geopolitical tensions that could disrupt tourism flows. The CEO transition from Goldstein to Dumont, while orderly, occurs alongside a family-aligned controlled-company governance structure where the Adelson family retains significant influence [2]. Glassdoor reviews citing political culture and lack of innovation suggest the organization may struggle to adapt beyond its current operational playbook. The 3-year dividend history since COVID resumption does not yet meet the 5-year requirement for Dividend sleeve classification.
