Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Department Stores ·NYSE ·United States
Macy's, Inc. operates approximately 700 retail stores across three brands: Macy's department stores, Bloomingdale's luxury stores, and Bluemercury beauty specialty stores. Under CEO Tony Spring's 'Bold New Chapter' strategy launched in 2024, the company is closing 150 underperforming locations, investing in Reimagine 200 top-performing stores, deploying AI-powered tools including the 'Ask Macy's' shopping assistant, and expanding its luxury positioning through Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury. Q1 FY2026 delivered 3.0% comparable sales growth — the best Q1 in four years — with net sales of $4.7B. Full-year FY2026 guidance: $21.5-$21.75B revenue, $2.00-$2.20 adjusted EPS.

The leadership development investments and cultural transformation show intentionality, but the competitive language centers on defending market position rather than building something enduring beyond the current retail format.

Investment Thesis
Macy's at 10.3x forward earnings with a 3.4% dividend yield represents deep value in a turnaround story showing genuine traction. Q1 FY2026 delivered 3.0% comparable sales growth — the best Q1 in four years — beating consensus on every metric with net sales of $4.7B (+1.8%), adjusted EPS of $0.13 (vs. $0.03 consensus), and five consecutive quarters of better-than-expected results [1]. Bloomingdale's posted record Q1 sales with 10.2% comp growth for seven consecutive quarters, validating the luxury expansion strategy. The company raised full-year guidance to $2.00-$2.20 adjusted EPS. The balance sheet supports the turnaround: $1.3B cash, $2.0B available credit facility, no material debt maturities until 2030, and a 36% payout ratio funding $0.77 annual dividend alongside $50M quarterly share buybacks [1]. The 'Ask Macy's' AI assistant driving 5x engagement per session demonstrates Macy's can leverage technology to enhance the retail experience [2].
The bear case centers on secular decline and margin fragility. Revenue has contracted from $25.45B (FY2023) to guided $21.5-$21.75B (FY2026) — a 15% shrinkage over three fiscal years driven by store closures and structural consumer shift from department stores to e-commerce, off-price (TJX, Ross), and direct-to-consumer brands [1][4]. Net profit margin of 2.94% provides virtually zero buffer against tariff headwinds (gross margin already 30bps lower from tariff impact in Q1), consumer spending weakness, or operational missteps. The 2024 accounting scandal — a single employee hiding $151M in delivery expenses over three years — exposed internal control failures and damaged institutional credibility [3]. The dividend was suspended during COVID in 2020 with only 5 consecutive years of payments since reinstatement, and the 5yr dividend growth CAGR of 5.01% falls below the 6% target. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors despite 13 positive ESG attributions [3].
