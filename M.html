Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Department Stores ·NYQ ·United States
Macy's, Inc. operates approximately 700 retail stores across three brands: Macy's department stores, Bloomingdale's luxury stores, and Bluemercury beauty specialty stores. Under CEO Tony Spring's 'Bold New Chapter' strategy launched in 2024, the company is closing ~150 underperforming locations (timeline extended to 2028 to maximize real estate sale values), investing in approximately 200 Reimagine top-performing stores, and deploying AI-powered tools including the 'Ask Macy's' Gemini-powered shopping assistant. FY2026 (ending Jan 2026) revenue was $22.6B with 3.0% Q1 comp sales growth — the best Q1 in four years. Reimagine stores saw 2.7% same-store sales growth.

Investment Thesis
Macy's at $23.62 with a $6.2B market cap trades at 9.9x forward earnings with a 3.4% dividend yield, representing deep value in a turnaround showing genuine traction. Revenue growth turned positive at 1.2% YoY, ROE crossed above 15% to 16.0%, and D/E improved to 103% as the company deleverages [4]. The Bold New Chapter turnaround delivered Q1 FY2026 comparable sales growth of 3.0% — best in four years — with Bloomingdale's posting record sales with 10.2% comp growth for seven consecutive quarters [1]. The 'Ask Macy's' Gemini-powered AI assistant driving 5x engagement per session demonstrates technology-driven value creation, and the automated China Grove distribution center improves fulfillment efficiency [2]. The closure timeline extension to 2028 to maximize property sale values signals a company operating from financial strength, not distress. The balance sheet supports the turnaround: $1.3B cash, $5.1B debt with no material maturities near-term, and a 27.4% payout ratio funding the growing dividend alongside buybacks [4].
The bear case centers on secular decline and margin fragility. Revenue has contracted from $25.45B (FY2023) to $22.6B (FY2026) — an 11% shrinkage over three fiscal years driven by store closures and the structural consumer shift from department stores to e-commerce, off-price (TJX, Ross), and direct-to-consumer brands [1]. Net profit margin of 3.3% provides virtually zero buffer against tariff headwinds, consumer spending weakness, or operational missteps. Forward EPS of $2.38 implies earnings decline from the $2.72 trailing, and PEG of 2.64 suggests overvaluation relative to growth. The 2024 accounting scandal ($151M in hidden delivery expenses) exposed internal control failures. The dividend was suspended during COVID in 2020 with only 5 consecutive years since reinstatement, and the absolute dividend level ($0.77/share) remains far below the pre-COVID $1.51.
