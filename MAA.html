Mid-America Apartment Communities is an S&P 500 real estate investment trust focused on the ownership, management, acquisition, development, and redevelopment of quality apartment communities primarily in the Southeast, Southwest, and Mid-Atlantic regions of the United States. As of June 30, 2026, MAA owned approximately 104,698 apartment units across 16 states and the District of Columbia. Q2 2026 revenue was $555M (up 1.0% YoY) with Core FFO of $2.08/share. FY2026 Core FFO guidance midpoint is $8.53/share. Founded in 1977, headquartered in Germantown, Tennessee.

Investment Thesis
MAA at $116 is a high-quality Sunbelt apartment REIT trading at a 5.26% dividend yield — the highest yield in recent history — with 14 consecutive years of dividend growth (8.3% 5yr CAGR) and Core FFO payout ratio of 71.7% providing ample coverage [1]. Q2 2026 delivered $555M revenue with Core FFO of $2.08/share ($0.02 above guidance midpoint), and management raised FY2026 earnings guidance to $4.18-$4.50 ($4.34 midpoint) with Core FFO of $8.35-$8.71 ($8.53 midpoint) [1]. The 104,698-unit portfolio in Sunbelt markets benefits from structural migration tailwinds — population and employment growth in the Southeast, Southwest, and Mid-Atlantic — while new apartment supply deliveries are expected to peak mid-2026 and decline meaningfully in 2027-2028, setting up an improving rent growth environment.
The bear case centers on the prolonged Sunbelt supply cycle and deteriorating near-term earnings. Same-store revenue growth is projected at just 0.55% for FY2026, and Core FFO guidance of $8.53 represents a roughly 2.4% decline YoY at the midpoint [1]. The stock has declined 19% from its 52-week high of $144.41 as the market questions when the supply overhang in key markets (Charlotte, Raleigh, Phoenix) will clear. Forward GAAP P/E of 35.5x and PEG of 7.03 are expensive for a company with near-zero revenue growth, suggesting the market is pricing in a recovery that could take longer than expected. ROE of 7.05% is low even by REIT standards (depressed by GAAP depreciation), and D/E of 102% represents meaningful leverage. Management characterized 2026 as a 'transition year,' but transition years can extend — and each additional quarter of supply pressure delays the rent growth recovery that justifies the current valuation [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. New apartment supply deliveries expected to peak mid-2026 and decline meaningfully in 2027-2028, creating a multi-year tailwind for rent growth across MAA's 104,698-unit Sunbelt portfolio [1]
  2. ReiMAAgined Operating Platform rollout expected to expand margins by reducing on-site staffing requirements and automating leasing processes across the portfolio, driving NOI growth independent of rent increases [2]
  3. 5.26% dividend yield at a 14-year low stock price with 32+ years of consecutive payment and 14 years of growth — the highest income yield available from a Sunbelt apartment REIT with this quality and track record [1]
Key Risks
  1. Sunbelt apartment supply cycle is extended — Charlotte, Raleigh, Phoenix, and other key MAA markets face construction completions through 2027, limiting new lease pricing recovery and pressuring same-store revenue growth near 0% [1]
  2. Forward GAAP P/E of 35.5x and PEG of 7.03 leave limited margin of safety for a company with 0.55% projected same-store revenue growth — any further earnings downgrades could drive additional stock price declines from the current 52-week low level [1]
  3. CEO transition from 20+ year veteran Bolton to Hill introduces execution uncertainty during a challenging supply cycle, and the elevated D/E of 102% limits financial flexibility for opportunistic acquisitions or development acceleration [1][2]
