Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Macerich is a fully integrated, self-managed REIT and leading owner, operator, and developer of high-quality retail real estate in densely populated U.S. markets. The portfolio of approximately 39 million square feet across 38 retail centers is concentrated in California, the Pacific Northwest, Phoenix/Scottsdale, and the Metro New York to Washington, D.C. corridor. Under CEO Jackson Hsieh (appointed March 2024), the company's Path Forward Plan targets balance sheet improvement and operational acceleration, with Q2 2026 delivering FFO of $100.4M and 3.8% go-forward NOI growth. Macerich acquired Annapolis Mall for $272M in 2026, marking a shift from net dispositions to selective acquisitions.

Investment Thesis
Macerich offers exposure to Class A retail real estate in top-tier U.S. markets with improving operating fundamentals and strong CEO alignment. Q2 2026 FFO of $100.4M ($0.35/share) grew from $88.7M in Q2 2025, go-forward portfolio NOI grew 3.8%, and management guides acceleration to 8%+ NOI growth in 2027-2028 as the $140M SNO pipeline contributes rent [1]. The $272M Annapolis Mall acquisition marks a shift from net dispositions to selective growth, and CEO Hsieh has backed the turnaround with $3M in personal stock purchases [2][6]. The 2.93% dividend yield is supported by a 32-year payment history, GRESB #1 U.S. retail ranking for 10 consecutive years positions the company for ESG-focused capital flows, and an Inspire Impact Score of 55 (ALIGNED) adds appeal for faith-based investors [4].
The bear case centers on persistent negative GAAP fundamentals and cultural concerns. Profit margin of -17.1%, ROE of -6.18%, and D/E of 171.5% reflect a balance sheet still under stress despite improving NOI trends [2]. Revenue of $249.7M in Q2 2026 was nearly flat YoY, and six-month revenue declined 1.6%, indicating the topline has not yet inflected. The dividend was cut 80% in 2020 with zero consecutive years of growth since, and the 133% GAAP payout ratio (distorted by REIT depreciation) highlights the thin margin between FFO and distribution obligations. Hsieh is the third CEO in a decade, and Glassdoor reviews (3.3/5) describe cultural deterioration — employees say 'new leadership has changed much of the things people loved about the company' — adding execution risk to a plan that requires sustained operational improvement [5]. The secular shift to e-commerce continues, with AI-enhanced online shopping potentially accelerating the trend, and the portfolio contraction from 47 to 38 properties since 2019 underscores structural headwinds for mall-based retail.

  2. Third CEO in a decade creates strategic execution risk, with Glassdoor reviews (3.3/5) citing cultural deterioration under Hsieh's leadership and employee concerns about nepotism in upper management [5]
