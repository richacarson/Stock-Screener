Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Macerich is a fully integrated, self-managed, self-administered real estate investment trust (REIT) and leading owner, operator, and developer of high-quality retail real estate in densely populated U.S. markets. The portfolio is concentrated in California, the Pacific Northwest, Phoenix/Scottsdale, and the Metro New York to Washington, D.C. corridor, currently owning approximately 39 million square feet across 38 retail centers.

Investment Thesis
Macerich offers exposure to Class A retail real estate in top-tier U.S. markets (California, Pacific Northwest, Metro NYC-DC corridor) with improving operating fundamentals. Q1 2026 FFO of $92.4M grew 5.7% YoY, TTM revenue reached $1.01B (up 4.97% YoY), and management guides at least 3% go-forward NOI growth for 2026 with acceleration expected in 2027-2028 as SNO pipeline tenants open [1]. The 3.2% dividend yield provides income supported by a 32-year payment history, and GRESB #1 ranking for 10 consecutive years in U.S. retail positions the company for ESG-focused capital flows [4]. Shares trade at $24.75 against properties in affluent, high-barrier-to-entry locations.
The bear case centers on persistent negative GAAP fundamentals: -17.9% profit margin, -7% ROE, and 199% D/E ratio reflect a balance sheet still under stress despite improving NOI trends [2]. The dividend was cut 80% in 2020 and consecutive years of growth remains zero, creating uncertainty about future payout sustainability. CEO Jackson Hsieh is the third leader in a decade, and Glassdoor reviews describe culture deterioration under his leadership ('driving people away in droves'), adding execution risk to the 'Path Forward Plan' [5]. The secular shift toward e-commerce continues to pressure mall-based retail despite Class A positioning, and the portfolio contraction from 47 to 38 properties reflects managed decline rather than a growth trajectory. With no clear path to GAAP profitability, the REIT's required dividend payments from thinning FFO create value trap risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. Path Forward Plan delivering at least 3% NOI growth in 2026 with acceleration to higher rates in 2027-2028 as SNO pipeline tenants begin paying rent, validating the leasing turnaround thesis [1]
  2. Mixed-use redevelopment completions converting underperforming retail into residential and dining, diversifying revenue and increasing property values in high-demand urban markets
  3. Potential interest rate cuts reducing refinancing costs on the substantial debt load, directly improving FFO and making the 3.2% dividend more sustainable while compressing cap rates on Class A retail assets [2]
Key Risks
  1. GAAP profitability remains negative (-17.9% margin, -7% ROE) and the 199% D/E ratio limits financial flexibility — if FFO stagnates or interest rates remain elevated, a second dividend cut becomes a real possibility [2]
  2. Third CEO in a decade (Jackson Hsieh appointed 2024) creates strategic execution risk and cultural instability, with Glassdoor reviews citing leadership 'driving people away in droves' from a small 596-person organization [5]
  3. Continued secular shift to e-commerce and experiential retail could reduce tenant demand, with anchor store closures or rent concessions compressing NOI across the shrinking 38-property portfolio [1]
