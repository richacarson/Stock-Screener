Investment Thesis
ManpowerGroup's Q2 2026 earnings beat ($1.13 EPS vs expectations, $4.9B revenue with 6% CC growth) confirms a broadening staffing recovery, with US revenue surging 16% driven by manufacturing, retail, aerospace, and logistics demand [1]. The stock has rallied 64% from its April trough to $51.33 but still trades at just 12.6x forward earnings — cheap relative to the recovery trajectory. The $200M AI-powered sales targeting engine in France demonstrates that ManpowerGroup can be a technology-enabled staffing leader rather than a traditional intermediary threatened by AI [2]. The $200M strategic transformation program should deliver permanent cost savings by 2028, and the 37-year dividend payment history at the stabilized $1.44 annual rate (2.79% yield, 65.5% payout ratio) provides income while the recovery plays out. Management guides for continued 6% growth into Q3 2026 [1].
The bear case centers on structural AI disruption and the cyclical nature of staffing demand. Despite the Q2 recovery, revenue of $18.38B TTM remains 11% below the 2021 peak of $20.7B, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of -2.4% suggests secular headwinds from AI hiring platforms and gig economy alternatives persist [5]. The trailing P/E of 23.33x is elevated relative to the 0.56% net margin, and ROE of 5.08% indicates the business generates poor returns on equity even in recovery. The dividend was cut 53% in 2025 — future economic slowdowns could force another reduction. ManpowerGroup's Glassdoor rating of 3.6/5 with criticism of outdated technology suggests internal culture may not support the AI transformation required to defend against platform competitors. The staffing industry faces a structural triple threat: AI automating talent matching, gig platforms bypassing agencies, and economic cyclicality compressing demand during downturns [3].
