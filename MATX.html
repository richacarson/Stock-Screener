Company Profile
Industrials ·Marine Shipping / Logistics ·NYSE ·United States
Matson, Inc. is a 142-year-old ocean transportation and logistics company providing container shipping services primarily in the Pacific trade lanes. The company operates Matson Navigation (Hawaii, Alaska, Guam, China-Long Beach Express), Matson Logistics (supply chain services), and Matson Terminals (Hawaii terminal operations). Protected by the Jones Act in domestic trade lanes. Q2 2026 delivered $969.4M revenue (+16.7% YoY), operating income of $158.9M (+40.6%), and EPS of $4.27. Full-year 2026 operating income guided to exceed 2025's $499.8M. Market cap approximately $6.7B.

Investment Thesis
Matson delivered a strong Q2 2026 with $969.4M revenue (+16.7% YoY), operating income of $158.9M (+40.6%), and EPS of $4.27, with full-year 2026 operating income guided to exceed 2025's $499.8M [1]. At 13.10x forward earnings, Matson offers compelling value for a Jones Act-protected franchise with a dominant duopoly position in Hawaii shipping, an ultra-low D/E of 26%, and accelerating China-Long Beach Express demand. The logistics segment's 30.4% revenue growth to $202M demonstrates successful diversification. Matson's $1B green fleet investment, 25% Scope 1 emissions reduction, and 2026 USCG Benkert Environmental Award position the company ahead of tightening maritime emissions regulations [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of 39 (ALIGNED) and 83% employee satisfaction make Matson an attractive holding for values-oriented investors.
The bear case centers on growth limitations and cyclical exposure. Revenue normalized from the 2022 pandemic peak of $4.3B, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of only 7% reflects a mature, cyclical business with limited growth levers beyond Jones Act-protected trade lanes [4]. Profit margins of 12.92% are modest for a company with regulatory protection, and the 0.68% dividend yield provides minimal income support despite 14 consecutive years of increases. The Jones Act is politically controversial — any erosion of this regulatory moat would expose Matson to competition from lower-cost foreign-flag carriers. Transpacific shipping rates remain volatile and influenced by geopolitical dynamics beyond Matson's control, with Q4 2026 demand expected to reflect 'more traditional seasonality patterns,' suggesting deceleration from peak-season strength [1].
