Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NASDAQ ·United States
Microbot Medical is a medical device company focused on transforming endovascular procedures through advanced robotic technology. Its flagship LIBERTY Endovascular Robotic System is the first single-use, remotely operated robotic solution that allows physicians to conduct catheter-based procedures from outside the catheterization laboratory, reducing radiation exposure and physical strain. The company achieved first commercial revenue in Q1 2026 and reported >100% QoQ revenue and customer growth in Q2 2026.
43 Employees ·microbotmedical.com

Investment Thesis
Microbot Medical offers early-stage exposure to the endovascular robotics market through LIBERTY, the first single-use, remotely operated robotic system for catheter-based procedures. Q1 2026 marked first commercial revenue ($105K), followed by >100% QoQ revenue and customer growth in Q2 2026 as leading health systems in Massachusetts, North Carolina, Michigan, and Pennsylvania adopted the system [1][2]. The company is expanding from 8 to 12 U.S. sales territories, secured Israeli MOH clearance for international expansion, and partnered with Sanmina for manufacturing scale-up [6]. With $80.2M cash, near-zero debt (D/E 1%), and FY2026 revenue targeting $4M+, the $120M market cap prices in significant growth optionality. The addressable market exceeds 2 million U.S. endovascular procedures annually, and LIBERTY's single-use design eliminates the capital equipment barrier limiting competing systems.
The bear case is dominated by commercial execution risk and competitive pressure. A May 2026 short seller report from White Diamond Research questioned LIBERTY's commercial viability, citing an anonymous surgeon who noted the system increases procedure costs by $3,000-$4,000 without significant advantages over manual techniques — the stock fell 7%+ following the report [4]. With only $105K Q1 revenue after 16 years of development, the $4M FY2026 target requires dramatic acceleration. The 43-person organization must compete against Intuitive Surgical ($180B+), Siemens Healthineers, and J&J, whose sales forces and clinical evidence dwarf MBOT's capabilities. Average daily volume of 620K shares at $1.78 produces $1.1M daily traded value — barely above the $1M liquidity floor, creating execution risk for institutional investors [5]. Additional capital raises will likely be needed despite the current $80M cash position, creating dilution risk in a stock already trading near 52-week lows.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Microbot's core business involves manufacturing and selling physical robotic surgical systems with FDA regulatory clearance — AI cannot substitute for the hardware that must be manufactured, sterilized, and deployed in catheterization labs [1]. Physical-world medical robotics has strong regulatory barriers that protect against pure-software AI disruption. However, the endovascular robotics space sits directly adjacent to autonomous AI-driven surgical systems, where Intuitive Surgical, Siemens Healthineers, and J&J are investing heavily in AI-enhanced navigation capabilities. If autonomous AI-guided catheter navigation advances rapidly, MBOT's human-operated remote robotics approach could face obsolescence pressure from AI-native competitors with vastly greater resources. AI Opportunity (1/1): The Emory University collaboration on autonomous robotics in endovascular procedures positions Microbot to integrate AI capabilities into future LIBERTY iterations [3]. While no AI revenue is quantifiable today, the autonomous robotics R&D represents direct AI product development with a credible academic partner.
