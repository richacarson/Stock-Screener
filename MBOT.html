Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NASDAQ ·United States
Microbot Medical is a medical device company focused on transforming endovascular procedures through advanced robotic technology. Its flagship LIBERTY Endovascular Robotic System is the first single-use, remotely operated robotic solution allowing physicians to conduct catheter-based procedures from outside the catheterization laboratory, reducing radiation exposure and physical strain. Following full market release in April 2026, the company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $241K (>100% QoQ growth) with YTD revenue of $346K, expanding from 4 to 8 U.S. sales territories and targeting 12 by year-end. Israeli MOH clearance was secured for international expansion.
43 Employees ·microbotmedical.com

Investment Thesis
Microbot Medical offers early-stage exposure to endovascular robotics through LIBERTY, the first single-use, remotely operated robotic system for catheter-based procedures. Q2 2026 revenue of $241K represented >100% QoQ growth from Q1's $105K, with YTD revenue of $346K as leading health systems across Massachusetts, North Carolina, Michigan, and Pennsylvania adopt the system [1]. The company is scaling rapidly — expanding from 4 to 8 U.S. territories with 12 targeted by year-end — and Israeli MOH clearance opens the first international market [2]. With $67.4M cash, near-zero debt (D/E 1%), and 67.16M shares outstanding, the ~$97M market cap prices in significant growth optionality against a 2M+ annual U.S. endovascular procedure addressable market. LIBERTY's single-use design eliminates the capital equipment barrier limiting competing systems, and the Sanmina manufacturing partnership enables production scale-up [2].
The bear case is dominated by commercial execution risk and competitive pressure. A May 2026 short seller report from White Diamond Research questioned LIBERTY's commercial viability, citing an anonymous surgeon who noted the system increases procedure costs by $3,000-$4,000 without significant advantages over manual techniques [4]. With only $346K YTD revenue after 16 years of development, dramatic acceleration is required to justify the current valuation. The 43-person organization must compete against Intuitive Surgical ($180B+), Siemens Healthineers, and J&J, whose sales forces and clinical evidence dwarf MBOT's capabilities. Cash position has declined from $80.2M to $67.4M, and at the current ~$22M annual burn rate, additional capital raises will be needed by mid-2029, creating dilution risk at a depressed stock price of $1.45 [4]. Average daily traded value of ~$1.45M is dangerously close to the $1M liquidity floor — any further stock price decline or volume reduction could breach the threshold.
Key Catalysts
  1. Accelerating commercial adoption with >100% QoQ revenue growth in Q2 2026 and expansion from 4 to 8 U.S. territories, targeting 12 by year-end — each new territory represents a step-function increase in the addressable customer pipeline [1][2]
  2. Israeli MOH clearance as the first international market approval, with potential additional country clearances in 2027, significantly expanding the total addressable market beyond the 2M+ annual U.S. endovascular procedures [2]
  3. Sanmina manufacturing partnership enabling production scale-up and unit cost reduction, potentially improving gross margins from near-breakeven levels as volume increases [2]
Key Risks
  1. Short seller White Diamond Research questioned LIBERTY's value proposition — if surgeons broadly conclude the $3-4K cost increase per procedure lacks sufficient clinical benefit over manual techniques, adoption will stall regardless of territory expansion [4]
  2. Liquidity floor risk: avg daily traded value of ~$1.45M sits dangerously close to the $1M threshold — a further stock price decline from $1.45 or volume reduction could trigger an infrastructure score of zero and signal institutional uninvestability
  3. Competitive response from Intuitive Surgical, Siemens (Corindus), and J&J (Monarch), whose combined R&D budgets exceed $10B+ annually, could produce competing single-use or AI-enhanced endovascular platforms that neutralize LIBERTY's first-mover advantage

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Microbot's core business involves manufacturing and selling physical robotic surgical systems with FDA regulatory clearance — AI cannot substitute for the hardware that must be manufactured, sterilized, and deployed in catheterization labs [1]. Physical-world medical robotics has strong regulatory barriers that protect against pure-software AI disruption. However, the endovascular robotics space sits directly adjacent to autonomous AI-driven surgical systems, where Intuitive Surgical, Siemens Healthineers, and J&J are investing heavily in AI-enhanced navigation and autonomous catheter guidance. If AI-native autonomous catheterization advances rapidly, MBOT's human-operated remote robotics approach could face obsolescence pressure from competitors with vastly greater resources. AI Opportunity (1/1): The Emory University collaboration on autonomous robotics in endovascular procedures positions Microbot to integrate AI capabilities into future LIBERTY iterations [3]. While no AI revenue is quantifiable today, the autonomous robotics R&D represents direct AI product development with a credible academic partner.
