Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·United States
Moelis & Company is a leading global independent investment bank providing strategic advisory services to corporations, financial sponsors, and governments across approximately 20 global offices. Founded by Ken Moelis in 2007 during the financial crisis, the firm specializes in mergers and acquisitions, recapitalizations, restructurings, and other corporate finance advisory services. In October 2025, co-founder Navid Mahmoodzadegan succeeded Ken Moelis as CEO. The firm reported record Q2 2026 revenues of $409.4 million, up 12% year-over-year, with an announced advisory pipeline up over 80% versus the prior year.

Investment Thesis
Moelis delivered record Q2 2026 revenues of $409.4M (+12% YoY) with an advisory pipeline up 80%+ year-over-year, positioning for a strong H2 as the M&A cycle continues recovering [1]. The forward P/E of 15.67 with a 3.79% dividend yield and 44.68% ROE offers value for an advisory firm operating near net-cash ($13.8M net cash position). Adjusted pre-tax margin improved to 18.6% in Q2, demonstrating operating leverage as revenue scales, and the 12.50% 5-year dividend growth CAGR reflects management's commitment to returning capital across the volatile M&A cycle [3].
The bear case centers on cultural deterioration and cyclical risk. Glassdoor reviews describe a 'rapid move away from boutique identity' under new CEO Navid Mahmoodzadegan, with reports that 'most capable individuals have left' — threatening the firm's core asset: its people [2]. Ken Moelis's consistent insider selling (11 sells, 0 buys over 5 years) raises alignment questions. Revenue collapsed 38% from the 2021 peak ($1,540.6M) to 2022 ($985.3M), and another M&A downturn could replicate this compression. The estimated 87% payout ratio on GAAP earnings leaves thin dividend coverage if deal activity reverses, and AI tools for financial modeling and due diligence could structurally reduce demand for junior bankers, pressuring advisory economics over time.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record advisory pipeline (up 80%+ YoY at Q2 2026 end) converting to strong H2 revenue, potentially driving full-year results above consensus and triggering a valuation re-rating from the 15.67x forward P/E [1]
  2. Expansion into private capital advisory and new business lines under the institutional model creates revenue diversification beyond traditional M&A, reducing cyclical earnings volatility
  3. Continued M&A cycle recovery driven by pent-up deal activity, private equity sponsor exits, and corporate restructuring needs supports multi-year revenue tailwind
Key Risks
  1. CEO transition driving cultural deterioration and talent attrition — Glassdoor reports 'most capable individuals have left' since the leadership change, threatening the relationship-driven advisory model's core competitive advantage [2]
  2. Cyclical M&A market reversal from geopolitical shock or economic downturn would compress revenue 30-40% as demonstrated by the 2021-2022 collapse from $1,540.6M to $985.3M, with limited visibility into fee timing
  3. AI-powered financial modeling and due diligence automation could structurally reduce demand for junior bankers and compress advisory fee pools, particularly as clients adopt AI tools that reduce reliance on external advisors
