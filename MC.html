Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·United States
Moelis & Company is a leading global independent investment bank providing strategic advisory services to corporations, financial sponsors, and governments across approximately 20 global offices. Founded by Ken Moelis in 2007 during the financial crisis, the firm specializes in mergers and acquisitions, recapitalizations, restructurings, and other corporate finance advisory services. Navid Mahmoodzadegan succeeded Ken Moelis as CEO effective October 1, 2025 and has publicly pledged to push workaholic 'hazing' culture out of the firm. The firm reported record Q2 2026 revenue of $409.4 million (+12% YoY) and H1 2026 revenue of $729.2 million (+9% YoY), and declared a quarterly dividend of $0.65 per share, an increase from the prior $0.60 rate.

Investment Thesis
Moelis delivered record Q2 2026 revenue of $409.4M (+12% YoY) and H1 2026 revenue of $729.2M (+9% YoY), with the board raising the quarterly dividend to $0.65 per share, signaling confidence in the M&A cycle recovery [1]. The forward P/E of 14.98 with a 4.43% dividend yield and 41.45% ROE offers value for an advisory firm operating with low leverage (D/E 40.96%), and new CEO Mahmoodzadegan's public commitment to reform the firm's workaholic culture addresses a real organizational risk head-on rather than ignoring it [2][3].
The bear case centers on cultural transition risk and cyclicality. Mahmoodzadegan's public pledge to remove 'hazing' from the culture is itself an admission that the prior environment was dysfunctional, and it remains unproven whether the institutional-scale pivot will preserve the relationship-driven talent that built the firm's franchise [2][3]. The estimated 88% payout ratio on trailing GAAP earnings leaves thin dividend coverage if deal activity slows, and Ken Moelis's history as a net seller of shares raises alignment questions even as he remains executive chair. AI tools for financial modeling and due diligence could structurally reduce demand for junior bankers over time, and any reversal in the current M&A recovery would quickly compress advisory fee revenue as it has in prior cycles.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record H1 2026 revenue and pipeline strength converting into a strong H2, potentially driving full-year results that justify a re-rating from the current 14.98x forward P/E [1]
  2. Successful culture reform under CEO Mahmoodzadegan retaining senior banking talent through the institutional-model transition, preserving the relationship-driven franchise value [2][3]
  3. Continued M&A cycle recovery and expansion into private capital advisory diversifying revenue beyond traditional M&A, reducing cyclical earnings volatility
Key Risks
  1. Unproven culture reform under new leadership — the CEO's public 'hazing' pledge confirms past dysfunction, and failure to retain senior bankers during the institutional-scale pivot would erode the firm's core relationship-driven advantage [2][3]
  2. Elevated ~88% payout ratio on trailing earnings leaves limited dividend cushion if a cyclical M&A downturn compresses fee revenue, as occurred in the 2022-2023 downturn
  3. AI-powered financial modeling and due diligence automation could structurally reduce demand for junior bankers and compress advisory fee pools over time
