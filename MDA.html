Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·Canada
MDA Space Ltd. is Canada's largest space technology developer and manufacturer, founded in 1969. The company operates through three business areas: Satellite Systems (communications and Earth observation satellites, 67% of revenue), Robotics & Space Operations (Canadarm technology for NASA and ISS, next-generation SKYMAKER platform), and Geointelligence (satellite imagery and analytics). MDA built the original Canadarm, Canadarm2, and Dextre, and is developing AI-enabled Canadarm3 for the Lunar Gateway. Listed on NYSE following a March 2026 US IPO, the company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $498.6M (+33.6% YoY) and raised full-year guidance to CAD $1.85B midpoint. Market cap is approximately $4.8B with a record backlog of $4B.

Investment Thesis
MDA Space is executing a powerful growth trajectory with Q2 2026 revenue of $498.6M (+33.6% YoY), a raised full-year guidance midpoint of CAD $1.85B, and a record $4B backlog providing 8 quarters of revenue visibility [1][3]. The valuation has become significantly more attractive with Forward P/E declining to 19.2x (from 38.6x at the prior screen) as earnings catch up to revenue growth, and estimated forward PEG of ~0.64 suggests the growth rate more than justifies the current multiple [6]. Revenue doubled from ~$808M in 2023 to $1.633B in 2025, driven by Telesat Lightspeed and Globalstar constellation programs. With D/E of just 20%, $1.1B in total liquidity, and expanding access to US defense markets post-NYSE IPO, MDA has a strong foundation. Canadarm3 for Lunar Gateway positions the company for a multi-decade franchise in cislunar space operations.
The bear case centers on profitability and execution risk. Profit margin of 5.66% and ROE of 6.68% remain low, reflecting the heavy investment phase with $225-275M capex keeping free cash flow neutral-to-negative [6]. The stock has declined 15% from $34.26 to $29.64 since the last screen, and significant insider selling ($4.6M net over 12 months, $10.5M+ from Director Risley) raises governance concerns [5]. Backlog concentration in Telesat Lightspeed and Canadarm3 creates program risk — delays, cost overruns, or cancellations in either flagship program would materially impact the revenue trajectory. The Blue Canyon acquisition ($620M) adds integration risk and diluted EPS growth despite strong top-line expansion. The company faces competitive pressure as the space industry grows and new entrants challenge incumbents in satellite manufacturing and Earth observation analytics.

  1. Canadarm3 development for Lunar Gateway represents a multi-decade franchise extension of MDA's most iconic technology, with potential for follow-on contracts as cislunar infrastructure expands and NASA/international partners commit to sustained lunar presence [2]
