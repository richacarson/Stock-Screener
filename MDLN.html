Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NMS ·United States
Medline manufactures and distributes medical-surgical products serving hospitals, surgery centers, physician offices, post-acute facilities, and nursing homes across two segments: Medline Brand (surgical solutions, front line care, laboratory/diagnostics) and Supply Chain Solutions (third-party distribution, logistics optimization, consulting). The company IPO'd on Nasdaq in December 2025 at a ~$46B valuation backed by Blackstone, Carlyle, and Hellman & Friedman, raising approximately $8B. FY2025 net sales rose 11.5% to $28.4B with net income of $1.2B, and 2026 guidance projects 8-9% organic sales growth with adjusted EBITDA of $3.5-3.6B.

Investment Thesis
Medline at $35.19 is a recently public healthcare distribution giant with $29.9B in TTM revenue and a dominant position as North America's largest med-surg manufacturer and distributor. The company's vertically integrated model — manufacturing 300,000+ SKUs across surgical, front line care, and laboratory products while operating 70+ distribution centers — provides structural advantages in procurement efficiency, product margin, and customer stickiness [1][2]. FY2025 net sales grew 11.5% to $28.4B, Q4 revenue surged 14.8% to $7.8B, and 2026 guidance projects 8-9% organic growth with $3.5-3.6B adjusted EBITDA, supporting the earnings normalization thesis from trailing $0.39 EPS to forward $1.58 EPS [1][3].
The bear case centers on the PE-loaded capital structure and razor-thin margins. Total debt of $12.6B against $2.7B cash reflects the 2021 leveraged buyout, and the 2.3% profit margin leaves minimal room for error in a business where distribution margins are inherently compressed [3]. The trailing P/E of 90x is misleading due to IPO-related charges, but even the forward P/E of 22x prices in significant earnings recovery that depends on margin expansion in a competitive distribution market where McKesson, Cardinal Health, and Owens & Minor operate at similar scale. Organic growth guidance of 8-9% for 2026 already decelerates from FY2025's 11.5%, and the company faces margin pressure from healthcare cost containment efforts by hospitals and payers [1]. PE sponsor sell-downs post-lockup expiration could create sustained selling pressure on the stock.
Key Catalysts
  1. Earnings normalization from trailing $0.39 EPS to forward $1.58 EPS as IPO-related charges and interest costs stabilize, potentially re-rating the stock from 90x trailing to 22x forward earnings [1][3]
  2. Post-IPO deleveraging from $12.6B total debt using $2.0B operating cash flow and IPO proceeds, with each turn of leverage reduction expanding equity value and reducing interest expense that currently compresses net margins [3]
  3. Cross-selling Medline Brand products into the Supply Chain Solutions customer base to drive margin mix improvement, as proprietary products carry meaningfully higher margins than distributed third-party brands [2]
Key Risks
  1. PE sponsor sell-downs by Blackstone, Carlyle, and Hellman & Friedman post-lockup expiration could create sustained technical selling pressure on a $47B market cap stock with limited public float history [1]
  2. Razor-thin 2.3% profit margin leaves no buffer for execution missteps, input cost inflation, or pricing pressure from hospital systems and GPOs (group purchasing organizations) demanding better terms [3]
  3. Healthcare cost containment by hospitals and payers could compress med-surg procurement budgets, pressuring Medline's 8-9% organic growth guidance and limiting pricing power in a competitive distribution market [1]
