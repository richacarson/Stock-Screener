Investment Thesis
MercadoLibre at $1,920 is Latin America's dominant commerce-and-fintech ecosystem delivering extraordinary top-line growth — Q2 2026 record revenue of $10.2B (+50% YoY) with commerce GMV accelerating, fintech AUM up 77%, and TTM revenue of $31.8B (+42%) [1][2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 45% reflects sustained secular growth as Latin American e-commerce penetration (~15%) converges toward developed-market levels (~30%). ROE of 31.26% demonstrates efficient capital deployment, and the forward PEG of 1.41 is reasonable relative to the growth trajectory. The orderly CEO transition with Galperin remaining as Executive Chairman focused on strategy, culture, and AI preserves institutional continuity while bringing operational focus under Szarfsztejn [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of 51 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions and 14 positive categories makes MELI accessible to faith-based mandates.
The bear case centers on margin compression, valuation risk, and LatAm macro volatility. Operating income declined 17% in Q2 2026 despite 50% revenue growth as management deliberately invested in credit cards, fulfillment, and first-party retail — EPS actually fell 12.5% YoY, meaning revenue is growing far faster than profits [2]. At 47.48x forward P/E with only 4.6% profit margin, the $97B market cap prices in substantial and sustained profit acceleration that has not yet materialized. D/E of 170% reflects growing balance sheet risk from the fintech lending book where credit losses could spike during a LatAm economic downturn. Currency risk is structural: the Argentine peso, Brazilian real, and Mexican peso introduce significant FX headwinds (Q2 FX-neutral growth was 43% vs. reported 50%). The CEO transition from founder Galperin introduces execution risk as Szarfsztejn must prove he can maintain MELI's innovation cadence and aggressive investment posture without the founder at the operational helm [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Latin American e-commerce penetration at ~15% has significant runway to converge toward developed-market levels (~30%), providing a multi-year secular tailwind for GMV growth across MELI's 18-country footprint [1]
  2. Mercado Pago's fintech ecosystem — with 77% AUM growth and expanding credit card, lending, and insurance products — is building a digital banking moat that could re-rate the stock toward fintech multiples as the business achieves profitability scale [2]
  3. Deliberate margin compression from investment in fulfillment infrastructure and first-party retail should drive operating leverage in 2027-2028 as fixed costs are absorbed against growing revenue, potentially accelerating EPS growth after the current investment cycle completes [2]
Key Risks
  1. EPS declined 12.5% YoY despite 50% revenue growth, reflecting aggressive investment spending that compresses margins — at 47.48x forward P/E with 4.6% profit margin, any failure to convert top-line growth into earnings acceleration will pressure the valuation severely [2]
  2. Growing credit portfolio through Mercado Credito exposes MELI to consumer credit risk across volatile Latin American economies — a recession in Brazil or Argentina could spike non-performing loans and force provisions that devastate earnings from the fintech segment [1]
  3. CEO transition from founder Marcos Galperin to Ariel Szarfsztejn introduces execution risk during a period of accelerating investment and margin compression, testing whether MELI's culture of long-term thinking and aggressive capital deployment survives without the founder as CEO [3]
