Meta Platforms develops products enabling people to connect and share through mobile devices, PCs, VR headsets, and AI glasses. Investment Thesis
Meta at 19.27x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.90 is executing a generational AI infrastructure buildout while delivering 28% revenue growth. Q2 2026 revenue of $60.8B and Q3 guidance of $61-64B demonstrate the Family of Apps advertising engine is accelerating through AI optimization — ad impressions up 14%, average price per ad up 12%, with LLMs driving 8.3% more ad clicks and 15.7% higher conversions on Facebook [1]. ROE of 29.85%, profit margin of 29.8%, and 5-year revenue CAGR of ~20% reflect world-class profitability sustained at scale. The $130-$145B 2026 CapEx program positions Meta as one of three companies (alongside Microsoft and Google) building the foundational AI compute layer, with the AI flywheel already working: Meta AI sessions show double-digit growth, AI glasses daily users tripled YoY, and Family of Apps Other revenue surged 73% in Q2 2026 [1][2]. The stock trades below the S&P 500 P/E despite delivering superior growth, profitability, and strategic positioning.
The primary risk is the magnitude and uncertainty of AI investment returns. The $130-$145B 2026 CapEx represents ~57% of trailing revenue — an unprecedented capital intensity for a digital advertising business. Q2 2026 already showed the strain: net income fell 14% to $15.8B and operating margin compressed from 43% to 31% as $31.1B in quarterly CapEx flowed through [1]. If AI infrastructure spending does not generate proportional returns through improved ad monetization, new AI products, or AI-as-a-service revenue, the capital destruction would be massive. Reality Labs continues to burn billions annually with no clear path to profitability. Regulatory risks are escalating across multiple jurisdictions: EU fines exceeding EUR 2B cumulatively for antitrust and GDPR violations, FTC antitrust appeal, and European challenges to Meta's AI privacy policies [3]. The platform faces persistent criticism for harmful content (misinformation, mental health impacts on minors, data exploitation) creating regulatory headwinds. Zuckerberg's dual-class share structure concentrating voting power means the $130-$145B CapEx decision is effectively one person's judgment call. The Inspire Impact Score of -93 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors [3].
