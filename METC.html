Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Coking Coal ·NasdaqGS ·United States
Ramaco Resources is a dual-platform company operating as a producer of high-quality, low-cost metallurgical coal in Central Appalachia and a developing producer of rare earth elements and critical minerals from coal deposits in Wyoming. Approximately two-thirds of met coal production is exported. The Brook Mine project in Sheridan, Wyoming represents a potentially transformative rare earth initiative, with proprietary carbochlorination processing technology, DOE NETL partnership, and an MOU with REalloys. FY2025 revenue was $536.6M (down 19.5%) with a net loss of $51.4M.

Investment Thesis
Ramaco Resources is a speculative dual-platform story — a struggling met coal producer making a bold pivot into rare earth elements and critical minerals. The core met coal business generated $536.6M in FY2025 revenue (down 19.5%) with a $51.4M net loss, while Q2 2026 showed sequential improvement with $144.8M revenue beating estimates but a continued $0.26 EPS loss [1][3]. The investment case rests on the Brook Mine rare earth project, where Ramaco has developed proprietary carbochlorination processing technology, partnered with DOE NETL, signed an MOU with REalloys for MREC supply, and raised nearly $1B for development [4][6]. Analysts have raised fair value estimates from $27 to $50, reflecting the critical minerals optionality [8]. The national security thesis is compelling — U.S. dependence on Chinese rare earth supply is a strategic vulnerability, and CEO Atkins' appointment to the National Coal Council positions Ramaco at the policy-commercial intersection.
The fundamental challenge is that Ramaco is a commodity met coal company with deeply negative financials — negative profit margin, -12.16% ROE, revenue declining 19.5% — attempting a capital-intensive technology pivot into unproven rare earth processing [1][3]. The Brook Mine carbochlorination flowsheet has not been demonstrated at commercial scale, and the strategic shift toward mixed rare earth carbonate adds technological risk. D/E of 97% is elevated for a cyclical commodity business in a downturn, and the company is paying a 2.6% dividend and repurchased $37M in shares while operating at a loss — capital allocation that prioritizes shareholder appeasement over financial prudence [3]. Met coal pricing remains depressed with limited near-term recovery catalysts, and the global met coal market faces secular headwinds from electric arc furnace steelmaking and hydrogen-based direct reduction. Director Lawrence's $27.9M in stock sales undermines the insider conviction narrative despite the corporate buyback activity.
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful commissioning of the Brook Mine pilot processing plant and validation of proprietary carbochlorination technology at scale, which would de-risk the critical minerals thesis and potentially trigger significant re-rating toward the $50 analyst fair value
  2. U.S. government policy support for domestic critical minerals production — DOE grants, tax credits, strategic reserves purchases, or defense procurement mandates — accelerating Brook Mine commercialization
  3. Recovery in global metallurgical coal pricing driven by infrastructure spending or supply disruptions, restoring core business profitability and self-funding the REE pivot
Key Risks
  1. Core met coal business is unprofitable with -9.6% profit margin, -19.5% revenue decline, and negative EBITDA, creating cash burn that could force dilutive capital raises or compromise the rare earth pivot timeline
  2. Brook Mine rare earth project is pre-revenue with unproven processing technology — the carbochlorination flowsheet has not been demonstrated at commercial scale, and the shift toward MREC adds technological complexity and execution risk
  3. Paying dividends and repurchasing shares while operating at a loss, combined with $27.9M in insider selling by a board director, raises capital allocation discipline and insider conviction concerns

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Ramaco's core businesses — underground metallurgical coal mining and rare earth element extraction — are fundamentally physical operations that AI cannot substitute [1]. Mining requires geological expertise, heavy equipment operation in underground environments, safety management, and physical processing of extracted materials. The commodity nature of met coal means the product has no AI-related obsolescence — steel production requires met coal as a chemical reductant regardless of AI advancement. The Brook Mine REE project is equally physical, involving mineral extraction, chemical processing (carbochlorination), and supply chain logistics [4]. The indirect risk — AI accelerating hydrogen-based direct reduction iron steelmaking — is a multi-decade transition, not a near-term threat. Physical extraction industries remain among the most AI-immune business models. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI-specific revenue or products deployed. AI could theoretically enhance mining through predictive maintenance and geological modeling, but Ramaco has not disclosed any AI initiatives.
