Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Mortgage ·NYSE ·United States
MFA Financial, Inc. is a self-advised real estate investment trust (REIT) primarily engaged in investing, on a leveraged basis, in residential mortgage assets including non-QM loans, business purpose loans, and agency mortgage-backed securities. Through its subsidiary Lima One Capital, MFA originates business purpose loans for residential real estate investors. Q2 2026 GAAP net income was $36.2M ($0.35/share), beating estimates, with the residential investment portfolio growing to $13.0B. Founded in 1998, headquartered in New York City.

Investment Thesis
MFA Financial trades at a deeply discounted 5.98x forward earnings with a 16.7% dividend yield, offering significant income optionality if earnings trajectory holds [1][4]. Q2 2026 marked a clear inflection: GAAP net income of $36.2M ($0.35/share) beat estimates by 25.9%, revenue grew 20.2% YoY, and Lima One originations surged 44% to $316M, driving the residential investment portfolio to $13.0B [1]. The earnings recovery from Q1's loss to Q2's dividend coverage provides evidence that the Lima One vertical integration thesis is working. Profit margin of 48.95% demonstrates the spread-based model generates strong returns when credit conditions are favorable, and 27 years of dividend payments through multiple cycles show institutional commitment to income generation. The Inspire Impact Score of 39 (ALIGNED) enables faith-based allocation.
The bear case centers on structural leverage and spread vulnerability. D/E of 657% is mechanical for a mortgage REIT but leaves zero margin for error if credit conditions deteriorate [4]. Net interest spread narrowed to 1.56% in Q2 2026 from 1.98% a year earlier, meaning MFA must grow the portfolio significantly just to maintain earnings [1]. The ~100% payout ratio means every dollar of earnings goes to dividends with no retained earnings buffer — any credit event forces either a dividend cut or equity raise. The 5yr dividend CAGR of -1.7% (reflecting the 2023 cut from $0.44 to $0.35) demonstrates income fragility that the 16.7% yield cannot disguise. ROE of 8.24% is well below the cost of equity for a leveraged REIT, meaning the company is destroying value on a levered basis. If interest rates spike or non-QM credit deteriorates, the stock's 12% decline from $9.50 to $8.36 could accelerate significantly given the leverage amplification.
