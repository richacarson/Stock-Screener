Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
MidCap Financial Investment Corporation is an externally managed, publicly traded Business Development Company (BDC) focused on providing senior secured first lien loans to U.S. middle market companies. Externally managed by Apollo Investment Management, an affiliate of Apollo Global Management ($785B AUM). The company was formed through the 2024 merger of Apollo Investment Corp (AINV), Apollo Senior Floating Rate Fund, and MidCap Financial Investment Corp. Portfolio of ~229 companies totaling $2.77B at fair value. Headquartered in New York, New York.

Investment Thesis
MFIC offers a 10.28% dividend yield backed by NII of $0.40/share in Q2 2026, providing 129% coverage of the $0.31 quarterly dividend, with the portfolio yielding 9.5% on secured debt (100% floating rate) [1]. The Forward P/E of 8.91 and share repurchases at a 13% discount to NAV ($11.58 vs $13.37 NAV) demonstrate management conviction in intrinsic value. Apollo's $785B platform provides institutional-quality deal sourcing and credit expertise, while the 94% first lien secured portfolio composition limits downside in severe credit scenarios. Available credit facility capacity of $925M provides liquidity cushion.
The bear case is severe and immediate. NAV has declined 8.8% in three quarters (from $14.66 to $13.37), the dividend was cut 18% from $0.38 to $0.31, and the board explicitly warned no assurances exist for maintaining even the reduced dividend [1]. Net portfolio losses of $(50.3M) in Q2 alone — concentrated in software credits — reveal credit underwriting failures in a sector Apollo was supposedly expert in. The portfolio is actively contracting ($(160M) net investment activity in Q2), total assets fell from $3.07B to $2.86B, and the company is reducing exposure to stressed positions [1]. The stock trades at a 29% discount to NAV ($9.55 vs $13.37), signaling the market expects further NAV erosion. External management by Apollo creates structural misalignment — Apollo earns fees on assets regardless of shareholder returns, and the management agreement lacks meaningful performance accountability. The 140.7% GAAP payout ratio reflects that actual earnings (including realized and unrealized losses) do not cover the dividend.
Key Catalysts
  1. Stabilization of NAV decline if credit losses moderate in H2 2026, with portfolio concentration in first lien secured debt (94%) providing recovery potential as stressed borrowers restructure
  2. Potential dividend increase if NII remains at $0.38-0.40/share level while the $0.31 distribution builds supplemental income reserves, rebuilding investor confidence
  3. Share buyback program generating NAV accretion — Q2 repurchases at $11.58 added $0.07/share, and continued repurchases below $13.37 NAV would be incrementally accretive
Key Risks
  1. Continued NAV erosion from further credit losses in the portfolio — the 8.8% decline over three quarters could accelerate if macroeconomic conditions deteriorate and additional software or middle market credits default
  2. Dividend sustainability risk with board explicitly providing 'no assurances' of maintaining the $0.31 quarterly distribution — a second dividend cut would likely trigger significant additional stock price decline
  3. External management structure misaligns incentives — Apollo earns management fees on gross assets regardless of shareholder returns, and the portfolio contraction ($(160M) net in Q2) may reflect Apollo redirecting deal flow to higher-fee vehicles
