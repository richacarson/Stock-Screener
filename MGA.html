Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto Parts ·NYSE ·Canada
Magna International is one of the world's largest automotive suppliers, operating through four segments: Body Exteriors & Structures, Power & Vision, Seating Systems, and Complete Vehicles. The company serves global OEMs across ICE, hybrid, and EV platforms with a 'powertrain agnostic' strategy. Under CEO Swamy Kotagiri (since January 2021, 21+ year Magna veteran), Q2 2026 delivered record adjusted EPS of $1.86 (+29% YoY) on $11.0B revenue with EBIT margin expanding to 6.2%. The company raised its 2026 outlook and recently partnered with NVIDIA to combine technologies for autonomous driving and ADAS systems.

Investment Thesis
Magna International delivered record Q2 2026 results — adjusted EPS of $1.86 (+29% YoY), EBIT margin expanding to 6.2%, and a raised full-year outlook — demonstrating that sustained operational restructuring is translating into tangible profitability improvement [1]. At 9.41x forward P/E on improving guidance, the stock represents deep value in the auto parts sector. The 3.03% dividend yield backed by 41 consecutive years of payment, 14 years of growth, and a 5.8% 5-year CAGR provides reliable income, while the 44% GAAP payout ratio leaves substantial room for dividend increases [5]. The NVIDIA partnership for ADAS and autonomous driving positions Magna at the intersection of physical manufacturing and AI-driven mobility technology, and complete vehicle assembly contracts with Chinese EV OEMs diversify growth exposure [2]. Revenue of $11.0B in Q2 2026 (+3% YoY) is steady with margin expansion doing the heavy lifting on earnings.
The bear case centers on thin margins, cyclical exposure, and the software-defined vehicle transition. Despite improvement, GAAP profit margin of 2.89% and ROE of 8.70% leave minimal buffer for cost overruns, warranty claims, or input cost inflation in a 156,000-employee global manufacturing operation [5]. Revenue growth of 3% YoY is modest and dependent on global vehicle production volumes — cyclical auto demand weakness, tariff escalation for a Canadian-headquartered company, or EV adoption delays could compress volumes and margins simultaneously. The structural shift toward software-defined vehicles concentrates value in technology layers where Magna is the junior partner (NVIDIA leads the ADAS partnership), and pure-play tech competitors (Mobileye, Tesla) have deeper AI expertise in autonomous driving [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) and Glassdoor rating of 3.3-3.4/5 indicate neither strong alignment for faith-based investors nor a distinctive culture that could weather prolonged industry disruption [3].
