Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Magnolia Oil & Gas is a Houston-based independent E&P focused on South Texas — the Giddings field (Austin Chalk/Eagle Ford) and Karnes County. Founded in 2018 by former Occidental CEO Steve Chazen and led since September 2022 by CEO (and since July 2025 Chairman) Christopher Stavros, it runs a low-reinvestment, free-cash-flow-and-dividend model. On September 10, 2026 it closed the ~$4.1B acquisition of WildFire Energy (~810,000 net Giddings acres plus a sand mine), lifting Q4 2026 production guidance to 159-161 Mboe/d from 106.1 Mboe/d in Q2 2026 and raising the quarterly dividend 9% to $0.18. Shares outstanding ~267M; pre-deal market cap roughly $6.7-6.9B.

Investment Thesis
Magnolia is a disciplined, high-margin Giddings consolidator. TTM profit margin is 28.8%, ROE 20.9%, and the stock trades at 11.0x trailing and 8.0x forward earnings, with Yahoo showing revenue up 50.1% and earnings up 136.6% in the latest reported quarter year over year [4]. The ~$4.1B WildFire Energy acquisition (closed September 10, 2026) adds ~810,000 net Giddings acres and a sand mine supplying ~80% of Magnolia's sand needs, with management targeting more than $100M of annual synergies and guiding Q4 2026 production to 159-161 Mboe/d — roughly 50% above Q2's 106.1 Mboe/d [1][2]. Pro forma net debt of ~$1.9B is below 1.0x 2027E EBITDA at strip, and the board raised the quarterly dividend 9% to $0.18 alongside the deal [1][2].
The bear case is that Magnolia just abandoned the conservative balance sheet that defined it. The deal was funded with $2.65B of cash (revolver draws, new senior notes, a ~$1-1.2B equity offering), 32.2M new shares to WildFire, and $600M of assumed 7.5% notes — taking net debt from near zero to ~$1.9B and the share count to ~267M [1][2]. One-time transaction and integration costs of $65-75M hit Q3 2026, and Q4 D&C spending steps up to ~$235M [1]. As an oil-weighted producer (49-50% oil), earnings remain fully exposed to commodity prices — FY2025 revenue was flat at $1.31B and net income fell to $325M from $366M before 2026 price gains [3]. The 2026 proxy also discloses routine flaring on properties acquired in 2023 that is inconsistent with company standards, now being remediated [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. WildFire integration: delivery of the targeted >$100M of annual cost savings and synergies and Q4 2026 production of 159-161 Mboe/d [1][2]
  2. Q3 2026 results on November 5, 2026 — first look at combined free cash flow and the pace of debt reduction from ~$1.9B net debt [1]
  3. Dividend growth: the 9% raise to $0.18/quarter (an annualized $0.72) extends five consecutive years of per-share increases, with buybacks possible once leverage normalizes [2]
Key Risks
  1. Leverage and integration: net debt rose to ~$1.9B with $600M of assumed 7.5% notes and new senior notes; a commodity downturn during integration would pressure the dividend and buyback capacity [1][2]
  2. Commodity exposure: oil is ~49-50% of volumes, so the 50% YoY revenue and 137% earnings growth in the latest quarter can reverse quickly with crude prices — FY2025 revenue was flat at $1.31B [3][4]
  3. Dilution and execution: ~81M additional shares (equity offering plus 32.2M to WildFire) mean per-share growth requires the acquired acreage to perform as underwritten [1][2]
