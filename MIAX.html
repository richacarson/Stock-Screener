Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·United States
Miami International Holdings operates regulated financial exchanges across options, equities, futures, and international listings. Platforms include MIAX Options, MIAX Pearl, MIAX Emerald, and MIAX Sapphire for options; MIAX Pearl Equities for U.S. equities; MIAX Futures for derivatives; and the Bermuda Stock Exchange (BSX) and The International Stock Exchange (TISE) for international listings. Also owns Dorman Trading, a full-service futures commission merchant. IPO'd on NYSE in August 2025. Q2 2026 revenue grew 35% YoY to $141.1M with adjusted EBITDA margin of 54%. Market cap approximately $3.5B.

Investment Thesis
Miami International Holdings is delivering accelerating growth as a regulated exchange operator with Q2 2026 revenue of $141.1M (+35% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $76.8M (+57%), and adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 54% [2]. The company's Q1 2026 was even stronger at 40% revenue growth with a 51% adjusted EBITDA margin [1]. At $35.64, the stock now trades at 18.4x forward earnings (down from 28.4x) following a pullback from $47, offering a more attractive entry point for a high-growth exchange operator with virtually zero debt ($23.1M against $701.7M cash), 18.1% ROE, and structural operating leverage [5]. Options market ADV grew 25% YoY to 11.0 million contracts in Q2, and the secular growth in U.S. options trading volume provides a durable tailwind. CEO Gallagher's FOW Chief Executive of the Year award and TabbFORUM 40 recognition validate industry standing [3].
The primary risk is cyclical volume dependence: exchange revenues are correlated with market volatility, and the elevated volatility driving record 2026 volumes may normalize, compressing revenue growth from the current 35-40% trajectory [1][2]. GAAP profit margin remains only 9.7% as IPO-era charges (including $107.7M debt extinguishment) continue to depress reported profitability relative to adjusted metrics [5]. The options exchange market is fiercely competitive — CBOE (~33%), NYSE (~20%), and Nasdaq (~19%) have vastly greater resources and institutional relationships, and MIAX's share gains could plateau as incumbents respond with pricing and technology investment. Limited public company history (IPO August 2025) means only three quarters of public financial data exist, providing minimal track record for valuation models. CEO insider selling of approximately $2M in shares continues the post-IPO selling pattern, creating overhang [3]. The stock's 16% decline from $42.51 SMA-50 suggests near-term momentum weakness.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued U.S. options market share gains as MIAX Sapphire ramps and retail options participation maintains secular growth, with each share percentage point worth approximately $30M+ in incremental annual revenue [1][2]
  2. GAAP profitability normalization as IPO-era one-time charges fully roll off, closing the gap between 9.7% GAAP profit margin and 54% adjusted EBITDA margin and driving earnings re-rating [2]
  3. Forward P/E compression to 18.4x from 28.4x creates a potential value re-rating opportunity if Q3-Q4 results maintain 30%+ revenue growth trajectory and the stock re-rates toward high-growth exchange peers [5]
Key Risks
  1. Options market volumes are cyclical and correlated with volatility — the record Q1-Q2 2026 volumes driven by elevated VIX may reflect a transient volatility spike rather than sustainable growth, risking revenue deceleration in normalized markets [1][2]
  2. Incumbent exchanges (CBOE, Nasdaq, NYSE) with vastly greater resources may respond to MIAX's share gains with aggressive pricing, technology investment, or regulatory lobbying that compresses margins and slows share capture [1]
  3. Limited public company track record (IPO August 2025) with only three quarters of public earnings data, combined with continued CEO insider selling, creates uncertainty about sustainable earnings power and insider conviction [3][5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): MIAX's core business is SEC-registered national securities exchange infrastructure — deeply regulated, essential market plumbing that AI structurally cannot displace [3]. Operating regulated exchanges requires SEC licensing, extensive compliance frameworks, market surveillance systems, clearinghouse connectivity, and regulatory reporting capabilities that represent institutional barriers far beyond AI's reach. Network effects (liquidity attracts liquidity), high switching costs for integrated market participants, and proprietary data center infrastructure create additional structural defenses [1]. AI-driven algorithmic and high-frequency trading actually increases exchange volumes rather than displacing them — every AI trading strategy needs an exchange to execute on. The regulatory moat alone provides near-permanent protection against AI disruption [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): MIAX does not generate quantifiable revenue from AI-specific products or services. The exchange technology uses sophisticated matching algorithms and low-latency infrastructure, but these are not marketed or reported as AI revenue [1][2].
