Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Packaged Foods ·NYSE ·Netherlands
The Magnum Ice Cream Company is the world's largest ice cream company, spun off from Unilever in July 2025 and independently listed in December 2025. It operates iconic brands including Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto, and Wall's across four continents with more than 30 manufacturing plants. Headquartered in Amsterdam, Netherlands. The company is embroiled in a significant governance dispute with Ben & Jerry's co-founders over the brand's social mission.

Investment Thesis
TMICC is the world's largest ice cream pure-play with EUR 7.9B in revenue and iconic global brands (Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto, Wall's) [1]. The stock has rallied 40% from $13.23 to $18.50 since listing, driven by Q1 2026 organic sales growth of 4.5% with volume acceleration to 2.9% across all regions [2]. At 16.60x forward earnings with ROE of 17.88%, the market is pricing in standalone margin expansion as EBITDA margins improve from 15.9% toward management's guided 40-60bps improvement [2]. CEO Peter ter Kulve brings 35+ years of ice cream expertise, and the HCLTech AI Force partnership positions TMICC for digital-era distribution and operational efficiency [1]. Organic growth acceleration from 4.2% (FY2025) to 4.5% (Q1 2026) demonstrates the underlying health of the brand portfolio.
The bear case has intensified since the April screen. The Ben & Jerry's governance crisis escalated significantly in July 2026 with the Foundation announcing it will shut down after TMICC cut off funding and evicted staffers — co-founder Ben Cohen calls it an attempt to 'destroy the social values of Ben & Jerry's' [3]. This risks permanent damage to one of the most valuable brands in the portfolio, potential consumer boycotts, and ongoing litigation costs. D/E of 539.65% ($4.01B debt against minimal equity) creates financial fragility — the company has $517.79M cash against $4.01B debt [5]. Profit margin of 3.70% is razor-thin for this leverage level. Unilever's 19.9% overhang creates selling pressure. The ice cream category faces structural headwinds from GLP-1 weight loss drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy) reducing indulgent food consumption, health-conscious consumer trends, and rising cold-chain costs from climate change. The trailing P/E of 34.85x is elevated for a low-growth consumer staples company with these risk factors.
