Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Resorts & Casinos ·NASDAQ ·Hong Kong
Melco Resorts & Entertainment operates integrated casino resorts in Macau (City of Dreams, Studio City, Altira, Mocha Clubs), the Philippines (City of Dreams Manila), and Cyprus (City of Dreams Mediterranean). Controlled by Chairman and CEO Lawrence Ho, the company is one of only six licensed Macau casino concessionaires, focused on premium-mass gaming and luxury hospitality. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.37B grew 11% YoY with Adjusted Property EBITDA of $381M (+12%). Moody's projects full-year 2026 revenue of $5.4B with EBITDA of $1.3B. Market cap is approximately $3.15B against an enterprise value of $9.55B, reflecting $6.4B+ in net debt.

Investment Thesis
Melco delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $1.37B (up 11% YoY) with Adjusted Property EBITDA of $381M (+12%), confirming the Macau premium-mass recovery trajectory [1]. Moody's projects full-year 2026 revenue of $5.4B with EBITDA of $1.3B, supporting a deleveraging path from the current $6.4B+ net debt position. At a forward P/E of 13.66, the stock trades at a significant discount to Macau peers and to its own historical multiples — 12 analysts maintain a consensus price target implying substantial upside from $5.21 [5]. Lawrence Ho's 22-year founder-led tenure provides strategic continuity, and the REM luxury hotel opening at City of Dreams Macau in Q3 2026 adds premium room inventory to capture the highest-margin gaming segment. Only six Macau gaming concessions exist, creating a regulatory moat that limits competition regardless of market conditions.
The bear case is severe and structural. Total debt of approximately $6.4B against negative book equity produces a D/E of -561% and an enterprise value of $9.55B — more than 3x the $3.15B market cap — reflecting extreme leverage that leaves virtually no equity cushion [5]. Profit margin of 3.56% is razor-thin for the leverage carried, and the negative PEG ratio suggests analysts expect earnings growth to decelerate. The stock has declined significantly from its 52-week highs, reflecting market skepticism about the deleveraging timeline. China regulatory risk remains omnipresent — further crackdowns on cross-border capital flows, junket operations, or digital yuan capital controls could impair Macau GGR at any time without warning. The 'Gambling' Inspire negative attribution and -1 score signal fundamental concerns about the business model's social impact for faith-based investors. Geographic diversification into Cyprus and the Philippines has not yet proven it can offset Macau concentration, and the company faces structural challenges in achieving the profit margins needed to service its debt load.
