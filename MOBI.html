Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
Mobia Medical, Inc. (formerly MicroTransponder) is a commercial-stage medical device company focused on stroke recovery. Its Vivistim Paired VNS System is the first and only FDA-approved implantable device designed to improve upper limb motor function in chronic ischemic stroke survivors with moderate to severe impairments. The company IPO'd in May 2026 at $15/share, raising $134M. Q2 2026 revenue doubled to $13.5M with 83.2% gross margin. Full-year 2026 guidance: $54-$56M revenue (69-75% growth). The company has $177M cash and approximately 40 territory managers across the U.S.
250 Employees ·www.mobia.com

Investment Thesis
Mobia Medical addresses a massive unmet need — 6.5M stroke survivors in the U.S. with limited rehabilitation options — with the only FDA-approved paired VNS system for motor recovery [1]. Revenue doubled to $13.5M in Q2 2026 (+102% YoY) with 35.5 active territories (up 92%), and full-year 2026 guidance of $54-$56M represents 69-75% growth over 2025 [1]. Gross margin of 83.2% indicates strong unit economics once the company scales past the profitability threshold. The May 2026 IPO raised $134M, providing a fortress balance sheet with $177M cash against minimal debt to fund commercial expansion without near-term dilution risk [1][3]. Clinical data showing Vivistim improves post-stroke motor function by two-fold creates a compelling value proposition, and the sole-provider market position eliminates direct competitive risk for the foreseeable future. Recent board additions from PROCEPT BioRobotics and Intuitive Surgical signal strategic sophistication and potential for pipeline expansion [4].
The bear case centers on commercial execution risk, profitability timeline, and single-product concentration. At a $446M market cap against ~$55M guided 2026 revenue, MOBI trades at approximately 8x revenue for a company with -142% net margins and a widening quarterly net loss of $21M [1][3]. The Vivistim procedure requires surgical implantation and intensive rehabilitation, creating a high-friction adoption pathway that limits the addressable market to specialized stroke centers and motivated patients. Revenue concentration in a single product with no disclosed pipeline extensions creates binary clinical, regulatory, and reimbursement risk — any adverse event, CMS coverage decision, or competitive entry from larger medical device companies (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific) could materially impact the business. The stock has fallen 29% from its $15 IPO price to $10.61, reflecting market skepticism about the path to profitability. The company burns approximately $21M per quarter in net losses; even with $177M in cash, achieving profitability before requiring additional capital is not guaranteed.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued acceleration of Vivistim implant volumes with 35.5 active territories expanding further, driving the $54-$56M 2026 revenue guidance toward the high end and demonstrating a scalable commercial model for stroke rehabilitation [1]
  2. Expansion of clinical indications beyond chronic ischemic stroke to hemorrhagic stroke, traumatic brain injury, or other neurological conditions amenable to paired VNS therapy, broadening the addressable market beyond the initial 6.5M stroke survivor population [1]
  3. Demonstration of improving unit economics and operating leverage as revenue scales past $60-80M annually, with 83.2% gross margins providing a clear path to operating profitability once SG&A and R&D investments are absorbed [1][3]
Key Risks
  1. Single-product company risk — Vivistim is the only revenue source with no disclosed pipeline extensions, creating binary exposure to clinical setbacks, CMS reimbursement changes, or competitive entry from larger medical device companies (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific) with superior commercial infrastructure [1]
  2. Quarterly net loss of $21M ($1.10/share) with no profitability timeline communicated — even with $177M cash, sustaining current burn rates for 8-9 quarters without additional capital is not guaranteed if revenue growth decelerates below guidance [1][3]
  3. High-friction adoption model requiring surgical implantation, intensive 6-week rehabilitation protocol, and specialized stroke center infrastructure potentially caps the addressable market well below the theoretical 6.5M stroke survivor population [1]
