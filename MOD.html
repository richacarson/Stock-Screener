Company Profile
Industrials ·Thermal Management / Auto Parts ·NYSE ·United States
Modine Manufacturing Company is a global leader in thermal management solutions for data centers, HVAC, commercial vehicles, and automotive markets. Founded in 1916, the company is executing a transformative strategic pivot from legacy auto parts thermal management to high-growth data center cooling, with data center revenue surging 73% to $1.1B in FY2026. FY2026 net sales rose 23% to $3.18 billion with record adjusted EBITDA of $471M and adjusted EPS of $5.02. The company is planning a spin-off of its Performance Technologies business to become a pure-play climate solutions company. CEO Neil Brinker has led the transformation since 2020.

Modine at 17.96x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.64 is a compelling growth-at-a-reasonable-price story in AI infrastructure, upgraded from HOLD to BUY on improved valuation metrics following analyst estimate revisions. FY2026 delivered record revenue of $3.18B (+23% YoY) with data center cooling sales surging 73% to $1.1B, and FY2027 guidance calls for 20-25% revenue growth with data center sales expected to grow 70%+ [1]. Forward EPS of $11.00 implies a 4x earnings inflection from trailing GAAP EPS of $2.69, driven by margin expansion as the data center mix increases and acquisition integration costs normalize. The company IS AI infrastructure — high-density AI chips generate significantly more heat, directly driving demand for Modine's full-stack cooling portfolio including liquid cooling, immersion cooling, and the patent-pending Cooling AI Control System [2]. The bear case centers on execution risk, margin quality, and end-market concentration. Trailing P/E of 73.46x versus forward P/E of 17.96x creates a massive gap that demands the $11.00 forward EPS estimate materializes — any disappointment would trigger severe multiple compression [4]. Profit margin of only 4.28% is thin for a company priced for sustained high growth, and ROE of 13.09% remains below the 15% target, suggesting capital efficiency has not yet caught up to revenue growth. GAAP earnings declined 34% in FY2026 despite 23% revenue growth, reflecting acquisition integration costs and capacity expansion expenses [1]. The rapid pivot concentrates risk in a single end market — data center cooling — dependent on continued hyperscaler AI capex, which could slow if AI model efficiency improves faster than expected, macroeconomic conditions deteriorate, or data center buildout cadence normalizes. The planned spin-off of the century-old automotive business during a critical growth phase introduces organizational and execution risk. Glassdoor culture scores declining 9% suggest the transformation is straining the organization [2].
