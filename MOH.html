Company Profile
Healthcare ·Healthcare Plans ·NYSE ·United States
Molina Healthcare, Inc. is a Fortune 500 managed care organization providing government-sponsored healthcare programs to low-income individuals and families through Medicaid, Medicare, and ACA Marketplace plans across 20+ US states. Founded by Dr. C. David Molina in 1980, the company serves approximately 5.0 million members under CEO Joseph Zubretsky (since 2017). Q2 2026 premium revenue was $10.2B with full-year guidance raised to at least $5.25 adjusted EPS [1]. The company navigates Medicaid rate-trend imbalances that management characterizes as stabilizing, with 2026 viewed as the trough year for Medicaid pretax margins.

Investment Thesis
Molina Healthcare offers a margin recovery play in defensive government-sponsored managed care, trading at 21.98x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.95 after management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to at least $5.25 [1][4]. The Medicaid rate-trend imbalance appears to be stabilizing, with management characterizing 2026 as the trough year for Medicaid pretax margins, implying margin recovery in 2027-2028 [1]. Forward EPS of $9.74 implies substantial earnings normalization beyond the $5.25 guidance year. The business serves 5.0 million members across 20+ states with $42B+ in annual premium revenue, anchored by high-barrier government contracts requiring state-by-state regulatory licensing. The balance sheet holds $8.9B cash against $4.0B debt, and free cash flow of $700M provides resilience. The Inspire Impact Score of 48 (ALIGNED) with 12 positive and zero negative attributions makes MOH attractive for faith-aligned healthcare exposure.
The bear case centers on the severity of the profitability collapse and cultural concerns. Trailing profit margin of -0.02% and ROE of -0.16% reflect a business generating $42.5B in revenue with essentially zero profit — an alarming state for a healthcare plan that should be earning 2-3% margins [4]. The -5.7% revenue decline in the most recent quarter suggests membership attrition from Medicaid redeterminations is ongoing. Employee culture is deeply problematic: Glassdoor 3.3/5 with 18% employee turnover and 24% leadership turnover point to an organization 'squeezing employees, members, and providers' to improve margins [2]. DOJ False Claims Act investigations over risk adjustment coding practices and state Medicaid enforcement actions create legal overhang [3]. The company's $42B revenue base is entirely government-dependent, making it vulnerable to political shifts in healthcare policy, Medicaid rate adjustments, and regulatory changes beyond management's control.
