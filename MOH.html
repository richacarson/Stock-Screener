Company Profile
Healthcare ·Healthcare Plans ·NYSE ·United States
Molina Healthcare, Inc. is a Fortune 500 managed care organization that provides government-sponsored healthcare programs to low-income individuals and families, including Medicaid, Medicare, and Marketplace (ACA exchange) plans. Founded by Dr. C. David Molina in 1980 to serve underserved communities, the company now operates in over 20 US states and serves approximately 5.0 million members as of March 2026. Under CEO Joseph Zubretsky (since 2017), Molina has transformed from a troubled regional health plan into a disciplined, margin-focused national managed care company with approximately $42B in annual premium revenue.

Investment Thesis
Molina Healthcare offers a turnaround opportunity within government-sponsored managed care, trading at a trailing P/E of 10.99 following the dramatic 2026 EPS guidance cut that triggered a 23% stock decline [4][6]. The company serves 5.0 million members across 20+ states with $42B in annual premium revenue, anchored by long-term Medicaid contracts with high barriers to entry [1]. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.35 exceeded analyst expectations by 31%, with MCR of 91.1% showing disciplined medical cost management [1]. Management has set ambitious 2027-2029 growth targets and CEO Zubretsky's contract extension through 2027 signals leadership continuity [2]. The Inspire Insight ALIGNED rating with 12 positive attributions and zero negatives makes MOH a strong candidate for faith-aligned portfolios seeking healthcare exposure. Revenue growth 5-year CAGR of 16% demonstrates consistent scale expansion.
The bear case is severe: Molina slashed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to 'at least $5,' far below the $13.71 consensus, driven by $2/share retroactive Medicaid rate adjustments and $1.50/share Florida contract costs [4]. This guidance cut exposed structural margin pressures -- the 1.04% TTM net profit margin is razor-thin for any business, and Medicaid membership attrition is expected to increase to 6% in 2026. The forward P/E of ~38x (at company guidance) is elevated for a managed care company with declining near-term earnings, and the stock trades at a significant premium to its five-year median P/E of 14.69 [6]. Regulatory risk is persistent -- Medicaid rate adequacy varies by state, and political changes in healthcare policy could impact the company's government-dependent revenue base. The company's aggressive acquisition strategy must be balanced against integration risk and medical cost management in newly acquired health plans.
Key Catalysts
  1. Medicaid rate adequacy improvements as states adjust payments to reflect the sicker post-PHE (Public Health Emergency) member population, potentially restoring margins toward historical MCR levels below 89%
  2. New state contract wins and ACA exchange expansion driving revenue toward the $46B 2026 target and supporting the multi-year 2027-2029 growth framework
  3. Resolution of the $2/share retroactive Medicaid adjustments and $1.50/share Florida contract costs as one-time headwinds that normalize in subsequent quarters
Key Risks
  1. 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5 (vs. $13.71 consensus) exposes structural margin pressures from rising medical costs, Medicaid redetermination attrition (6% expected), and retroactive rate adjustments that may persist beyond 2026
  2. Forward P/E of ~38x at company guidance is far above the managed care industry average of 17x and Molina's own five-year median of 14.69x, leaving significant downside if margins do not recover
  3. Government-dependent revenue model creates regulatory risk from Medicaid rate changes, state budget pressures, and potential federal healthcare policy shifts that are beyond management's control
