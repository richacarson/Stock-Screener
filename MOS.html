Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Agricultural Inputs ·NYSE ·United States
The Mosaic Company produces and markets concentrated phosphate and potash crop nutrients through three segments: Phosphates, Potash, and Mosaic Fertilizantes. The company operates mines and processing facilities in the U.S., Brazil, and Canada, serving global agricultural markets. Q2 2026 results reflected severe margin compression with a $273M net loss driven by elevated sulfur costs from the Strait of Hormuz disruption, though potash operations remained profitable with $278M adjusted EBITDA. Mosaic Biosciences continues targeting $130M+ revenue in 2026 with high-margin biological crop nutrition products.

Investment Thesis
Mosaic at 16.2x forward earnings with a 4.71% dividend yield represents a deep cyclical value play at trough-level profitability, with forward estimates reflecting analyst consensus for earnings recovery as sulfur costs normalize and global phosphate and potash demand reaches record levels [1][4]. The company's low-cost potash production ($75/ton at Esterhazy), record Miski Mayo phosphate rock output, and $150M in cumulative cost savings provide operational leverage when commodity prices recover. Biosciences is targeting $130M+ in 2026 with 40%+ margins and 8-10 new product launches, providing high-margin diversification from commodity fertilizers [2]. Multiple board members purchased shares in 2025-2026 — Kuzenko ($210K), Westbrook ($108K), and Beebe ($175K) — signaling insider conviction at cyclical lows [3]. D/E of 48% provides substantial balance sheet flexibility for a commodity producer.
The bear case reflects severe cyclical stress with no clear recovery timeline. Q2 2026 delivered a $273M net loss (-$0.86/share) as soaring sulfur and ammonia costs from the Strait of Hormuz disruption crushed phosphate stripping margins, forcing production curtailments at Bartow and Louisiana and creating a projected $250M EBITDA headwind [1]. Trailing ROE has turned negative at -1% and profit margin has collapsed to -1%, rendering the dividend unsustainable on current earnings — the annual payout exceeds trailing net income by a wide margin. Free cash flow turned negative at -$290M as working capital absorbed $960M in 2025, and net debt increased $829M. Brazilian Fertilizantes operations remain idled at Araxa and Fospar with no restart timeline, while credit constraints and Chinese phosphate import competition weigh on segment volumes. If sulfur costs persist or fertilizer prices decline further, a dividend cut becomes probable [1]. The 5yr dividend growth CAGR of 34.6% — earned during peak commodity conditions — is not sustainable at trough earnings.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sulfur price normalization from Strait of Hormuz disruption levels, potentially restoring phosphate segment profitability from the $104M operating loss and validating the forward P/E of 16.2x recovery thesis [1]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Mosaic's core business — mining phosphate and potash from underground deposits, processing them into fertilizer, and shipping physical product to agricultural markets — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. The operations require massive physical infrastructure (mines, chemical processing plants, port terminals, railways) that AI cannot substitute. Fertilizer demand is driven by biological necessity — crops need phosphorus and potassium regardless of technological advancement, and the global population continues to grow toward 9 billion by 2050. No analysts have flagged AI as a disruption risk; AI-driven precision agriculture improves fertilizer application efficiency without reducing overall demand. The AI data center buildout increases demand for rare earth elements, providing indirect tailwind to Mosaic's rare earth extraction partnership [2]. AI Opportunity (0/1): Mosaic does not generate revenue from AI-related products. CEO Bodine discusses AI-driven farming as a future opportunity for the agricultural industry broadly, but the company has not deployed commercially meaningful AI capabilities [2].
