Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Medical Properties Trust, Inc. is a self-advised real estate investment trust (REIT) that acquires, develops, and invests in net-leased hospital facilities. Founded in 2003 and headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama, MPT is the world's largest pure-play hospital REIT, owning approximately 378 facilities with roughly 38,000 licensed beds across nine countries and three continents. The company changed its ticker from MPW to MPT effective February 2026. H1 2026 revenue totaled $511.3M with net income of $30.2M, swinging to profitability after the Steward Health Care crisis. Management executed a $2.4B refinancing eliminating all debt maturities through 2027. Market cap approximately $2.6B.

Investment Thesis
Medical Properties Trust is emerging from its darkest chapter with tangible signs of stabilization. H1 2026 marked a critical inflection: $511.3M revenue with $30.2M net income, returning to profitability after a $2.4B net loss in FY2024 and $277M loss in FY2025 [5]. The $2.4B refinancing eliminating all debt maturities through 2027 is a transformative de-risking event, giving management runway to stabilize the portfolio without near-term refinancing pressure [2]. The company has executed $5.5B in asset sales, successfully transitioned 15 Steward hospitals to replacement operators, and targets $1B in annualized cash rent by year-end 2026. At $4.40 per share with an 8.2% dividend yield (recently increased from $0.08 to $0.09 quarterly), significant discount to book value, and the worst operational crisis now behind it, MPT offers deep value for investors who believe the turnaround is real [7].
The bear case remains formidable. MPT carries $9.7B in total debt ($14.7B assets) against a $2.6B market cap, with D/E of 215% creating structural leverage risk even after the refinancing [2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately -5% and 5-year dividend growth CAGR of -20.9% reflect genuine portfolio deterioration, not a temporary setback [6][7]. Governance concerns persist: Viceroy Research's 21 reports alleging revenue round-tripping through circular lending to distressed tenants, and Massachusetts senators' 'Ponzi scheme' characterization have not been fully resolved [3]. The hospital sector faces secular headwinds from labor shortages, reimbursement pressure, and rural hospital closures. While Q2 2026 showed a $2.6M net loss after Q1's $33.1M profit, indicating lumpy profitability, the company must demonstrate sustained quarterly earnings and successful operator performance on restructured leases before recovery can be confirmed. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Medical Properties Trust owns physical hospital buildings leased to healthcare operators under long-term net leases — a business model with near-complete immunity to AI disruption [1]. Hospitals are essential physical infrastructure that cannot be digitized or replaced by artificial intelligence. Patient care, surgical procedures, emergency services, and inpatient treatment all require physical facilities regardless of AI advancement. The net-lease structure means tenants bear operating costs, further insulating MPT from operational AI disruption. The only marginal vulnerability is that AI-driven telemedicine, remote diagnostics, and ambulatory surgical centers could reduce inpatient hospital utilization over decades, but acute care, surgery, and emergency medicine will remain facility-dependent indefinitely. AI Opportunity (0/1): MPT has no meaningful AI opportunity as a property owner with approximately 100 employees. While AI could improve hospital tenant operations (diagnostics, scheduling, staffing optimization), these benefits accrue to operators, not to the landlord.
