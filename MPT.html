Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Medical Properties Trust is a self-advised REIT that acquires and develops net-leased hospital facilities worldwide. Founded in 2003 by CEO Edward Aldag Jr., MPT was once one of the largest hospital-focused REITs globally with over $15B in assets and 39,000 hospital beds, but has undergone significant portfolio contraction following the Steward Health Care bankruptcy in May 2024 and subsequent asset dispositions. H1 2026 revenue of $511.3M represents a modest recovery, with the company returning to a small net income of $30.2M after a $277M loss in FY2025.

Investment Thesis
Medical Properties Trust at $3.11 with a $1.86B market cap is a distressed hospital REIT showing early signs of stabilization — H1 2026 revenue of $511.3M grew modestly from $464.2M, and the company returned to a small net income of $30.2M after a $277M loss in FY2025 [1]. The bull case rests on the deep value thesis: at 0.12x TTM revenue and an 11.6% dividend yield ($0.09/quarter), MPT is priced for bankruptcy that may not materialize. The company's independent investigation found no evidence of wrongdoing on key short-seller allegations, normalized FFO of $0.15/share provides some dividend coverage, and the portfolio stabilization from Steward-related asset dispositions may be nearing completion [1][4]. Hospital real estate demand benefits from aging demographics and the structural need for acute care facilities that cannot be replaced by outpatient alternatives.
The bear case is severe and multi-layered. D/E of 215.7% represents dangerously high leverage for a REIT with impaired assets and troubled tenants, and the company carries trailing EPS of just -$0.05 with forward EPS of only $0.05, meaning the 62.2x forward P/E reflects near-zero expected profitability [1]. The Steward Health Care bankruptcy ($6.6B in lease liabilities to MPT) exposed fundamental flaws in the company's tenant underwriting and risk management, and congressional scrutiny from Senator Warren requesting SEC investigation creates ongoing regulatory overhang [5][6]. The Boston Globe investigation documented that MPT may have obscured Steward's deteriorating finances from investors, and if SEC enforcement action materializes, the consequences could be existential. The 85% stock price decline from 2022 highs (~$20+) to $3.11 reflects the market's severe loss of confidence in management credibility. The dividend has been cut by 70% from $0.28/quarter to $0.09, with the 5-year dividend CAGR of -20.3% destroying the income thesis that originally attracted REIT investors [1][4][5].

AI Risk Protection (6/9): MPT's core assets are physical hospital buildings with long-term net leases — AI cannot replace the need for hospital real estate, and the company's revenue comes from contractual rent payments rather than services that could be automated [1][2].
