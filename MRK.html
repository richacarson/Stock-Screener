Investment Thesis
Merck is one of the world's premier pharmaceutical companies with the best-selling oncology drug in history (Keytruda, $8.4B Q2 2026 family sales) and a diversifying portfolio of growth drivers including Winrevair (+75% QoQ) and Welireg (+67% QoQ) [1]. FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to $66.3-67.3B after solid Q2 results, and the forward P/E of 15.2x is attractive for a company with 26% R&D intensity, $356B market cap, and 50+ years of consecutive dividend payments. The subcutaneous Keytruda Qlex approval extends franchise life beyond the 2028 LOE, while the Daiichi Sankyo ADC collaboration and Cidara/Terns acquisitions provide pipeline depth for the next decade [1]. The 2.36% dividend yield with a normalized 36% payout ratio and 5.51% 5yr CAGR offers reliable income with growth. Merck's AI-augmented drug discovery platform (ranked 2nd in pharma AI readiness) and $17B annual R&D budget position it to sustain innovation leadership [2].
The bear case centers on the 2028 Keytruda patent expiration cliff and current earnings distortion. Keytruda family represents approximately 50%+ of total revenue, and biosimilar competition beginning in 2028-2029 could erode $15-20B in annual revenue over 3-5 years — the single largest LOE event in pharma history [1]. Trailing profitability metrics are severely distorted by $14.7B in acquisition charges: GAAP profit margin of 4.77%, ROE of 6.96%, and payout ratio of 269% all reflect one-time items rather than ongoing economics. D/E of 128% has risen from acquisition financing and may limit financial flexibility if post-LOE revenue declines faster than expected. FY2026 EPS guidance of $2.66-2.76 (including charges) versus forward consensus of $9.49 creates confusion around normalized earnings power. Gardasil revenue faces ongoing China destocking headwinds, and the $14.7B spent on Cidara and Terns remains unproven — pipeline acquisitions in pharma carry high binary risk of clinical failure [1][3]. AI Risk Protection (7/9): Merck's core business — discovering, manufacturing, and distributing pharmaceuticals and vaccines — has deep structural moats against AI disruption. Drug development requires physical wet-lab research, multi-year clinical trials, FDA regulatory approval, and complex biological manufacturing that AI cannot replicate or substitute [2]. The pharma value chain from molecule discovery through Phase III trials to GMP manufacturing involves irreducible physical and regulatory complexity. However, AI tools are increasingly capable of accelerating portions of the drug discovery pipeline (target identification, molecular design, clinical trial design), which could theoretically lower barriers to entry for competitors over time — preventing a top-tier immune score. AI is primarily an enabler that Merck is actively adopting rather than a disruptive threat.
