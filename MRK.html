Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - General ·NYSE ·United States
Merck & Co., Inc. operates as a global healthcare company, offering pharmaceutical products led by Keytruda (pembrolizumab), the world's top-selling drug at $33B+ annual run-rate. Also produces vaccines (Gardasil, Vaxneuvance, Capvaxive), cardiovascular (Winrevair), and animal health products (Bravecto). Pursuing 20 new drug launches by 2030 to diversify beyond Keytruda ahead of its 2028-2029 US patent cliff. Q2 2026 revenue grew 5% to $16.6B with Keytruda family at $8.4B. Full-year 2026 guidance raised to $66.3-$67.3B. Founded 1891, headquartered in Rahway, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
Merck at 15.13x forward earnings offers pharma-grade defensiveness with meaningful pipeline upside. Q2 2026 revenue grew 5% to $16.6B with Keytruda family at $8.4B and strong uptake in earlier-stage cancers, driving raised full-year guidance to $66.3-$67.3B [1]. The 2.54% dividend yield is supported by 60+ consecutive years of payment, 14 consecutive years of growth, and a manageable ~38% adjusted payout ratio. Free cash flow generation of ~$14B annually funds both the dividend and the ambitious post-Keytruda diversification strategy targeting $70B+ annual revenue by mid-2030s through 20 new drug launches [1]. The pipeline includes Winrevair (PAH/heart failure expansion), three Daiichi Sankyo ADC candidates, MK-7240 (IBD from Prometheus), Capvaxive (pneumococcal vaccine), and Keytruda Qlex subcutaneous formulation for franchise extension. Merck's #2 ranking in Pharma AI readiness and GPTeal platform deployment to 50,000+ employees position the company for AI-driven R&D productivity gains [2].
Merck's Keytruda concentration risk remains existential — the franchise generates approximately 50% of total revenue at a $33B+ annual run-rate, and US loss of exclusivity beginning in 2028-2029 could result in significant biosimilar erosion [3]. Samsung Bioepis, Amgen, and Indian manufacturers are advancing biosimilar programs, with analysts projecting 15-30% EU price erosion in Year 1 declining to 45-60% by Year 3 [3]. GAAP profitability is severely distorted by one-time acquisition charges: TTM profit margin of 4.77% and ROE of 6.96% reflect the balance sheet impact of $28B+ in acquisitions rather than operational deterioration, but these charges mechanically compress the infrastructure score [4]. Revenue growth of only 5% YoY and ~6% 5yr CAGR are modest for a company needing to replace $33B+ in Keytruda revenue within 3-5 years. The Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare drug price negotiation directly threatens Keytruda and future high-revenue drugs. D/E of 128% reflects acquisition-related leverage at 2x the 70% target. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Pharmaceutical drug development, clinical trials, FDA regulatory approval, and GMP manufacturing are deeply regulated physical-world processes that AI structurally cannot substitute [1]. Drug molecules must physically interact with biological systems — no AI model can replace the $2B+ and 10+ years required to bring a new drug through Phase I-III trials and FDA approval. Keytruda's competitive moat is protected by clinical data exclusivity, manufacturing complexity, and physician trust built over a decade of real-world evidence across 30+ tumor types [1]. AI actually enhances drug discovery through faster target identification and trial design optimization, making pharma an AI beneficiary. However, AI-driven drug discovery could lower barriers to entry for competitors over time, and AI tools may accelerate biosimilar development timelines for the approaching Keytruda patent cliff — preventing an IMMUNE rating [3].
