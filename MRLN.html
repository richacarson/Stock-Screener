Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NGM ·United States
Merlin develops AI-powered autonomous flight software and systems for both military and civil aircraft. The company's aircraft-agnostic Merlin Pilot platform enables full-stack autonomy from takeoff to touchdown on legacy and next-generation airborne systems. Merlin went public on Nasdaq in March 2026, generating Q2 2026 revenue of $2.2M (doubled sequentially from Q1) against a net loss of $58.4M, with $183.9M in cash and no debt [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Merlin at $1.81 with a $183M market cap is a high-risk, high-reward bet on autonomous aviation reaching commercial and military deployment. The company holds $183.9M in cash with no debt, providing approximately two years of runway at current burn rates, and has achieved genuine technical milestones: first fully automated takeoffs on certification candidate aircraft, a fully autonomous landing at AirVenture Oshkosh, and completion of CDR for the C-130J USSOCOM autonomy program [1][2][3]. Total contract value exceeds $100M, with partnerships including Dynamic Aviation (55 King Air fleet) and Israel Aerospace Industries (Part 25 cargo aircraft), and Fast Company named Merlin among the Most Innovative Companies of 2026, validating the technology's potential [4]. Revenue doubled sequentially from $1.0M in Q1 to $2.2M in Q2 2026, with H2 guidance of $4-6M if planned milestones are achieved.
The bear case is stark: Merlin is a pre-profit company burning $58-90M per quarter with only $2.2M in quarterly revenue, trading 42% below its 50-day SMA of $3.15 and well below the SPAC IPO price [1][2]. At current burn rates, the $183.9M cash position provides roughly 18-24 months of runway before the company needs additional capital, creating dilution risk for existing shareholders. FAA certification for autonomous flight faces an uncertain and potentially multi-year regulatory timeline with no precedent for fully autonomous commercial aviation. The company competes against much larger players (Boeing, Shield AI, Reliable Robotics) with deeper resources, and the addressable market for autonomous legacy aircraft retrofits is unproven at scale. Revenue of $6.8M annualized against a $183M market cap and -29.4% YoY revenue decline offers no valuation floor [1][2].
Key Catalysts
  1. C-130J USSOCOM autonomy program advancing from CDR to flight testing — successful military deployment would validate the autonomous flight platform and could unlock additional defense contracts across the $200B+ military airlift and tanker fleet [1][3]
  2. FAA certification milestones for autonomous flight operations — achieving Part 135 or Part 121 certification would open the commercial cargo and eventually passenger aviation markets, targeting 7,000+ U.S. airline cockpits [3]
  3. Dynamic Aviation King Air fleet deployment — the planned 55-aircraft autonomous fleet would represent the first commercial-scale deployment of fully autonomous fixed-wing aircraft, potentially generating meaningful recurring revenue [3]
Key Risks
  1. Cash runway exhaustion risk — at $58-90M quarterly burn rate, the $183.9M cash position lasts only 18-24 months, forcing likely dilutive capital raises in a challenging small-cap market environment where MRLN trades at $1.81 [1][2]
  2. FAA certification uncertainty — no precedent exists for fully autonomous commercial fixed-wing flight certification, and the regulatory path could take 3-5+ years with no guarantee of approval, during which competitors with deeper resources may advance alternative approaches [3]
  3. Revenue concentration and execution risk — virtually all revenue depends on a small number of defense contracts, and the -29.4% YoY revenue decline indicates milestone-dependent revenue that is lumpy and unpredictable, with H2 2026 guidance of only $4-6M contingent on milestone achievement [1][2]
