Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NASDAQ ·United States
Merlin, Inc. is an aerospace and defense technology company building the operating system of record for autonomous flight. The company's Merlin Pilot is an AI-powered autonomous flight system designed to fly any aircraft, military or civilian, from takeoff to touchdown, with or without a human crew on board. Merlin holds a $105M IDIQ contract with US Special Operations Command for C-130J autonomous integration. The company went public via SPAC merger with Inflection Point Acquisition Corp. IV in March 2026. Headquartered in Boston, Massachusetts.
·www.merlinlabs.com

Investment Thesis
Merlin is a first-mover in certified autonomous flight AI, occupying a category-defining position with no competitor having yet achieved takeoff-to-touchdown autonomy certification on a military or civilian aircraft [1][2]. The $105M SOCOM IDIQ contract for C-130J autonomous integration provides foundational defense revenue, and the $53B defense autonomy line item offers massive TAM expansion potential. Post-PIPE cash of $183M with no debt provides approximately 2 years of runway at current burn rates ($23.3M adjusted EBITDA loss/quarter). Named to Fast Company's 2026 Most Innovative Companies, the technology has been validated by first automated takeoffs on certification candidate aircraft in both the U.S. and New Zealand, and the Condor product targets the large multi-crew aircraft market [1][3].
The bear case is the classic deep-tech startup risk profile. Revenue is just $1M/quarter ($7.68M TTM) against a $395M market cap (37.6x P/S), making this a venture-style investment with binary outcomes. Adjusted EBITDA loss of $(23.3M)/quarter means the $183M cash runway could be exhausted by mid-2028 without additional funding, creating dilution risk. The GAAP net loss of $(499.6M) TTM — even adjusted for SPAC non-cash charges — reflects enormous investment with minimal return to date [1][4]. The $105M SOCOM contract is an IDIQ ceiling, not a firm backlog — funded task orders may be a fraction of the ceiling and subject to defense procurement delays. Autonomous flight certification timelines are inherently uncertain and could slip by years, as FAA/military certification processes are notoriously slow. The stock has declined 76% from its $17 high to $4.10, suggesting the market is pricing in significant execution and dilution risk. Competitors with substantially more resources (Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Shield AI backed by Andreessen Horowitz) could accelerate their own autonomy programs.
Key Catalysts
  1. Achieving full takeoff-to-touchdown autonomous flight certification — would be the first in the industry and likely catalyze a re-rating from venture-style to defense technology premium valuation
  2. Funded task orders under the $105M SOCOM IDIQ contract for C-130J integration, converting contract ceiling into firm revenue and validating defense adoption of Merlin's autonomous platform
  3. Expansion of defense autonomy budget — the $53B autonomy line item with Autonomous Warfare Group funding creates a growing TAM that could support additional IDIQ contracts across military aircraft platforms
Key Risks
  1. Pre-revenue burn rate of $93M+ annually with $183M cash implies mid-2028 runway exhaustion without additional capital raises, creating significant dilution risk for existing shareholders
  2. Autonomous flight certification timelines are inherently unpredictable — FAA and military certification processes can take years longer than expected, delaying revenue while cash burns continue
  3. Stock has declined 76% from $17 52-week high to $4.10, reflecting market skepticism about the path from $1M/quarter revenue to commercial viability despite the compelling technology narrative
