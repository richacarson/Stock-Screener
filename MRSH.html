Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance Brokers ·NYSE ·United States
Marsh & McLennan Companies, Inc. is the world's largest insurance broker and risk advisor, operating through four business segments: Marsh (insurance brokerage and risk management), Guy Carpenter (reinsurance brokerage), Mercer (workforce and investment consulting), and Oliver Wyman (management consulting). The company serves clients in over 130 countries with approximately 85,000 employees. Ticker changed from MMC to MRSH on NYSE in January 2026. Founded in 1905, headquartered in New York City.

Investment Thesis
Marsh McLennan is a world-class compounder operating as the global leader in insurance brokerage and risk advisory. Full year 2025 revenue of $27.0B grew 10.3% with 4% underlying growth, achieving the 18th consecutive year of reported margin expansion [1]. The dividend profile is exceptional: 17 consecutive years of growth with a 14.12% 5-year CAGR and a just-announced 10% increase to $3.96 annually [5]. At 17.84x forward earnings, the stock trades at a reasonable premium for a capital-light business generating 29.35% ROE with ~$2B annual share repurchases [1]. The 'Thrive' program should drive $400M in annualized savings while the 20-acquisition-per-year pace adds organic growth optionality.
The primary concern is that Marsh McLennan's IOWN score is constrained by limited innovation (scored 4/10) — this is fundamentally an insurance brokerage, not a technology company, and the framework penalizes lack of innovation and AI deployment. Employee reviews note underinvestment in technology relative to the company's size [3]. At $86.5B market cap, growth sustainability depends on continued acquisition execution and pricing power in a competitive insurance market. The 140% D/E ratio, while manageable for a capital-light services firm, reflects acquisition-funded leverage that could constrain flexibility in a downturn. PEG ratio of 2.68 suggests the market prices in continuation of double-digit earnings growth that may prove difficult to sustain as the company scales beyond $27B in revenue [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Thrive restructuring program generating $400M in annualized savings could accelerate adjusted margin expansion beyond the 18-year streak already achieved
  2. Data center and climate risk creating secular demand growth for Marsh's specialty insurance brokerage and risk advisory capabilities across commercial property and casualty markets
  3. Continued M&A execution at 20+ acquisitions per year adding $250M+ in incremental annual revenue while maintaining double-digit adjusted EPS growth
Key Risks
  1. PEG ratio of 2.68 implies the market expects sustained ~8% earnings growth; any deceleration in underlying revenue growth from the current 4% pace or margin expansion stall could trigger multiple compression from the 22x trailing PE
  2. Technology underinvestment relative to insurtech competitors and AI-enabled advisory tools creates a latent competitive risk as clients increasingly demand digital-first risk management solutions
  3. Elevated D/E of 140% from acquisition-funded leverage limits balance sheet flexibility if a recession or insurance pricing cycle downturn reduces organic growth and acquisition targets become scarce
