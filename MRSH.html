Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance Brokers ·NYSE ·United States
Marsh McLennan is the world's largest insurance broker and risk advisor, operating through four businesses: Marsh (insurance brokerage and risk management), Guy Carpenter (reinsurance brokerage), Mercer (workforce and investment consulting), and Oliver Wyman (management consulting). The company reported 2025 full-year revenue of $27.0B (+10% YoY) with adjusted operating income of $7.3B and adjusted EPS of $9.75. Q2 2026 consolidated revenue grew 6.2% to $7.4B with 5% underlying growth. Market cap approximately $89B.

Investment Thesis
Marsh McLennan at 17.84x forward earnings offers reasonable value for the world's largest insurance broker, with Q2 2026 revenue of $7.4B (+6.2% YoY, 5% underlying) demonstrating steady growth momentum [1]. The Thrive restructuring program targeting $400M in annualized savings should drive further margin expansion beyond the 18 consecutive years already achieved. The dividend profile is exceptional: 17 consecutive years of growth with a 14.12% five-year CAGR, and a 10% increase to $3.96 annually declared in August 2026 [4]. ROE of 29.35% reflects strong capital efficiency, and the $89B market cap provides deep liquidity. The insurance broking model generates highly recurring revenue with 90%+ client retention rates, and Marsh's dominant market position creates pricing power in a consolidating industry.
The bear case centers on decelerating growth momentum and moderate leverage. Revenue growth has slowed from 10.3% in full-year 2025 to 6.2% in Q2 2026, with underlying organic growth of only 5% — suggesting the acquisition-driven boost is fading [1]. Q1 2026 EPS declined to $2.37 from $2.81 in Q1 2025, indicating lumpy quarterly earnings. D/E of 140% reflects acquisition-funded leverage that could constrain flexibility in a downturn. PEG ratio of 2.68 suggests the market prices in sustained growth that may prove difficult as the company scales beyond $27B in revenue [4]. Oliver Wyman's management consulting and portions of Mercer face AI disruption risk as analytical advisory work becomes increasingly automatable [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) with no attributions provides no signal for faith-based investors.
