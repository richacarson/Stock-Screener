Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
Marvell Technology designs and sells data infrastructure semiconductor solutions spanning data center core to network edge. The company's portfolio includes custom AI accelerators (XPUs), electro-optics (PAM4/coherent DSPs), networking switches, storage controllers, and SerDes IP. Under CEO Matt Murphy, Marvell transformed from a consumer chip company into a leading AI data infrastructure semiconductor platform through the acquisitions of Cavium ($6B), Inphi ($10B), and Innovium ($1.1B). Q2 FY2027 delivered record revenue of $2.739B (+37% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.94, and AI data center revenue exceeding $2B per quarter. Data center revenue now exceeds 80% of total company revenue. FY2028 revenue target raised to $16.5B.
7,480 Employees ·www.marvell.com

Investment Thesis
Marvell Technology is a premier AI data infrastructure semiconductor company delivering accelerating growth under CEO Matt Murphy's strategic vision. Q2 FY2027 set a new revenue record of $2.739B (up 37% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.94 and AI data center revenue exceeding $2B per quarter [1]. Data center revenue now represents over 80% of total company revenue, up from less than 10% a decade ago, confirming the transformative strategic pivot. FY2026 delivered $8.195B in revenue (up 42% YoY) with custom silicon doubling to $1.5B across hyperscaler customers [2]. The balance sheet is clean at 31% D/E with $3.84B cash, ROE exceeds 15% at 16.52%, and profit margins of 29% reflect the high-value nature of custom silicon and electro-optics products [5]. The NVIDIA alliance, multi-year hyperscaler design wins, and the 1.6T electro-optics transition provide exceptional revenue visibility toward the $16.5B FY2028 target.
The primary risk is valuation and execution. Marvell's stock pulled back approximately 20% from the July high of $272 to ~$217 after Q2 FY2027 earnings despite beating estimates, reflecting market concerns about execution risk and the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending at current levels [5]. The trailing P/E of 79.2x and forward P/E of 50.2x with a PEG of 2.10 still price in extraordinary growth expectations — any deceleration from the $16.5B FY2028 target or moderation in hyperscaler AI capex spending would trigger significant further compression. Customer concentration remains acute: a small number of hyperscaler accounts generate the majority of AI-related revenue. Broadcom remains the dominant custom ASIC provider with deeper customer relationships and greater scale, while NVIDIA's expanding networking portfolio creates competitive pressure in electro-optics and switches [3]. CEO Matt Murphy has been a net insider seller, disposing of 52,500 shares versus purchasing 13,600 shares, though sales were via a pre-arranged 10b5-1 plan and he retains significant holdings [6]. The semiconductor industry is inherently cyclical, and the current AI capex super-cycle may moderate as hyperscalers evaluate ROI on their massive investments.
