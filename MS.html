Investment Thesis
Morgan Stanley is executing at the highest level in its history. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $21.3B (+26.8% YoY) with EPS of $3.46, beating estimates of $2.93, following a record 2025 ($70.6B revenue, 28% EPS growth) [1][2]. The firm reached the strategic milestone of $10T in total client assets, with Wealth Management posting record $8.86B revenue (+14%) and $148.1B in net new assets — more than double the prior year [2]. At 17.80x forward P/E with a dividend raised 15% to $4.60 annualized (2.03% yield, 26.7% 5-year CAGR, 36.9% payout ratio), the stock offers a compelling total return profile for a franchise consistently earning above its cost of equity [6]. AI deployment across 3,500+ tools is driving measurable advisor productivity, with record equities revenue of $6.3B and investment banking up 58% to $2.4B demonstrating broad-based strength [2][3].
The bear case centers on valuation, cyclical exposure, and the sustainability of record results. The stock has appreciated significantly — now at $227 with forward P/E of 17.80x — reducing the margin of safety compared to historical averages near 12-14x [6]. Despite the wealth management pivot, approximately 35% of revenue still comes from Institutional Securities, which is highly sensitive to market conditions, M&A volumes, and trading activity — Q2's 58% investment banking surge and record equities reflect exceptional market conditions that may not persist. D/E of 502% (typical for systemically important banks) creates balance sheet vulnerability in a severe credit cycle. CEO Pick's $16M in share sales and net insider selling across multiple executives suggest internal caution about current valuations [5]. Wealth management fee compression from robo-advisors, AI-native fintech platforms, and regulatory pressure remains a secular risk.
