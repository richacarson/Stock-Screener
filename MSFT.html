Investment Thesis
Microsoft remains the preeminent AI infrastructure compounder in global equities. Q4 FY2026 revenue of $90.01B (+18% YoY) beat consensus, Azure crossed $100B in annual revenue for the first time on 43% constant-currency growth, and Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats jumped from 20M to over 30M in a single quarter [1]. Commercial remaining performance obligations rose 84% to $678B, giving multi-year visibility into AI-driven demand, and forward guidance of ~45% constant-currency Azure growth for Q1 FY2027 signals accelerating rather than decelerating momentum. At 25.57x forward earnings with 40.31% profit margins, 34.04% ROE, and 18% revenue growth, valuation remains reasonable for a company compounding at this quality [6].
The bear case centers on capital intensity and organizational disruption. The ~$175B calendar-2026 capex commitment — reduced from $190B via an accounting change (extending data center useful life from 15 to 25 years) rather than reduced ambition — drew scrutiny over whether the adjustment obscures true capital intensity risk if AI demand growth decelerates [1]. Internally, Nadella has dismantled the decades-old Senior Leadership Team, demoted veteran executives like Charlie Bell to individual-contributor roles, and is layering a rigid February 2026 return-to-office mandate on top of 15,000+ prior-year layoffs — a level of organizational churn that could erode execution quality even as financial results remain strong [2]. Microsoft's total emissions rose 25% YoY in FY2025 as the AI buildout accelerated, with Scope 2 emissions nearly 10x higher, creating mounting investor pressure on water and energy disclosure across the hyperscaler cohort [5]. The Inspire Impact Score of -93 (MISALIGNED) continues to limit allocation appeal for faith-based investors despite strong operational ESG practices [7].
