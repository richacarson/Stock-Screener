Investment Thesis
Strategy offers leveraged, capital-markets-native exposure to bitcoin that no other public company replicates at scale. The company holds approximately 843,775 BTC (~4.00% of total supply) at a Forward P/E of just 2.84x, and Q2 2026 saw net debt reduced 18% to $6.7B even while raising $8.4B in fresh capital, including $5.5B in digital credit — the largest quarterly raise in company history [1][2]. The newly formalized three-layer capital architecture (Bitcoin, STRC digital credit, stablecoin-linked digital money) targets a 30% annual BTC yield and digital credit issuance equal to 10-20% of BTC reserves, giving investors clearer visibility into how Saylor intends to compound bitcoin-per-share over time. The legacy software business is also re-accelerating, with subscription revenue up 54% YoY in Q2 2026.
The bear case centers on extreme concentration and volatility risk. Strategy posted an $8.6B net loss in Q2 2026 driven entirely by non-cash mark-to-market bitcoin losses as BTC fell to ~$58,700, and ROE of -63.56% with a profit margin near -6,400% reflect how violently GAAP results swing with bitcoin's price [1]. The legacy software business, while re-accelerating in subscriptions, still generated flat-to-declining revenue over the past five years ($510.8M in FY2021 to $477.2M in FY2025) and remains a small fraction of the company's ~$77B-plus asset base. Saylor's stated ambition to become the 'world's largest company by market cap' through bitcoin dominance, alongside his direct involvement in Bitcoin governance debates (opposing BIP-110), signals a finite, dominance-first posture that could invite regulatory or community pushback. Bitcoin ETFs (IBIT, FBTC) continue to offer cleaner, unlevered BTC exposure, structurally capping how much valuation premium MSTR can sustain over its bitcoin NAV.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued execution of the three-layer capital architecture — Bitcoin, STRC digital credit, and stablecoin-linked digital money — could drive bitcoin-per-share compounding toward the stated 30% annual yield target if capital markets access remains open [2]
  2. STRC ETF integration, which Saylor has flagged as 'the next unlock' for both bitcoin and MSTR demand, would broaden the investor base able to access Strategy's digital credit instruments [2]
  3. Any bitcoin price recovery from the ~$58,700 Q2 2026 quarter-end level would sharply reverse the GAAP mark-to-market losses that dominated recent headline results, given the leveraged 843,775 BTC position [1]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme concentration risk in a single volatile asset — the $8.6B Q2 2026 net loss illustrates how quickly GAAP results and reported equity can swing with bitcoin's price, and further declines could pressure preferred-dividend coverage despite the 18% net debt reduction [1]
  2. Saylor's public ambition to dominate bitcoin market cap rankings and his direct involvement in Bitcoin protocol governance disputes (BIP-110) create reputational and regulatory risk if perceived as self-interested influence over the underlying asset [2][3]
  3. Bitcoin ETF competition (IBIT, FBTC) continues to offer cleaner, unlevered exposure, structurally capping the premium MSTR can sustain over bitcoin NAV as the market matures
