Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Exploration & Production ·NYSE ·United States
Matador Resources Company is an independent energy company engaged in the exploration, development, production, and acquisition of oil and natural gas resources in the United States, focused primarily on the oil and liquids-rich portion of the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring plays in the Delaware Basin in Southeast New Mexico and West Texas. The company also operates midstream assets through its San Mateo subsidiary. Founded by Joseph Wm. Foran in 2003 and headquartered in Dallas, Texas, Matador reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.19B (+32.5% YoY) and raised full-year oil production growth guidance from 4% to 7%.

Investment Thesis
Matador Resources at 6.78x forward earnings offers deep value in the Permian Basin, with Q2 2026 delivering a blowout quarter — revenue of $1.19B (+32.5% YoY) and adjusted EPS of $2.61 beating consensus by 59% [1]. The company raised full-year oil production growth guidance from 4% to 7%, projects ~$900M in 2026 free cash flow (>11% FCF yield on a ~$7.5B market cap), and is actively de-leveraging from the $1.9B Advance Energy acquisition. D/E of 69.1% is well-managed, the $1.50/share annualized dividend provides a 2.45% yield, and exceptional insider alignment — 32 purchases and zero sales by C-suite and board in the trailing 12 months — signals management conviction [4][5]. Reserves of 703M BOE (up 5% YoY), integrated midstream operations through San Mateo, and structural energy demand growth from AI data center buildout create a favorable multi-year setup.
The bear case centers on commodity cyclicality, governance risk, and organizational fragility. Oil and gas prices remain the dominant variable in Matador's earnings, and the 160.6% EPS growth reflects a cyclical rebound that analyst consensus expects to normalize to just 4.4% full-year growth ($6.10 EPS) [1]. The company's culture presents the most significant non-financial risk: employee reviews describe 'total micromanagement' from the CEO, a 'toxic' culture rated 2.4/5 stars (29% below Energy sector peers), and only 41% of employees recommending the company as a workplace [2]. This CEO-centric governance model with no HR department creates severe key-person risk — if Foran departs, there is no institutional culture or evident succession plan to sustain operations. The Inspire Impact Score of 23 (MIXED) reflects clean ESG positioning for an E&P but limited positive impact. At 58 overall, Matador is a strong financial story undermined by weak purpose and organizational culture.
