Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance - Specialty (Mortgage Insurance) ·NYSE ·United States
MGIC Investment Corporation, through its principal subsidiary Mortgage Guaranty Insurance Corporation, is one of the largest private mortgage insurers in the U.S., protecting mortgage lenders and investors from credit losses on low-down-payment home loans and enabling homebuyers to purchase with less than 20% down. Q2 2026 net income was $182.1 million ($0.86/share, 14.5% annualized ROE) on new insurance written of $18 billion (+8.5% YoY), though revenue declined 2.9% YoY to $295.4 million as the persistence of the existing insured book and a benign credit environment offset slower new-loan volume. The company reinstated its dividend in 2021 after suspending it during the financial crisis and has since raised it for five consecutive years while maintaining a conservative, low-leverage balance sheet.

Investment Thesis
MGIC is a conservatively capitalized, highly profitable mortgage insurer trading at a modest 9.43x forward earnings with a 59.6% profit margin, 14.5% annualized ROE, and a debt-to-equity ratio of just 13% [4]. New insurance written grew 8.5% YoY to $18 billion in Q2 2026, and the company has returned to a shareholder-friendly capital allocation posture, raising its dividend for five straight years (19.3% five-year CAGR) while maintaining a low 17.2% payout ratio that leaves ample room for continued increases or buybacks [1][4]. The persistency of MGIC's existing insured book, combined with a still-tight housing supply backdrop, supports a durable earnings base even as new business volumes ebb and flow with mortgage origination cycles.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, innovation stagnation, and governance signals. Revenue fell 2.9% YoY in Q2 2026 and the five-year revenue CAGR is essentially flat at 0.6%, reflecting a mature, non-growing insured-portfolio base that is highly sensitive to home-purchase volumes and mortgage rates [1]. Glassdoor reviews describe a company that has become 'antiquated' with slow technology adoption and a leadership team increasingly disconnected from staff, raising doubts about MGIC's ability to compete if AI-driven underwriting tools reshape risk-based pricing industry-wide [2]. Perhaps most notably, CEO Timothy Mattke and President/COO Salvatore Miosi combined to sell roughly $9.74 million of stock in mid-2026 with zero offsetting insider purchases, and the company's own history — near-failure and a 13-year dividend suspension following reckless pre-2008 underwriting — is a cautionary reminder that mortgage insurance economics can deteriorate sharply and quickly in a housing downturn [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued dividend growth from a low 17.2% payout ratio, with a 19.3% five-year dividend CAGR and five consecutive years of increases since the 2021 reinstatement, supporting total shareholder return even without book growth [4]
  2. New insurance written growth of 8.5% YoY to $18 billion in Q2 2026 signals resilient primary market share even as overall mortgage origination volumes remain constrained by elevated rates [1]
  3. Persistently benign credit performance (14.5% annualized ROE, 59.6% profit margin) as low unemployment and home-price appreciation keep claim incidence low across the existing insured book [1][4]
Key Risks
  1. Flat-to-declining top line (-2.9% YoY revenue in Q2 2026, 0.6% five-year CAGR) with no evident growth catalyst beyond housing-cycle volume, capping upside in a business increasingly viewed as a mature, no-growth cash cow [1]
  2. Reported net insider selling of $9.74 million with zero offsetting buys in 2026, including sales by both the CEO and President/COO, which is a discouraging signal for a stock trading near multi-year highs [3]
  3. Glassdoor-documented resistance to technology adoption and a widening leadership-employee disconnect leave MGIC poorly positioned if AI-enabled underwriting tools compress the pricing and risk-selection advantages that have historically differentiated mortgage insurers [2]

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Mortgage insurance is fundamentally a regulated, capital-backed risk-transfer business — MGIC must hold state- and GSE-mandated capital reserves against insured risk-in-force, a structural function AI cannot replicate or disintermediate [5].
