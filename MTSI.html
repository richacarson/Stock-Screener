Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
MACOM Technology Solutions designs and manufactures analog semiconductor solutions for wireless and wireline applications across the RF, microwave, millimeter wave, and lightwave spectrum. The company serves data center, industrial and defense, and telecom markets with integrated circuits, multi-chip modules, diodes, amplifiers, switches, and subsystems used in 5G infrastructure, radar, electronic warfare, fiber optics, and AI data center interconnects.

Investment Thesis
MACOM Technology Solutions is a direct beneficiary of the AI infrastructure buildout, with Q2 FY2026 delivering record $289M revenue (22% YoY growth) and management raising data center revenue growth guidance from 35-40% to over 60% on 1.6T optical demand [2]. Q3 FY2026 guidance of $331-339M implies significant continued acceleration. The company's analog semiconductors — TIAs, linear drivers, laser drivers — are essential, non-substitutable components of AI data center optical interconnects. The balance sheet is conservatively managed with D/E of 29%, and adjusted gross margins are expanding above 59%. Strategic investments in IQE compound semiconductors and Hot Via chip-scale packaging strengthen the long-term technology position [3].
The bear case centers on extreme valuation and execution risk. At ~105x trailing P/E and 58.7x forward P/E with a PEG of 1.96, MACOM is priced for sustained 25%+ growth in a cyclical semiconductor industry — any deceleration in hyperscaler AI spending or competitive share loss to Broadcom/Marvell could trigger severe multiple compression [8]. Massive insider selling totaling $348M over 12 months by major shareholders and executives suggests insiders view current prices as stretched [7]. Profit margins of 16.5% remain below semiconductor peers, and the low Inspiration score reflects no articulated purpose beyond semiconductor product excellence, raising questions about long-term organizational resilience. Geopolitical risks including AI chip export controls and rising Chinese semiconductor competition create additional headwinds for the entire group [3].

  3. Geopolitical risks including tighter AI chip export controls, rising Chinese semiconductor competition, and supply chain disruption in compound semiconductors (GaN, InP) could pressure the growth trajectory
