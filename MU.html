Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
Micron Technology designs, develops, and manufactures memory and storage products including DRAM, NAND, and High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) for AI, data center, mobile, and automotive applications. Founded in 1978 and headquartered in Boise, Idaho, with approximately 53,000 employees. Micron is in a historic AI-driven profit supercycle — Q3 FY2026 (ended May 28, 2026) delivered record revenue of $41.46B (+346% YoY), GAAP net income of $28.24B, and 84.6% gross margins. Q4 FY2026 guidance of $50B revenue with ~86% gross margins would represent the most profitable quarter in semiconductor history. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra has led the company since 2017. Market cap is approximately $991B at a stock price of $877.57.

Investment Thesis
Micron is in a once-in-a-generation AI-driven profit supercycle that continues to accelerate — Q3 FY2026 revenue of $41.46B (+346% YoY) with $28.24B GAAP net income ($24.67 EPS) and 84.6% gross margins shattered records, and Q4 guidance of $50B revenue with ~86% gross margins and $31 EPS would make it the most profitable quarter in semiconductor history [1]. At just 5.61x forward P/E and 0.03x PEG, Micron remains remarkably inexpensive for a company delivering triple-digit revenue growth in a structural AI demand environment where the HBM TAM exceeds $100B by 2027 [5]. The fortress balance sheet (D/E 14.9%, massive operating cash flow of $25.39B in Q3 alone) funds $25B+ in CapEx and CHIPS Act MegaFab construction without leverage risk. As one of only three global HBM producers with entire 2026 supply sold out under binding agreements, Micron has pricing power and volume visibility that is unprecedented in memory history.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, peak margins, and emerging risks. Memory is historically the most volatile semiconductor segment, and 84.6% gross margins with ~86% Q4 guidance represent all-time peaks — any normalization toward 50-60% would cause a significant earnings correction [1]. At $877.57 per share and $991B market cap, the stock prices in continued supercycle execution. A UBS survey indicating 60% of businesses are cutting AI spending raises demand risk, and discussions around windfall profit taxes on AI-focused memory companies have emerged as a new policy overhang [2]. Chinese competitor CXMT is making memory manufacturing advances that could eventually pressure conventional DRAM pricing, though it remains technology generations behind in HBM. Samsung and SK Hynix competition remains fierce — any NVIDIA procurement shift could reduce Micron's premium HBM share. Hyperscaler CapEx concentration (Microsoft, Meta, Google, Amazon) means Micron's fortunes are tied to a handful of AI infrastructure budgets. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors despite Micron's extensive ESG programs across 21 positive attributions [4].

Key Risks
  1. Memory cycle peak risk — HBM gross margins of 84.6% (guiding to 86%) are historically unprecedented and structurally unsustainable; any normalization toward 50-60% would cause dramatic earnings decline and stock correction from $991B market cap [1]
  2. AI spending slowdown signals — UBS survey showing 60% of businesses cutting AI spend, potential windfall profit tax legislation, and hyperscaler CapEx concentration in 4-5 customers create demand vulnerability if AI infrastructure ROI disappoints [2]
  3. Competitive and geopolitical risk — Samsung/SK Hynix HBM competition, Chinese CXMT advancing in conventional memory, and potential U.S.-China trade tensions affecting Micron's access to Chinese markets (Micron was previously restricted in China) [2][4]
