Company Profile
Technology ·Scientific & Technical Instruments ·NCM ·United States
MicroVision develops and commercializes lidar sensors and perception solutions for automotive, industrial, security, and defense applications. Product lineup includes MAVIN (MEMS-based 905nm long-range), MOVIA (flash-based short/mid-range), IRIS and HALO (1550nm long-range), and Scantinel (1550nm FMCW lidar). The company acquired Luminar Technologies' lidar assets for $33M in February 2026 and Scantinel Photonics in April 2026, executed a 1-for-15 reverse stock split in August 2026, and cut 15% of its workforce. Q2 2026 revenue was approximately $1.5M with 2026 full-year guidance of $10-16M and target gross margins of 40-45%. TTM revenue is $2.87M with a $36.7M market cap.

Investment Thesis
MicroVision at $1.20 is a speculative lidar play with an expanding product portfolio and multi-market strategy. The 'Lidar 2.0' approach under CEO Glen DeVos targets industrial automation, defense/security, and automotive applications with five distinct sensor platforms (MAVIN, MOVIA, IRIS, HALO, Scantinel FMCW) acquired partly through the $33M Luminar asset purchase [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.5M and full-year guidance of $10-16M at 40-45% gross margins represent early commercial traction, and the company's diversification beyond automotive-only lidar reduces single-market dependency. At $36.7M market cap, even modest commercial success in defense or industrial markets could generate outsized returns.
The bear case is severe and fundamental. MicroVision has operated for 33 years without achieving commercial scale, generating only $2.87M in TTM revenue while burning $67.5M annually in operating cash flow [1][3]. The $45.5M in total debt against $27.2M cash, combined with the convertible note issuance and 1-for-15 reverse stock split, signals acute capital needs and potential further dilution. The core automotive lidar thesis faces existential risk from camera-only AI vision systems (Tesla FSD approach), and industrial/defense markets are earlier-stage with unproven demand. The company reduced headcount by 15% post-acquisition, and leadership changed in September 2025, creating execution uncertainty during a critical commercialization period. Competitive pressure from Hesai, Valeo, and other established lidar manufacturers with actual production contracts and revenue makes it unclear why OEMs would choose MicroVision's technology. The stock has fallen from $19.05 to $1.20 (94% decline from 52-week high), reflecting the market's skepticism about the commercial thesis.
Key Catalysts
  1. First automotive OEM or Tier 1 supplier production contract announcement would validate MicroVision's Lidar 2.0 technology and transform the commercial narrative from pre-revenue to production-committed [1][2]
  2. Industrial and defense revenue ramp toward the $10-16M 2026 guidance at 40-45% gross margins would demonstrate the multi-market strategy's viability and reduce automotive-only dependency [1]
  3. Integration of Luminar and Scantinel technology assets could yield a comprehensive lidar platform with unique 905nm and 1550nm capabilities covering all major automotive and industrial use cases [1]
Key Risks
  1. Existential camera-versus-lidar risk — Tesla's camera-only Full Self-Driving approach could prove sufficient for autonomous driving, eliminating the largest addressable market for lidar sensors and the core investment thesis [2]
  2. Cash burn of $67.5M annually against $27.2M cash and $2.87M revenue creates near-term survival risk, with further dilutive financing likely required to fund operations through 2027 absent a significant revenue inflection [3]
  3. 33 years of operation without commercial scale raises fundamental questions about whether MicroVision's technology can win production contracts against established competitors Hesai and Valeo who already ship sensors to automotive OEMs in volume [1]

AI Risk Protection (5/9): MicroVision faces genuine AI-driven substitution risk — Tesla's camera-only Full Self-Driving approach demonstrates that pure vision AI with neural networks may replace lidar sensors entirely for autonomous driving, the largest addressable market for lidar technology [1][2]. If camera-based AI vision systems prove sufficient for regulatory-approved autonomous driving, the core investment thesis for automotive lidar collapses. However, industrial applications (warehouse automation, robotics), defense (perimeter security, target detection), and agriculture/mining provide partially protected markets where lidar's precision advantages over cameras are more defensible. The broader AI boom drives demand for autonomous systems that use lidar (robot taxis, delivery vehicles, industrial robots), providing some tailwind.
