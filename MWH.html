Company Profile
Industrials ·Engineering & Construction ·NASDAQ ·United States
SOLV Energy is the largest utility-scale solar engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contractor in the United States, providing design, construction, commissioning, operations, maintenance, and repowering services for solar and battery storage projects. The company has completed over 300 utility-scale solar installations totaling 15+ gigawatts across 30 states. Founded in 2008 as a subsidiary of Swinerton Builders, acquired by American Securities in 2021, and IPO'd on NASDAQ in February 2026 at a $6B valuation. Headquartered in San Diego, California.

Investment Thesis
SOLV Energy is the dominant US utility-scale solar EPC contractor entering a once-in-a-generation growth cycle, with Q1 2026 revenue surging 66% YoY to $677M and full-year guidance of $3.72-3.82B with adjusted EBITDA of $435-455M [1]. The company's $8B project backlog provides 8.5 quarters of revenue visibility, and the fundamental demand driver — the intersection of AI data center buildout and renewable energy mandates — is structural and multi-decade. The balance sheet is conservatively managed post-IPO with $304M net cash and D/E of only 9.9% [4]. At 17.0x forward earnings, SOLV Energy is attractively valued for a category leader delivering 51% revenue growth with rapidly expanding margins (adjusted EBITDA margin guidance raised to 11.7-11.9%).
The bear case centers on political and regulatory risk: the Trump administration's anti-solar policies, potential IRA (Inflation Reduction Act) rollbacks, and tariff uncertainty on imported solar panels could materially impact project economics and demand [2]. Net profit margin of 5.99% reflects the inherent challenge of construction businesses — thin margins mean execution missteps, weather delays, or supply chain disruptions can quickly erode profitability [4]. As a newly public company (February 2026 IPO), SOLV Energy lacks a track record as a public company and carries IPO-related costs including a $52M non-cash charge in Q1 [1]. The 45.5x trailing P/E reflects the IPO-year earnings distortion, and the $6B market cap prices in substantial growth execution. Revenue concentration risk exists in utility-scale solar — any slowdown in project permitting or interconnection queues could defer revenue recognition.
Key Catalysts
  1. $8B project backlog converting to revenue at an accelerating pace, with 2026 guidance already raised to $3.72-3.82B on the back of hyperscale data center demand driving corporate PPA growth for renewable power
  2. Battery energy storage expansion providing a second growth vector beyond solar, with storage attach rates increasing across the project portfolio as grid-scale batteries become economically essential
  3. Margin expansion through operational leverage and productivity gains — adjusted EBITDA margin guidance of 11.7-11.9% represents significant improvement from historical levels as the company scales past fixed costs
Key Risks
  1. Political and regulatory risk from potential IRA rollbacks, solar tariff increases, or federal policy changes that could reduce the economic incentive for utility-scale solar projects and compress demand
  2. Construction execution risk inherent in a 5.99% net margin business — weather delays, labor shortages, supply chain disruptions, or interconnection queue backlogs could materially impact project timelines and profitability
  3. Newly public company risk — IPO'd February 2026 with no public market track record, PE sponsor overhang, and elevated trailing P/E (45.5x) that leaves limited margin for error in growth execution
