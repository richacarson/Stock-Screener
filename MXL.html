Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
MaxLinear, Inc. designs, develops, and markets integrated, high-performance analog and mixed-signal semiconductor SoC solutions for broadband, connectivity, and infrastructure markets. Key product is the Keystone 5nm CMOS PAM4 DSP for optical data center interconnects. Founded in 2003 by CEO Kishore Seendripu, the company is a fabless semiconductor designer. Market cap approximately $7.6 billion.
1,400 Employees ·www.maxlinear.com

Investment Thesis
MaxLinear is executing a dramatic turnaround driven by the Keystone 5nm PAM4 DSP platform, which is powering the company's pivot into AI data center optical interconnects. Q2 2026 revenue of $168.8M grew 55% YoY with infrastructure revenue surging 145%, and management raised 2026 optical data center revenue guidance to $210-230M on robust customer orders [1]. The company returned to positive GAAP EPS ($0.02) for the first time since the downcycle began, with non-GAAP gross margins expanding to 59.5% and guided to 60% in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue guidance of $210-220M (30% sequential growth) signals continued acceleration. The conservative balance sheet (D/E 33%, $93.7M cash) provides financial flexibility.
MaxLinear remains a high-risk turnaround with significant execution uncertainty. The company's revenue collapsed from $1.12B in FY2022 to $360M in FY2024 — a 68% decline — and while the recovery is strong, FY2026 revenue will likely reach only $700-750M, still well below the prior peak [5]. Forward P/E of 53.31 prices in substantial earnings growth that has yet to materialize at the GAAP level — the company is barely breakeven [2]. The stock has appreciated 579% over the past 52 weeks, creating significant downside risk if the Keystone ramp disappoints or AI data center capex decelerates. Competition from Broadcom and Marvell in optical DSPs intensifies as data rates move to 1.6T, where larger competitors have deeper design resources. The failed $3.8B Silicon Motion merger in 2023 raised governance questions about capital allocation discipline.
Key Catalysts
  1. Keystone PAM4 DSP production ramp driving optical data center revenue to $210-230M in 2026, with next-gen 1.6T products in customer sampling that could extend the growth cycle into 2027-2028
  2. Q3 2026 revenue guidance of $210-220M represents 30% sequential growth, with trajectory toward annualized $800M+ run-rate that would approach the prior revenue peak
  3. Gross margin expansion from 59.5% to guided 60%+ as infrastructure mix increases, with operating leverage on fixed costs driving potential path to sustained GAAP profitability
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 53x and 579% 52-week price appreciation embed optimistic expectations — any deceleration in the Keystone ramp or AI data center capex could trigger severe multiple compression
  2. Revenue of $468M in FY2025 is still 58% below the $1.12B FY2022 peak, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of -0.5% reflects a deeply cyclical business that may face another downturn
  3. Broadcom and Marvell have significantly larger optical DSP portfolios and deeper customer relationships at hyperscalers, creating competitive risk as data rates transition to 1.6T and beyond
