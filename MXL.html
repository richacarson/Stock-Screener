Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
MaxLinear designs, develops, and markets integrated high-performance analog and mixed-signal semiconductor SoC solutions for broadband, connectivity, and infrastructure markets. The company's Keystone 5nm CMOS PAM4 DSP platform for optical data center interconnects is driving a fundamental business transformation, with Infrastructure growing 130%+ YoY in Q1 2026 to become the largest end market. H1 2026 revenue reached $306M (+49% YoY), recovering from a severe FY2023-2024 downcycle. Founded in 2003 by CEO Kishore Seendripu. Market cap approximately $8.5B.

Investment Thesis
MaxLinear is executing a dramatic turnaround driven by the Keystone 5nm PAM4 DSP platform, pivoting into AI data center optical interconnects. H1 2026 revenue of $306M (+49% YoY) demonstrates accelerating demand from multiple hyperscale AI customers, with Infrastructure growing 130%+ to become the largest segment [2]. The stock has appreciated from ~$12 to $93.84 as the Keystone ramp validated the pivot. The conservative balance sheet (D/E 30.6%) provides financial flexibility, and Forward P/E of 35.9x has compressed from 53x as earnings estimates rise, reflecting improving visibility on the profitability path. Q2 2026 guidance of $160-170M implies annualized run-rate above $640M, approaching a scale where GAAP profitability becomes achievable [2].
The bear case centers on execution risk, profitability, and valuation. Despite 55% YoY revenue growth, MaxLinear remains GAAP unprofitable with -18.2% profit margin and -21.3% ROE — the recovery has not yet translated to bottom-line results [4]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of ~2% masks a severe boom-bust cycle ($1.12B peak FY2022 to $360M trough FY2024), raising questions about the company's ability to sustain growth through full cycles. The stock's 600%+ appreciation from its 2024 trough embeds substantial expectations — any deceleration in the Keystone ramp or AI data center capex could trigger severe multiple compression. Broadcom and Marvell have significantly larger optical DSP portfolios and deeper customer relationships at hyperscalers, creating competitive risk as data rates transition to 1.6T [1]. The failed $3.8B Silicon Motion merger in 2023 raised governance questions about capital allocation discipline.
Key Catalysts
  1. Keystone PAM4 DSP production ramp driving optical data center revenue growth 130%+ YoY, with design wins at multiple hyperscale customers providing revenue visibility into 2027 and next-gen 1.6T products in development [2]
  2. Path to GAAP profitability as revenue scales — non-GAAP operating margin improved from -19% (FY2024) to +9% (FY2025), with further operating leverage expected as Infrastructure mix increases and revenue approaches $700M+ annualized [1]
  3. Forward P/E compression from 53x to 35.9x in 8 weeks reflects improving earnings visibility, with continued estimate revisions likely as the profitability inflection approaches [4]
Key Risks
  1. GAAP profitability remains elusive with -18.2% profit margin and -21.3% ROE despite 55% revenue growth — stock-based compensation and amortization of acquired intangibles consume operating leverage [4]
  2. Concentration risk in hyperscale AI customers — if one or two major customers slow orders or shift to competing optical DSP solutions from Broadcom or Marvell, revenue could decline sharply [1]
  3. Boom-bust revenue history ($1.12B to $360M to recovery) and the failed Silicon Motion acquisition raise questions about management's ability to build durable, through-cycle revenue growth rather than riding cyclical demand [1][3]
