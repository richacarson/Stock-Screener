Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
N-able is a global software company providing unified endpoint management, security operations, and data protection solutions to IT service providers (MSPs) and organizations worldwide. The company offers remote monitoring and management (RMM), endpoint detection and response (EDR/XDR), backup and disaster recovery, and professional services automation through a cloud-based SaaS platform. Spun off from SolarWinds in July 2021, N-able reported Q2 2026 revenue of $138.2M (+5.9% YoY) and ARR of $544M, while guiding full-year 2026 revenue to $539-542M (+6-7% YoY) after reducing guidance from 8-9% due to weaker UEM/EDR renewals and a go-to-market leadership transition [1]. The company recently appointed a Chief Innovation Officer and Chief AI Officer to advance AI-driven platform capabilities [2].

Investment Thesis
N-able at 8.9x forward earnings is a deeply discounted SaaS platform generating $544M ARR, $70M free cash flow (13% FCF margin), and serving a growing IT managed services market with sticky, multi-tenant infrastructure [1]. The valuation implies the market prices in permanent single-digit growth, but if the go-to-market transformation (new CRO, AI investments, CAIO appointment) stabilizes renewal rates and reaccelerates growth toward the original 8-9% guidance, the forward multiple has meaningful expansion potential from today's depressed levels [2]. The company's security portfolio (EDR, XDR, MDR) addresses a growing MSP demand vector, and the low D/E ratio of 54% provides financial flexibility for M&A or accelerated product investment.
The bear case centers on decelerating growth and competitive displacement. Full-year guidance was cut from 8-9% to 6-7% after weaker UEM and EDR renewals and FX headwinds, with Q3 2026 guided at only 3% YoY growth — a concerning trajectory for a software company trading at any multiple [1]. The 6% headcount reduction signals operational distress rather than strength, and the go-to-market leadership transition (new CRO) creates execution uncertainty during a critical period. N-able faces intensifying competition from better-funded security platforms (CrowdStrike, SentinelOne) embedding AI-native capabilities, and from consolidated MSP platforms (ConnectWise, Kaseya) that offer broader functionality. Near-zero GAAP profitability (profit margin -0.96%, ROE -0.63%) limits N-able's ability to invest aggressively in AI R&D while competitors spend billions. The Inspire Impact Score of 45 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors, but the fundamental growth trajectory must improve to justify the current valuation.

AI Risk Protection (6/9): N-able's core RMM, security, and data protection platform serves IT service providers who manage complex multi-tenant environments requiring regulatory compliance, deep integration with client infrastructure, and 24/7 monitoring [1]. The MSP channel creates meaningful switching costs — migrating thousands of managed endpoints between platforms is operationally painful, providing structural protection against AI-driven displacement. However, AI tools could commoditize basic monitoring and alerting functions, and larger security vendors with more data (CrowdStrike, Microsoft, SentinelOne) are embedding AI into competing products at a pace N-able may struggle to match. The company's appointment of a CAIO signals awareness of the competitive AI imperative [2]. N-able's regulated security operations (EDR, MDR) provide additional moats, as compliance requirements limit pure-AI substitution.
