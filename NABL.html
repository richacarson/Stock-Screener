Company Profile

N-able, Inc. provides cloud-based software solutions for IT services management to managed service providers (MSPs). Spun off from SolarWinds in July 2021. Products include remote monitoring and management (RMM), data protection, endpoint detection and response (EDR), and security solutions. FY2025 ARR of $540M growing 12% with 30% adjusted EBITDA margin. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $554-559M representing 8-9% growth.

Investment Thesis
N-able is a pure-play MSP software platform with $540M ARR growing 12% in FY2025, 30% adjusted EBITDA margins, and $114-118M projected unlevered free cash flow for 2026 — a high-quality SaaS profile at an attractive forward P/E of approximately 10x [1][5]. The MSP market is structurally growing as SMBs increasingly outsource IT management amid rising cybersecurity complexity and AI adoption challenges. N-able's 25,000+ MSP partners provide a sticky, recurring revenue base with strong net retention. The appointment of dedicated Chief Innovation Officer and Chief AI Officer in May 2026 signals commitment to AI-powered product evolution [2]. At $4.30 per share with $793M market cap, the stock trades at a significant discount to SaaS peers on both revenue and cash flow multiples.
The bear case centers on GAAP profitability concerns and competitive threats. Trailing net margin of -3.3% and negative ROE reflect significant intangible asset amortization from the SolarWinds spinoff, masking the underlying unit economics but creating real GAAP-basis losses [5]. Total debt of $394M at 1.8x net leverage is manageable but limits flexibility. Revenue growth is decelerating from 12% to 8-9% in 2026 guidance, raising questions about the growth runway. AI poses a dual threat: Microsoft and Google embedding IT management capabilities into platform products could reduce MSP tool demand, while AI automation could compress the billable services that MSPs sell, reducing their need for N-able's tools [2]. The stock has traded down to $4.30 from higher post-spinoff levels, and the SolarWinds heritage association may weigh on brand perception in security-conscious markets.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI product launches following the May 2026 Chief AI Officer appointment that embed AI-powered automation into RMM and security tools driving accelerated ARR growth beyond the 8-9% 2026 guidance
  2. GAAP profitability inflection as spinoff-related intangible amortization declines and operating leverage from 30% EBITDA margins translates to positive net income validating the 10x forward PE multiple
  3. MSP market expansion as rising cybersecurity threats shadow AI governance requirements and AI complexity drive SMB outsourcing to managed service providers increasing demand for N-able's platform
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration from 12% to 8-9% continues into 2027 as MSP market saturation or large platform competition from Microsoft and Google erodes N-able's growth premium
  2. AI disruption compresses MSP billable hours as automated monitoring and troubleshooting reduce the volume of work requiring N-able's tools potentially shrinking the addressable market
  3. GAAP losses persist with -3.3% net margin and negative ROE deterring institutional investors despite strong underlying cash flow generation and adjusted EBITDA profitability

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
5
AI Risk Protection (5/9): MSP software faces meaningful AI disruption risk from both directions. AI can automate routine IT monitoring, troubleshooting, and patching tasks that currently require MSP technician involvement using N-able's tools — potentially reducing the volume of billable work MSPs perform and their demand for N-able's platform [2]. Microsoft, Google, and other large platform providers are embedding AI-powered IT management capabilities directly into their enterprise products, which could displace third-party MSP tools. However, AI also creates new security threats that increase demand for MSP services (shadow AI governance, AI-powered cyberattacks, compliance complexity), and N-able's role as the technology platform connecting MSPs to these challenges provides structural relevance. CEO Pagliuca explicitly warns that AI is becoming a competitive risk for MSPs, suggesting the company recognizes the threat [2]. AI Opportunity (0/1): While N-able has appointed a Chief AI Officer and plans AI integration, no quantifiable AI-specific revenue has been reported. The company is in the investment phase rather than the monetization phase of its AI strategy.
