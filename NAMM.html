Namib Minerals operates as a gold producer in Africa, owning and operating How Mine, Mazowe Mine, and Redwing Mine in Zimbabwe. The company was incorporated in 2024 as a subsidiary of The Southern SelliBen Trust and is based in Grand Cayman, Cayman Islands. TTM revenue is approximately $97M with gross margins of 56.8%. New CEO Tulani Sikwila was appointed March 2026, subsequently taking the dual chairman-CEO role in July 2026. Market cap of $64M at $1.14/share, down 85% from the 52-week high of $7.42.

Investment Thesis
Namib Minerals at $1.14 with a $64M market cap offers leveraged exposure to elevated gold prices through a small-scale Zimbabwean producer with a credible growth trajectory. The company guides 2026 production of 28,000-31,500 oz with adjusted EBITDA of $50-$62M at $4,500/oz gold, representing 12-26% production growth over FY2025's 25,000 oz [1]. TTM revenue of $97M with 39.7% YoY growth and gross margins of 56.8% demonstrate operational momentum at current gold prices [4]. New CEO Sikwila brings institutional knowledge and a mining engineering background including AI/blockchain integration in the minerals value chain [2]. The Redwing Mine restart could meaningfully scale production over the medium term. Trading volume has improved materially, with daily dollar volume now at $1.18M, above the IOWN liquidity floor [4].
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. The stock has fallen 85% from its $7.42 52-week high to $1.14, destroying over $300M in market value, and the infrastructure score of 10 is misleading — it is based on only three available metrics, with the remaining seven unavailable due to the company's recent incorporation [4]. The 111.5% profit margin and 0.57 trailing P/E are almost certainly distorted by non-cash revaluation gains, not sustainable operating economics — operating margin of 34.8% provides a more realistic picture. Operating exclusively in Zimbabwe exposes the company to political instability, currency devaluation, potential resource nationalism, and regulatory unpredictability [4]. Cash of only $1.75M against negative free cash flow of -$33M creates severe balance sheet constraints — the Redwing restart requires external financing that may not materialize. The Cayman Islands incorporation and controlling trust ownership through The Southern SelliBen Trust create governance opacity. Rapid leadership turnover — new CEO, new chairman-CEO dual role, new CFO all in 2026 — signals organizational instability [2][3].
