Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Navient Corporation is a specialty finance company that originates and services private education loans and provides business processing solutions. Spun off from Sallie Mae in 2014, Navient was permanently banned by the CFPB from federal student loan servicing in 2024 and ordered to pay $120M for systemic borrower abuses. The company is now pivoting to private education lending through its Earnest platform, with originations surging 63% YoY to $815M in Q2 2026. Activist investor Edward Bramson (Sherborne Investors) was appointed CEO/Chairman in June 2026 to lead a turnaround. Market cap is approximately $850M with only 670 employees.

Investment Thesis
Navient at 9.4x forward earnings and 0.35x book value is a deep-value turnaround play with activist catalyst. Q2 2026 core EPS of $0.29 (+45% YoY) and private education loan originations of $815M (+63% YoY) demonstrate the strategic pivot from federal servicing to private lending is gaining traction under new CEO Edward Bramson [1][2]. Operating expenses declined 18% under the restructuring, and $340M in operating cash flow covers the $0.64 annual dividend (7.1% yield). Forward EPS consensus of $0.97 implies a rapid swing from TTM losses of -$0.48 to profitability, with the forward P/E of 9.4x pricing in minimal recovery and creating asymmetric upside if the turnaround succeeds.
The bear case is substantial: trailing GAAP earnings remain negative (-$0.48 EPS), ROE is -1.98%, and the balance sheet carries $44.4B in debt against $796M in cash — a leverage structure (D/E 1,853%) that leaves no margin for error if credit losses spike [5]. Revenue declined 27% YoY in Q1 2026 as the federal loan portfolio runs off faster than private originations can replace it, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately -20% reflects structural shrinkage. The CFPB's permanent ban and $120M settlement for systematically harming borrowers creates permanent reputational damage and ongoing regulatory scrutiny [3]. The 73% dividend cut in 2020 ($0.60 to $0.16/quarter) demonstrates unsustainable capital returns history, and the current 7.1% yield funded from operating cash flow rather than earnings is a classic value trap signal. Activist CEO Bramson's turnaround playbook focuses on financial engineering rather than building durable competitive advantages, and Earnest competes against SoFi, Sallie Mae, and incumbents with deeper pockets and stronger brands.
