Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NASDAQ ·United States
NioCorp Developments is a pre-revenue critical minerals company developing the Elk Creek Project in Nebraska, which would become the only U.S. domestic source of niobium alongside scandium, titanium, and rare earth elements — all currently imported. CEO Mark Smith, who has led the company since 2013 and previously ran Molycorp and Largo Resources, guided the 2026 Feasibility Study to a $4.1B pre-tax NPV, and construction of the project's main access portal began in 2026 as the company pursues a $780M EXIM Bank loan and targets full project financing [1].

Investment Thesis
NioCorp offers exposure to a strategically vital, category-defining critical minerals project: the 2026 Feasibility Study values Elk Creek at $4.1B pre-tax NPV (24% IRR) with $37.4B in projected life-of-mine revenue and a 67% average EBITDA margin, and the company would become the only U.S. domestic producer of niobium alongside scandium, titanium, and rare earth elements [1]. Construction of the main access portal has begun, 75% of niobium production is already under offtake agreements, and the company is advancing a $780M EXIM Bank loan application — concrete signs the long-delayed project is moving toward financing and construction in 2026, backed by a CEO with a 13-year track record on this exact project and prior leadership of Molycorp and Largo Resources.
The bear case is that NioCorp remains a pre-revenue, single-asset development company after more than a decade of effort, with no production, no current earnings, and Infrastructure metrics that mechanically score near zero because there is nothing yet to measure beyond a debt-free balance sheet. Full project financing (beyond the pending EXIM Bank application) has not been finalized, and mining project construction carries well-documented risks of cost overruns, permitting delays, and timeline slippage that have already stretched Elk Creek's development well past original targets. Until the EXIM Bank loan and remaining project financing close and construction proceeds on schedule, the stock remains a binary, execution-dependent bet on a critical minerals thesis rather than a company with demonstrated operating results.
Key Catalysts
  1. Closing of the $780M EXIM Bank loan application, which would substantially de-risk the remaining Elk Creek project financing need [1]
  2. Continued construction progress on the Elk Creek main access portal ($44.6M budget) and subsequent full project construction milestones through 2026
  3. Expansion of niobium offtake agreements beyond the current 75% coverage, and potential additional agreements for scandium, titanium, or rare earth output as U.S. critical minerals policy support continues to build [1]
Key Risks
  1. Project financing risk: full construction funding beyond the pending EXIM Bank loan has not been finalized, and any delay or shortfall would push back the already 13-year development timeline further [1]
  2. Construction execution risk: mining project buildouts are prone to cost overruns and schedule slippage, and Elk Creek has no production track record to validate feasibility study assumptions
  3. Commodity price risk: the project's $4.1B NPV and 67% EBITDA margin assumptions depend on niobium, scandium, titanium, and rare earth prices remaining favorable through the multi-year construction and ramp-up period [1]
