Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Apparel Manufacturing ·NasdaqCM ·Hong Kong
Neo-Concept International Group Holdings Limited is a one-stop apparel solution services provider based in Hong Kong, offering market trend analysis, product design and development, raw material sourcing, production and quality control, and logistics management services. The company serves brand owners, apparel sourcing agents, and online fashion retailers in European and North American markets, and sells owned-brand knitwear products under the 'Les100Ciels' and 'SIU' brands through retail stores in the UK and UAE and e-commerce. Incorporated in 2021 and headquartered in Kwun Tong, Kowloon, Hong Kong.

Investment Thesis
Neo-Concept International is a micro-cap apparel company attempting a strategic pivot from private-label sourcing to owned-brand retail through the Les100Ciels and SIU brand acquisitions [4]. Owned-brand revenue grew 138.8% in H1 2025 to HK$27.5M, demonstrating early traction in the new business model [1]. Net income turned positive at HK$2.0M in H1 2025 versus HK$1.4M in the prior period, suggesting the business model shift may be gaining momentum despite severe private-label headwinds. The company trades at $12.13 with $6.5M daily liquidity, and successful scaling of the owned-brand retail channel could create meaningful upside from current depressed levels.
The fundamental problem is that Neo-Concept's core private-label business has collapsed — revenue declined 50.8% year-over-year and 51.7% in the private-label segment specifically, driven by China-USA tariff policies that may persist or worsen [1]. With total H1 2025 revenue of just HK$60.2M (~$7.7M USD) and net income of HK$2.0M (~$0.3M USD), the company is operating at razor-thin margins (0.24% profit margin) with no margin of safety [1]. ROE of 0.58% and trailing P/E of 151.6 reflect a business generating virtually no economic value for shareholders. The D/E of 82.0% on a declining revenue base creates leverage risk. Board instability (March 2026 director resignation), the lack of analyst coverage or institutional ownership, and missing fundamental financial data (no Forward P/E, no PEG ratio) make this effectively uninvestable for most capital allocators [3][5]. The owned-brand pivot is promising but unproven at scale, and the company is competing against far larger, better-capitalized global apparel companies.
