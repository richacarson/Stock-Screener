Company Profile
Financial Services ·Financial Data & Stock Exchanges ·NMS ·United States
Nasdaq operates as a global technology company serving capital markets and other industries through three segments: Capital Access Platforms, Financial Technology, and Market Services. The company runs one of the world's largest stock exchanges, provides market data and index products, and offers financial crime detection (Verafin), regulatory reporting (AxiomSL), and cross-asset trading solutions (Calypso). Q2 2026 net revenue reached $1.5B (up 15% YoY) with diluted EPS of $1.07 (up 25% YoY) and ARR of $3.3B growing 12% organically [1].

Investment Thesis
Nasdaq at $96.12 with a $53.7B market cap has successfully transformed from a cyclical exchange operator into a high-margin financial technology platform, with Q2 2026 net revenue reaching $1.5B (up 15% YoY), diluted EPS of $1.07 (up 25%), and ARR of $3.3B growing 12% organically [1]. The Adenza integration is delivering with Solutions revenue growing 17% in Q2 and the Financial Technology segment contributing recurring, high-margin revenue that reduces dependence on volatile trading volumes. The AI-powered anti-financial crime business (Verafin) is the fastest-growing division, deployed across 650+ financial institutions with the Agentic AI Workforce platform, while index revenue surged 38% in Q2 driven by $750B+ in benchmarked assets [1][2]. Operating margins of 49.3% and free cash flow of $1.7B support rapid debt reduction while funding continued technology investment.
The bear case centers on valuation and integration execution. At 20.5x forward earnings and a PEG of 1.52, Nasdaq trades at a premium to traditional exchange peers, pricing in continued double-digit growth that requires sustained Adenza cross-sell acceleration and AI-driven revenue expansion [1]. Debt-to-equity of 77.7% remains elevated from the $10.5B Adenza acquisition, and while deleveraging is on track, annual interest expense constrains capital flexibility. The board diversity rule controversy — though primarily a regulatory/legal issue — created reputational headwinds, and the SEC's evolving stance on exchange-imposed governance requirements adds regulatory uncertainty [3]. Market Services revenue remains tied to trading volumes that are inherently cyclical, and while this segment is a declining share of total revenue, a sustained downturn in capital markets activity would pressure overall growth. Competition from S&P Global, ICE/LSEG, and Bloomberg in financial data and technology is intensifying as multiple players target the same regulatory technology spending pool.
Key Catalysts
  1. Adenza integration delivering with Solutions revenue growing 17% in Q2 2026 and ARR reaching $3.3B; cross-selling AxiomSL and Calypso to Nasdaq's existing 6,000+ client base creates a large untapped revenue opportunity [1]
  2. Verafin's Agentic AI Workforce platform expanding rapidly across 650+ financial institutions with 20%+ growth, positioning Nasdaq as the leading AI-powered anti-financial crime platform as regulatory enforcement intensifies globally [2]
  3. Index revenue surging 38% in Q2 2026 driven by the secular passive investing trend and $750B+ in assets benchmarked to Nasdaq-branded indices, with new product launches expanding the addressable market [1]
Key Risks
  1. Elevated debt-to-equity of 77.7% from the $10.5B Adenza acquisition constrains financial flexibility; while deleveraging is progressing with strong free cash flow, interest expense reduces near-term earnings power [1]
  2. Premium valuation at 20.5x forward earnings and PEG of 1.52 requires sustained double-digit organic growth execution; any integration stumbles or ARR growth deceleration could trigger multiple compression toward exchange-peer averages [1]
  3. Market Services revenue remains exposed to cyclical trading volume fluctuations, and regulatory uncertainty around exchange-imposed governance requirements and SEC oversight creates ongoing headline risk [3]
