Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Noble Corporation is an offshore drilling contractor operating a fleet of 36 mobile offshore drilling units (floaters and jackups) for the oil and gas industry worldwide, formed through the 2022 merger with Maersk Drilling and the 2024 acquisition of Diamond Offshore. The company serves clients in Africa, Far East Asia, the North Sea, Oceania, South America, and the U.S. Gulf of America, with a $7.5B contract backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility.

Investment Thesis
Noble Corporation offers offshore drilling exposure through the industry's most consolidated fleet, built via the 2022 Maersk Drilling merger and 2024 Diamond Offshore acquisition, with a $7.5B backlog (as of April 2026) providing multi-year revenue visibility [1][3]. Q1 2026 net income rose 12% YoY to $121M even as revenue dipped on rig-transition timing, and full-year 2025 revenue grew 6.46% to $3.11B [1][3]. A conservative D/E of 42.66% and a fully-implemented, fleet-wide AI safety platform delivering a documented 43% reduction in incidents and 30% engagement gain both stand out as differentiated evidence versus offshore-driller peers [2]. The 5.1% dividend yield adds income while the earnings recovery plays out.
The bear case centers on cyclicality and payout sustainability. Q1 2026 revenue fell 10% YoY to $786M on rig reactivation and transition timing, and ROE of just 4.96% and profit margin of 6.97% show the offshore day-rate recovery remains incomplete [3][5]. The 139% dividend payout ratio is not covered by current earnings and depends on continued EBITDA recovery or reduced capex. Offshore drilling remains tied to oil prices and E&P capital budgets, and the 2020 bankruptcy is a reminder this is a boom-bust industry; ESG pressure on financing and compliance costs adds further execution risk to the 20% carbon-intensity reduction target [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. $7.5B backlog (as of April 2026) with continued bookings providing near-term earnings visibility as reactivated rigs (e.g., Noble Deliverer) return to full utilization [3]
  2. Tightening offshore rig supply industry-wide, giving Noble pricing power on contract renewals across its 36-rig fleet [1]
  3. Continued fleet-wide rollout of AI-powered predictive safety technology, with the documented 43% incident reduction and 30% engagement gain supporting both cost efficiency and Sinek-style trusting-teams differentiation [2]
Key Risks
  1. Oil price volatility and E&P budget cuts could trigger contract deferrals and lower day rates, further compressing the already-thin 6.97% profit margin [5]
  2. Dividend sustainability — a payout ratio near 139% is not covered by current earnings; a cut would damage investor confidence given the stock's income-oriented shareholder base [5]
  3. ESG and regulatory pressure on offshore drilling could constrain financing access and limit contract wins with ESG-focused operators [4]
