Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Noble Corporation is one of the world's largest offshore drilling contractors, operating a fleet of ultra-deepwater drillships and jackup rigs for oil and gas exploration and production companies globally. Noble emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in February 2021 and subsequently acquired Diamond Offshore Drilling in September 2024, creating the second-largest offshore drilling fleet. The company reported Q2 2026 contract drilling revenue of $679M with adjusted EBITDA of $212M, and lowered full-year 2026 guidance to $2.8B-$2.9B revenue and $850M-$925M adjusted EBITDA due to operational suspensions on two rigs in Brazil. Backlog stood at $7.5B as of April 2026.

Investment Thesis
Noble Corporation at $48.08 offers exposure to the offshore drilling upcycle through the second-largest global fleet, backed by a $7.5B backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility and a 4.39% dividend yield ($2.00/share annually) [1]. The Diamond Offshore integration creates scale advantages in rig maintenance, customer relationships, and negotiating leverage, while ultra-deepwater fixtures reaching the mid-$400K/day range signal improving day rate economics. Management expects a meaningful earnings inflection in H2 2027 as the offshore supply-demand balance tightens — the global offshore rig count remains well below pre-2014 peaks while deepwater project sanctions are accelerating. The conservative D/E of 43% provides balance sheet flexibility, and the AI-powered predictive safety platform (Fennex partnership) demonstrates operational innovation that reduces costs and improves safety metrics [2].
The bear case is substantial: Noble lowered full-year 2026 guidance to $2.8B-$2.9B revenue and $850M-$925M adjusted EBITDA due to operational suspensions on two rigs in Brazil, and Q2 revenue declined 15.2% YoY to $719.7M [1]. The Forward P/E of 45.45 prices in a recovery that management itself does not expect until H2 2027 — any delay in the offshore upcycle or additional rig suspensions could trigger significant multiple compression. ROE of 3.28% and net profit margin of 5.19% demonstrate that the company is not currently earning adequate returns on capital. Glassdoor reviews describe a 'toxic' corporate culture with leadership integrity concerns, which could undermine the Diamond integration and lead to continued talent attrition [2]. The offshore drilling industry remains deeply cyclical and exposed to oil price volatility — a sustained move below $60/barrel would compress day rates and defer deepwater project sanctions. The company only emerged from bankruptcy in 2021, and the aggressive dividend of $300M+ annually ($2.00/share) consumes significant free cash flow during a period of declining earnings.
