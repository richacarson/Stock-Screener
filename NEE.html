Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
NextEra Energy, Inc. generates, stores, transmits, distributes, and sells electric power to retail and wholesale customers in North America. Operates through Florida Power & Light Company (FPL), the largest electric utility in Florida serving approximately 6 million customer accounts, and NextEra Energy Resources (NEER), the world's largest generator of wind and solar energy. Generates electricity from wind, solar, nuclear, natural gas, and other clean energy sources.

Investment Thesis
NextEra Energy is executing a transformative strategy to become the world's largest and most diversified utility through the $67B Dominion Energy acquisition, which creates the largest regulated utility globally and positions NEE as the go-to energy partner for AI data centers with plans for 30+ hubs across the U.S. [6][7]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.15 (+9.5% YoY) beat consensus, with revenue of $7.53B (+12.4%) and management targeting the top end of $3.92-$4.02 full-year guidance with 8%+ EPS CAGR through 2032 [8]. The Google Cloud strategic partnership and record 3.6 GW renewables origination in Q2 alone underscore NEE's position as the energy backbone of AI growth [7][8]. The 30-year dividend growth streak (10.1% 5yr CAGR) with a sustainable 58.9% payout ratio and 2.83% yield make this a core dividend growth holding.
The bear case centers on integration risk, leverage, valuation expansion, and cultural concerns. The $67B Dominion acquisition faces regulatory hurdles in Virginia where political leaders have been wary and environmental groups filed motions arguing the merger application was incomplete [9]. D/E of 156.7% will increase significantly post-merger, making the combined entity highly sensitive to interest rate movements. Forward P/E has expanded to 23.9 (from 19.6 in July), above the 20 target, and the Forward PEG of 1.93 suggests limited margin of safety at current valuation. Glassdoor reviews describing 'absolutely terrible' senior management and an 'old school good old boy culture' reveal organizational weaknesses that could become more acute during a complex integration [4]. Additionally, analysts have raised concerns about natural gas overbuild risk in the combined entity that could create stranded costs for ratepayers [9]. Insider selling of $3.7M by officers in Q1 2026 remains a cautionary signal.
Key Catalysts
  1. $67B Dominion Energy acquisition creating the world's largest utility, with plans for 30+ data center hubs and positioning NEE as the go-to energy partner for AI infrastructure at unprecedented scale [6][7]
  2. Record renewables origination with 3.6 GW added in Q2 2026 alone, plus the Google Cloud strategic partnership to 'accelerate AI growth and transform the energy industry' [7][8]
  3. Management guidance for 8%+ adjusted EPS CAGR through 2032, supported by regulated rate base growth, renewables backlog execution, and structural AI-driven electricity demand growth
Key Risks
  1. Dominion acquisition regulatory risk — Virginia political leaders are wary, environmental groups have filed motions, and the merger requires approval from multiple state commissions, FERC, and the NRC, any of which could delay or block the deal [9]
  2. Post-merger leverage escalation on top of existing D/E of 156.7%, with natural gas overbuild risk in the combined entity that could create stranded costs for ratepayers and draw regulatory scrutiny
  3. Cultural disconnect between the visionary clean energy mission and employee experience ('absolutely terrible' management, 'old school good old boy culture' on Glassdoor) creates execution risk that intensifies during complex multi-state utility integration [4]
