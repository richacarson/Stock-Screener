Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
National Energy Services Reunited is the first and largest MENA-headquartered oilfield services company publicly listed on NASDAQ. Founded in 2017 as a SPAC by former Schlumberger executive Sherif Foda, NESR provides production services (hydraulic fracturing, coiled tubing, cementing, stimulation, water management), drilling & evaluation services, and an ESG Impact segment across the Middle East and North Africa. Primary customers include Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, and other national oil companies. Market cap approximately $2.7 billion.

Investment Thesis
NESR delivered a record Q1 2026 with $404.6M revenue (+33.5% YoY), $23.8M net income (+129%), and adjusted EPS of $0.26 (+86%), significantly beating consensus estimates of $376.4M revenue and $0.21 EPS [1]. Full-year 2026 consensus calls for $1.84B revenue (+29%) and $1.60 EPS (+150%), implying a forward P/E of just 13.67x — a steep discount to global oilfield services peers [2]. The balance sheet is conservatively managed (D/E 32%) with $335M debt and strong operating cash flow, providing flexibility for continued Jafurah ramp-up and new contract wins. NESR's positioning as the dominant MENA-native energy services provider gives it preferential access to Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, and other national oil company spending programs that global majors cannot easily penetrate [3]. The Gulf sovereign AI data center buildout creates incremental demand for NESR's energy and water infrastructure services [4].
The bear case centers on profitability quality and insider behavior. Profit margin of 4.53% and ROE of 6.74% are well below IOWN targets, indicating that NESR's impressive topline growth is not translating into commensurate shareholder returns — the oilfield services business is inherently capital-intensive and low-margin even during upcycles [2]. Director Al-Nowais liquidated $51.4M worth of shares in June 2026 alone (89% of his holdings), following additional block sales exceeding 1.5M shares in May 2026, signaling that a key insider believes current prices are stretched after the stock's 340% run over 52 weeks [6]. Geographic concentration in MENA creates severe customer risk — Saudi Aramco spending cuts, sustained oil price declines, or regional geopolitical disruption would impact the entire revenue base. The trailing P/E of 39.24x implies the market is pricing in perfect execution of the growth trajectory, leaving minimal margin of safety.
