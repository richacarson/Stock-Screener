Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NASDAQ ·United States
NextDecade Corporation develops and constructs LNG liquefaction and export facilities in the United States. Its primary asset is the Rio Grande LNG project near Brownsville, Texas, with Phase 1 (Trains 1-3) at an estimated $18.0B, Train 4 at $6.7B, and Train 5 at $6.7B — all fully financed. First gas into the facility is expected H2 2026, with first LNG production from Train 1 in H1 2027. Q1 2026 revenue was $49.9M with a net loss of $136.4M.

Investment Thesis
NextDecade offers a rare opportunity to invest in a fully-financed, pre-commercial LNG export platform at a discount to its ultimate production value. With $31.4B in total project financing secured across five trains, first gas expected H2 2026, and first LNG production in H1 2027, the company is approaching the inflection from capital-consuming construction to cash-generating operations [1][2]. Structural tailwinds from the AI-driven global electricity surge, European energy security diversification, and Asian coal-to-gas switching support long-term LNG demand growth [3]. At a $1.91B market cap, the stock trades at a fraction of the facility's replacement cost, and the fully-financed Trains 4 and 5 provide additional growth optionality beyond the Phase 1 base case [2].
The bear case centers on execution risk across a $31.4B construction program, with quarterly net losses of $136.4M and debt-to-equity of 342% leaving no margin for error [1][7]. Any construction delay, cost overrun, or regulatory setback at Rio Grande LNG could be existential for a single-project company with $6.76B in total debt. Environmental opposition remains active — FERC acknowledged 'disproportionate and adverse' impacts on local communities, and the CCS withdrawal has invited greenwashing criticism from environmental groups [4][6]. LNG market pricing is cyclical, and if global gas prices decline during the production ramp as competing supply from Qatar and other U.S. projects comes online, realized margins could disappoint relative to the massive capital investment.
