Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
NextDecade Corporation is a pre-revenue energy company developing the Rio Grande LNG facility, a 27 million metric ton per annum (mtpa) LNG export project located near Brownsville, Texas. The company has taken final investment decisions on Trains 1-5, with Trains 1-2 at 67.8% completion and first LNG production from Train 1 targeted for H1 2027. Ten trains are planned in total, with enough capacity to supply energy for 65 million households. CEO Matt Schatzman has led the company since 2018.

Investment Thesis
NextDecade offers asymmetric upside as a pre-revenue LNG developer approaching its first commercial production in H1 2027, with Rio Grande LNG Trains 1-2 at 67.8% completion and electric commissioning of Train 1 underway [1]. The company has secured FIDs on 5 trains representing 27 mtpa of capacity, with long-term LNG sale and purchase agreements providing contracted revenue visibility and forward projections of $2.1B revenue and $388M earnings by 2029 [1]. The $3.50B senior secured notes offering in June 2026 extends debt maturities to 2031-2041, reducing near-term refinancing risk. At $2.0B market cap, the stock trades at a fraction of the facility's replacement cost. CEO Schatzman's 45.5% insider ownership aligns management with shareholders, and the Russell index inclusion broadens the investor base [4]. Global LNG demand growth driven by European energy security needs, Asian coal-to-gas switching, and AI data center electricity demand provides structural tailwinds.
The bear case is severe and multi-dimensional. NextDecade carries $8.7B+ in project debt against zero revenue, with Q1 2026 net losses widening to $136.4M ($0.51/share) as construction spending accelerates [1]. The company is entirely dependent on construction completion and LNG market conditions — any delay, cost overrun, or regulatory setback at Rio Grande LNG could be existential. The Rainforest Action Network has accused NextDecade of greenwashing after the company canceled its CCS initiative following a court ruling that vacated environmental permits, revealing the 'green LNG' positioning as marketing rather than genuine commitment [3]. LNG supply growth from Qatar, Australia, and other US projects could create an oversupplied market by 2028-2029 when NextDecade's full Phase 1 capacity comes online, compressing margins. The stock's $7.58 price represents a 37% decline from its 52-week high of $12.12, reflecting persistent market concerns about execution risk. With $8.7B in debt and 360 employees managing one of the largest infrastructure projects in the Americas, the company has extreme operational leverage — there is no margin for error [4].

AI Risk Protection (8/9): LNG infrastructure is among the most AI-immune businesses — constructing and operating a 27 mtpa liquefaction facility involves massive physical engineering, $25B+ in capital investment, federal regulatory compliance (FERC, DOE export authorizations), and long-term bilateral commodity contracts that AI structurally cannot substitute [1]. The physical-world operations — gas processing, cryogenic liquefaction, marine loading, and tanker shipping — require infrastructure that AI cannot replicate. AI may actually increase LNG demand as data centers require enormous electricity generation, boosting natural gas consumption as a baseload fuel. No analysts have flagged AI as a risk to LNG infrastructure developers. AI Opportunity (0/1): NextDecade does not generate AI-related revenue and has no disclosed AI strategy.
