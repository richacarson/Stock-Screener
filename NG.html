Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE American ·Canada
NovaGold (Vancouver; founded 1984) is a pre-revenue, single-asset gold developer whose sole material asset is the Donlin Gold project in the Kuskokwim region of southwestern Alaska (493 claims, ~29,008 hectares) [3]. After Barrick's 2025 exit, NovaGold holds 60% of Donlin with Paulson Advisers at 40%, and in 2026 agreed to acquire Paulson's 40% in an all-share deal expected to close in calendar Q4 2026 [1][6]. A Bankable Feasibility Study is under way for completion in 2027 (prior initial capex estimate ~$7.4B on a 100% basis); fiscal Q3 2026 (ended August 31) net loss was $36.0M and cash and term deposits were $343.4M [1]. CEO Greg Lang has led the company since 2012 [4].

Investment Thesis
NovaGold offers leveraged exposure to one of the largest undeveloped gold deposits in North America: after consolidating Barrick's stake in 2025 and agreeing to acquire Paulson's 40% in an all-share deal closing in calendar Q4 2026, it will own 100% of Donlin [1][6]. The company holds $343.4M of cash and term deposits against D/E of 46%, and a Bankable Feasibility Study due in 2027 is the key de-risking step for a project in a stable U.S. jurisdiction [1][2].
The bear case is that NovaGold produces nothing and will need enormous capital: initial capex was previously estimated at ~$7.4B (100% basis) and is expected to be updated in the BFS, the net loss widened to $36.0M in fiscal Q3 2026, and consensus shows losses through at least 2030 [1][2]. Litigation from Kwethluk, Tuluksak and Bethel tribes and sustained AVCP opposition threaten permits, and the stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $14.40 to $6.17 [6][3]. On the IOWN framework, weak Innovation, Inspiration and Infrastructure scores yield an overall 39, a SELL.
Key Catalysts
  1. Closing of the all-share acquisition of Paulson's 40% Donlin interest, expected in calendar Q4 2026, giving NovaGold 100% ownership [1]
  2. Completion of the Donlin Bankable Feasibility Study in 2027 with updated capex, reserves and economics [1]
  3. Gold price strength raising the project's NPV and improving financing options for a ~$7.4B+ build [1]
Key Risks
  1. Permitting and litigation risk from Yukon-Kuskokwim tribal lawsuits and coalition opposition centered on fisheries and the tailings dam [6]
  2. Financing and dilution: a ~$7.4B initial capex (prior estimate, 100% basis) dwarfs the $343.4M cash balance, and the Paulson deal is paid in shares [1]
  3. Single-asset concentration: any adverse BFS finding at Donlin directly impairs the entire company [4]
