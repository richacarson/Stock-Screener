Company Profile
Utilities ·Regulated Electric & Gas Utilities ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
National Grid plc is a British multinational electricity and gas utility company operating regulated transmission and distribution networks. It owns and operates electricity and natural gas transmission networks across Great Britain and produces and supplies electricity and gas to customers in New York and Massachusetts through its US subsidiaries. Headquartered in London, the company is executing the largest five-year infrastructure investment program in its history to support grid modernization and electrification.

Investment Thesis
National Grid is executing the largest infrastructure investment program in its history — a record GBP 70bn five-year capital plan supporting ~10% annual regulated asset growth — directly positioned to capture the AI-driven, data-center-fueled surge in electricity demand that management forecasts will double grid load by 2030 [1][2]. As a regulated transmission and distribution monopoly across Great Britain, New York, and Massachusetts, the business is structurally immune to AI disruption and instead benefits from it, with FY2027 guidance already raised to 13-15% underlying EPS growth [1]. Forward P/E of 13.25 is attractive relative to the 20x target, and meaningful insider buying (GBP 3.06M over 24 months by the former CEO and CFO) signals leadership conviction in the long-term investment thesis [1][2].
The primary offsets are balance sheet strain and dividend quality: D/E of 109% is above target, ROE of 8.25% is well below the 15% benchmark, and a payout ratio near 75% combined with a slightly negative 5-year dividend growth CAGR reflects a rebasing that prioritized capex over income growth — a real cost for income-focused holders [1]. Revenue has contracted over the trailing five years (-3.4% CAGR) due to asset divestitures, and heavy capex has produced negative free cash flow, meaning execution risk on the GBP 70bn program is the central variable determining whether asset growth translates into durable earnings growth. Regulatory outcomes in the UK's RIIO price control framework and US state rate cases remain the key swing factor for realized returns on this capital.
Key Catalysts
  1. GBP 70bn five-year capital investment program executing on schedule, translating ~10% annual regulated asset growth into the guided 13-15% FY2027 underlying EPS growth
  2. AI and data-center driven electricity demand growth (forecast to double UK/US grid load by 2030) creating sustained regulatory support for accelerated transmission and distribution investment
  3. Continued insider buying by senior executives signaling confidence as the company transitions to a new CEO following Pettigrew's November 2025 departure
Key Risks
  1. Elevated leverage (D/E 109%) and negative free cash flow from the capex program increase sensitivity to interest rate and regulatory allowed-return changes
  2. Payout ratio near 75% with a negative 5-year dividend growth CAGR (-0.27%) signals limited near-term dividend growth capacity for income-focused holders
  3. Execution risk on the record GBP 70bn investment plan — cost overruns, supply chain bottlenecks (30% power transformer shortfall projected industry-wide), or adverse UK/US rate-case outcomes could compress realized returns
