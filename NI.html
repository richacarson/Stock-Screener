Company Profile
Utilities ·Regulated Gas ·NYSE ·United States
NiSource (Merrillville, IN; roots to 1847) is a regulated natural gas and electric utility operating Columbia Gas distribution (~37,300 miles of main across Ohio, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Kentucky and Maryland) and NIPSCO electric service to ~0.5M customers in northern Indiana [3]. Under CEO Lloyd Yates, its GenCo structure serves large data-center loads: an October 2026 20-year Microsoft agreement adds ~1,100 MW, ~5 GW of GenCo capacity is signed, and a $40B 2027-2033 capital plan targets 10-11% consolidated adjusted EPS CAGR [1]. Q3 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.27 (vs $0.20), and 2026 adjusted EPS guidance was narrowed to the upper half of $2.02-$2.07 [1][2].

Investment Thesis
NiSource has become a data-center growth utility: the October 2026 Microsoft agreement brings signed GenCo capacity to ~5 GW, and management raised its 2027-2033 consolidated adjusted EPS CAGR target to 10-11% on a $40B capital plan, with 2027 EPS guided to $2.22-$2.30 [1]. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.27 beat the $0.22 consensus [2]. The stock trades at ~19.9x 2027 consensus EPS with a 2.68% yield, 11 straight years of dividend growth and a 6.4% five-year dividend CAGR, earning a 9/10 Infrastructure score [6].
The bear case is balance-sheet strain and regulatory risk: debt-to-equity is 191%, free cash flow is deeply negative (capex is ~166% of operating cash flow), and the $40B plan will require heavy external financing [6]. Analysts note no update on Indiana's review of utility allowed returns, data-center contracts concentrate risk in a few hyperscalers, and coal retirements are already being delayed by federal reliability orders [2][5]. Weak Inspiration (3/10) and a FINITE Infinite Game score (4/10) cap the overall at 64, a HOLD.
Key Catalysts
  1. GenCo execution: converting the ~2 GW under strategic negotiation into signed contracts and delivering the guided $0.05-$0.09 EPS contribution in 2027 [1]
  2. 2027 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.22-$2.30 and the 6-8% base-plan growth extended through 2033 [1]
  3. Indiana regulatory decisions on allowed returns and GenCo cost allocation protecting customer affordability [2]
Key Risks
  1. Financing burden: a $40B capital plan against negative free cash flow and 191% debt-to-equity implies sustained debt and equity issuance [1][6]
  2. Hyperscaler concentration: GenCo economics depend on long-term contracts with a small number of data-center customers such as Microsoft [1]
  3. Regulatory and reliability risk, including federal orders delaying the Schahfer coal plant retirement and possible changes to Indiana utility profit rates [5][2]
