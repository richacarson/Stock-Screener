Investment Thesis
NIQ Global Intelligence delivered strong Q1 2026 results — revenue of $1.1B (+11.1% reported, +5.1% organic CC), adjusted EBITDA of $224.8M (+19.1% with 150bps margin expansion to 21%), and adjusted EPS of $0.15 that beat estimates of $0.10 by 50% [1]. The company is the world's leading consumer intelligence platform, serving CPG manufacturers and retailers across 90+ countries with unmatched retail measurement and consumer panel data. At 10.7x forward adjusted P/E, NIQ is deeply discounted relative to its data/analytics peers, reflecting both the $3.2B debt burden and the opportunity for significant equity value creation if deleveraging proceeds as planned (targeting sub-3.0x leverage by year-end) [4]. The $400M technology investment is deploying AI across the platform, with the Activation segment growing 12% YoY, and full-year guidance implies continued margin expansion toward 23.5-23.8% adjusted EBITDA margins.
The bear case is dominated by NIQ's catastrophic debt burden — D/E of 1,175% from GfK acquisition financing creates existential risk if revenue growth stalls or interest rates remain elevated [4]. Despite $4.31B TTM revenue and improving operational metrics, the company posted a GAAP net loss of -$323.6M, with negative profit margins and ROE [4]. Employee morale is deteriorating with Glassdoor ratings declining YoY, multiple layoff rounds, and gutted employee programs creating organizational instability during a critical post-merger integration [2]. The core business faces structural AI disruption risk as CPG companies build in-house analytics capabilities and AI-native platforms offer cheaper alternatives to traditional panel and retail measurement. The stock has traded down to $11.56 — a 39% decline from its 52-week high of $19.00 — reflecting persistent market skepticism about the company's ability to grow its way out of its debt burden while simultaneously defending against AI disruption of its traditional data business model.
