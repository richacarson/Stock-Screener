Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Footwear & Accessories ·NYSE ·United States
Nike designs, markets, and distributes athletic footwear, apparel, equipment, and accessories globally under the Nike and Jordan brands. CEO Elliott Hill, who returned to Nike in October 2024 after 32 years at the company, is executing a 'Win Now' turnaround plan that reverses the prior Direct-to-Consumer-first strategy and rebuilds wholesale partnerships with Foot Locker, department stores, and other retailers. FY2026 revenue was roughly flat at $46.4B (+0.2%) after a 9.8% decline in FY2025, with North America showing early signs of recovery (+9% in Q2 FY2026) while China remains weak (-12%).

Investment Thesis
Nike at 23.77x forward earnings and 1.80 PEG trades well below its 52-week range (down ~44%) as the market prices in an uncertain turnaround, but the fundamentals of the Hill-led reset are showing early traction — North America revenue grew 9% and wholesale grew 8% in Q2 FY2026, and full-year FY2026 revenue stabilized at $46.4B after the prior year's 9.8% decline [1]. Nike's balance sheet remains a source of strength (ROE 22.14%, D/E 74%) and the dividend — 24 consecutive years paid, 23 consecutive years of increases, ~8.5% 5-year dividend CAGR, now yielding 4.03% — offers meaningful income support while the operational recovery plays out. The brand's global scale and IP remain largely insulated from AI substitution risk, giving the company time to execute.
The bear case is that this is a company correcting its own strategic mistakes under repeated reorganization rather than a clean growth story: profit margin of just 6.70% and a 5-year revenue CAGR near zero reflect years of DTC missteps, tariff pressure, and China weakness (-12%) that have not been resolved [1][3]. Management's own guidance for 'flattish' earnings through the first half of FY2027 and further gross margin contraction signals the turnaround is not yet inflecting, and the 78.1% payout ratio leaves little dividend growth cushion if earnings stay depressed. Persistent supply-chain labor and wage-theft allegations tied to Uzbek cotton sourcing and Asian garment factories create ongoing reputational and ESG overhang, and the December 2025 C-suite reorganization — the second major leadership shake-up in just over a year — raises execution risk during a critical reinvestment phase [3][5]. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED), driven by abortion and LGBT activism attributions, will exclude Nike from many faith-based portfolios regardless of financial recovery.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued North America and wholesale recovery (North America +9%, wholesale +8% in Q2 FY2026) validating the reversal of the failed Direct-to-Consumer-first strategy under CEO Elliott Hill [1]
  2. Inventory normalization targeted for Q2 FY2026 combined with AI-driven demand planning and generative design tools reducing markdowns and improving gross margin trajectory [2]
  3. Stabilization or recovery in China (-12% YoY) as a swing factor — even flat China performance would materially improve consolidated revenue growth given its historical scale
Key Risks
  1. Turnaround has not yet inflected profitability — management guides to 'flattish' earnings through the first two quarters of FY2027 and further gross margin contraction of 3.0-3.75 points from tariffs [1]
  2. Supply chain labor controversies (Uzbek cotton sourcing, unpaid wage claims exceeding $9.3M in a sample of factories, investor criticism of Nike's 'silence' on worker rights) create reputational and regulatory risk [5]
  3. Organizational instability from a second major leadership reorganization in just over a year (December 2025 C-suite consolidation) could disrupt execution of the turnaround plan at a critical juncture [3]
