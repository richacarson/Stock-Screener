Investment Thesis
Annaly Capital Management at 5.89x forward earnings with a 16.4% dividend yield, 29 consecutive years of payments, and improving earnings trajectory offers deep income value as the largest mortgage REIT with $135B+ in assets [4]. Q2 2026 delivered EAD of $0.79/share (beating estimates), book value per share of $20.15 (+1.7% QoQ), and the ninth consecutive quarter of earnings exceeding the dividend [1]. The dividend raise to $0.75/quarter signals management confidence in sustained spread economics. Record $7.1B in residential credit loan purchases diversifies beyond pure agency MBS, adding higher-yielding assets. ROE of 19.6% exceeds targets, and the stock trades at just $18.30 — a 9.2% discount to book value of $20.15 [1][4]. The 5-year dividend growth CAGR of ~25% reflects recovery from deeply depressed post-COVID levels, and the Inspire Insight score of 54 (ALIGNED) with 9 positive attributions confirms clean ESG practices [3].
The bear case centers on structural leverage risk, price decline, and interest rate sensitivity. The stock has fallen 25% from its 52-week high of $24.52 to $18.30, reflecting market concern about rate environment normalization and mREIT sustainability [4]. D/E of 737.5% — while structural for mortgage REITs — means Annaly is a leveraged bet on interest rate spreads, and any yield curve flattening or spike in short-term funding costs could rapidly compress net interest income. The revenue growth of 479% YoY is driven by MBS valuation swings rather than organic growth and cannot be extrapolated. The PEG ratio of 32.03 (effectively meaningless for an mREIT) and the history of multiple dividend cuts over the past decade undermine the income thesis despite the current recovery [4]. At a 69.9% payout ratio, the dividend is covered but with limited cushion — EAD of $0.79 vs dividend of $0.75 provides only 105% coverage. With $125B+ in repo-funded mortgage assets, any disruption to funding markets could create forced selling at distressed prices. Total return performance for mREITs has historically underperformed simpler fixed-income alternatives over full rate cycles.
