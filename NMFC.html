Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
New Mountain Finance Corporation is an externally managed business development company (BDC) focused on providing direct lending solutions — primarily senior secured loans — to U.S. upper middle market companies backed by leading private equity sponsors. Managed by New Mountain Capital, the company targets 'defensive growth' industries with recurring revenue and low cyclicality. As of Q1 2026, NAV per share declined to $10.92 (down 5.2% sequentially), with 2.6% of the portfolio at fair value and 5.6% at cost on non-accrual. The company completed a ~$470M portfolio sale year-to-date and repurchased ~$66M of shares at $8.01 per share.

Investment Thesis
NMFC trades at a 37% discount to NAV ($6.89 vs. $10.92 NAV per share) and offers a 14.5% dividend yield on the reduced $0.25 quarterly distribution, providing deep value if credit quality stabilizes [1]. The company's focus on defensive growth industries — recurring revenue businesses with low cyclicality — provides structural portfolio resilience, and the $66M share repurchase at $8.01 (below NAV) demonstrates management confidence in the portfolio's underlying value. The forward P/E of 7.41 based on adjusted net investment income of $0.32/share suggests the market has priced in significant credit deterioration that may not fully materialize [4].
The bear case is severe. NMFC reported a Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $50.9M (-$0.51/share), NAV declined 5.2% sequentially to $10.92, and non-accruals are elevated at 2.6% of fair value and 5.6% at cost [1]. The 21.9% dividend cut from $0.32 to $0.25/quarter reflects the permanent loss of earning assets from the $470M portfolio sale and signals structurally impaired income coverage. Revenue of $68.79M missed consensus by $1.85M, and the portfolio sale program reduces the asset base that generates future investment income. Rising private credit market stress — particularly in software sector exposure — poses ongoing credit risk, and the non-accrual rate could worsen if economic conditions deteriorate. The 52-week range of $6.73-$10.71 with the stock near lows suggests continued market distrust [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Stabilization of non-accrual rates and portfolio credit quality would narrow the 37% discount to NAV, with potential for $3-4 per share of upside to book value [1]
  2. Completion of the $470M portfolio sale de-risks the balance sheet and provides dry powder for redeployment into higher-quality credits at potentially wider spreads
  3. Accretive share repurchases at current prices ($6.89 vs. $10.92 NAV) mechanically increase NAV per share and signal management conviction in remaining portfolio value [1]
Key Risks
  1. Continued credit deterioration in the private credit market, particularly in software and technology portfolio companies, could drive further NAV writedowns and additional dividend cuts below the $0.25 run rate [1]
  2. The $470M portfolio sale permanently reduces the earning asset base, creating a structural income gap that cannot be filled without re-leveraging or accepting lower-quality credits — adjusted NII coverage of the $0.25 dividend may erode
  3. BDC sector contagion risk from broader private credit market stress could drive further share price declines regardless of NMFC-specific fundamentals, widening the already-severe 37% NAV discount [4]
