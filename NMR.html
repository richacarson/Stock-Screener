Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·Japan
Nomura Holdings, led by President and Group CEO Kentaro Okuda, is Japan's largest investment bank and brokerage, operating globally across wealth management, investment banking, and wholesale markets businesses. FY2025/26 (fiscal year ended March 2026) net revenue rose 15% YoY to ¥2,167.7B and net income reached a record ¥362.1B (+6% YoY), with ROE of 10.1% hitting the top of management's 2030 target range (8-10%+) for a second consecutive quarter [1]. Management has framed ongoing "business transformation initiatives," including plans to use AI and data to improve decision-making speed and develop new client-facing services, as a driver of the improved profitability [2]. The company's recent history has also been marked by regulatory and compliance issues, including a 2024 administrative penalty for unlawful JGB futures trading and separate employee fraud arrests in 2024 and February 2025.

Investment Thesis
Nomura enters FY2026/27 with genuine operating momentum: FY2025/26 net revenue grew 15% to ¥2,167.7B, net income hit a record ¥362.1B for the second consecutive year, and ROE of 10.1% reached the top of management's 2030 target range for a second straight quarter, reflecting real traction from the firm's multi-year business transformation program [1]. The stock offers a well-covered 5.32% dividend yield on a 45.9% payout ratio, trades at a modest 12.15x trailing / 18.66x forward P/E, and is backed by Nomura's position as Japan's dominant domestic investment bank and brokerage with a growing global wealth-management and wholesale-markets franchise.
The bear case centers on governance and culture rather than financial performance. Nomura's recent history includes a 2024 administrative penalty from Japan's Financial Services Agency for unlawful JGB futures trading and two separate employee fraud arrests (2024 and February 2025), a pattern of compliance failures that directly undercuts the Trusting Teams and Courage to Lead dimensions of the Infinite Game score (2/10, FINITE) [3]. The extremely high D/E of 946%, while structurally typical for a broker-dealer balance sheet, still floors the corresponding Infrastructure sub-metric, and the Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MIXED, driven by an LGBT Activism attribution) may be a consideration for faith-based allocators. Combined with only moderate AI Resilience (6/10) given competitive pressure on lower-touch trading and research functions from better-resourced global peers, the stock lands in WATCH territory.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued execution of the multi-year business transformation program, which has already driven ROE to the top of management's 8-10%+ 2030 target range for two consecutive quarters and produced record net income of ¥362.1B in FY2025/26 [1]
  2. AI-driven operational efficiency and new client-service initiatives, explicitly flagged by leadership as a strategic priority, could further improve productivity and fee generation if execution follows through on stated intent [2]
  3. A well-covered 5.32% dividend yield (45.9% payout ratio) with a multi-year growth trajectory offers an attractive income profile at a modest 12-19x earnings multiple
Key Risks
  1. Recurring compliance and conduct failures -- the 2024 JGB futures trading penalty and separate 2024/2025 employee fraud arrests -- signal unresolved internal control weaknesses that could produce further regulatory or reputational damage [3]
  2. Extremely high leverage (D/E of ~946%), while typical of broker-dealer balance sheets, leaves the firm structurally exposed to market-liquidity or funding-cost shocks during periods of stress
  3. Lower-touch trading, research, and retail brokerage functions face real medium-term AI-driven margin compression risk from both AI-native fintech entrants and larger global peers investing more heavily in AI capability
