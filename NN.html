Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NASDAQ ·United States
NN, Inc. is a global diversified industrial company that designs and manufactures high-precision components and assemblies for the electrical, automotive, aerospace and defense, and medical markets. The company operates approximately 27 facilities across North America, South America, Europe, and China, specializing in close-tolerance manufacturing with single-digit micron precision. Under CEO Harold Bevis (joined 2023), the company is pivoting toward higher-value markets including Electric Grid & Data Center, Defense & Electronics, and Medical.

Investment Thesis
NN, Inc. is a turnaround story under CEO Harold Bevis (joined 2023), who is pivoting this $191M market cap precision components manufacturer from declining legacy auto/industrial markets toward higher-value segments — Electric Grid & Data Center, Defense & Electronics, and Medical [1][2]. Q1 2026 was a strong proof point: revenue of $118.5M, adjusted EBITDA of $14.1M, and EPS of $0.02 that beat estimates by 133%, prompting the company to raise full-year 2026 guidance to $460M+ in sales with $57M adjusted EBITDA at 12% margin [1]. Trailing twelve-month adjusted EBITDA of $52.6M reached the highest level in approximately five years. New business wins target of $80-90M and the acceleration of 2030 targets to 2029 signal management confidence. At $3.62 per share with analyst consensus target of $5.50 (+52% upside), the stock offers deep-value exposure to the AI-driven data center infrastructure buildout.
The bear case centers on financial fragility and unproven turnaround execution. NN lost $54.1M on a GAAP basis with TTM revenue declining 3.1% — the FY2025 decline of 9.06% reflects real demand weakness in legacy markets that may not be fully offset by new market growth [4]. The $191M market cap and $3.62 stock price indicate a company in distress, with negative ROE, negative profit margins, and a forward P/E of 34.5x that is extremely elevated for an industrial company [4]. The turnaround depends on Bevis's ability to ramp new business wins ($80-90M target) fast enough to offset legacy declines, which requires both commercial execution and manufacturing capacity reallocation. Factory closures and restructuring have reduced the workforce and created organizational instability. Debt levels are unclear but likely significant given the restructuring history. The stock trades near multi-year lows, and the small market cap creates liquidity risk and vulnerability to dilutive capital raises if the turnaround stalls.
