Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Northrop Grumman Corporation is a leading global aerospace and defense technology company. The company designs, develops, integrates, and sustains advanced systems spanning aeronautics (B-21 Raider stealth bomber), defense systems (missiles, munitions), mission systems (sensors, networks, cyber), and space systems (satellites, launch vehicles). It is the prime contractor for the U.S. Air Force's B-21 Raider and the Sentinel ICBM replacement program. Headquartered in Falls Church, Virginia.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 7 /10
Northrop Grumman demonstrates mixed infinite game characteristics. The company's commitment to multi-decade programs like B-21 and Sentinel shows long-horizon thinking, and Kathy Warden has provided stable leadership since 2019. However, the defense industry's dependence on government budget cycles and contract competition introduces inherently finite dynamics — quarterly EAC adjustments and margin pressure on fixed-price contracts reflect the tension between long-term capability building and short-term financial performance.

Investment Thesis
Northrop Grumman delivered a strong Q2 2026 with revenue of $10.9B (up 5% YoY), adjusted EPS of $7.68 (beating consensus of $6.82 by 13%), and raised full-year guidance to $43.75-$44.25B in sales and $28.60-$29.10 in MTM-adjusted EPS [1][2]. Record backlog of $105B (up 17% YoY) with a 1.84x book-to-bill ratio provides exceptional multi-year revenue visibility. The B-21 Raider production ramp drove Aeronautics revenue up 13%, and the $7.6B Sentinel award anchors decades of future work. With $3.65B in free cash flow, 22 consecutive years of dividend growth, and an international expansion strategy targeting $10B in annual overseas sales by 2031, Northrop offers a rare combination of defense-grade revenue durability and long-term growth catalysts [2][5].
The primary concern is margin compression and program execution risk. Operating margin fell to 10.1% in Q2 2026 from 13.8% a year ago, driven by $68M and $91M unfavorable EAC adjustments on the Stand-In Attack Weapon and GEM 63XL programs respectively [2]. The Sentinel ICBM program remains a high-profile execution risk — schedule delays or cost overruns on this $100B+ lifecycle program could create significant financial and reputational headwinds. Forward PEG of 3.28 reflects that growth is modest (~5% organic revenue growth) relative to the valuation premium the stock commands. The company's 10.48% profit margin is well below the 20% target for infrastructure scoring, reflecting the inherently lower-margin nature of cost-plus and fixed-price defense contracting versus commercial technology [1][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. B-21 Raider transition from low-rate to full-rate production driving Aeronautics revenue growth beyond the current 13% YoY pace, with potential for international variants
  2. Sentinel ICBM program execution milestones that would de-risk the $100B+ lifecycle program and unlock sustained Space Systems revenue growth through the 2030s and beyond
  3. International defense sales expansion toward the $10B annual target by 2031, driven by allied nations increasing defense spending in response to global security threats
Key Risks
  1. Sentinel ICBM program schedule delays or cost overruns could trigger additional EAC adjustments and damage credibility on the company's most strategically important program
  2. Operating margin compression from fixed-price contract losses — $159M in combined unfavorable adjustments in Q2 alone — may persist if engineering challenges continue on GEM 63XL and Stand-In Attack Weapon
  3. Forward PEG of 3.28 and ~5% organic revenue growth leave limited valuation upside; any slowdown in defense budget growth or shift in political priorities could compress the multiple

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Northrop Grumman's core business is structurally immune to AI disruption. Defense contracting requires security clearances, classified facilities, decades-long government relationships, and physical manufacturing of stealth aircraft, missiles, satellites, and launch vehicles — none of which AI can substitute [1][2]. The B-21 Raider and Sentinel ICBM programs represent multi-decade sole-source contracts with the U.S. government that cannot be replicated by software. Rising geopolitical tensions and increasing defense budgets globally actually increase demand for Northrop's products. Regulatory barriers (ITAR, security clearances, facility requirements) create insurmountable moats. AI Opportunity (1/1): Northrop actively deploys AI in autonomous systems, sensor fusion, electronic warfare, and cyber defense products. The company integrates AI/ML into mission systems for real-time threat detection and autonomous operations, generating meaningful defense AI revenue as the DoD accelerates AI adoption [2][3].
