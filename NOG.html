Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Northern Oil and Gas is the largest publicly traded non-operating working interest company in the United States, with diversified exposure across the Williston Basin (Bakken/Three Forks), Permian Basin, Uinta Basin, and Appalachian Basin. The company acquires minority working interests in oil and gas wells operated by major E&P companies, generating revenue through production without operating wells directly. NOG reported Q2 2026 revenue of $745M (+17% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $1.13 beating estimates, and guides to $1.4-1.5B adjusted EBITDA and $375-500M+ free cash flow for FY2026 [1]. Market cap is approximately $2.75B.

Investment Thesis
NOG at 5.99x forward earnings and 4.52x EV/EBITDA offers compelling deep value in a differentiated E&P model. Q2 2026 demonstrated the operating leverage: revenue of $745M (+17% YoY), adjusted EPS of $1.13 (beating the $1.03 estimate by 10%), free cash flow of $159M (up 400%+ from Q1), and 17% sequential EBITDA growth driven by diversification across Williston, Uinta, and Appalachian basins offsetting Permian curtailments [1]. Management guides to $1.4-1.5B adjusted EBITDA and $375-500M+ free cash flow for FY2026 at current strip pricing, supporting the 6.94% dividend yield ($1.80/share) and aggressive buyback program (2.95M shares repurchased in Q2 at $20.37) [1]. The multi-basin model reduces single-basin risk, with natural gas volumes reaching record levels (+35% YoY) positioning NOG for the growing power demand narrative from AI data centers and LNG exports.
The bear case centers on commodity exposure, leverage, and the limitations of the non-operator model. TTM GAAP net income is -$486M, reflecting significant impairment charges that highlight NOG's vulnerability to oil price volatility — the entire business is a leveraged bet on commodity prices with no pricing power or differentiated product [4]. The debt-to-equity ratio of 137% and enterprise value of $4.6B against $2.75B market cap signal substantial leverage that amplifies downside in a sustained commodity price decline. As a non-operator, NOG has zero control over drilling pace, well performance, completion timing, or operating costs — the Permian curtailments in Q2 due to Waha pricing economics illustrate this dependency risk [1]. Revenue growth has turned negative on a TTM basis (-3.9%) despite the strong Q2, and the 4-year dividend track record provides less income stability than established dividend aristocrats. If OPEC+ increases supply or global demand weakens further, NOG's thin equity cushion and operator dependency could force dividend cuts.
