Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Independent Power Producers ·NYSE ·United States
NRG Energy, Inc. is a Houston-based Fortune 200 integrated power and home services company serving approximately 7.4 million residential, commercial, and industrial customers across the United States and Canada through retail brands including Reliant, Green Mountain Energy, and Vivint Smart Home. Following the LS Power acquisition (18 natural gas/dual-fuel facilities, ~13 GW), NRG operates approximately 16 GW of competitive generation capacity and is positioning as a critical infrastructure provider for AI data center power demand across ERCOT and PJM markets.

Investment Thesis
NRG Energy is a premier infrastructure beneficiary of the AI data center power-demand super-cycle. FY2025 delivered adjusted EPS of $8.24 (+21%), adjusted EBITDA of $4.1B, and free cash flow of $2.2B [1]. 2026 guidance is materially higher: adjusted EBITDA midpoint $5.575B (+37% reflecting LS Power integration), adjusted EPS midpoint $8.90, and free cash flow before growth of $2.8-3.3B [1][2]. NRG owns ~16 GW of competitive generation capacity across ERCOT and PJM — the two highest-growth US power markets — and has signed 445 MW of long-term data center retail power agreements with 1 GW+ additional contracts targeted for 2026 [1]. New CEO Robert Gaudette (25-year NRG veteran, appointed April 2026) is leaning into the 'bring your own power' model for hyperscalers and investing in virtual power plants using AI-driven demand response [3]. At forward P/E of 18.02 and PEG of 0.86 with 14%+ annual EPS growth targeted through 2030, valuation is compelling. The Inspire Impact score of +44 (ALIGNED) adds faith-based quality [4].
Risks center on leverage, execution, and the consensus nature of the AI power thesis. The LS Power acquisition pushed D/E to 989% ($16.63B debt vs $1.68B equity) — any power market dislocation or interest rate spike could pressure financial flexibility and credit ratings [5]. GAAP profit margin of 2.81% is thin and payout ratio of 177.82% on GAAP earnings is unsustainable long-term without earnings growth. The CEO transition from Coben to Gaudette introduces leadership execution risk during a critical integration period. The AI data center power thesis is now consensus — NRG, Vistra, and Constellation are all competing for the same hyperscaler contracts — and any moderation in AI capex or model-efficiency breakthroughs (e.g., DeepSeek-style optimization) could reduce power demand assumptions. Gaudette himself has noted that tight power markets risk consumer price spikes from data center diversion, a politically charged issue that could invite regulatory intervention [3]. Natural gas generation faces long-term carbon-transition risk despite net-zero-by-2050 ambitions.
