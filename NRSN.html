Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Israel
NeuroSense Therapeutics is a clinical-stage Israeli biotech developing PrimeC, an oral fixed-dose combination therapy for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). Phase 2b data published in JAMA Neurology demonstrated a 65% reduction in risk of death and greater than 14-month median survival benefit. The FDA cleared the pivotal Phase 3 PARAGON trial (~300 patients), and the company targets a Health Canada New Drug Submission by December 2026. NeuroSense ended 2025 with only $166K cash and faces Nasdaq compliance issues regarding minimum bid price ($0.542 current) and market value requirements ($18.4M market cap). The company is seeking strategic investors and pharma partners to fund Phase 3.
25 Employees ·www.neurosense-tx.com

Investment Thesis
NeuroSense Therapeutics offers a high-risk/high-reward proposition based on exceptionally strong Phase 2b clinical data for PrimeC in ALS — a 65% reduction in risk of death and greater than 14-month median survival benefit published in JAMA Neurology, one of medicine's most rigorous journals [1]. FDA clearance for the pivotal Phase 3 PARAGON trial (~300 patients) and a Health Canada NDS filing targeted for December 2026 provide two regulatory pathways to market [2][4]. ALS is an area of extreme unmet medical need with ~30,000 patients in the U.S. and limited treatment options after Amylyx's Relyvrio withdrawal. If PrimeC succeeds, the commercial opportunity is significant in a market hungry for effective therapies. The founding mission (inspired by an ALS patient) and Inspire Impact Score of 28 (ALIGNED) make this suitable for mission-driven faith-based investors.
The investment risks are existential and numerous. NeuroSense ended 2025 with only $166K cash — functionally insolvent for a company planning a 300-patient Phase 3 trial that will cost tens of millions [3]. Financing has 'taken longer than expected' and the company is pursuing a mix of strategic investors, pharma partners, grants, and non-dilutive funding, with no confirmed commitments disclosed. The stock trades at $0.542 with an $18.4M market cap, well below Nasdaq's minimum bid price ($1.00) and market value thresholds, creating material delisting risk that could trigger forced selling and further equity destruction [4][5]. Infrastructure scores 0 (AT RISK) due to the liquidity floor failure, and every financial metric is at floor values. The 43 IOWN score (WATCH) reflects the tension between genuinely promising clinical data and fundamentally broken financial infrastructure. Phase 3 clinical risk remains substantial — many Phase 2b successes fail to replicate in larger, longer trials. This is a binary outcome investment suitable only for investors who can accept total loss.
