Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·ASE ·United States
NeurAxis is a medical technology company developing neuromodulation therapies for chronic gastrointestinal conditions in children and adults. Its flagship product, IB-Stim, is an FDA-cleared percutaneous electrical nerve field stimulation (PENFS) device for functional abdominal pain associated with irritable bowel syndrome in patients aged 8+. The company also offers the Rectal Expulsion Device (RED), a screening tool for evacuation disorders. NeurAxis reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.9M (up 116% YoY) and TTM revenue of $5.3M, driven by the AMA Category I CPT code effective January 2026 that enabled insurance reimbursement. The company remains deeply unprofitable with a TTM net loss of $7.7M against $8.3M cash on hand and a going-concern warning.

Investment Thesis
NeurAxis at $6.77 with an $85M market cap is a micro-cap medical device company experiencing explosive revenue growth — Q2 2026 revenue surged 116% YoY to $1.9M and Q1 2026 rose 80% to $1.6M, driven by the AMA Category I CPT code for PENFS that took effect January 2026 and enabled insurance reimbursement [1][2]. The IB-Stim device is the only FDA-cleared PENFS system for pediatric functional abdominal pain associated with IBS, giving NeurAxis a first-mover regulatory advantage in a growing neuromodulation market. Average selling price rose 33% to $1,017 as the revenue mix shifted from discounted financial assistance to fully reimbursed procedures, and ordering accounts expanded to 66 from 56, with gross margins of 86% demonstrating strong unit economics once scaled [1].
The bear case is substantial: NeurAxis lost $7.8M in FY2025 on only $3.6M revenue (-144% profit margin) and carries a going-concern warning in its SEC filings, with $8.3M cash providing approximately 12-15 months of runway at current burn rates without additional equity financing [3]. The stock is severely illiquid at $718K daily dollar volume, making position sizing and exit nearly impossible for institutional investors. Revenue remains trivially small ($5.3M TTM) with only 66 ordering accounts nationwide, and the path to profitability requires scaling from dozens to hundreds of accounts while maintaining 86% gross margins — a commercialization challenge that many single-product medical device companies fail to achieve. Insider transactions are entirely compensation-related with no open-market buying, and the company's $85M market cap implies the market prices in significant growth that has not yet been validated by sustained profitability [3][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. AMA Category I CPT code driving insurance reimbursement adoption — only 66 of an estimated thousands of pediatric GI practices have adopted IB-Stim, suggesting early-innings penetration with significant runway for ordering account expansion as reimbursement removes the payment barrier [1]
  2. Potential IB-Stim label expansions for additional functional GI disorders beyond IBS, including functional dyspepsia and chronic nausea, which would expand the addressable patient population within existing ordering accounts [5]
  3. Cash flow breakeven target by late 2026 — if achieved, would eliminate the going-concern warning and potentially trigger institutional interest in a company with 86% gross margins and 100%+ revenue growth [3]
Key Risks
  1. Going-concern warning with $8.3M cash and ~$6M annual burn rate implies need for dilutive equity financing within 12-15 months — any capital raise at current market cap would significantly dilute the 12.6M shares outstanding [3]
  2. Severe illiquidity at $718K daily dollar volume makes the stock untradeable for meaningful position sizes and creates elevated volatility risk — a single institutional seller could move the price dramatically [3]
  3. Single-product concentration risk — the entire company depends on IB-Stim adoption, and competitive entry of alternative neuromodulation devices, negative clinical outcomes, or CPT code reclassification could undermine the growth trajectory with no revenue diversification [1][5]
