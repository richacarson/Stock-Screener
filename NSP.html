Company Profile
Industrials ·Staffing & Employment Services ·NYSE ·United States
Insperity is a leading U.S. professional employer organization (PEO) providing comprehensive HR outsourcing solutions including payroll, benefits administration, workers' compensation, compliance, and talent management to small and medium-sized businesses. The company co-employs roughly 300,000-307,000 worksite employees (WSEEs) and offers HR360, HRCore, and the Workday-powered HRScale platform for mid-market clients. Founded in 1986 by Paul Sarvadi and headquartered in Kingwood, Texas, Insperity posted Q2 2026 revenue of $1.7B (+2% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA up 13% and adjusted EPS up 31%, following a difficult FY2025 marked by elevated benefits costs; 2026 WSEE guidance was revised down to 303,000-307,000 (a 1-2.3% decline from 2025) alongside full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $1.60-$2.60.

Investment Thesis
Insperity at 17.7x forward earnings with a 5.0% dividend yield and 14 consecutive years of dividend growth offers a contrarian recovery play on a 40-year founder-led PEO with a track record of navigating margin cycles [1]. CEO Sarvadi's $12.5M in personal stock purchases — the largest insider buying in company history — signals deep conviction in the recovery, and Q2 2026 results showed adjusted EPS growth of 31% and adjusted EBITDA growth of 13% YoY even as GAAP profitability remains under pressure [1][5]. The HRScale platform with Workday expands the company's addressable market into the mid-market segment, and management's full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $1.60-$2.60 implies a meaningful earnings recovery is underway.
The bear case centers on deepening structural and cyclical headwinds. ROE has turned negative (-18.5%) and profit margin is now -0.23%, reflecting persistent healthcare cost inflation and workers' compensation repricing pressure on the PEO model [1]. D/E of 783.6% is extremely elevated, and the GAAP payout ratio above 500% on negative earnings is unsustainable without a rapid earnings recovery. WSEE guidance was cut to 303,000-307,000 (a 1-2.3% decline), indicating client attrition persists. AI-powered HR platforms (Gusto, Rippling, Deel) continue automating core PEO functions at lower cost, and sustained DOL Wage and Hour Division enforcement actions plus IRS payroll tax scrutiny over worker-classification practices add regulatory overhang beyond the immediate earnings recovery narrative.
Key Catalysts
  1. Margin recovery trajectory with full-year adjusted EPS guided to $1.60-$2.60 and Q2 2026 showing 31% adjusted EPS growth YoY as healthcare and workers' comp cost inflation potentially normalizes [1]
  2. CEO Sarvadi's $12.5M in insider buying during 2026 represents the largest personal investment in company history, signaling management confidence in earnings recovery and potentially marking a cycle bottom [5]
  3. HRScale platform launch with Workday targeting mid-market clients opens a new addressable segment and could increase revenue per client beyond the traditional PEO model [1]
Key Risks
  1. Negative ROE (-18.5%) and near-zero profit margin reflect deepening PEO cost pressure; D/E of 783.6% leaves limited balance sheet flexibility if the margin recovery stalls [1]
  2. GAAP payout ratio above 500% on negative earnings is unsustainable — if margin recovery does not materialize, the $0.60 quarterly dividend and 14-year growth streak could come under pressure
  3. Sustained DOL Wage and Hour Division enforcement actions and IRS payroll tax scrutiny over PEO client worker-classification practices, combined with AI-enabled HR platforms undercutting the co-employment value proposition, create compounding regulatory and competitive risk [1][4]
