Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Nutanix sells a hybrid multicloud infrastructure software platform (AOS, AHV hypervisor, Nutanix Cloud Platform) that replaced legacy hyperconverged appliances with a subscription-based, software-defined model. The company is increasingly positioning itself around enterprise AI infrastructure via GPT-in-a-Box and Nutanix Enterprise AI, and is capturing switching demand from customers fleeing VMware's post-Broadcom pricing changes. Q3 FY2026 revenue reached $703.1M (+10% YoY) with ARR of $2.43B (+15% YoY) and non-GAAP operating margin of 22.3%, prompting a raise to full-year FY2026 guidance of $2.82-$2.84B revenue.

Investment Thesis
Nutanix is benefiting from a genuine, multi-year secular tailwind: enterprises fleeing VMware's post-Broadcom price increases are driving ARR to $2.43B (+15% YoY) and Q3 FY2026 revenue to $703.1M (+10% YoY), with management raising full-year guidance to $2.82-$2.84B revenue and $760-780M free cash flow [1]. Non-GAAP operating margin of 22.3% and trailing-twelve-month net income of $275.9M show the subscription-model transition has reached durable profitability, while GPT-in-a-Box and Nutanix Enterprise AI position the company to capture on-premises enterprise AI infrastructure spend as a complement, not a competitor, to hyperscale cloud AI [2]. A PEG of 1.9 and forward P/E of 30.2x are reasonable for a company compounding ARR in the mid-teens with expanding margins.
The bear case centers on durability of the current growth driver and thin qualitative moats beyond switching costs. Much of Nutanix's recent momentum is opportunistic share capture from a single competitor disruption event (Broadcom's VMware pricing) rather than category creation, and once that displacement wave normalizes, growth could decelerate back toward high-single digits [1]. Data gaps in debt-to-equity and return-on-equity metrics (excluded from the Infrastructure score) limit balance-sheet visibility, and Glassdoor reviews flag management inconsistency in sales and support functions that could affect execution as the company pushes into the more technically demanding AI infrastructure category [2]. No insider buying was found in research — only recent insider selling by the President/CCO — which does not by itself signal a lack of confidence but offers no positive counter-signal either.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued VMware customer migration following Broadcom's pricing and licensing changes, which management and Morgan Stanley cite as a durable driver of the 15% ARR growth and raised FY2026 guidance [1]
  2. Expansion of Nutanix Enterprise AI / GPT-in-a-Box adoption, including the NVIDIA NIM and Hugging Face integrations and the Cisco Compute Hyperconverged partnership, as enterprises seek on-premises alternatives to hyperscale AI infrastructure [2]
  3. Margin expansion toward the high-20s/low-30s non-GAAP operating margin range as the subscription transition fully matures and free cash flow guidance of $760-780M is realized [1]
Key Risks
  1. Growth is heavily dependent on a one-time competitor-displacement event (VMware/Broadcom); once the migration wave is largely complete, YoY growth could decelerate meaningfully from the current 10-15% ARR pace [1]
  2. Missing debt-to-equity and ROE data create balance-sheet visibility gaps that were excluded from the Infrastructure score rather than penalizing it, potentially understating leverage risk
  3. Sales and support management inconsistency flagged in Glassdoor reviews could impede execution as Nutanix pushes into the more complex, support-intensive enterprise AI infrastructure category [2]
