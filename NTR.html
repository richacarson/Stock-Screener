Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Agricultural Inputs ·NYSE ·Canada
Nutrien is the world's largest provider of crop inputs and services, operating through three segments: Nutrien Ag Solutions (retail), Potash, and Nitrogen. Formed from the 2018 merger of PotashCorp and Agrium, the company operates the world's largest potash production capacity, significant nitrogen and phosphate assets, and approximately 2,000 retail locations across North America, South America, and Australia. Q2 2026 revenue was $10.8B (+4% YoY) with record potash sales volumes and H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $3.54B (+6% YoY). Market cap approximately $35B.

Investment Thesis
Nutrien at 15.66x forward earnings offers exposure to the world's largest crop inputs platform with improving fundamentals — H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA grew 6% to $3.54B on record potash volumes and 8% revenue growth, while management raised potash volume guidance and lowered capex, signaling structural free cash flow improvement [1]. The D/E ratio of 42% provides balance sheet flexibility, and the 3.16% dividend yield with 8 consecutive years of growth and a 44% payout ratio offers sustainable income with room for dividend acceleration. Operating cash flow grew 12% to $1.63B in H1 2026, and the company returned $848M to shareholders through dividends ($528M) and buybacks ($320M). Global food security dynamics — a growing population requiring more efficient agricultural production — provide a secular demand tailwind for crop inputs that is insensitive to technology disruption [2].
The bear case centers on commodity cyclicality, margin compression, and limited growth upside. Nutrien's revenue has been violently cyclical — surging to $37.9B in 2022 on Ukraine-driven fertilizer price spikes before declining to ~$24B in 2024 as prices normalized, producing a flat 5-year revenue CAGR [4]. ROE of 9.22% significantly trails the 15% target, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of mining and chemical processing on a $25.9B equity base. Profit margin of 8.74% is structurally constrained by commodity economics and competitive retail distribution. The phosphate segment posted a 48% decline in H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA to $80M, illustrating ongoing segment volatility [1]. Nutrien generates no meaningful AI revenue and has limited innovation pipeline beyond the speculative clean ammonia facility. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects a neutral faith alignment profile. Potash pricing remains subject to geopolitical risk, with Belarus sanctions and Russian export restrictions creating supply uncertainty that could swing earnings in either direction.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record potash sales volumes with raised 2026 guidance of 14.2-14.8M tonnes, supported by strong offshore demand in India, China, and Southeast Asia — continued volume momentum could drive upside to full-year EBITDA targets [1]
  2. Clean ammonia facility evaluation at Geismar, Louisiana with potential 1.2M tonnes annual capacity could position Nutrien as a first-mover in low-carbon agricultural inputs, accessing premium pricing from sustainability-focused buyers [5]
  3. Capital efficiency improvements with lowered capex guidance ($1.95-2.05B from $2.0-2.1B) and accelerating share buybacks in Q3 2026, combined with 12% operating cash flow growth, suggest expanding free cash flow for shareholder returns [1]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme commodity price cyclicality — Nutrien's revenue swung from $18.2B (2020) to $37.9B (2022) to ~$24B (2024), making earnings inherently unpredictable and driving a flat 5-year revenue CAGR despite current growth momentum [4]
  2. Phosphate segment deterioration with a 48% decline in H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA to $80M, reflecting oversupply and pricing pressure that could spread to other nutrient segments if global agricultural spending contracts [1]
  3. ROE of 9.22% on a $25.9B equity base indicates structurally low capital returns for a commodity business — without sustained higher fertilizer prices or meaningful cost reduction, the stock may struggle to re-rate above commodity-sector multiples [4]
