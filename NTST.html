Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Retail (Net Lease) ·NYSE ·United States
NETSTREIT is an internally managed REIT based in Dallas, Texas that acquires single-tenant net lease retail properties nationwide. The portfolio focuses on e-commerce resistant tenants with healthy balance sheets, including grocery, convenience stores, quick-service restaurants, and auto service retailers. IPO'd in August 2020, the company has grown to 864 properties with 100% occupancy (excluding developments) and over $3B in gross assets. Q2 2026 revenue reached $61.3M (+26.9% YoY) with 135 properties added year-to-date. Raised 2026 AFFO guidance to $1.37-$1.39/share.
29 Employees ·www.netstreit.com

Investment Thesis
NETSTREIT delivered a strong Q2 2026 with revenue of $61.3M (+26.9% YoY, beating $56.3M consensus) and AFFO of $0.35/share (+6.1%), driven by record investment activity of $298.9M at 7.4% blended cash yields [1]. The portfolio has scaled to 864 properties with 100% occupancy across essential retail categories (grocery, QSR, convenience stores, auto service). Raised AFFO guidance to $1.37-$1.39/share and investment guidance to $700M-$800M signal continued growth momentum. The 4.57% dividend yield at ~60% AFFO payout ratio provides current income with reinvestment capacity, and the conservative balance sheet (3.2x leverage, no maturities until 2028) positions the company to capitalize on acquisition opportunities [2]. At $18.17, the stock trades at 13.2x AFFO guidance midpoint — a discount to larger net-lease peers.
The bear case centers on GAAP valuation distortion, limited dividend growth, and competitive disadvantage versus larger peers. Forward P/E of 44.3x and GAAP ROE of 0.96% reflect REIT depreciation mechanics rather than operational weakness, but they screen poorly for quantitative investors [4]. The 1.5% 5yr dividend growth CAGR significantly trails inflation, meaning real income erodes over time despite the attractive 4.57% current yield. NETSTREIT competes in a crowded net lease sector against much larger incumbents — Realty Income ($70B+ assets) and NNN ($13B) — with superior cost of capital, creating structural disadvantage on acquisition spreads. The stock's 15.7% decline from $21.55 to $18.17 reflects broader REIT sector pressure from elevated interest rates. Most critically, the 6-year operating history means the portfolio has not been stress-tested through a recession or major tenant default cycle, creating uncertainty about credit quality resilience, and the 29-person team creates key-person risk with no role redundancy.
