Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYQ ·Brazil
Nu Holdings operates the world's largest digital banking platform by customer count, serving over 135 million customers across Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. The company offers credit and prepaid cards, personal and business accounts, personal loans, insurance, investments, cryptocurrency trading, and mobile phone services through its app. Founded in 2013 by David Vélez and headquartered in São Paulo, Nu reported Q2 2026 revenue of $5.9B (up 39% YoY) with net income of $1.1B and ROE of 29%.

Investment Thesis
Nu at $13.43 with a $64.9B market cap is the world's largest digital bank executing a powerful growth trajectory — Q2 2026 revenue reached $5.9B (up 39% YoY) with net income crossing $1.1B for the first time, while Q1 2026 delivered $5B+ revenue and $871M net income [1][2]. The company serves 135M+ customers across Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia with a 42.7% profit margin and 31.6% ROE, driven by near-zero customer acquisition costs through viral adoption and a technology-first operating model that scales efficiently. At 11.9x forward earnings and a PEG of 0.62, Nu trades at a steep discount to its growth rate, with the Mexican expansion (15M customers, third-largest institution) providing a massive second growth vector that is still early-stage and the OCC-approved US charter opening a potential third continent [2][5].
The bear case centers on emerging market risk, currency translation, and competitive intensity. Nu's revenue is predominantly denominated in Brazilian reais, exposing ADR holders to currency depreciation — the BRL has weakened approximately 30% against the USD since Nu's IPO, mechanically compressing dollar-denominated returns. Credit risk is elevated as Nu scales unsecured lending in an economy with ~27% informal employment, and loan loss provisions could spike during a Brazilian recession as the $32B+ credit portfolio has never been tested through a severe downturn [1][2]. The June 2026 surprise departure of CFO Guilherme Lago during a tougher Brazilian credit cycle triggered a BofA downgrade and raises near-term execution uncertainty. Competition is intensifying from traditional banks digitizing (Itau's Iti, Bradesco's Next) and regional fintechs (Mercado Pago, PicPay), and the stock trades 29% below its 52-week high of $18.98, reflecting market concerns about LatAm macro conditions [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Mexico expansion reaching inflection — 15M+ customers making Nu the third-largest financial institution in Mexico, with credit card and lending products ramping in a $200B+ addressable market still in early penetration stages [1][2]
  2. ARPAC expansion from $16/month as Nu cross-sells insurance, investments, crypto, and lending products to its 135M customer base, with each new product increasing per-customer monetization on a near-zero marginal cost basis [1]
  3. US national bank charter advancing from OCC conditional approval toward operating launch, opening the largest banking market globally to Nubank's digital platform and technology stack [2]
Key Risks
  1. Brazilian real depreciation risk — Nu earns primarily in BRL, and further currency weakness against the USD compresses reported revenue and earnings growth for ADR holders, as the BRL has already weakened ~30% since IPO [5]
  2. Unsecured lending credit risk in LatAm economies with high informality — a Brazilian recession or tightening cycle could spike loan loss provisions and compress the 42.7% profit margin as Nu's $32B+ lending book has never been tested through a severe downturn [1][2]
  3. CFO transition (Guilherme Lago to Rob Livingston) during a tougher Brazilian credit cycle triggered a BofA downgrade and raises near-term execution and continuity questions at a critical scaling moment [3]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Nu's core business — deposit-taking, lending, insurance, and payments across Latin America — is protected by banking licenses, capital requirements, and the switching costs inherent in 135M+ customer relationships with linked accounts, cards, and credit histories [1][2]. Physical-world regulatory barriers prevent AI-only challengers from replicating a licensed banking operation without significant capital and compliance infrastructure. However, as a digital-first bank without branch networks, Nu faces moderate AI exposure: AI-powered lending platforms could compete on credit decisioning, AI agents could commoditize customer service (currently a Nu differentiator), and larger tech platforms deploying AI-native financial services in LatAm could pressure margins. Nu is proactively mitigating this risk by embedding AI across operations and hiring for AI agent orchestration roles [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): Nu is actively deploying AI in HR transformation, credit scoring, and fraud detection, and is hiring for AI agents and orchestration, but has not yet disclosed specific quantifiable revenue from AI-powered products or services [3].
