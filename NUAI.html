Investment Thesis
New Era Energy & Digital at $2.97 with a $359M market cap is a speculative, story-driven bet on the AI data-center buildout: the company controls a 438-acre Texas Critical Data Centers campus in Odessa with a proposed pathway to 1GW+ of capacity, and the July 2026 leadership transition brought in COO José Rodriguez with hyperscale data center engineering experience alongside new CEO Charlie Nelson [2]. For investors willing to underwrite pure execution risk on land and power-infrastructure development, the redeployment of capital away from a declining legacy helium/gas business toward AI infrastructure is directionally aligned with where hyperscale capital is flowing.
The bear case is overwhelming. The stock has fallen from $5.07 to $2.97 (down 41%) since August 2026, extending the decline from the former all-time high. Revenue collapsed 82.5% YoY to approximately $900K TTM against ~$54M in net losses, the company carries a going-concern qualification, and operates with only 5 employees following the July 2026 leadership overhaul [1][2]. Securities fraud class actions allege New Era overstated permitting and regulatory progress on the TCDC project, with a lead-plaintiff deadline of June 2026, while a separate New Mexico state lawsuit alleges the company (via predecessor entity and former CEO Gray) transferred liability-bearing oil and gas wells into bankruptcy to dodge plugging and remediation obligations — a governance crisis that the leadership transition only partially addresses [3]. A $50M senior secured promissory note represents existential refinancing risk for a company burning cash at this rate. The AI Resilience score of 8/10 reflects only that AI cannot disrupt physical infrastructure construction — it says nothing about whether this specific company can survive its going-concern risk or execute its ambitious data center vision.
Key Catalysts
  1. Conversion of the Stream Data Centers joint-venture letter of intent into a definitive, financed agreement, providing the first credible external validation of the AI data-center pivot and a path to revenue generation [2]
  2. Resolution of securities fraud class actions and the New Mexico AG lawsuit in the company's favor, removing legal and reputational overhang that constrains institutional investor interest [3]
  3. Any milestone demonstrating actual construction progress, signed anchor tenant, or power purchase agreement at the Odessa TCDC site, converting the current land-and-narrative story into tangible infrastructure with revenue visibility
Key Risks
  1. Going-concern qualification combined with ~$54M TTM net loss on ~$900K of revenue signals real insolvency risk or highly dilutive emergency financing before the AI data-center pipeline generates meaningful revenue — the $50M senior secured note is a near-term refinancing cliff [1]
  2. Active securities fraud class-action lawsuits alleging the company overstated TCDC permitting and regulatory progress directly threaten management credibility and could result in material legal liabilities or settlements [3]
  3. New Mexico state lawsuit alleging the company and former CEO shifted well-remediation liabilities into bankruptcy-bound shell entities raises governance and legal-exposure concerns that the July 2026 leadership transition only partially addresses — new CEO Nelson inherits these liabilities [3]
