Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NYSE ·United States
Nucor Corporation is the largest steel producer in the United States, operating through Steel Mills, Steel Products, and Raw Materials segments built on a low-cost electric-arc-furnace (mini-mill) model. The company's Steel Mills segment posted a record 7 million tons shipped in Q1 2026 and carried a steel mills backlog of 4.7 million tons -- the highest since Q2 2021 -- driven by data center, energy, and border-fence demand extending into 2027-2028 [1]. Nucor is famous for a decentralized, teammate-ownership culture with extensive profit-sharing and a historical no-layoff philosophy pioneered by Ken Iverson, and is led by Chair and CEO Leon Topalian [2].

Investment Thesis
Nucor enters the back half of 2026 with the strongest operating momentum in years: record Q1 steel mill shipments, a backlog at its highest level since 2021, and Q2 2026 net earnings of $1.16B (nearly double the prior-year quarter), fueled by data-center, energy, and infrastructure-linked steel demand extending into 2027-2028 [1]. At 13.1x forward earnings with D/E of just 30.5%, the balance sheet comfortably supports continued capacity investment, and the company's decentralized, profit-sharing culture remains a genuine structural advantage for talent retention and bottom-up efficiency gains that peers struggle to replicate [2]. Nucor is also a clear beneficiary, not victim, of the AI infrastructure buildout, with data center construction explicitly cited as a backlog driver.
The bear case centers on cyclicality and valuation: Nucor's 2025 earnings fell 14% even as revenue grew, illustrating how sensitive margins are to steel pricing, and the current PEG of 5.21 suggests the stock has re-rated faster than consensus earnings growth estimates, leaving limited room for disappointment if the current recovery in shipments and pricing proves temporary [1][4]. Trade policy (tariffs, border-wall-linked demand) is a material and non-repeatable tailwind that could reverse with policy changes, and Nucor's 7.99% profit margin remains structurally thin, meaning earnings leverage cuts both ways in a downturn. The ~9% estimated 5-year revenue CAGR also reflects a business still working through the aftermath of the 2021 steel-price super-cycle rather than secular, self-sustaining growth.
Key Catalysts
  1. Steel mills backlog of 4.7M tons (highest since Q2 2021) extending into 2027, with border-wall-related demand visible into 2028, provides multi-quarter revenue visibility uncommon in a historically spot-driven commodity business [1]
  2. Data center and energy infrastructure buildout is cited explicitly by management as a new, structural demand pocket, positioning Nucor as a direct beneficiary of AI-driven capital spending rather than a target of AI disruption [1]
  3. Continued capacity discipline and the lowest-cost EAF structure in U.S. steel should allow further market share gains from higher-cost integrated mills and import-exposed competitors as domestic policy continues to favor U.S. producers [1]
Key Risks
  1. Steel is a cyclical commodity business; the 14% earnings decline in 2025 despite revenue growth shows how quickly margin can compress on pricing softness, and a PEG of 5.21 leaves the stock vulnerable to multiple compression if the current shipment/backlog strength moderates [1][4]
  2. A meaningful share of current demand strength (border-wall projects, tariff-protected pricing) is policy-contingent rather than organic, creating headline risk if trade or infrastructure policy shifts
  3. Profit margin of 8.0% is thin even by steel-industry standards, leaving limited buffer if scrap input costs rise faster than finished steel pricing
