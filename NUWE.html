Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices (Fluid Management / Ultrafiltration) ·NASDAQ ·United States
Nuwellis (formerly CHF Solutions) makes the Aquadex ultrafiltration platform for fluid removal in patients with heart failure and fluid overload, with a growing focus on pediatric and critical care markets following its acquisition of Rendiatech, which adds kidney-monitoring capability to the cardiorenal platform. The company reported 20% revenue growth for the first half of 2026 and installed-base growth (24 consoles sold in H1 2026 vs. 5 in the prior-year period), but remains deeply unprofitable with a $4.5 million Q1 2026 net loss against roughly $2.2 million of cash, prompting a going-concern warning. Mike McCormick, previously Chief Commercial Officer, became CEO effective June 30, 2026, and the company has executed two reverse stock splits (1-for-35 in 2024, 1-for-42 in 2025) to maintain Nasdaq listing compliance.

Investment Thesis
Nuwellis is showing genuine commercial traction on a small base — H1 2026 revenue grew 20% YoY, installed-base console placements jumped from 5 to 24 units year-over-year, and gross margin expanded to 76% in Q2 2026 from 56% a year earlier on better pricing and contract-manufacturing terms [1]. The Rendiatech acquisition extends the Aquadex platform into kidney monitoring, broadening the addressable cardiorenal opportunity, and new CEO Mike McCormick brings three decades of med-tech commercialization experience to a company that has historically struggled to scale beyond a niche position [2].
The bear case is overwhelming and centers on solvency: Nuwellis reported a $4.5 million Q1 2026 net loss against just $2.2 million of cash, triggering a going-concern warning, and average daily dollar volume of roughly $160,000 sits far below the $1 million threshold IOWN requires for tradeable liquidity — this stock automatically scores zero on Infrastructure regardless of its operating momentum [3]. The company has executed two reverse stock splits in as many years (1-for-35 in 2024, 1-for-42 in 2025) simply to maintain Nasdaq listing compliance, and its most recent financing was a dilutive $6 million equity and warrant offering priced at $0.30 per share that triggered a sharp selloff — a pattern of continuous shareholder dilution that is likely to persist given the ongoing cash burn relative to revenue scale [3]. At a $2.8 million market cap, Nuwellis is a speculative, pre-scale medical device story that does not meet IOWN's liquidity or balance-sheet durability standards.
