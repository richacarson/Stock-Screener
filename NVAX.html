Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Novavax develops protein-based vaccines using its Matrix-M adjuvant technology, best known for its COVID-19 vaccine. As pandemic-era demand has faded, the company has pivoted to a capital-light out-licensing strategy, most notably a partnership with Sanofi advancing a combination COVID-19/influenza vaccine toward Phase 3, with near-term milestone payments including $125M tied to Phase III trial initiation and $75M for manufacturing technology transfer around mid-2027. Q2 2026 total revenue fell 76% YoY to $57M, though the company raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $235-275M. CEO John Jacobs faced significant shareholder pushback at the 2026 annual meeting, with 38-41% of votes opposing director re-election amid activist investor Shah Capital's calls for governance changes and share buybacks.

Investment Thesis
Novavax's out-licensing pivot has begun to bear fruit: the Sanofi partnership on a combination COVID-19/influenza vaccine is advancing through Phase 3 regulatory discussions with $200M in near-term milestone payments identified ($125M for trial initiation, $75M for tech transfer around mid-2027), and full-year 2026 revenue guidance was raised to $235-275M, an upward revision from prior guidance [1]. The capital-light model shifts commercialization risk and cost to Sanofi, a partner with far greater scale in the combination-vaccine category where Sanofi intends to be a first-mover. A PEG of 1.32 is the one metric suggesting the market may be underpricing forward earnings potential relative to growth expectations, if the Sanofi milestones materialize on schedule.
The bear case is substantial. Total revenue fell 76% YoY to $57M in Q2 2026, and every core Infrastructure profitability metric — margin, forward earnings, growth — floors at zero, reflecting a company that has not found a sustainable standalone commercial footing since pandemic-era demand evaporated [1]. The June 2026 shareholder revolt, in which Shah Capital publicly criticized management and 38-41% of votes opposed director re-election including CEO John Jacobs, signals a governance crisis that could distract leadership or force a change in strategic direction (activists are explicitly calling for board downsizing and buybacks rather than R&D reinvestment) [3]. No insider buying was found in the trailing 12 months, offering no counter-signal of leadership conviction, and the entire near-term thesis is now concentrated in a single partner-dependent program (Sanofi combination vaccine) whose Phase 3 timing is still subject to regulatory discussions rather than a confirmed start date [1].
