Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Nuvectis Pharma is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company developing targeted oncology therapies for unmet medical needs. Its pipeline includes NXP800 (an HSF1 pathway inhibitor), NXP900 (a SRC/YES1 kinase inhibitor in Phase 1b, now focused on combinations in non-small cell lung cancer and pancreatic cancer), and newer programs ciprocopan (NXP100) and NXP200, with IND submissions expected in Q4 2026. Founded in 2020 and based in Fort Lee, New Jersey, the company is led by co-founder, Chairman and CEO Ron Bentsur.

Investment Thesis
Nuvectis is a clinical-stage oncology company with a newly funded runway and multiple shots on goal. The July 2026 follow-on offering raised $115M gross (about $106.3M net), extending cash runway into 1H 2029 and removing near-term financing risk after cash had fallen to $22.2M at June 30, 2026 [1]. The pipeline now includes ciprocopan (NXP100) and NXP200 with IND submissions expected in Q4 2026, plus NXP900 combination programs with osimertinib in NSCLC and daraxonrasib in pancreatic cancer [1]. The company carries no debt, and co-founder CEO Ron Bentsur - who previously led the U.S. launches of Jelmyto and Auryxia - owns about 13.9% of shares [3][5].
The bear case is binary clinical risk with no revenue. Every program is early-stage, NXP900 has been redirected away from monotherapy, and the pancreatic combination depends on access to a third party's drug [1]. ROE is -214.6%, the forward P/E is negative, and the July raise significantly diluted holders [1][6]. Trading liquidity is thin at about $3.7M per day, the stock has ranged from $5.55 to $29.28 over the past year, and ESG data providers flag elevated risk (Sustainalytics ranks it 624 of 858 pharmaceutical companies) [6][7].
Key Catalysts
  1. IND submissions for ciprocopan (NXP100) and NXP200 expected in Q4 2026 [1]
  2. Initiation and early data from NXP900 combination studies with osimertinib in NSCLC and with daraxonrasib in pancreatic cancer [1]
  3. Potential partnering of pipeline assets, leveraging CEO Bentsur's track record of commercial launches and partnerships [3]
Key Risks
  1. Binary clinical and regulatory risk across an entirely early-stage pipeline with no product revenue [1]
  2. Dependence on third-party drug availability (daraxonrasib) for the pancreatic cancer combination program [1]
  3. Thin trading liquidity (~$3.7M/day) and high volatility (52-week range $5.55-$29.28), plus future dilution if runway assumptions slip [6]
