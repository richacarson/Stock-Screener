Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - General ·NYSE ·Denmark
Novo Nordisk is a global pharmaceutical leader in diabetes care and obesity treatment, anchored by its GLP-1 receptor agonist franchise (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus). The company has been pioneering diabetes treatment since 1923 and recently transformed the obesity market with semaglutide-based therapies. Q2 2026 delivered $12.1B in revenue, beating estimates, with adjusted sales growing 7% at constant exchange rates. The Wegovy pill achieved the strongest-ever GLP-1 launch by volume with 5M+ U.S. prescriptions. NVO raised its FY2026 outlook to 0% to -6% adjusted sales growth at CER (improved from -12% to -4%). Market cap approximately $210B, down significantly from 2024 peaks due to pricing pressure and competitive concerns.

Investment Thesis
Novo Nordisk at 13.32x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.71 is a category-defining pharma franchise trading at a deep discount to its intrinsic value following a 60%+ drawdown from 2024 peaks — Q2 2026 beat estimates with $12.1B revenue and $0.95 EPS (vs $0.78 consensus), and management raised FY2026 guidance to 0% to -6% CER from the prior -12% to -4% [1][3]. The financial profile is exceptional: 71.4% ROE, 37.2% profit margin, 3.7% dividend yield with 40+ consecutive years paid, and a 15% 5-year dividend growth CAGR [5]. The GLP-1 franchise dominates the world's fastest-growing drug class — the Wegovy pill achieved the strongest-ever GLP-1 launch by volume (5M+ U.S. prescriptions), and the obesity treatment market is projected to exceed $100B by 2030 [2]. The 100-year history, Novo Nordisk Foundation governance structure, and exceptionally strong pipeline (Amycretin, oral semaglutide, UBT251) provide long-term durability that few pharmaceutical companies can match.
The bear case centers on secular pricing pressure, competitive intensification, and near-term revenue deceleration. FY2026 guidance of 0% to -6% adjusted sales at CER vs 2025 represents the first meaningful revenue decline in NVO's modern history, driven by mounting GLP-1 pricing pressure from pharmacy benefit managers and government negotiations [3]. Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) is a formidable worthy rival that has demonstrated superior weight loss efficacy in head-to-head trials, and emerging competitors (Amgen, Viking Therapeutics, Structure Therapeutics) could fragment the market further. The 72% D/E ratio — 279% above NVO's 10-year median — reflects aggressive manufacturing investment that adds risk if GLP-1 demand decelerates faster than expected. The stock price decline from $140+ to $47 has priced in substantial pessimism, but further multiple compression is possible if competitive dynamics worsen. A 2026 cybersecurity breach exposed AI model checkpoints and training datasets, highlighting operational risk in the company's AI transformation efforts [4].
