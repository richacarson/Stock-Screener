Company Profile
Industrials ·Environmental Services ·NYSE ·United States
Enviri Corporation (formerly Harsco) is an industrial environmental services company. In June 2026, Enviri completed the sale of its Clean Earth segment to Veolia for $3.04 billion and spun off its remaining Harsco Environmental and Harsco Rail businesses as New Enviri (Enviri II Corporation), which now trades under the NVRI ticker. Harsco Environmental provides steel mill byproduct processing and recycling services at 150+ customer sites in 30+ countries. Harsco Rail manufactures railway track maintenance equipment. Founded in 1853, headquartered in Philadelphia.

Investment Thesis
Enviri is a post-restructuring industrial services company trading at ~$652M market cap after the transformative $3.04B sale of Clean Earth to Veolia and spin-off as New Enviri in June 2026 [2]. Shareholders received $15/share cash plus New Enviri shares, and the remaining stock at $22.58 represents the Harsco Environmental and Rail businesses with ~$1.1-1.3B in estimated revenue. Harsco Environmental's global leadership in steel mill services across 150+ customer sites in 30+ countries provides embedded, recurring revenue with high switching costs [1]. The Inspire Impact Score of 53 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions and nine positive ESG attributes reflects clean faith alignment [3]. 2026 EBITDA guidance of $144-161M (combined) provides a floor valuation, and any improvement in Rail segment performance from current $(26)-$(19)M range could meaningfully boost earnings.
The bear case is dominated by the post-restructuring reality: Enviri has sold its best segment and retained the weaker assets. The remaining businesses are unprofitable at the net income level, with estimated D/E still around 341% and negative ROE of -23.17% [5]. Revenue has declined at approximately -3% CAGR over 5 years, and 2026 guidance projects flat performance with no growth catalysts [1]. The Rail segment is actively losing money ($(26)-$(19)M EBITDA) and drags on the overall enterprise. The steel industry's cyclicality exposes Harsco Environmental to volume risk — any downturn in global steel production directly impacts revenue. The company has no dividend, no clear path to profitability at the net income level, and operates in a shrinking revenue base. The 170-year-old company has been reduced to its least dynamic segments, raising questions about long-term strategic viability as a standalone entity.
Key Catalysts
  1. Post-Clean-Earth-sale deleveraging — $3.04B in sale proceeds should significantly reduce the $1.70B pre-restructuring debt burden, improving financial flexibility [2]
  2. Harsco Environmental EBITDA guidance of $170-180M at 150+ steel mill sites globally provides a baseline for valuation; any steel production recovery would drive upside [1]
  3. Rail segment turnaround — addressing the $(26)-$(19)M EBITDA drag through contract restructuring or cost actions could add meaningful value on a small market cap base [1]
Key Risks
  1. Post-restructuring entity retains the weaker businesses — Clean Earth (the growth engine) was sold, leaving declining Harsco Environmental and money-losing Rail as the entire company [1][2]
  2. Estimated D/E of 341% with negative ROE and profit margin indicates the remaining business cannot cover its cost structure at current operating levels [5]
  3. Steel industry cyclicality — Harsco Environmental's revenue is directly tied to global steel production volumes; any sustained downturn threatens the $170-180M EBITDA floor [1]
