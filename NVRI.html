Company Profile
Industrials ·Environmental Services ·NYSE ·United States
Enviri Corporation (formerly Harsco, renamed June 2023) is an industrial environmental services company. In June 2026, Enviri completed the sale of its Clean Earth segment to Veolia for $3.04B and reconstituted as New Enviri (Enviri II Corporation) under the NVRI ticker. The remaining company operates two segments: Harsco Environmental, which provides on-site environmental services at 150+ steel mill customer sites in 30+ countries (slag processing, byproduct recycling, value-added downstream products); and Harsco Rail, which manufactures railway track maintenance equipment. Founded in 1853, headquartered in Philadelphia. Market cap approximately $581M.

Investment Thesis
Enviri is a post-restructuring industrial environmental services company trading at ~$581M market cap after the transformative $3.04B sale of Clean Earth to Veolia in June 2026, which slashed D/E from 341% to 61.56% [1][4]. The remaining Harsco Environmental segment provides embedded, recurring revenue at 150+ steel mill sites across 30+ countries with high switching costs and long-term contracts. New Enviri Proforma EBITDA is guided at ~$140M for 2026, implying an approximately 4x EV/EBITDA multiple on the remaining businesses [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 53 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions and nine positive ESG attributes reflects clean faith alignment for an environmental services business. Stock trades at 0.9x book value, suggesting the market prices in significant risk but also creating deep value potential if profitability improves.
The bear case is dominated by the post-restructuring reality: Enviri sold its best segment and retained the weaker assets. ROE of -78.08%, profit margin of -24.38%, and forward EPS of -$1.07 paint a picture of a company unable to generate positive returns on its remaining businesses [4]. The Rail segment is actively losing money, dragging on overall enterprise profitability with 2026 EBITDA guidance well below breakeven [2]. Revenue has declined at approximately -3% CAGR over 5 years before the Clean Earth divestiture, and the -66.7% reported revenue decline reflects the loss of the growth engine. The steel industry's cyclicality exposes Harsco Environmental to volume risk — any downturn in global steel production directly impacts revenue and the $140M EBITDA target. Average daily volume of only ~393K shares raises secondary liquidity concerns despite clearing the floor. The company has no dividend, no clear path to net income profitability, and is managing what remains of a company whose most dynamic segment has been sold.
Key Catalysts
  1. Post-Clean-Earth-sale balance sheet improvement — D/E reduced from 341% to 61.56%, dramatically improving financial flexibility and potentially enabling reinvestment in the Harsco Environmental segment [1][4]
  2. Harsco Environmental EBITDA improvement guided above 2025 levels at 150+ steel mill sites globally; any recovery in global steel production volumes would provide operating leverage [2]
  3. Stock trades at 0.9x book value with approximately 4x EV/EBITDA on ~$140M guided EBITDA — significant upside if Rail segment losses are addressed or the business is divested [2][4]
Key Risks
  1. Post-restructuring entity retains the weaker businesses — Clean Earth (the growth engine) was sold to Veolia, leaving unprofitable Harsco Environmental and money-losing Rail as the entire company with ROE of -78.08% [1][4]
  2. Steel industry cyclicality — Harsco Environmental's revenue is directly tied to global steel production volumes at 150+ embedded sites; any sustained downturn threatens the ~$140M EBITDA target [2]
  3. No clear path to net income profitability with forward EPS of -$1.07, and average daily volume of only ~393K shares reduces liquidity for institutional investors [4]
