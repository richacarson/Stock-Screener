Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NGM ·United States
Navitas Semiconductor designs, develops, and markets next-generation gallium nitride (GaN) power ICs and silicon carbide (SiC) power devices for power conversion and charging applications across automotive, data center, energy infrastructure, and consumer electronics. The company completed its 'Navitas 2.0' transition, exiting the mobile charger market to focus on high-power markets including AI data centers and grid infrastructure. Founded in 2014 and headquartered in Torrance, California, Navitas reported Q2 2026 revenue of $10.5M with high-power markets growing 50%+ YoY.

Investment Thesis
Navitas at $12.40 with a $3.3B market cap is a pre-profitability GaN/SiC power semiconductor company positioned at the intersection of two secular megatrends — AI data center power efficiency and clean energy electrification [1]. The Navitas 2.0 pivot is showing early commercial traction: Q2 2026 revenue of $10.5M grew 22% sequentially with high-power markets up 50%+ YoY, Q3 guidance implies 28% sequential growth, and full-year 2026 targets mid-single-digit revenue growth as the business inflects [1]. The company holds $557M in cash against virtually zero debt ($5M), providing a multi-year runway to achieve profitability, and its GaN power IC technology leadership positions it to benefit from the exponential scaling of AI data center power demands where next-generation GPU clusters require increasingly efficient power conversion.
The bear case is significant: Navitas remains deeply unprofitable with -52.6% ROE, -255% operating margins, and -$66M operating cash flow, while annual revenue of $46M is dwarfed by competitors Infineon ($16B+), ON Semi ($7B+), and Texas Instruments ($16B+) who are all aggressively targeting the same AI data center power opportunity [4]. The stock trades at 73x revenue despite negative earnings, pricing in years of execution that remains highly uncertain. The September 2025 CEO transition from founder Gene Sheridan to external hire Chris Allexandre introduces leadership risk, and the Wolfspeed patent infringement lawsuit creates legal overhang across core GaN and SiC product families [3]. Revenue declined 45% from $83M (2024) to $46M (2025) during the mobile market exit, and the path to profitability requires sustained double-digit sequential growth that has yet to be demonstrated over multiple quarters. The stock is down 64% from its 52-week high of $34.17 [4].
