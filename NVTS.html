Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
Navitas Semiconductor designs and manufactures gallium nitride (GaN) and silicon carbide (SiC) power semiconductors for applications in AI data centers, grid and energy infrastructure, industrial electrification, and consumer electronics. The company is executing 'Navitas 2.0,' a strategic pivot from mobile and low-end consumer markets toward high-power AI infrastructure and grid applications. Q2 2026 revenue was $10.5M (+22% sequentially), with high-power markets growing 50% YoY. However, the deliberate exit from mobile/consumer caused full-year 2025 revenue to decline 45% to $45.9M. FY2026 is expected to deliver mid-single-digit revenue growth as the high-power transition gains traction. The company is the first semiconductor company to be CarbonNeutral certified. Market cap approximately $2.2B.

Investment Thesis
Navitas Semiconductor at $12.08 is a speculative bet on the AI data center power bottleneck — the company's GaN and SiC power semiconductors offer 3x better efficiency and power density than legacy silicon, positioning NVTS at the critical intersection of exponential AI compute growth and the physical constraints of power delivery [3]. Q2 2026 showed inflection: revenue grew 22% sequentially to $10.5M, high-power markets surged 50% YoY, non-GAAP gross margin improved to 39%, and Q3 guidance of $13.5M implies continued sequential acceleration [1]. The AI infrastructure buildout is a structural multi-year tailwind — every new AI data center rack requires more efficient power conversion from grid to chip, and Navitas's integrated GaN and SiC platforms address this bottleneck directly. The balance sheet is essentially debt-free (D/E 1%), the 5-year revenue CAGR of ~9% demonstrates long-term growth despite the transitional dip, and the Inspire Impact Score of 46 (ALIGNED) reflects strong ESG practices including CarbonNeutral certification [4].
The bear case is substantial: Navitas remains deeply unprofitable with a GAAP net loss of $228.2M in Q2 2026 (including estimated non-cash impairments) on just $10.5M in revenue, and ROE of -35.15% reflects cash burn funded by equity dilution rather than operational earnings [5]. Revenue declined 45% in 2025 ($83.3M to $45.9M) due to the China consumer exit, and even with the Navitas 2.0 pivot gaining traction, full-year 2026 revenue growth is expected at only mid-single digits [6]. The competitive landscape is daunting — Infineon, STMicroelectronics, ON Semi, Texas Instruments, and Monolithic Power Systems all have vastly larger scale, established data center customer relationships, and resources to outspend Navitas in GaN/SiC development [3]. The leadership transition (new CEO Chris Allexandre replacing founder Gene Sheridan, CTO/COO Kinzer departure) introduces execution risk at the most critical strategic juncture [4]. The market cap of $2.2B on ~$40M TTM revenue implies a 55x price-to-sales ratio, requiring extraordinary growth to justify current valuation. If the AI power delivery opportunity takes longer to materialize or Navitas loses design wins to larger competitors, the equity could face significant further downside.
