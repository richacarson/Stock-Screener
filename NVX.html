Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NASDAQ ·Australia
NOVONIX Limited develops and manufactures synthetic graphite anode materials for lithium-ion batteries, with a primary production facility in Chattanooga, Tennessee. The company is positioning as the first large-scale North American producer of synthetic graphite anode material, supported by a $100M U.S. Department of Energy grant, a conditional $754.8M DOE loan commitment, and $103M in U.S. tax credit allocations. Dual-listed on NASDAQ and ASX. Founded in 2017, headquartered in Brisbane, Australia.
300 Employees ·www.novonixgroup.com

Investment Thesis
NOVONIX occupies a unique strategic position as the first company to produce synthetic graphite anode material at mass-production scale in North America, with the June 2026 C-sample delivery to Panasonic Energy representing a critical qualification milestone [1]. The company is backed by substantial government support: $100M DOE grant, conditional $754.8M DOE loan commitment, and $103M tax credit allocation to build out its Chattanooga facility [1]. The growing global demand for lithium-ion batteries driven by EV adoption and energy storage, combined with geopolitical pressure to reduce dependence on Chinese supply chains, creates a secular tailwind for domestic anode material production. At $102M market cap, the stock trades below the value of its government commitments alone.
The bear case is severe and multidimensional. NOVONIX generates only $5.62M in TTM revenue (down 4.1% YoY) while burning $92.73M annually in net losses and $153.5M in negative free cash flow, creating urgent funding concerns despite government commitments [5]. The March 2026 ITC decision removing anti-dumping protection against Chinese anode imports eliminated a key assumption underpinning the domestic pricing thesis [1]. The sale of the revenue-generating BTS unit to former CEO Burns leaves the company effectively pre-revenue until Panasonic or other customers complete qualification — a decision and timeline NOVONIX does not control [2]. Daily dollar volume of ~$137K makes the stock essentially untradeable for institutional investors, the shares have declined 67.8% over the past year, and the conditional DOE loan commitment may not convert to actual funding if the company cannot meet milestones [5][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Panasonic C-sample qualification decision could unlock the first commercial supply agreement for domestically-produced synthetic graphite anode material in North America
  2. Conversion of the conditional $754.8M DOE loan commitment to definitive funding would provide the capital needed to complete Chattanooga facility buildout and reach commercial scale
  3. Potential reinstatement of trade protections against Chinese graphite imports through new tariff actions or policy changes that would improve domestic pricing dynamics
Key Risks
  1. Cash burn of $92.73M annually against $5.62M revenue with negative net cash position creates existential liquidity risk — DOE loan commitment is conditional and may not fund if milestones are not met
  2. ITC removal of anti-dumping protection in March 2026 eliminates pricing support against Chinese synthetic graphite producers who benefit from massive scale and lower production costs
  3. Daily dollar volume of ~$137K renders the stock illiquid for institutional investment and creates severe price impact risk for any meaningful position — shares have already declined 67.8% in 52 weeks
