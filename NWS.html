Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
News Corporation is a diversified media and information services company operating through five segments: Digital Real Estate Services (REA Group, Move/Realtor.com), Dow Jones (Wall Street Journal, Barron's, MarketWatch, Factiva), Book Publishing (HarperCollins), News Media (The Times, New York Post, The Australian), and Other (Storyful). Controlled by the Murdoch family through dual-class share structure. CEO Robert Thomson has led the company since 2013 and has aggressively pursued AI content licensing deals with OpenAI, Meta, and others.

Investment Thesis
News Corp delivered Q3 FY2026 revenue of $2.2B (up 9% YoY) with segment EBITDA of $343M (up 18%, margin expanding from 14.4% to 15.7%), marking the 12th consecutive quarter of profit growth [1]. The AI content licensing strategy is materializing into significant recurring revenue: $250M OpenAI deal, $50M/year Meta deal, and expected Anthropic settlement revenue, with additional deals in advanced negotiation [3][4]. Digital Real Estate revenues surged 17% to $473M, and Dow Jones set a 5-year target of $1B EBITDA. At $16.2B market cap, News Corp's portfolio of premium content brands (WSJ, HarperCollins), digital platforms (REA Group), and growing AI licensing revenue creates diversified value that the market may be underappreciating.
The bear case centers on structural media industry decline and valuation. Forward P/E of ~29x and PEG of 3.53 imply the market already prices in growth that may not materialize, while ROE of 5.33% and profit margin of 6.81% demonstrate the company is not generating adequate returns on capital [5]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -6.6% reflects a legacy shrinking business that AI licensing revenue must offset. AI-generated content and AI search summaries (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT) pose existential threats to the advertising and subscription model that funds professional journalism — the very content News Corp licenses to AI companies may accelerate its own displacement. The Murdoch family's dual-class share structure limits minority shareholder influence and creates succession uncertainty. The $0.65% dividend yield provides negligible income support, and News Corp competes with dramatically larger digital platforms for advertising and subscription dollars.
Key Catalysts
  1. Additional AI content licensing deals closing in H2 2026, with Thomson confirming advanced negotiations with multiple AI companies, potentially creating a $200M+ annual AI licensing revenue stream
  2. Dow Jones executing toward its 5-year $1B EBITDA target through digital subscription growth, AI-enhanced data analytics products, and professional information expansion
  3. Anthropic settlement revenue beginning in late 2026, validating the legal strategy and establishing precedent for future content protection and monetization against AI scrapers
Key Risks
  1. AI-generated content and AI search summaries cannibalizing website traffic and undermining the advertising and subscription model — News Corp's own AI licensing deals may accelerate the displacement of the content it licenses
  2. Forward P/E of ~29x and PEG of 3.53 leave no margin of safety, while 5.33% ROE and 6.81% profit margin signal inadequate capital efficiency — any growth disappointment could trigger significant multiple compression
  3. Murdoch family succession dynamics and dual-class share structure create governance concentration risk, limiting minority shareholder influence and creating strategic uncertainty beyond Thomson's CEO tenure

AI Risk Protection (5/9): News Corp faces meaningful AI disruption risk across its media and publishing segments. AI-generated content threatens traditional journalism economics, AI search summaries reduce click-through traffic to news websites, and HarperCollins faces emerging AI-generated book competition [1]. However, News Corp has proactively monetized this threat through content licensing: the $250M OpenAI deal, $50M/year Meta deal, and expected Anthropic settlement revenue create significant defensive revenue from its proprietary data assets [3][4].
