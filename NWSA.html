Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
News Corporation is a global media and information services company operating Dow Jones (The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, Factiva, Dow Jones Risk & Compliance and Dow Jones Energy), Digital Real Estate Services (a majority stake in Australia's REA Group and Realtor.com operator Move), Book Publishing (HarperCollins) and News Media (The Times, The Sun, The Australian, New York Post). Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30) revenue rose 6.8% to a record $9.03B with net income of $573M [2][3]. Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue rose 11% to $2.34B, total segment EBITDA jumped 31% to $423M, and net income from continuing operations rose 167% to $230M, led by REA (+21%) and Dow Jones (+7%, EBITDA margin 28.1%) [3][4].

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Sinek would see a company with an enduring franchise but a finite posture. News Corp's portfolio moves — selling Foxtel, building B2B data and digital real estate — have been shrewd, and Robert Thomson has led since 2013, yet the company's public language is about fighting Google, AI companies and rivals for value, its governance remains family-controlled and litigated, and its cause is defined more by what it opposes than by a future it is building.
Just Cause
4
Just Cause (1/3): The aspiration to provide authoritative journalism and information has substance, and Thomson's campaign that quality content must be paid for is consistent [5]. But the stated purpose is largely descriptive, and the phone-hacking legacy and nepotism allegations [7] undercut the claim that the cause shapes behaviour.

Rivals are adversaries, not teachers.

Investment Thesis
News Corp has turned into a collection of high-quality digital and information assets wrapped in a media holding company: fiscal 2026 revenue reached a record $9.03B, net income rose 23% to $573M [2], and fiscal Q4 accelerated with revenue +11%, total segment EBITDA +31% and Digital Real Estate EBITDA +46% [3][4]. REA Group's dominance in Australian property search, Dow Jones' 28.1% EBITDA margin, and a balance sheet with only 31.6% debt-to-equity [1][4] give the company options, while the $250M+ OpenAI deal shows its content can be monetised in the AI era [6]. Management guides to further margin expansion in fiscal 2027 [4].
The bear case is valuation and structure. At 28x trailing and a 2.61 PEG, the stock prices in growth that its 8.0% ROE and 6.3% net margin do not yet support [1], and as-reported revenue is below FY2021 levels after the Foxtel sale [2]. AI search could erode traffic to the WSJ, The Times and New York Post faster than licensing deals replace it [5], HarperCollins faces generative-content pressure, Realtor.com remains a distant number two in US property search, and Murdoch dual-class control plus governance litigation [7] limit the value minority holders can unlock from the REA stake.
Key Catalysts
  1. Fiscal 2027 margin expansion at Dow Jones as digital advertising (+10% in Q4) and B2B Energy/Risk & Compliance growth outpace print declines [3][4]
  2. Continued double-digit REA Group growth from price increases and rising Australian residential listings [3]
  3. Additional AI content licensing agreements following the $250M+ OpenAI deal, adding high-margin revenue [6]
Key Risks
  1. AI answer engines reducing referral traffic and ad revenue for WSJ, The Times, The Sun and New York Post faster than licensing replaces it [5]
  2. Australian housing slowdown hitting REA listings volumes, the largest single EBITDA driver [3]
  3. Dual-class Murdoch family control and governance litigation alleging nepotism limiting minority shareholder value [7]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
5
AI Risk Protection (4/9): Generative AI answer engines directly threaten the traffic and advertising economics of News Media and parts of Dow Jones; publishers argue AI models trained on their content have created chatbots that 'now compete with them as sources of information' [5][6]. The defences are real but partial: subscription paywalls, proprietary B2B datasets (Factiva, Risk & Compliance, Dow Jones Energy pricing) and REA's two-sided property marketplace are much harder to substitute, and Thomson has chosen to 'woo rather than sue' to get paid for content [5]. AI Opportunity (1/1): The OpenAI licensing deal, valued at more than $250M over five years, is quantifiable AI-derived revenue [6].
