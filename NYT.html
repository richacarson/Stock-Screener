Company Profile
Communication Services ·Publishing ·NYSE ·United States
The New York Times Company is a global media organization that includes The New York Times, The Athletic, NYT Games (Wordle, Spelling Bee), NYT Cooking, and Wirecutter. Founded in 1851, the company has transformed from a print newspaper into a digital subscription platform with 12.52 million digital-only subscribers as of Q1 2026. Controlled by the Ochs-Sulzberger family via dual-class share structure. Headquartered in New York City.

Investment Thesis
NYT delivered a strong Q1 2026 with $712.2M revenue (up 12% YoY), $87.9M net income (up 77.4%), and 12.52M digital-only subscribers growing 16.1% in subscription revenue [1]. The bundle strategy (News + Games + Cooking + Athletic + Wirecutter) drives cross-product engagement and subscriber retention, while digital advertising surged 31.6% to $93.3M on first-party data strength. Operating margins expanded to 12.7% with TTM free cash flow of $542.2M, reflecting the operating leverage inherent in digital subscriptions at scale [1]. The balance sheet is pristine — near-zero long-term debt with $255M+ cash — supporting both a 24.6% five-year dividend CAGR and share repurchases. Full-year 2026 consensus revenue of $3.09B and $2.77 EPS imply continued acceleration from the $2.8B FY2025 base.
The primary risk is valuation — at 26.2x forward earnings and 33x trailing, NYT is priced for sustained double-digit growth that historically has been difficult for media companies to maintain [2]. Net profit margin of 12.2% remains structurally below digital platform peers, capping earnings leverage. The AI disruption threat is real and existential: generative AI tools can summarize news without driving traffic to publishers, and the OpenAI/Microsoft/Perplexity lawsuits — now in their third year — have uncertain outcomes; a loss could legitimize AI use of copyrighted journalism at scale [3]. CEO Levien's net stock sales of $6.2M over the past 12 months create optically negative insider sentiment. Glassdoor reviews citing high turnover and leadership development gaps suggest organizational challenges that could constrain execution as the company scales its digital ambitions [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Digital subscription growth continuing above 15% with a path to 15M+ subscribers as the bundle value proposition drives conversion and reduces churn across News, Games, Cooking, Athletic, and Wirecutter
  2. Operating margin expansion toward 18-20% as digital subscription economics scale — each incremental digital subscriber adds near-100% gross margin recurring revenue to the $542M TTM free cash flow base
  3. AI licensing revenue expansion beyond the Amazon deal, with potential OpenAI/Microsoft settlement establishing industry-standard content pricing that generates high-margin recurring licensing income
Key Risks
  1. Generative AI disintermediation — ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews can summarize news without delivering traffic, a long-term existential threat to subscription and advertising economics if AI content quality improves sufficiently
  2. Forward P/E of 26.2x and 33x trailing leave limited margin of safety — any subscriber growth deceleration below double digits or advertising softness could trigger multiple compression from premium publishing multiples
  3. CEO Meredith Kopit Levien's net stock selling of $6.2M combined with Glassdoor-cited high turnover and leadership development gaps raise questions about insider confidence and organizational health at scale
