Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Realty Income Corporation is a real estate investment trust that invests in single-tenant commercial properties under long-term net-lease agreements. Branded as 'The Monthly Dividend Company,' Realty Income owns over 15,600 properties across the US, UK, and Europe, leased to ~1,550 clients across 90 industries. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.55B (+9.7% YoY) beat expectations, AFFO per share rose 3.8% to $1.09, and full-year AFFO guidance was raised to $4.44-$4.45. Investment volume guidance raised to $10B. Portfolio occupancy 98.8%. Market cap approximately $53B.

Investment Thesis
Realty Income is the gold standard of net-lease REITs — a ~$53B S&P 500 member with 55+ years of consecutive monthly dividends, 31 years of dividend growth, and 98.8% portfolio occupancy across 15,600+ properties [1]. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.55B beat expectations (+9.7% YoY), AFFO per share rose 3.8% to $1.09, and full-year AFFO guidance was raised to $4.44-$4.45 [2]. The 5.87% dividend yield at $55.55 provides compelling income with 73% AFFO payout ratio ensuring healthy coverage. At ~12.5x P/AFFO, the stock trades at the low end of its historical range, offering potential for multiple expansion if interest rates decline. Investment volume guidance raised to $10B — the largest in company history — supported by Apollo/GIC private capital partnerships that expand deployment capacity without proportional equity dilution [2]. D/E of 74.7% is well-managed, and the triple-net-lease model creates highly durable cash flow with 98.8% occupancy and rent recapture rates above 100%.
The bear case focuses on growth deceleration, interest rate sensitivity, and structural REIT challenges. Per-share AFFO growth of 3.8% in Q2 and 5.2% YTD is modest given the massive $10B deployment target, raising questions about whether the 933M+ share base makes per-share accretion increasingly difficult at scale [2]. GAAP Forward P/E of 35.8 creates sticker shock despite a more reasonable ~12.5x P/AFFO. The ~$31.3B in total debt creates meaningful interest rate sensitivity — if rates stay elevated, the spread between investment yields and cost of capital narrows [6]. Employee culture strain persists with concerns about below-market pay and cultural deterioration as headcount scales [3]. Tenant concentration risk remains: Walgreens (largest tenant) faces pharmacy closures, and Dollar General/Dollar Tree face operational headwinds. CEO Roy's zero insider transactions in 18 months, combined with net insider selling by other executives, sends a neutral-to-negative conviction signal [5]. Dividend growth CAGR of ~3.4% over five years, while consistent, trails inflation-adjusted returns compared to higher-growth dividend payers.
Key Catalysts
  1. Raised $10B 2026 investment volume target (up from $9.5B) with Q2 deploying $2.6B, and AFFO guidance raised to $4.44-$4.45 reflecting accelerating deployment capacity and portfolio quality [2]
  2. Private capital fund strategy — Apollo/GIC partnerships and $1.7B U.S. Core Plus fund — creating new fee income streams and expanding investment capacity beyond traditional equity REIT model [2]
  3. Interest rate decline cycle could compress cap rates and expand Realty Income's valuation multiple while reducing the ~$31.3B debt cost burden, with 5.87% dividend yield providing attractive current income during the wait [6]
Key Risks
  1. Per-share AFFO growth of only 3.8-5.2% despite $10B annual deployment suggests diminishing per-share accretion as the 933M+ share base absorbs massive equity issuance for acquisitions [2]
  2. Interest rate sensitivity — ~$31.3B total debt means sustained higher-for-longer rates compress investment spreads and increase refinancing costs, while GAAP forward P/E of 35.8 reflects elevated valuation multiples [6]
  3. Tenant credit risk — largest tenants including Walgreens (pharmacy closures), Dollar General, and Dollar Tree face operational headwinds; occupancy dipped from 98.9% to 98.8%, and any acceleration in tenant defaults would impact distributable cash flow [2]
