Our Bond, Inc., doing business as Bond, designs, develops, and provides an AI and machine learning-based Preventative Personal Security platform, delivering real-time protective services via a mobile app, drone services, executive protection, and 24/7 Bond Command Centers staffed by Personal Security Agents. Founded in 2017 by CEO Doron Kempel, the company serves enterprises (Ralph Lauren, Apple, Disney, Roche), universities, and cities across the United States, Israel, and France, and completed a direct listing on Nasdaq on February 4, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Our Bond's AI-native personal security platform continues to win notable enterprise and public-sector business: a government-funded contract worth over $3M in annual recurring revenue, a city deployment covering 270,000+ residents, a university rollout to 4,000 students, and a top-5 global VC firm introducing Bond to 1,000 portfolio companies [2]. TTM revenue of $10.07M grew 4.3% YoY, and founder Doron Kempel — with two prior successful exits (Diligent Technologies to IBM, SimpliVity to HPE, ~$900M combined) — has personally funded the company for nearly a decade, reflecting real conviction in the long-term vision of democratizing personal security [3]. AI Resilience is exceptional: the company's entire revenue base is AI-native, meaning it cannot be disrupted by the technology it is built on.
The bear case is severe and immediate. Net loss widened to -$15.84M against $10.07M revenue (a profit margin near -157%), the balance sheet shows negative stockholders' equity, and the company has been actively restructuring its capital stack — swapping notes for Series G Convertible Preferred Stock and repricing warrants — signaling real financing distress [1][4]. The stock has fallen 97.3% over the past year to $0.5581, and average daily dollar volume of roughly $316,952 sits well below the IOWN $1M liquidity floor, forcing Infrastructure to zero regardless of operational progress. The June 2026 departure of the Head of Commercial Operations, filed alongside a 10M-share resale registration, adds execution and dilution risk on top of the existing financing overhang [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Conversion of the $3M+ ARR government contract and 270,000-resident city deployment into recognized recurring revenue, validating the B2G2C channel management calls 'a multi-million-dollar opportunity in 2026 alone'
  2. Expansion of the top-5 VC firm's introduction of Bond to 1,000 portfolio companies into signed enterprise contracts beyond the current Ralph Lauren, Apple, Disney, and Roche relationships
  3. Achieving management's targeted breakeven in late 2026 or early 2027, which would remove the dilution overhang from the current note-and-warrant restructuring cycle
Key Risks
  1. Sub-$1M daily liquidity (~$316,952) triggers the IOWN hard liquidity floor, forcing Infrastructure to zero and making the stock effectively untradeable at institutional scale regardless of business progress
  2. Ongoing dilution and refinancing risk from negative stockholders' equity, Series G Convertible Preferred note swaps, warrant repricing, and a newly filed 10M-share resale registration
  3. Deepening losses (-$15.84M net loss, ~-157% profit margin) against still-modest 4.3% revenue growth raise real questions about the path to management's late-2026/early-2027 breakeven target
