Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NYSE ·United States
Owens Corning is a global building and construction materials leader operating through three segments: Roofing (asphalt shingles and roofing components), Insulation (thermal, acoustical, and specialty insulation including the iconic PINK FIBERGLAS brand), and Doors (residential interior and exterior doors via the 2024 Masonite acquisition). Completed divestiture of glass reinforcements business on April 30, 2026, finalizing transformation to a focused branded building products leader. Q2 2026 revenue from continuing operations was $2.8B (flat YoY) with 24% adjusted EBITDA margin. Long-term targets include mid-20% EBITDA margin and $5B cumulative FCF by 2028.

Investment Thesis
Owens Corning is a market-leading building products franchise that has completed a transformative portfolio shift to branded building products (Roofing, Insulation, Doors), with Q2 2026 revenue from continuing operations of $2.8B and 24% adjusted EBITDA margin demonstrating successful execution [2]. The stock has declined to $123.90, creating a compelling forward P/E of 10.32x — a significant discount to both historical averages and the broader industrials sector — with consensus implying ~$12 forward EPS [5]. The dividend has compounded at ~20% CAGR over 5 years with a conservative 26% forward payout ratio and 2.55% current yield. Masonite integration is delivering $125M+ synergies ahead of schedule with an additional $75M in structural savings planned [1]. OC's #1-2 market positions in roofing and insulation, the iconic PINK FIBERGLAS brand, and 13 consecutive years on the DJSI World Index validate a durable competitive franchise.
The bear case centers on cyclical housing exposure and GAAP optics. The stock's 12.5% decline to $123.90 from $141.63 reflects weak housing demand — Q1 2026 revenue from continuing operations was down 10% YoY due to subdued repair/remodel activity and a less active storm season, though Q2 showed stabilization at flat growth [1][2]. D/E of 150.33% from Masonite acquisition debt creates elevated financial risk during a housing downturn, and TTM ROE of -9.45% and profit margin of -6.81% — distorted by glass reinforcements divestiture charges — will persist in trailing metrics for several quarters, potentially screening poorly for quantitative investors [5]. Prolonged elevated mortgage rates could suppress both new construction and renovation activity across all three segments. The 5yr revenue CAGR of 1.2% (net of acquisitions and divestitures) raises questions about organic growth potential absent a housing recovery. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors despite industry-leading ESG practices (MSCI AA, Sustainalytics 15.3 low risk, World's Most Ethical Companies 5x) [4].
