Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Omega Healthcare Investors is a Real Estate Investment Trust providing financing and capital to the long-term healthcare industry in the United States and United Kingdom, with a focus on skilled nursing and assisted living facilities. As of Q1 2026, Omega's portfolio includes over 1,000 operating facilities operated by approximately 90 different operators across 42 states, the District of Columbia, and the UK/Jersey.

Investment Thesis
Omega Healthcare Investors offers a compelling 5.83% dividend yield backed by 33 consecutive years of payments and an accelerating earnings recovery. Q1 2026 revenue climbed 16.6% YoY to $322.9M with net income jumping 39% to $151M, and AFFO of $0.82/share (+9.3% YoY) led management to narrow and raise full-year 2026 AFFO guidance to $3.19-$3.25/share [2]. The $1.1B capital deployment in 2025 — with two-thirds directed toward senior housing and UK assets — is now producing visible revenue acceleration. The aging demographic tailwind (65+ population growing ~3% annually) provides secular demand support, and Omega's 1,000+ facility portfolio across approximately 90 operators creates scale advantages in deal sourcing and underwriting. At a forward P/E of 23.1x with 0.58 beta, the stock offers defensive income characteristics with improving growth visibility.
The bear case centers on dividend stagnation and operator credit risk. Omega's dividend has been frozen at $2.68/share since 2020 — six consecutive years without a raise — while inflation has eroded the real yield by approximately 20% [6]. The GAAP payout ratio of 138% is unsustainable on a reported earnings basis, though AFFO coverage of approximately 1.2x provides adequate cushion. The Genesis Health Care bankruptcy exposed the operator concentration risk inherent in triple-net healthcare REITs, and persistent skilled nursing staffing shortages (vacancy rates above 20% nationally) continue to pressure operator profitability and rent coverage ratios. The RIDEA strategy introduces operating risk that pure triple-net REITs avoid. D/E of 85.6% is manageable but leaves limited room for accretive leveraged acquisitions without equity issuance. Interest rate sensitivity as a high-yield REIT means OHI competes with fixed income for capital allocation, and sustained higher rates compress the yield spread that attracts income investors [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Resumption of dividend growth — Q1 2026 AFFO of $0.82/share annualizes to $3.28, comfortably above the $2.68 dividend, providing coverage for the first potential increase since 2019 that could catalyze meaningful re-rating among income investors [2]
  2. RIDEA strategy execution and senior housing exposure generating higher risk-adjusted returns than traditional triple-net leases, expanding the growth profile and addressable market beyond skilled nursing [1]
  3. Aging population demographic wave — the 65+ cohort is the fastest-growing segment of the U.S. population at ~3% annually, driving secular demand for skilled nursing beds and post-acute care facilities that directly benefits occupancy and rent coverage
Key Risks
  1. Operator credit deterioration — Omega's triple-net lease model depends on operator profitability, and persistent skilled nursing staffing shortages (vacancy rates above 20% nationally) pressure operator margins and rent coverage, creating potential for lease restructurings or defaults [1]
  2. Interest rate sensitivity — as a high-yield REIT with D/E of 85.6% and $4.3B debt, sustained higher-for-longer rates increase borrowing costs while compressing the yield spread that attracts income investors, potentially driving capital to fixed-income alternatives [6]
  3. Dividend stagnation risk — six consecutive years without a dividend increase has frustrated income growth investors, and if AFFO growth disappoints or operator challenges emerge, Omega may extend the freeze while peers resume growth distributions
