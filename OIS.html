Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Oil States International, Inc. provides engineered capital equipment and consumable products for energy, industrial, and military sectors worldwide. Operates through three segments: Offshore Manufactured Products (subsea connectors, marine risers, cranes), Completion and Production Services, and Downhole Technologies (perforating systems, shaped charges). CEO Lloyd Hajdik assumed leadership in May 2026. Market cap approximately $507 million.

Investment Thesis
Oil States achieved a decade-high backlog of $451M (up 24% YoY) with a 1.2x book-to-bill ratio in Q2 2026, driven by diversification into military products (48% of backlog) and offshore/international markets (70%+ of revenue) [1]. The company returned to profitability in Q2 with $6M GAAP net income, and the balance sheet is effectively debt-free ($18M debt vs $20M cash). Management expects $35-40M in full-year free cash flow and guided FY2026 revenue of $640-660M with EBITDA of $77-83M [1]. The military backlog provides multi-year visibility that insulates against oil price volatility, and shaped charge volumes are doubling QoQ in the Downhole segment. At $8.42 per share with a forward P/E of 18.08, valuation is reasonable for a cyclical recovery.
Revenue continues to decline — FY2026 guidance of $640-660M represents a ~1-4% decline from FY2025's $669M despite record backlog, as geopolitical uncertainty in the Middle East delays connector and production facility orders into 2027 [1]. The company has contracted from its $782M revenue peak in FY2023 to an expected $650M in FY2026, a 17% decline that raises questions about the backlog's ability to convert into sustained top-line growth [4]. Profitability remains fragile — TTM ROE of -17.45% and profit margin of -16.78% indicate the company has only just returned to GAAP profitability and margin expansion is uncertain [2]. The 48% military backlog concentration means 12-month conversion rates are lower (~55% vs historical 65-70%), creating extended revenue recognition timelines that could frustrate near-term expectations. Small market cap ($507M) and oilfield services sector exposure add volatility risk.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record $451M backlog with 1.2x book-to-bill provides 2.8 quarters of revenue visibility, with military product awards offering multi-year conversion timelines that reduce cyclical volatility
  2. Resolution of Middle East geopolitical delays could unlock deferred connector and production facility orders currently pushed from 2026 into 2027, providing upside to revenue guidance
  3. Shaped charge volumes doubling quarter-over-quarter in Downhole Technologies segment signals a potential inflection point in US completions activity
Key Risks
  1. Revenue declining from $782M (FY2023) to guided $640-660M (FY2026) despite record backlog, as long-cycle military contracts and Middle East delays extend conversion timelines beyond near-term periods
  2. TTM profitability metrics remain deeply negative (ROE -17.45%, profit margin -16.78%), and the Q2 2026 return to GAAP profitability at $6M net income could reverse if revenue guidance misses or margins compress
  3. Small market cap ($507M) and 52-week price range of $4.75-$14.50 reflect extreme stock volatility typical of micro-cap oilfield services companies exposed to commodity cycles and geopolitical risk
