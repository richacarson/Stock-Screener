Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYQ ·United States
ONEOK is a premier midstream service provider operating gathering, processing, fractionation, transportation, storage, and marine export services across four segments: Natural Gas Gathering and Processing, Natural Gas Liquids, Natural Gas Pipelines, and Refined Products and Crude. The $18.8B Magellan Midstream Partners acquisition in September 2023 transformed ONEOK into a diversified midstream infrastructure leader with TTM revenue of $39.4B [1]. Q2 2026 delivered net income of $967M (up 13% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $2.12B (up 7%), with 2026 guidance raised to $8.2-8.5B adjusted EBITDA [1][2]. Founded in 1906 and headquartered in Tulsa, Oklahoma, ONEOK serves producers, utilities, industrial companies, and exporters.

Investment Thesis
ONEOK at $93 with a $59B market cap is a transformed midstream infrastructure leader delivering accelerating earnings growth after the $18.8B Magellan Midstream integration — Q2 2026 net income rose 13% to $967M with adjusted EBITDA up 7% to $2.12B, driven by record NGL volumes and expanding refined products throughput [1]. Management raised 2026 guidance to $8.2-8.5B adjusted EBITDA and $3.41-3.79B net income, with the 4.6% dividend yield covered at a 72.5% payout ratio and 4 consecutive years of increases [1][2]. The Magellan combination created a unique diversified platform spanning NGL, refined products, crude, and natural gas infrastructure that no single midstream peer replicates, with $2.7-3.2B in annual organic growth capex expanding capacity in the Permian Basin and Gulf Coast.
The bear case centers on leverage, commodity exposure, and the structural limitations of midstream infrastructure returns. D/E of 143.1% and net debt of $32.9B reflect the acquisition-driven balance sheet, with free cash flow of only $900M barely covering the $4.28/share annual dividend (FCF per share of ~$3.88 does not fully cover the dividend, requiring retained cash flow discipline) [1]. The 5-year dividend growth CAGR of just 2.0% and a payout ratio of 72.5% limit the capacity for meaningful dividend acceleration that income investors typically demand from midstream names yielding 4.6%. Revenue growth of 52.8% is inflated by the Magellan integration and commodity price effects; underlying organic volume growth is mid-single digits, which more accurately represents the business's sustainable growth trajectory. Profit margin of 9.3% is structurally thin for a midstream pass-through model, and PEG of 1.76 suggests the stock is not cheap relative to its earnings growth rate [2]. Natural gas demand faces long-term headwinds from energy transition, though AI-driven data center power demand is emerging as a partial offset [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Magellan integration synergies exceeding targets and organic volume growth driving 2026 guidance raises, with adjusted EBITDA guided to $8.2-8.5B and net income to $3.41-3.79B representing continued earnings acceleration [1][2]
  2. Permian Basin and Gulf Coast expansion projects including the 325-mile West Texas pipeline (240,000 bpd capacity) positioning ONEOK to capture production growth from the most prolific U.S. basin and growing NGL/refined products export demand [2]
  3. AI-driven data center power demand creating a structural tailwind for natural gas infrastructure as hyperscalers build 1-5GW campuses requiring firm, dispatchable power — ONEOK's natural gas pipeline network is positioned to serve this emerging demand source [4]
Key Risks
  1. Leverage and FCF coverage — net debt of $32.9B (D/E 143.1%) limits financial flexibility, FCF per share of $3.88 does not fully cover the $4.28 annual dividend, and net debt-to-EBITDA of 4.3x exceeds the 3.0x threshold many income investors require [1]
  2. Revenue growth normalization — the 52.8% YoY growth reflects Magellan integration and commodity effects rather than sustainable organic growth, which is mid-single digits; as the acquisition anniversary passes, reported growth will decelerate significantly [1][2]
  3. Energy transition and regulatory risk — long-term natural gas demand faces structural uncertainty from renewable energy deployment, electrification mandates, and potential methane emission regulations, though near-term fundamentals remain supportive with Permian production growth and data center demand [4]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): ONEOK's midstream pipeline infrastructure is among the most AI-resistant business models in the economy — physical pipelines gathering, processing, and transporting natural gas, NGLs, and refined products cannot be disrupted, replicated, or substituted by artificial intelligence [1]. The company's revenue is driven by physical volumes flowing through irreplaceable infrastructure assets with 30-50 year useful lives, underpinned by long-term fee-based contracts that insulate revenue from commodity price volatility. AI cannot replace the need to physically move hydrocarbons from production basins to consumption and export markets. Furthermore, AI adoption across the economy increases power demand from data centers, which is a direct tailwind for natural gas infrastructure [2]. Loses one point because AI-driven efficiency in energy consumption could marginally reduce long-term natural gas demand growth, and AI optimization of supply chains could reduce some midstream processing complexity. AI Opportunity (0/1): ONEOK uses standard industrial automation and SCADA systems for pipeline operations but has no material AI-driven revenue opportunity or differentiated AI deployment strategy — the opportunity is indirect through AI-driven power demand growth rather than direct AI monetization.
