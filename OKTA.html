Investment Thesis
Okta has completed the turn from cash-burning growth to profitable compounding: FY2026 revenue of $2.92B produced $235M of GAAP net income versus an $815M loss three years earlier, TTM free cash flow is $1.04B, and the balance sheet holds $2.3B cash against just $53M of debt after settling the remaining 2026 notes for $350M in cash [1][3][4]. Revenue has compounded at a 28.4% CAGR from $835M in FY2021, and Q2 FY2027 RPO of $4.858B grew 17% — faster than revenue — signaling durable bookings [1][3]. Management's thesis that 'AI security is identity security' is gaining traction: Okta for AI Agents, launched in November, had been purchased by dozens of companies and was in production at several by March 2026, positioning Okta as the system of record for non-human identities [2].
The bear case is decelerating growth at a premium multiple. Revenue growth has slowed to 10-11% (Q3 FY2027 guided at ~10%, with a ~1-point headwind from shifting professional services to partners), yet the stock trades at 42.2x forward and 111x trailing earnings [1][4]. ROE is only 4.3% and GAAP profit margin 9.6% because stock-based compensation remains heavy [4]. Microsoft Entra bundling and seat-based pricing pressure in an AI-agent world (the so-called 'SaaSpocalypse' debate McKinnon has had to confront) threaten pricing power [2]. Okta's security credibility was damaged by the 2022 Lapsus$ breach — which it initially did not disclose and then downplayed, prompting a class action and a 15% stock drop — and any repeat would be severe for an identity vendor [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-agent identity monetization: Okta for AI Agents and Auth0 for AI Agents scaling from 'dozens' of customers into a reported revenue line would re-rate growth expectations [2]
  2. RPO acceleration: Q2 FY2027 RPO grew 17% to $4.858B versus 11% revenue growth — conversion of that backlog could lift reported growth above the 10-11% guide [1]
  3. Capital return capacity: $1.04B TTM free cash flow and $2.3B cash with only $53M debt support buybacks and operating-margin expansion [4]
Key Risks
  1. Growth deceleration: Q3 FY2027 revenue guided at ~10% with cRPO growth of 11-12%, leaving a 42x forward P/E vulnerable if growth slips into single digits [1][4]
  2. Platform competition and pricing: Microsoft Entra bundling and AI-agent-driven pressure on seat-based SaaS pricing could compress Okta's workforce identity business [2]
  3. Breach/reputation risk: a repeat of the 2022 Lapsus$ incident — initially undisclosed, then downplayed, followed by a class action — would be especially damaging for a company whose product is trust [5]
