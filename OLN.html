Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·United States
Olin Corporation is a vertically integrated global manufacturer and distributor of chemical products and a leading U.S. manufacturer of ammunition. The company operates through three segments: Chlor Alkali Products and Vinyls (chlorine, caustic soda, VCM), Epoxy (resins and systems), and Winchester (ammunition and sporting products). Founded in 1892, Olin has paid 393 consecutive quarterly dividends. On August 25, 2026, shareholders approved an all-stock merger of equals with Huntsman Corporation, creating a $12.5B combined chemicals leader expected to close H1 2027. Q2 2026 revenue was $1,741.9M with a net loss of $13.3M and adjusted EBITDA of $191.3M. CEO Kenneth Lane appointed March 2024.

Investment Thesis
Olin at $17 with a 4.2% dividend yield, 134 years of operating history, 393 consecutive quarterly dividends, and complete AI immunity offers a deep-cyclical value play anchored by the transformative Huntsman merger [1][3]. The approved all-stock merger creates a $12.5B combined chemicals leader with $400M+ in identified synergies expected to close H1 2027, potentially unlocking significant value from purchasing efficiencies, raw material integration, and SG&A savings. Winchester ammunition ($500.3M Q2 revenue with $28.1M earnings) provides counter-cyclical strength, benefiting from tariffs on imported ammunition that create a competitive tailwind [1]. Adjusted EBITDA of $191.3M in Q2 demonstrates the business generates meaningful cash flow even at cyclically depressed margins. The Inspire Impact Score of 32 (ALIGNED) makes this suitable for faith-based portfolios seeking industrial exposure.
The bear case is severe: Olin reported a $13.3M net loss in Q2 2026 with revenue declining 1% YoY to $1,741.9M, D/E of 168%, ROE of -5.15%, and profit margin of -1.48%, reflecting a cyclically stressed business with a leveraged balance sheet paying dividends from cash reserves rather than earnings [1][5]. The VCM plant issues at Freeport materially reduced Chlor Alkali margins and demonstrate ongoing operational fragility. Forward P/E of 75x offers no value cushion — if the Huntsman merger synergies fail to materialize or integration proves disruptive, the stock faces further downside from an already depressed $17 level. The Shintech v. Olin litigation resulted in a Q4 2025 charge, adding legal overhang. The $0.20/quarter dividend has been flat for years with 0% growth, and paying dividends while generating net losses is unsustainable without the merger-driven earnings improvement. The 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately -5% reflects the structural challenge of operating in a cyclically exposed commodity chemicals business.
