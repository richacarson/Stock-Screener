Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Household & Personal Products ·NASDAQ ·United States
Olaplex Holdings, Inc. develops, manufactures, and sells bond-building hair care products globally. Its patented bis-aminopropyl diglycol dimaleate molecule repairs broken disulfide bonds in hair. Products are sold through professional salons, specialty retail (Sephora, Ulta), and direct-to-consumer channels. Founded in 2014, headquartered in Calabasas, California. Delisted from NASDAQ on July 7, 2026 following completion of $1.4B all-cash acquisition by Henkel AG at $2.06 per share.
278 Employees ·www.olaplex.com

Investment Thesis
Olaplex reported Q1 2026 revenue of $99.4M (+2.5% YoY) with gross margin of 72.1%, showing stabilization after three consecutive years of revenue decline from the $599M 2021 peak [1]. The Professional channel grew 12.3% and DTC grew 13.8%, partially offset by a 13.3% decline in specialty retail. Balance sheet carries $326.2M cash against $352.5M debt, and R&D investment of ~$32M annually preserves the company's science-led differentiation in bond-building hair care. The Henkel acquisition at $2.06/share ($1.4B total, 55% premium to pre-announcement price) provides certainty of value for shareholders and access to Henkel's global distribution and R&D infrastructure for the brand's next growth phase [1].
The fundamental bear case materialized: Olaplex's bond-building technology was commoditized faster than expected, with competitors like K18 capturing share while product liability lawsuits over hair loss and 'Made in USA' mislabeling destroyed brand equity and consumer trust [3]. The $47.5M securities class action settlement confirmed that IPO disclosures understated regulatory and product safety risks. Revenue contracted 29% from peak, profit margins turned negative with Q1 2026 net loss of $5.3M, and SG&A surged 37.4% on turnaround spending without commensurate revenue recovery [1]. The company's 278-person workforce operates in a low-trust culture (Glassdoor 2.7/5.0), and the Henkel buyout at a 95% discount to IPO-era valuations represents the market's verdict on the standalone franchise [2].
