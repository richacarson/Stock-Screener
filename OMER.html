Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Omeros is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company founded and led for over 31 years by Chairman, CEO, and President Gregory A. Demopulos, focused on first-in-class protein and small-molecule therapeutics for complement-mediated diseases. Its lead product, YARTEMLEA (narsoplimab-wuug), a first-in-class MASP-2 inhibitor, was FDA-approved in December 2025 for hematopoietic stem cell transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA) — a condition with no other approved treatment — and generated $28.5 million of net product revenue in Q2 2026, up from $9.9 million in Q1 2026. A permanent reimbursement J-code and CMS New Technology Add-on Payment status took effect in 2026, but in June 2026 the European Medicines Agency's CHMP adopted a negative opinion on narsoplimab's EU marketing authorization, triggering a sharp stock decline and a subsequent securities-fraud investigation into the company's related investor disclosures.

Investment Thesis
Omeros has crossed the hardest threshold in biopharma — regulatory approval and commercial launch of a first-in-class drug — with YARTEMLEA (narsoplimab) generating $28.5 million of net product revenue in Q2 2026, up 188% sequentially from $9.9 million in Q1, and roughly 231% above the $8.6 million of pre-launch royalty revenue in Q2 2025 [1]. As the only FDA-approved treatment for TA-TMA, a serious complication of stem-cell transplantation with high mortality and previously no approved therapy, YARTEMLEA holds a de facto monopoly position, and the July 2026 activation of a permanent reimbursement J-code plus CMS New Technology Add-on Payment status (effective October 2026) should further expand hospital-level access and adoption [1]. Founder-CEO Gregory Demopulos's three-decade, single-founder persistence through years of regulatory setbacks to reach this point is a genuine demonstration of long-term conviction that Sinek would recognize as courage to lead.
The bear case centers on financial fragility, governance risk, and execution uncertainty despite the approval win. Omeros carries a history of negative stockholders' equity from accumulated losses and $40-70 million of convertible notes due 2029, and its recently reported TTM net income appears materially inflated by a non-recurring gain unrelated to core operations, masking the fact that underlying profitability remains unproven even after the launch [4][5]. The June 2026 CHMP negative opinion on narsoplimab's EU marketing authorization triggered a 19% single-day stock decline and a Pomerantz LLP securities-fraud investigation into whether Omeros misled investors about the drug's European approval prospects — an unresolved legal overhang that could result in material liability or reputational damage regardless of outcome [3]. With only a single approved product driving the current re-rating and net insider selling by multiple executives following the post-earnings rally, the stock's durability depends heavily on continued U.S. commercial execution and successful resolution of the EU regulatory setback.
Key Catalysts
  1. CMS New Technology Add-on Payment (NTAP) status for YARTEMLEA becomes effective for hospital reimbursement in October 2026, which should meaningfully expand inpatient adoption beyond the initial launch cohort [1]
  2. Potential reversal of the EMA's negative opinion through the requested re-examination process would reopen the European market opportunity for narsoplimab and could resolve part of the securities-fraud overhang [1][3]
  3. Continued sequential product revenue growth (188% Q1-to-Q2 2026) as YARTEMLEA penetrates the U.S. transplant-center market could drive further upward analyst revisions and multiple re-rating from the current 20.6x forward P/E [1]
Key Risks
