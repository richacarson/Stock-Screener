Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Footwear & Accessories ·NYSE ·Switzerland
On Holding, founded in 2010 in Zurich, designs and distributes performance running footwear, apparel, and accessories under the On brand, sold through wholesale, direct-to-consumer, owned retail, and e-commerce channels globally. The company reported Q2 2026 net sales of CHF 850.3 million (+21.6% constant currency) with a record 65.4% gross margin, and returned to a founder-led Co-CEO structure (David Allemann and Caspar Coppetti) in March 2026 after Martin Hoffmann's 13-year tenure as CEO [1][2]. On is investing heavily in its robotic LightSpray manufacturing technology, including a dedicated South Korea factory, while deliberately managing wholesale sell-in to protect the launch runway for upcoming product innovations.

Investment Thesis
On Holding combines a rare growth-plus-value profile: Q2 2026 constant-currency revenue growth of 21.6% and a record 65.4% gross margin, alongside FY2026 EBITDA margin guidance of 19.5-20.0%, against a Forward P/E of just 13.77x and a Forward PEG of 0.56 [1]. ROE of 23.95% and D/E of only 29.4% reflect a lean, well-capitalized balance sheet, and the March 2026 return of founders David Allemann and Caspar Coppetti as Co-CEOs reconnects strategic vision with execution. The LightSpray robotic manufacturing platform, now scaling through a dedicated South Korea factory, is a genuine process innovation that could expand margins further as adoption scales, and management's willingness to absorb steep U.S. tariffs (40% Vietnam, 39% Indonesia) through premium product innovation rather than margin-compromising cost cuts signals long-term brand discipline [2].
The bear case starts with the market's own verdict: shares fell 19% after Q2 2026 earnings despite the strong headline growth, reflecting concern that management's deliberate wholesale sell-in deceleration signals a slower near-term growth trajectory than previously modeled, and reported YoY revenue growth of 13.5% (USD-reported) trails the 21.6% constant-currency figure, exposing meaningful FX sensitivity [1]. Inspiration evidence is thin — no community-impact, retention, or insider-buying data was found to support the mission floor score, and the company's infinite-game credentials remain unproven at scale given its 2021 IPO and limited operating history through a full economic cycle. Tariff exposure (40%/39% on key manufacturing geographies) is a structural cost headwind that must be continuously offset through premium pricing and innovation, a strategy that works only as long as consumer demand for higher-priced product persists [2]. AI Resilience is capped in the MODERATE band because no AI-specific revenue or efficiency gain was identified, leaving upside from automation/AI investment unrealized in current disclosures.
