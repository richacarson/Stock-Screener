Onterris, Inc. (formerly Montrose Environmental Group, rebranded April 2026) operates as a global environmental solutions company providing assessment, permitting, emergency response, environmental testing, laboratory services, and remediation. The company serves oil and gas, utilities, government, industrial manufacturing, and renewable energy across the U.S., Australia, Canada, and internationally. Q2 2026 revenue was $186.7M with EBITDA margin expanding to 17.1%, but management lowered FY2026 guidance to $740M–$790M revenue and $117M–$120M adjusted EBITDA, citing weaker pass-through, emergency response, and regulatory waivers [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Onterris at $13.28 (down 37% from August highs) is an environmental services platform positioned to benefit from secular regulatory tailwinds including PFAS remediation mandates, methane emission controls, and tightening environmental standards. The forward P/E of 8.19x represents deep value if the company can deliver on its FY2026 guidance of $740M–$790M revenue and $117M–$120M adjusted EBITDA with margins expanding to ~15% [1][2]. Q2 2026 EBITDA margin of 17.1% demonstrated operational leverage, and the patent-pending VeriPlume Capture System positions the company at the intersection of environmental regulation and clean technology [3]. Insider buying of $619K by Director Graham and CFO Dicks at $15–$17 provides a conviction signal, with zero insider sales, and the SBTi-validated net-zero goals embed environmental purpose into the business model [4][5]. Physical environmental services are structurally immune to AI disruption.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability and deteriorating fundamentals. Management cut FY2026 revenue guidance by $100M at the midpoint (to $740M–$790M from $840M–$900M), reflecting weaker emergency response demand, regulatory waivers delaying air-testing work, and reduced pass-through revenue [2]. The stock's 37% decline from $20.95 to $13.28 has rendered insider purchases at $15–$17 underwater. ROE of -2.39% and profit margin of -1.44% reflect continued GAAP losses despite $773M in TTM revenue, and the company carries $321M in debt with only $10M cash at a 2.8x leverage ratio [1][2]. Revenue growth of -20.4% YoY and earnings decline of -90.9% underscore the severity of the organic growth challenge once emergency response and acquisition contributions are stripped out. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 15% was driven entirely by acquisitions, and the acquisition pipeline is constrained by leverage. With no dividend and negative ROE, the investment case depends entirely on the company proving its environmental services platform can convert $800M+ in revenue into meaningful bottom-line profitability — a feat it has not yet accomplished in over a decade of operations [5].
