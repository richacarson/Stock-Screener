Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NASDAQ ·United States
Option Care Health is the largest independent home and alternate site infusion services provider in the United States, delivering complex pharmaceutical therapies including immunoglobulin, anti-infective, nutrition support, bleeding disorder, and other specialty infusion treatments through a national network of pharmacies and infusion suites. The company served over 315,000 unique patients in 2025 and reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.44B (+1.9% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.35. Headquartered in Bannockburn, Illinois.

Investment Thesis
Option Care Health is the largest independent home infusion provider in the U.S. with ~$5.7B in annual revenue, now trading at an attractive 11.5x forward P/E after recovering from its early-2026 guidance cut [6]. Q2 2026 showed improving execution: revenue of $1.44B grew 1.9% YoY (accelerating from Q1's 1.3%), EPS of $0.35, and management maintained full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance of $480-505M and narrowed EPS to $1.85-1.92 [1][2]. The secular site-of-care shift — payers pushing infusion therapy from hospitals ($300+/hour) to home settings ($100-150/hour) — provides a structural tailwind with multiple years of runway. ROE of 16.0% demonstrates capital efficiency, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of 10.5% shows sustained growth execution despite the current CID headwind [6]. Acute therapies growing high-single digits offset chronic portfolio pressure, and the Palantir AI partnership positions the company for operational optimization. Director insider buying of approximately $1.6M in August 2025 signals board conviction [4].
The bear case centers on thin margins, decelerating growth, and structural business model constraints. Revenue growth of 1.9% in Q2 2026 — while improving from Q1's 1.3% — represents a dramatic deceleration from FY2025's 13%, driven by the chronic inflammatory disease portfolio headwind expected to reduce gross profit by ~$55M in 2026 [1]. Profit margin of just 3.68% leaves almost no room for execution errors — drug pricing pressure, payer reimbursement cuts, or supply chain disruptions could quickly compress earnings [6]. D/E of 100.6% is elevated for a thin-margin healthcare services business, creating balance sheet vulnerability if earnings deteriorate. Employee reviews reveal mixed culture signals including 'high turnover' and concerns about a 'bottom-line mentality replacing patient-centered focus,' suggesting the company may be losing the very purpose that differentiates it [4]. Competition from CVS Coram and UnitedHealth's Optum brings scale advantages that could compress reimbursement rates. The Inspire Impact Score of 41 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors, but the narrow margin profile limits the quality of the financial story.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Home and alternate site infusion services are fundamentally physical-world healthcare delivery — registered nurses travel to patients' homes, mix and administer IV medications, monitor for adverse reactions, and manage complex therapy protocols [1][3]. AI cannot replace the hands-on clinical work of infusion nursing, the cold-chain pharmacy logistics, or the regulatory compliance (state pharmacy licensing, DEA-controlled substance handling) that underpin the business. The site-of-care shift trend from hospital to home is structurally driven by payer cost pressures and patient preference, not technology substitution. The 10-K acknowledges AI integration as an imperative but frames it as an efficiency opportunity, not a disruption threat [5].
