Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology & Diagnostics ·NASDAQ ·United States
OPKO Health is a diversified healthcare company engaged in diagnostics and pharmaceuticals, operating in more than 30 countries. Subsidiaries include BioReference Health (clinical laboratory testing), OPKO Biologics (pharmaceutical development), and ModeX Therapeutics (multi-specific antibody platform). The company raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $560-585M. Q2 2026 revenue of $163.6M grew 4.3% YoY with net loss narrowing 94% to $0.011/share. Led by Chairman and CEO Dr. Phillip Frost since 2007. Market cap approximately $1.04B.

Investment Thesis
OPKO Health's bull case rests on a potential profitability inflection — Q2 2026 net loss narrowed 94% to just $0.011/share from $0.19/share a year earlier, and management raised 2026 revenue guidance to $560-585M [1]. The Regeneron collaboration ($1B+ potential with $200M+ milestones per program) validates the ModeX multi-specific antibody platform. Dr. Frost's 31.23% personal ownership signals genuine alignment, and at $1.04B market cap the stock trades at less than 2x guided revenue, providing significant upside if pipeline programs succeed or the company reaches profitability [4]. D/E of only 34% provides balance sheet flexibility, and the Inspire Impact Score of 37 (ALIGNED) with nine positive attributions makes this accessible to faith-based healthcare investors.
The bear case remains compelling despite Q2 improvement. OPKO has been a perpetual value-destruction story — the stock trades at $1.39 after years of decline, and TTM losses of $225.68M at a -37.19% profit margin continue to erode the balance sheet [4]. Full-year 2026 revenue is still expected to decline approximately 5% from FY2025, continuing a multi-year contraction from $713M in FY2024 driven by the BioReference oncology divestiture and post-COVID volume normalization [1]. The 2018 SEC lawsuit settlement (naming both OPKO and CEO Frost) and a $704K False Claims Act settlement create persistent governance concerns [3]. The diversified conglomerate structure lacks strategic focus, spreading limited resources across diagnostics and pharma without achieving market leadership in either. R&D spending of $125-135M annually against a shrinking revenue base threatens long-term viability if pipeline milestones are not achieved.
