Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·United States
Optimum Communications (formerly Altice USA, rebranded November 2025) is one of the largest broadband communications providers in the United States, delivering broadband, video, mobile, and voice services to approximately 4.4 million residential and business customers across 21 states. Controlled by French billionaire Patrick Drahi through dual-class share structure. Operates the Lightpath fiber-based B2B infrastructure business. The stock has declined 65% over the past year from $2.79 to $0.96, reflecting a market cap of just $379M against $26B in debt.

Investment Thesis
Optimum Communications is a deeply distressed broadband incumbent whose financial and operational deterioration has accelerated. TTM revenue of $8.38B declined 4.4% with a catastrophic net loss of -$4.88B (-$10.52 EPS) and -58.23% profit margin [1]. The stock has fallen to $0.96 — down 65% from its 52-week high of $2.79 and down over 97% from its 2021 peak — reflecting a market cap of just $379M against approximately $26B in total debt and negative stockholders' equity [1][3]. The company carries a Debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 8.55x with a current ratio of 0.32, indicating severe liquidity constraints. Lightpath fiber B2B and Optimum Mobile provide pockets of growth, but they are too small to offset the structural decline in legacy cable subscribers facing fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless competition.
The bull case requires a successful debt restructuring that preserves equity value, stabilization of broadband subscriber trends, and Lightpath/mobile growth acceleration. AI-driven data consumption growth is a secular tailwind for broadband infrastructure. However, Patrick Drahi's controversial asset transfers have escalated the creditor conflict to active litigation, raising restructuring risk that could wipe out the equity entirely [2]. Revenue has declined every year since FY2021 (from $10.1B to $8.4B TTM), with losses deepening dramatically from -$103M in FY2024 to -$4.88B TTM [1]. Against fiber overbuilders with superior unit economics (Verizon Fios, AT&T Fiber) and fixed wireless competitors with zero last-mile capex (T-Mobile, Starlink), OPTU's structural disadvantages are deepening. Analyst consensus is Sell with a $1.06 price target (+10% upside), reflecting minimal conviction in equity recovery [3]. This is a highly leveraged equity stub on a telecom business in secular decline — SELL.
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful debt restructuring or refinancing that reduces the $26B burden and removes near-term balance-sheet risk without wiping out equity holders — any resolution preserving equity value could drive significant upside from $0.96 [2]
  2. Lightpath fiber B2B revenue acceleration and Optimum Mobile subscriber growth offsetting legacy cable broadband subscriber losses and stabilizing the overall revenue trajectory [1]
  3. AI and data-center demand driving increased broadband consumption — a secular tailwind for infrastructure providers that could improve ARPU and slow subscriber churn
Key Risks
  1. Equity wipeout risk from debt restructuring — with negative stockholders' equity, $26B debt, Debt/EBITDA of 8.55x, and current ratio of 0.32, a bankruptcy filing or adverse creditor settlement could eliminate all equity value [1][3]
  2. Escalating creditor conflict — controversial asset transfers and antitrust lawsuit against major creditors (Apollo, Ares, BlackRock) have created unprecedented legal complexity that could delay resolution and increase costs [2]
  3. Structural competitive decline accelerating — fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless entrants continue eroding broadband share while the $26B debt burden prevents OPTU from investing in competitive fiber upgrades at the pace needed to stem subscriber losses [1][2]
