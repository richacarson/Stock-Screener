Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYSE ·Canada
OR Royalties Inc. (formerly Osisko Gold Royalties, rebranded May 2025) acquires and manages precious metal royalties, streams, and other interests in mining operations primarily in Canada and internationally. Its primary asset is a 3-5% net smelter return royalty on the Canadian Malartic complex. The company holds 185+ royalties and streams, with pending acquisitions from Gold Fields Limited and Sailfish Royalty Corp. Founded in 2014, headquartered in Montreal, Canada.

Investment Thesis
OR Royalties offers one of the cleanest financial profiles in the mining sector — estimated 70% profit margins, 97% cash margins on Q1 2026 revenue, minimal debt (D/E ~8%), and extraordinary operating leverage to gold prices [1]. Q1 2026 revenue of $103M (+87% YoY) with 97% cash margins demonstrates the royalty model's scalability when gold is at $4,873/oz, implying a $400M+ annual revenue run rate [1]. The five-year plan targeting 110,000-125,000 GEOs by 2029 provides organic production growth independent of commodity prices, and pending Gold Fields and Sailfish acquisitions add near-term catalysts [1]. The fortress balance sheet and low ~20% cash payout ratio give management flexibility for accretive acquisitions during mining industry distress [4][6]. At 20.95x forward P/E with 87% revenue growth and industry-leading ESG credentials (Sustainalytics Top-Rated, MSCI AA), OR represents a premium gold exposure vehicle with downside protection from the zero operating cost structure [3][5].
The bear case centers on gold price dependency and leadership uncertainty. OR Royalties' Q1 2026 revenue surge is primarily driven by gold at $4,873/oz — a historical extreme that may not persist [1]. A gold price correction to $3,000/oz would compress revenue by ~40% and earnings by a larger percentage. The trailing P/E of 38.3x reflects current gold euphoria and could contract sharply in a risk-on environment [5]. The CEO succession has been turbulent — founder Roosen departed 2023, successor Sandeep Singh's 'unexpected departure' was flagged by analysts as negative, and Jason Attew (appointed January 2024) has yet to execute a transformative deal [2]. The stock's 28% decline from its April 2026 level of ~$40 to $29.20 despite record revenue suggests the market is pricing in gold price normalization risk. Dividend yield of 0.9% provides minimal income support, and ROE of approximately 10% underperforms the 15% target due to the large capitalized equity base from royalty acquisitions [4][5].
