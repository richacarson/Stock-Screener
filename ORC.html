Investment Thesis
ORC offers a headline 18% annualized dividend yield ($0.10/month, $1.20 annualized) that continues to attract income-seeking investors, and Q2 2026 demonstrated the earnings power of the portfolio with EPS of $0.44 and a 6.2% total return [1]. Book value per share recovered to $7.22, and the stock at $6.70 trades at a 7.2% discount to book value. The $11.4B Agency RMBS portfolio carries implicit government guarantees eliminating credit risk. Forward P/E of 5.32x provides apparent deep value [3]. Any yield curve steepening or mortgage spread tightening would benefit the leveraged portfolio, and CEO Cauley's 16-year tenure provides management continuity.
The bear case remains dominant despite Q2's recovery. The Q1 2026 net loss followed by Q2's strong quarter underscores the extreme earnings volatility inherent in a leveraged mREIT operating at 7.9:1 leverage [1]. The April 2026 dividend cut from $0.12 to $0.10 continues the chronic pattern of distribution reductions (from $0.14 peak to $0.04 trough), with 5-year dividend growth CAGR of -18.66% [3]. Massive share dilution via the ATM equity program has ballooned the share count from 13.4M (2020) to 126.7M+ by 2025, a 9.5x increase that has destroyed per-share value even as the aggregate portfolio grew [2]. The externally managed structure pays fees to Bimini Advisors based on assets under management, creating incentives to grow the portfolio through dilutive equity issuance regardless of shareholder returns. With zero Innovation, zero Inspiration, and a deeply finite mindset, ORC represents a classic yield trap where the high headline distribution masks chronic book value erosion and per-share value destruction.
Key Catalysts
  1. Yield curve steepening or continued Fed rate cuts could widen net interest margins, supporting earnings stability and the $0.10/month dividend at current levels — Q2 2026's strong EPS of $0.44 shows the portfolio's upside potential in favorable rate environments [1]

  3. Portfolio growth to $11.4B in average fair value provides larger asset base for income generation, potentially supporting dividend maintenance at the reduced $0.10 level [1]
Key Risks
  1. Yield trap dynamics — 18% headline yield masks chronic book value destruction, 5-year dividend growth CAGR of -18.66%, share count dilution of 9.5x since 2020, and a history of multiple dividend cuts from $0.14 to $0.04 to $0.12 to $0.10 [1][3]
  2. Interest rate risk amplified by 7.9:1 leverage — a 100bps rate spike or mortgage spread widening could materially impair book value, as demonstrated by Q1 2026's net loss that reversed $103.4M in Q4 2025 income within one quarter [1]
  3. External management structure (Bimini Advisors) creates misaligned incentives — management fees based on AUM incentivize continued dilutive equity issuance and portfolio growth regardless of per-share returns, fundamentally undermining long-term shareholder value [2]
