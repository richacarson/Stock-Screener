Company Profile
Industrials ·Engineering & Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Orion Group Holdings, Inc. is a leading specialty construction company operating through two segments: Marine (shoreline protection, jetties, breakwaters, dredging, marine transportation) and Concrete (structural concrete, site work, data center construction). The company acquired J.E. McAmis to strengthen marine capabilities. FY2025 revenue was $852.3M with $27B in pursuit pipeline. Founded in 1994, headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Investment Thesis
Orion Group Holdings is strategically positioned at the intersection of two secular growth themes: marine infrastructure investment (USACE-funded shoreline protection, beneficial use projects) and AI-driven data center construction, which powered 30%+ Concrete segment growth and 45% adjusted EBITDA growth in Q2 2026 [1][2]. FY2025 revenue of $852.3M with FY2026 guidance of $900-950M reflects mid-single-digit top-line growth, and the $27B pursuit pipeline with $1.6B in projects quoted awaiting award provides strong forward visibility [1]. The D/E ratio of 67.2% is well-managed, and Q2 2026 bookings of $275M+ (1.25x book-to-bill) signal backlog recovery after FY2025's 12% decline. At ~$410M market cap (~0.45x revenue), the stock is cheaply valued if margins normalize.
The bear case is structural profitability and execution risk. FY2025 GAAP net income of $2.5M on $852M revenue (0.29% margin) is barely breakeven, and Q2 2026 produced a net loss of $4.1M despite 8% revenue growth, driven by marine segment timing issues [2]. The Adjusted EBITDA margin of ~5.3% offers minimal buffer against cost overruns, weather delays, or project disputes common in construction. Interest coverage of just 1.3x means debt service consumes nearly all operating income [6]. Backlog declined 12% in FY2025 ($729M to $640M) before Q2 2026's recovery — if marine project awards continue to slip, the revenue pipeline could thin further. The stock has declined ~32% from its June 2026 levels following the Q2 miss, and the Forward P/E of ~40x (on adjusted earnings) is expensive for a construction company with sub-1% GAAP margins.
Key Catalysts
  1. Data center construction boom driving continued 30%+ Concrete segment growth as hyperscalers accelerate AI infrastructure buildout through 2027-2028
  2. Marine segment recovery as $1.6B in quoted projects convert to awards, particularly USACE shoreline protection and beneficial use infrastructure projects
  3. Margin normalization from the McAmis acquisition integration and marine project ramp-up, potentially lifting GAAP margins from sub-1% toward the 2-3% range typical of specialty construction
Key Risks
  1. Structural profitability — 0.29% GAAP net margin and 1.3x interest coverage leave zero margin of safety for project cost overruns, weather delays, or award timing slippage that are endemic to construction
  2. Marine segment execution risk: Q2 2026 marine revenue and profitability declined due to project award and startup timing, and if large projects continue to slip, the segment could drag consolidated results below guidance
  3. Backlog quality uncertainty — while the $27B pursuit pipeline is large, backlog declined 12% in FY2025 and the Q2 2026 recovery (1.25x book-to-bill) needs to be sustained to support $900-950M revenue guidance
