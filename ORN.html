Company Profile
Industrials ·Engineering & Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Orion Group Holdings, Inc. is a specialty construction company operating through two segments: Marine (shoreline protection, jetties, breakwaters, dredging, marine transportation) and Concrete (structural concrete, site work, data center construction). The company acquired J.E. McAmis to strengthen marine capabilities. Q2 2026 revenue of $221.9M brought TTM revenue to approximately $896.5M. FY2026 guidance of $900-950M revenue and $50-54M Adjusted EBITDA was maintained. The concrete segment is benefiting from AI data center construction demand with 30%+ revenue growth. $27B pursuit pipeline provides long-term visibility. Market cap approximately $340M with 2,076 employees. Headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Investment Thesis
Orion Group Holdings is positioned at the intersection of two secular growth themes: marine infrastructure investment (USACE-funded shoreline protection, beneficial use projects) and AI-driven data center construction, which powered 30%+ Concrete segment growth and 45% adjusted EBITDA growth in Q2 2026 [1][2]. FY2026 guidance of $900-950M revenue (9% growth at midpoint) with $50-54M Adjusted EBITDA (15% growth at midpoint) demonstrates improving operating leverage [4]. The $27B pursuit pipeline provides long-term visibility, and Q2 2026 bookings of $275M+ (1.25x book-to-bill) signal backlog recovery after FY2025's 12% decline. At $340M market cap (~0.38x revenue), the stock is cheaply valued relative to specialty construction peers, with Forward P/E of 18.69x below the 20x target and offering potential re-rating as profitability normalizes.
The bear case centers on structural profitability and balance sheet risk. FY2025 GAAP net income of just $2.5M on $852M revenue (0.29% margin) is barely breakeven, and Q2 2026 produced a $4.1M net loss despite 8% revenue growth due to marine segment timing issues [2]. D/E has increased to 98.9% (from 67.2% previously), and total debt of $162.7M against only $2.5M cash creates liquidity tightness [6]. The Forward PEG of 28.96 reflects negligible earnings growth relative to the trailing P/E, suggesting the market sees limited profitability improvement ahead. Revenue growth has decelerated from 15% in Q1 to 8.1% in Q2, and the marine segment's project timing dependency creates quarterly volatility. Free cash flow was -$20.9M TTM, meaning the company is consuming cash despite near-breakeven profitability. The stock has declined ~20% from June 2026 levels following the Q2 miss, and any further marine project delays or data center construction slowdown could pressure results below guidance.
Key Catalysts
  1. Data center construction boom driving continued 30%+ Concrete segment growth as hyperscalers accelerate AI infrastructure buildout — the $27B pursuit pipeline includes significant data center opportunities through 2027-2028 [1][2]
  2. Marine segment recovery as quoted projects convert to awards, particularly USACE shoreline protection and beneficial use infrastructure projects that provide multi-year revenue visibility [4]
  3. Margin normalization from McAmis integration, marine project ramp-up, and operating leverage on growing revenue base, potentially lifting GAAP margins from sub-1% toward the 2-3% range typical of specialty E&C [1]
Key Risks
  1. Structural profitability crisis — 0.4% GAAP net margin, 98.9% D/E, $2.5M cash, and -$20.9M free cash flow leave zero margin of safety for cost overruns, weather delays, or project timing slippage endemic to construction [2][6]
  2. Marine segment execution risk: Q2 2026 marine revenue and profitability declined due to project start-up timing and lower equipment utilization; continued slippage in large project awards would drag consolidated results below $900M revenue guidance [2]
  3. Balance sheet deterioration with D/E rising to 98.9% and total debt of $162.7M against only $2.5M cash; any working capital squeeze or delayed project payments could force dilutive capital raises at depressed valuation [6]
