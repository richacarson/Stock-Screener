Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NMS ·United States
OneStream was a cloud-native enterprise corporate performance management (CPM) platform providing financial consolidation, planning, reporting, and AI-driven analytics for enterprise finance teams. Recognized as a Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader for Financial Planning Software three consecutive years, the company's 'Intelligent Finance Platform' featured deterministic AI for forecasting and scenario planning [3]. FY2025 revenue reached $601.9M (up 23% YoY) with subscription revenue growing 27%, though the company remained GAAP unprofitable with a net loss of ~$50.3M [1]. OneStream was taken private by Hg Capital in April 2026 at $24/share ($6.4B equity value) and is no longer publicly traded [2]. Founded in 2010 and formerly headquartered in Birmingham, Michigan.

Investment Thesis
OneStream was a high-growth enterprise CPM platform delivering 23% revenue growth to $601.9M with subscription revenue up 27%, recognized as a Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader for three consecutive years, and positioned at the intersection of AI and enterprise finance transformation [1][3]. The company's deterministic AI approach embedded directly into finance workflows created genuine product differentiation against legacy incumbents Oracle, SAP, and Workday. However, OneStream was taken private by Hg Capital in April 2026 at $24/share ($6.4B equity value) and is no longer available as a public equity investment [2]. CEO Tom Shea stated the take-private would 'accelerate the AI strategy' for the company's finance platform, suggesting the long-term competitive dynamics favored private ownership with longer investment horizons.
The fundamental bear case that led to a modest public-market valuation centered on profitability and competitive risk. OneStream posted a GAAP net loss of ~$50.3M in FY2025 despite $602M in revenue, with heavy AI and go-to-market investment delaying the path to profitability that public investors demanded [1]. The adjusted EPS of approximately $0.48 (based on Q4 2025 run rate) implied a take-private valuation of ~50x adjusted earnings, pricing in substantial future growth. Competitive risk from Microsoft Copilot commoditizing basic finance planning, Oracle/SAP embedding AI into existing ERP modules, and AI-native startups targeting specific CPM use cases created strategic uncertainty [3]. The company's decision to go private after just 18 months as a public company — IPO'd mid-2024, taken private April 2026 — suggests the public market's quarterly scrutiny and the AI investment timeline were incompatible.
