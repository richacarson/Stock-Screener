Investment Thesis
Oscar Health at $33.47 is executing a remarkable profitability inflection — H1 2026 generated $1.1B in earnings from operations and $1B in net income on revenue of $9.53B (up 61% YoY), prompting management to raise full-year EFO guidance by $250M to $500-700M [1]. Q2 2026 revenue of $4.9B (+70% YoY) and membership of 3.0M (+46%) demonstrate both top-line momentum and pricing discipline, with MLR improving 12 percentage points YoY to 79.2% [1]. The 20+ deployed AI models driving a 1,660 bps operating cost reduction represent a structural advantage over legacy carriers, and the +Oscar technology licensing extends the growth runway beyond insurance underwriting [2]. At 15.1x forward earnings with revenue growing 70%, the valuation is attractive relative to health insurance peers trading at 18-22x forward. Inspire ALIGNED score (31) is favorable for faith-based investors.
The primary risk is earnings sustainability and regulatory concentration. The EFO guidance of $500-700M for the full year, despite H1 already delivering $1.1B, suggests management expects material H2 compression — and MLR guidance of 81.5-82.5% implies margin deterioration from Q2's 79.2% [1]. Trailing profit margin of just 3.6% leaves minimal buffer against medical cost inflation running at 7-8% annually, and the ACA's 80% MLR floor structurally caps upside. Oscar derives virtually all revenue from the ACA individual marketplace, making it acutely vulnerable to subsidy changes — enhanced premium subsidies face expiration risk, which could reduce enrollment by millions nationally and disproportionately impact Oscar's membership base [4]. Co-founder/CTO Mario Schlosser's June 2026 transition to advisory role raises questions about technology leadership continuity during the critical period when AI differentiation matters most [2]. Competition from UnitedHealth, Elevance, and CVS/Aetna intensifies as incumbents accelerate digital transformation with deeper data moats.
