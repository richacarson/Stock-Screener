Open Text Corporation designs, develops, and sells information management software and solutions globally, including content management, business network, cybersecurity, AI, and IT operations management. Following the $5.8B Micro Focus acquisition in 2023 and subsequent divestitures (including Vertica for $150M in 2026), OpenText is repositioning around cloud and 'Information Management for AI' under new CEO Ayman Antoun, a former IBM Americas President who took the helm on April 20, 2026.

Investment Thesis
OpenText trades at a striking 5.35x forward P/E and 1.06 PEG, an extreme discount for a company generating 34% adjusted EBITDA margin on a ~$6B FY2026 revenue run-rate, with Q3 FY2026 GAAP net income up 86% and cloud revenue extending its streak to 21 consecutive quarters of organic growth (content cloud +22%) [1][3]. New CEO Ayman Antoun, a 35-year technology veteran and former President of IBM Americas, took over April 20, 2026 with an explicit mandate to reposition OpenText's trusted enterprise data as core infrastructure for training agentic AI systems, and the Aviator AI suite is already live across every business pillar, contributing to cloud bookings growth exceeding 15% annually [2][3]. The 4.73% dividend yield, backed by roughly 13 consecutive years of payments and a 6% five-year dividend growth CAGR, provides income support while the turnaround plays out [1].
The bear case remains substantial. Glassdoor culture ratings of 3.2/5 describe an 'awful' environment with extreme micromanagement (tracking badge swipes, monitoring employee chats), a legacy of the prior CEO's centralized control that a few months of new leadership has not yet remediated [2]. TTM revenue is roughly flat (-0.2% YoY) and D/E of 159% from the Micro Focus acquisition constrains financial flexibility even as forward multiples compress further [1]. The AI repositioning is strategically coherent but unproven — OpenText's history of growth-by-acquisition rather than organic R&D investment raises real questions about whether Aviator can out-innovate cloud-native competitors like Microsoft Copilot before legacy content-management workflows are commoditized [2]. A new, largely unproven CEO adds near-term execution uncertainty on top of these structural questions.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued execution under new CEO Ayman Antoun validating the 'foundation for agentic AI' strategic repositioning, potentially closing the gap between the 5.35x forward P/E and peer enterprise software multiples
  2. Sustained cloud bookings growth above 15% annually, tied to Aviator AI adoption across content, experience, business network, and cybersecurity product lines
  3. Culture and leadership stabilization under Antoun that improves employee sentiment from the current 3.2/5 Glassdoor rating, supporting execution on the new AI strategy
Key Risks
  1. Unproven new CEO — Ayman Antoun took over only in April 2026 with no established track record at OpenText, adding execution uncertainty to an already complex strategic pivot
  2. AI disruption to legacy content management and information governance workflows from Microsoft Copilot, Google Workspace AI, and AI-native document tools that could commoditize OpenText's core value proposition faster than Aviator can differentiate it
  3. Deep-rooted culture problems (3.2/5 Glassdoor rating, reports of extreme micromanagement and employee surveillance) that predate the CEO transition and could undermine execution of the new AI strategy
