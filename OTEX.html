Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Open Text Corporation designs, develops, and sells information management software and solutions globally, including content management, business network, cybersecurity, AI (Aviator suite), and IT operations management. Following the $5.8B Micro Focus acquisition in 2023 and subsequent divestitures, OpenText is repositioning around cloud and 'Information Management for AI' under new CEO Ayman Antoun (former IBM Americas President, appointed April 20, 2026), who replaced Mark Barrenechea after 13 years. FY2026 (ended June 2026) revenue was $5.246B (+1.5% YoY), cloud revenue reached $1.959B (+5.5%), and GAAP net income jumped 47.5% to $643M. The company returned a record $677M to shareholders and raised its dividend 5%.

Investment Thesis
OpenText at 5.91x forward earnings is the cheapest enterprise software company of scale in the market, trading at a massive discount to peers while delivering improving profitability — FY2026 GAAP net income surged 47.5% to $643M ($2.58 EPS) on $5.246B revenue, adjusted EBITDA reached $1.903B (36.3% margin), and the company returned a record $677M to shareholders ($268M dividends + $409M buybacks) while raising the dividend 5% [1]. Cloud revenue of $1.959B (+5.5%) marked 21 consecutive quarters of organic cloud growth, and Aviator AI is deployed across all business pillars driving cloud bookings growth above 15% [2]. New CEO Ayman Antoun, a 35-year technology veteran from IBM, brings operational credibility to the 'foundation for agentic AI' repositioning. The 4.90% dividend yield with 12 consecutive years of growth, a 43.41% payout ratio, and ~6% 5-year dividend CAGR provide strong income support while the AI transformation plays out. ROE improved to 16.19% and D/E declined to 149% as acquisition debt is being methodically paid down [1].
The bear case centers on growth, AI disruption, and culture. Total revenue grew only 1.5% in FY2026 — the 3-year revenue growth forecast is -2.11%, suggesting the market expects on-premise decline to outpace cloud growth, potentially compressing the multiple further rather than re-rating it [1]. Microsoft Copilot embedded in Office 365 and SharePoint represents the most direct AI threat to OpenText's core content management franchise — Microsoft can bundle AI-powered document intelligence at zero marginal cost to its 400M+ commercial Office users, undercutting OpenText's standalone value proposition [2]. Critics note that OpenText's AI strategy may be partly 'rebranding of existing products' rather than breakthrough innovation, and the company's growth-by-acquisition history (rather than organic R&D) raises questions about innovation capacity [3]. Glassdoor culture ratings of 3.2/5 with reports of extreme micromanagement and employee surveillance remain a deep organizational liability that the new CEO has not yet had time to address, creating execution risk for the AI transformation [3]. An Altman Z-Score of 1.35 signals elevated financial distress risk despite the improving profitability metrics.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued cloud revenue acceleration beyond 5.5% growth toward high-single-digit or double-digit organic cloud growth under CEO Antoun, demonstrating the AI repositioning is driving customer adoption and narrowing the gap to the 5.91x forward PE versus enterprise software peer multiples [1][2]
  2. Aviator AI product traction measured by net-new cloud bookings conversion and customer expansion metrics, validating OpenText's 'foundation for agentic AI' positioning as genuine technology differentiation rather than marketing rebranding [2]
  3. Culture transformation under new CEO Antoun that improves the 3.2/5 Glassdoor rating and reduces the micromanagement legacy, enabling talent retention needed to execute the AI strategy [3]
Key Risks
  1. Microsoft Copilot embedded in Office 365 and SharePoint could commoditize core content management workflows at scale — Microsoft's ability to bundle AI-powered document intelligence at zero marginal cost to 400M+ commercial users directly threatens OpenText's standalone value proposition [2]
  2. Organic revenue stagnation — FY2026 total revenue grew only 1.5% and the 3-year forecast is -2.11%, suggesting on-premise declines may outpace cloud growth, creating a sustained top-line drag that the cheap valuation can't overcome [1]
  3. Deep-rooted culture problems (3.2/5 Glassdoor rating, extreme micromanagement, employee surveillance) inherited from the prior CEO's 13-year centralized control model create talent and execution risk for the AI transformation that may take years to remediate [3]
