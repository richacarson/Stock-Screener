Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp. is an externally managed Business Development Company (BDC) focused on direct lending to U.S. technology companies, primarily software businesses. The fund is managed by Blue Owl Capital (founded 2016 by Douglas Ostrover, Marc Lipschultz, and Craig Packer, formerly of GSO/Blackstone, KKR, and Goldman Sachs). OTF merged with Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp. II (OTF II) in March 2025, doubling the portfolio to $12.1B from $6.4B. As of Q2 2026, the portfolio comprised 205 companies at $14.7B fair value with just 0.1% non-accruals. The stock trades at $11.11, a 33% discount to NAV of $16.48, and pays a 10.91% dividend yield. Market cap is approximately $5.2B. Zero direct employees — all functions performed by the external manager.

Investment Thesis
Blue Owl Technology Finance at $11.11 per share trades at a 33% discount to NAV of $16.48, offering a potential value opportunity in a high-quality technology lending platform with 10.91% dividend yield [1][6]. The portfolio of 205 technology companies at $14.7B fair value carries just 0.1% non-accruals, reflecting disciplined credit underwriting by Blue Owl Capital's experienced team (founders from GSO/Blackstone, KKR, Goldman Sachs) [1][4]. Q2 2026 adjusted NII of $0.30 per share supports the $0.40 quarterly dividend (including $0.05 special dividend), and the 67% payout ratio leaves a buffer for dividend coverage [1]. Blue Owl's proactive de-risking of AI-vulnerable software positions demonstrates portfolio management sophistication, and the pivot toward AI infrastructure lending captures the growing private credit opportunity from Big Tech's capital-intensive AI buildout [4]. The conservative 0.93x net debt-to-equity leverage (well below the 2.0x regulatory cap) provides balance sheet flexibility for deal origination. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) with zero negative attributions means it is not excluded by faith-based screens.
The bear case centers on the 33% discount to NAV, which reflects legitimate market concerns about technology credit risk in the AI era. The portfolio's concentration in software company debt creates systemic exposure if AI disrupts traditional SaaS business models — default rates could spike during the 2028 refinancing wave that Blue Owl's CEO has specifically flagged as a risk [4][5]. The externally managed structure creates agency risk: Blue Owl Capital earns management fees on AUM regardless of stock price, incentivizing portfolio growth over quality. ROE of approximately 8% is below the cost of equity for a leveraged credit vehicle, suggesting the fund is not generating adequate returns on its risk profile. The OTF II merger doubled the portfolio but also increased exposure to the same risk factors and may have diluted portfolio quality by combining two separate underwriting tracks. Q2 2026 NII of $0.30 per share declined from Q1's $0.37, suggesting falling interest rates or credit margin compression could pressure future income. The fund's short public history limits the ability to assess performance through a full credit cycle, and private credit broadly faces scrutiny about valuation practices and hidden losses that surface only in stress scenarios [5]. At 0.67x NAV, the market is pricing in meaningful credit losses that have not yet materialized in the 0.1% non-accrual rate.
Key Catalysts
  1. NAV discount closure from 0.67x to 0.85-1.0x as credit quality holds — if the 0.1% non-accrual rate persists through the 2028 refinancing wave and AI disruption proves less severe than feared, the stock could appreciate 25-50% to narrow the discount [1][6]
  2. AI infrastructure lending opportunity as Big Tech's multi-hundred-billion-dollar capital buildout creates demand for private credit financing of data centers, GPU clusters, and supporting infrastructure — Blue Owl's technology focus positions it to capture this deal flow [4]
  3. Interest rate stability or gradual declines improving portfolio company cash flows and reducing default risk, while OTF's predominantly floating-rate loan portfolio continues generating attractive spreads against a slowly declining rate backdrop [1]
Key Risks
  1. Software portfolio credit deterioration as AI disrupts traditional SaaS business models — Blue Owl's CEO has flagged 2028 as a critical refinancing moment, and the 0.1% non-accrual rate may not survive if AI-displaced software companies cannot refinance maturing loans [4][5]
  2. External management structure creates misaligned incentives where Blue Owl Capital earns fees on AUM growth regardless of stock price performance, potentially incentivizing aggressive portfolio expansion over conservative credit selection [4]
  3. NII per share declined from $0.37 (Q1) to $0.30 (Q2), and falling interest rates or credit spread compression could reduce income below the $0.40 quarterly dividend, forcing a dividend cut that would trigger further stock price declines in a yield-dependent shareholder base [1]
