Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Otis Worldwide Corporation manufactures, installs, and services elevators and escalators worldwide, moving approximately 2.4 billion people daily through two segments: New Equipment and Service. Founded in 1853 by Elisha Otis, the inventor of the safety elevator, and spun off from United Technologies (now RTX Corporation) in April 2020. Q2 2026 net sales rose 7% to $3.9B with organic sales up 6%, driven by Service segment strength with 9% organic growth, 24% modernization growth, and double-digit repair growth [1]. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $4.01-$4.05 with $1.6-$1.7B adjusted free cash flow [1]. Headquartered in Farmington, Connecticut.
72,000 Employees ·www.otis.com

Investment Thesis
Otis delivered strong Q2 2026 results with $3.9B in net sales (organic growth of 6%), driven by exceptional Service segment performance — 9% organic revenue growth, 24% modernization growth, and double-digit repair growth [1]. The company's installed base of 2.2 million+ units creates a recurring revenue annuity through maintenance contracts, with Services generating higher margins than New Equipment. Modernization backlog surged 24%, signaling a multi-year upgrade cycle driven partly by AI data center construction demand [2]. Full-year 2026 guidance of adjusted EPS $4.01-$4.05 with $1.6-$1.7B adjusted free cash flow demonstrates resilient cash generation. The dividend was raised 14.7% in April 2026 with a manageable 45% payout ratio, and the WeMaintain acquisition strengthens the technology-enabled service platform [2][4].
The primary concern is Otis's negative shareholders' equity of approximately -$5.4B, a structural artifact of the 2020 spin-off and aggressive buybacks that makes traditional balance sheet metrics (D/E, ROE) appear alarming despite the company's strong cash flow profile [4]. Revenue growth remains modest at 2.6% TTM with 5-year CAGR of only 2.5%, reflecting secular headwinds in China's New Equipment market where intense local competition limits pricing power. At 18x forward earnings with a PEG of 2.74, the valuation prices in mid-single-digit EPS growth that leaves limited upside without re-acceleration in organic growth. The $8.2B debt load, while serviceable through strong free cash flow, creates vulnerability to rising interest rates. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors despite the company's comprehensive ESG programs [3].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Otis's core business — manufacturing, installing, and servicing physical elevators and escalators — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1][2]. The business requires physical-world operations (steel fabrication, shaft construction, mechanical installation), deeply regulated safety processes, and hands-on maintenance by trained technicians across 2.2 million installed units globally. AI cannot replace the physical act of moving people vertically in buildings. Moreover, AI-driven urbanization and smart building trends increase demand — US modernization orders surged 35% partly driven by AI data center construction [2]. The only minor exposure is that AI could enable competitors to narrow the service gap through better predictive analytics, but Otis's installed base advantage creates enormous switching costs. AI Opportunity (1/1): Otis ONE IoT platform and AI-powered predictive maintenance systems are shipping to customers, driving Service segment organic growth of 9% with modernization up 24% in Q2 2026 [1]. AI is actively enhancing service margins and customer retention.
