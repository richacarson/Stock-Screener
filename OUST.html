Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NASDAQ ·United States
Ouster designs, manufactures, and sells digital lidar sensors and perception software for autonomous vehicles, industrial automation, robotics, smart infrastructure, and defense applications. Following its 2023 Velodyne merger and 2026 Stereolabs acquisition, the company offers a unified Physical AI sensing and perception platform combining lidar, cameras, AI compute, and sensor fusion software. Products include the OS digital lidar, DF planar lidar, Gemini smart infrastructure system, and ZED stereo cameras. Approximately 320 employees, headquartered in San Francisco.

Investment Thesis
Ouster delivered record Q1 2026 revenue of $49M (+49% YoY) with product revenue of $48M (+55% YoY), marking the 13th consecutive quarter of growth driven by smart infrastructure and industrial verticals [1]. The company shipped 12,600+ sensors (65% lidar) and guided Q2 to $49.5-52.5M, implying sustained 30-50% annualized growth. The Stereolabs acquisition (February 2026) creates Physical AI's first unified sensing platform combining lidar, cameras, AI compute, and perception software. With $175M in liquid assets, minimal debt (D/E 6%), and GAAP gross margins improving to 43%, Ouster has runway to execute its platform strategy across a $18B industrial lidar TAM [1][4]. Analysts maintain a 'Strong Buy' consensus with a $49 price target representing 32% upside from $37 [4].
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability and technology risk. Despite 49% revenue growth, Ouster posted a $17.5M Q1 2026 net loss, -$55.8M TTM net loss, -30.1% profit margin, and -$69.3M TTM free cash flow, with sustained profitability unlikely before 2028 [1][4]. At $2.49B market cap (~13x TTM revenue), the stock prices in substantial growth that requires near-perfect execution. Camera-only perception approaches (Tesla FSD, Mobileye) continue advancing, threatening the fundamental value proposition of lidar as a necessary sensing modality for safety-critical applications. Glassdoor reviews raise cultural concerns about top-down decision-making and 'bad business leadership' that could impair talent retention as the company scales [3]. GAAP gross margin declined sequentially from 60% in Q4 2025 to 43% in Q1 2026, suggesting Q4's profitability was an outlier rather than a trend [1].
Key Catalysts

Key Risks
  1. Persistent unprofitability — $55.8M TTM net loss and -$69.3M FCF with sustained profitability unlikely before 2028; $175M liquid assets provides ~2 years of runway at current burn rate [1][4]
  2. Camera-only AI perception threat — Tesla FSD and Mobileye demonstrate advancing camera-based autonomy that could reduce lidar TAM if multi-sensor fusion consensus breaks down
  3. Valuation risk at $2.49B market cap (~13x TTM revenue) requires near-perfect execution on growth and margin expansion; Q1 gross margin decline from 60% to 43% sequentially raises sustainability questions [1]
