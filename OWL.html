Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Blue Owl Capital is an alternative asset manager with $319B in AUM as of June 2026, operating across three platforms: GP Strategic Capital (minority stakes in other asset managers), Direct Lending (private credit to middle-market companies), and Real Estate. Co-founded by Doug Ostrover and Marc Lipschultz, the firm went public via SPAC in May 2021 and has scaled rapidly through acquisitions (Owl Rock, Dyal Capital, Kuvare Asset Management) and organic growth. Q2 2026 revenue was $753.1M with fee-related earnings margin of 58.5%.
1,200 Employees ·www.blueowl.com

Investment Thesis
Blue Owl Capital at 10.90x forward earnings offers a high-yield (9.51%) entry into the fastest-growing segment of financial services — alternative asset management — with $319B AUM growing 12% YoY and fee-related earnings margin of 58.5% [1]. The GP Strategic Capital business (minority stakes in other asset managers) is a structurally unique model that generates permanent capital fees with minimal redemption risk. Revenue of $753.1M in Q2 2026 beat estimates by 9.6%, and management guided for full-year FRE and distributable earnings to exceed consensus. The founder-led structure with all four co-founders active and heavily invested in equity ensures alignment. D/E of 75% provides balance sheet conservatism unusual for financial firms.
The bear case centers on GAAP distortions, growth deceleration, and structural concerns. GAAP profit margin of 2.96% and ROE of 5.70% dramatically understate Blue Owl's economic profitability but create a low infrastructure score (6) in quantitative screens that use reported financials [4]. Revenue growth decelerated to 7.1% in Q2 2026 from 13-14% in Q1, raising questions about whether the rapid scaling phase is ending as AUM reaches $319B [1]. The distributable earnings payout ratio of ~105% leaves no margin for dividend growth without earnings acceleration — any shortfall in fee revenue would pressure the $0.92 annual dividend. The firm's 5-year track record as a public company provides limited evidence of durability through credit cycles, and a recession-driven increase in credit defaults could impair the direct lending portfolio. Innovation and Inspiration scores are structurally low (3 and 2) because IOWN's scoring framework rewards product innovation and purpose-driven leadership that financial firms rarely possess. The Inspire Impact Score of 29 (ALIGNED) is a relative positive for faith-based investors.
