Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
Oxford Square Capital Corp. is a publicly traded business development company (BDC) operating as a closed-end, non-diversified management investment company regulated under the Investment Company Act of 1940. The firm invests primarily in corporate debt securities and collateralized loan obligation (CLO) equity and debt tranches, targeting smaller technology-related companies. Externally managed by Oxford Square Management, LLC. Founded in 2003 as TICC Capital Corp., renamed to Oxford Square Capital in 2018. CEO Jonathan Cohen has led the company since its founding. Headquartered in Greenwich, Connecticut.

Investment Thesis
Oxford Square offers a headline 27.1% dividend yield and has maintained monthly distributions for 23 consecutive years, potentially appealing to income-focused investors willing to accept NAV erosion in exchange for current cash flow [4]. Q2 2026 total investment income was $9.4M with net investment income of $5.1M ($0.05/share), and the portfolio appears to be stabilizing after significant realized losses [3]. D/E of 123% is within BDC regulatory limits, and the $2.67M daily dollar volume provides adequate liquidity for individual investors. CEO Cohen's 23-year tenure provides management continuity and deep CLO market expertise.
The bear case is overwhelming and the data is worsening: NAV per share collapsed 36% from $2.06 to $1.32 in a single year, driven by $5.2M in realized losses and dilution from 11.3M new shares issued [1][3]. ROE of -26.56% and profit margin of -92.37% demonstrate the portfolio is generating losses, not income — the company is distributing capital, not earnings. Monthly distributions of $0.035/share ($0.42 annually) against EPS of -$0.43 represent pure return of capital, not investment return. The 27.1% yield is a value trap that masks systematic NAV destruction. The stock has declined approximately 80% since listing, and Q2 2026 slides confirm the pattern continues: portfolio stabilization rhetoric accompanies further NAV decline [3]. The dividend has been cut multiple times over the past decade with 0 years of consecutive growth and -8% five-year dividend CAGR. Further cuts are virtually certain as the gap between distributions and earnings income remains unsustainable.
Key Catalysts
  1. CLO equity yield stabilization and tech credit market recovery could slow NAV erosion and improve net investment income coverage of the $0.035 monthly distribution
  2. Technology sector lending recovery driven by AI adoption increasing borrowing demand from OXSQ's target market of smaller technology companies
  3. Potential strategic alternatives including merger with a larger BDC platform that could reduce external management costs, improve portfolio diversification, and halt NAV erosion
Key Risks
  1. Further dividend cuts are virtually certain given that EPS of -$0.43 cannot sustain $0.42 annual distributions — each month of uncovered payouts accelerates NAV destruction from the already-collapsed $1.32/share level
  2. Systematic NAV erosion from distributing capital in excess of earnings has destroyed 36% of book value in 12 months, with 11.3M new dilutive shares compounding the damage — the stock's 80% cumulative decline since listing demonstrates this is a structural, not cyclical, deterioration
  3. CLO equity concentration risk in a rising-default environment — compressed CLO yields and increasing leveraged loan defaults could simultaneously reduce OXSQ's income and impair its largest asset class, creating a reinforcing downward spiral
