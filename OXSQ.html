Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
Oxford Square Capital Corp. is an externally managed business development company (BDC) that invests primarily in corporate debt securities and collateralized loan obligation (CLO) structured finance investments. Founded in 2003 as TICC Capital Corp. and rebranded in 2018, the company is headquartered in Greenwich, Connecticut and managed by Oxford Square Management, LLC under CEO Jonathan H. Cohen. The company targets technology-related companies with annual revenues under $200M, investing $5M-$30M per transaction. As of Q2 2026, NAV per share stood at $1.29 with a market cap of approximately $137M.

Investment Thesis
Oxford Square Capital trades at $1.27 per share with a forward P/E of 5.77 and a headline dividend yield of 33.1%, which superficially appears attractive for income-seeking investors. Q2 2026 showed sequential improvement with NII increasing 24.4% to $5.1M ($0.05/share) as total investment income rose to $9.4M from $8.9M in Q1 [1]. The deeply discounted valuation (trading at 98.5% of book value of $1.29) and low beta of 0.37 could provide portfolio ballast in volatile markets, while elevated interest rates support CLO equity distributions.
The bear case is overwhelming. The 33.1% yield is a value trap — the $0.42 annual distribution exceeds NII of approximately $0.20/share, producing a payout ratio of 467% that mechanically erodes NAV every quarter ($1.32 to $1.29 in Q2 2026 alone) [1][3]. Since 2014, the annual dividend has been cut from $1.16 to $0.42 — a 64% reduction — while the stock price has fallen from ~$8 to $1.27, destroying 84% of shareholder value. The external management structure creates a permanent principal-agent conflict where management fees are earned regardless of returns. ROE of -25.0% and profit margin of -93.7% reflect structural value destruction [4]. With a market cap of just $137M, the company lacks scale to compete with larger BDCs that benefit from lower cost of capital and superior origination platforms. Without a dividend cut and strategic repositioning, continued NAV erosion appears inevitable.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sequential improvement in net investment income (+24.4% QoQ in Q2 2026 to $5.1M) could continue if interest rates remain elevated, potentially narrowing the gap between NII and dividend distributions and slowing NAV erosion [1]
  2. Trading near book value ($1.27 vs NAV $1.29) limits further downside from discount compression, and any CLO equity valuation recovery would directly improve NAV per share [1]
  3. A rational dividend cut to sustainable levels ($0.20-0.25/year) would halt NAV erosion and could attract fundamental investors focused on total return rather than unsustainable yield
Key Risks
  1. Unsustainable 467% payout ratio guarantees continued NAV erosion absent a dividend cut — at current pace, NAV declines approximately $0.03 per quarter, representing a ~9% annual NAV destruction rate that exceeds any potential capital appreciation [1][3]
  2. CLO equity concentration creates mark-to-market volatility and first-loss credit risk if corporate default rates rise above historical averages in an economic downturn, potentially accelerating NAV decline beyond current trajectory [2]
  3. External management structure with no disclosed fee waiver or alignment mechanism means management earns fees regardless of shareholder outcomes, creating zero incentive to cut the dividend or improve capital allocation [2]
