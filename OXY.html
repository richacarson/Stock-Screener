Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Occidental Petroleum is a major U.S. oil and gas exploration and production company with a leading Permian Basin position, organized around three pillars: Upstream, Midstream/Marketing, and Low Carbon Ventures. The company is executing a high-profile pivot toward carbon management through 1PointFive's Stratos facility — the world's largest Direct Air Capture plant — while reducing debt from the CrownRock acquisition. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway owns over 28% of shares outstanding and has continued adding to its position through 2026. CEO Vicki Hollub retires June 1, 2026, succeeded by current COO Richard Jackson [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Occidental trades at just 13.27x forward earnings with a 1.04 PEG despite delivering Q2 2026 free cash flow of roughly $3B — the highest since Q3 2022 — and cutting principal debt to $11.8B, the lowest since Q2 2019, saving ~$630M in annualized interest [1]. The Stratos DAC facility's Microsoft, Amazon, Shopify, and Airbus carbon-removal offtake agreements create a differentiated, AI-emissions-linked revenue stream layered on top of a core Permian business producing ~1.43M BOE/day, and Berkshire Hathaway's continued buying (28%+ stake, another $405M added in 2026) provides a strong informed-investor signal [1][2][3]. Profit margin of 30.32% and a clear path to $4B in sustainable annual cash flow by 2030 support the HOLD case at current valuation.
The bear case centers on cyclicality and execution risk on the low-carbon bet: revenue fell 1.95% YoY to $21.59B in 2025 and has declined at roughly a 4.5% 5-year CAGR as oil prices normalized from the 2022 spike, and ROE of 10.63% remains below the 15% target, reflecting the capital intensity of both the E&P business and the DAC buildout [5]. Occidental faces mounting ESG litigation risk as governments and private parties increasingly challenge companies' climate claims and net-zero goal execution, and critics have characterized the Microsoft/Amazon carbon-removal deals as enabling continued fossil fuel extraction (CO2 is partly used for enhanced oil recovery) rather than genuine decarbonization [7]. The upcoming CEO transition from Vicki Hollub — architect of the carbon-management strategy — to Richard Jackson introduces execution uncertainty for the still-unproven, capital-intensive DAC business at a time when oil price volatility could pressure the core cash-generating segment funding it [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Stratos DAC Phase 2 ramp through the remainder of 2026, expanding toward the 500,000 metric ton annual CO2 removal capacity and additional offtake agreements beyond Microsoft, Amazon, Shopify, and Airbus [2][6]
  2. Continued debt reduction toward the $4B sustainable annual cash flow target by 2030, with ~50% of that improvement expected between 2026-2027 from Stratos capital roll-off and interest savings [1]
  3. Sustained or rising oil prices supporting Permian cash generation (production averaging 1,426 Mboed, above guidance) while Berkshire Hathaway's continued accumulation signals confidence in the combined E&P/carbon-management strategy [1][3]
Key Risks

  2. Rising ESG litigation and greenwashing scrutiny around the Microsoft/Amazon carbon-removal deals, given captured CO2 is partly used for enhanced oil recovery, could create reputational and legal risk as government and private climate-claims litigation increases in 2026 [7]
  3. CEO transition from Vicki Hollub — the architect of the carbon-management pivot — to Richard Jackson in June 2026 introduces execution risk for the still-unproven, capital-intensive DAC strategy amid oil price volatility [1]
