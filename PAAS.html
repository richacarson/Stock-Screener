Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NYQ ·Canada
Pan American Silver Corp. is one of the world's largest precious metals producers, operating twelve silver and gold mines across Chile, Peru, Brazil, Mexico, Canada, Argentina, Bolivia, and Guatemala through Silver and Gold segments. Following the transformative $10B acquisition of Yamana Gold in 2023, the company generates TTM revenue of $4.3B with 32% profit margins, 22% ROE, and $1.3B in free cash flow, holding a net cash position of $864M. Founded in 1979 and headquartered in Vancouver, Canada, PAAS employs 9,348 people.

Investment Thesis
Pan American Silver at $46.27 with a $19.2B market cap delivers one of the most compelling value profiles in precious metals. The stock trades at just 10.1x forward earnings despite generating $1.3B in free cash flow over the trailing twelve months, with a net cash position of $864M providing a fortress balance sheet [1][3]. Q1 2026 produced $456M in net earnings on approximately $1.2B revenue, while Q2 saw $305M in net earnings on approximately $1.1B revenue, with the 38% YoY revenue surge driven by silver prices above $30/oz and gold exceeding $2,500/oz [1][2]. The twelve-mine portfolio across seven countries provides geographic and metal diversification, while profit margins of 32% and ROE of 22% reflect powerful operating leverage to sustained precious metal strength.
The bear case centers on commodity cyclicality and peak-earnings risk. Pan American's profitability is heavily dependent on near-record gold and silver prices — a 20% decline in precious metals would compress margins significantly, as 55-60% of revenue sensitivity is commodity-price driven with limited hedging [1][3]. The PEG ratio of 7.02 signals the market views current earnings growth as cyclically inflated rather than sustainably organic, and the Yamana integration still carries execution risk across complex Latin American jurisdictions. The Escobal mine in Guatemala remains suspended due to Xinka community opposition, representing significant invested capital generating zero revenue [4]. Management's Q3 2026 guidance already flagged gold production shortfalls of 3,000-6,000 ounces below the low end of quarterly guidance at El Penon and Jacobina, suggesting near-term disappointment risk [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained precious metal strength with gold above $2,500/oz and silver above $30/oz driving record free cash flow and further dividend increases — the quarterly payout was hiked 80% from $0.10 to $0.18 in 2026, with further raises likely if prices hold [1][2]
  2. La Colorada Skarn project development providing next-generation organic production growth in Mexico, potentially adding significant polymetallic output to replace depleting upper mine levels [1]
  3. Potential Escobal mine restart in Guatemala adding approximately 15-20M oz of annual silver production from one of the world's highest-grade silver deposits, pending community consultation resolution [4]
Key Risks
  1. Precious metal price cyclicality — reversion to gold below $2,000/oz and silver below $22/oz would compress profit margins from 32% toward single digits, eroding the free cash flow profile that supports current valuation and dividends [1][3]
  2. Escobal mine suspension extending indefinitely due to Indigenous community opposition in Guatemala, with significant invested capital generating zero returns and ongoing carrying costs while social license remains unresolved [4]
  3. Geographic and political risk concentration in Latin America, where seven-country operations expose Pan American to currency devaluation, regulatory changes, mining tax increases, and social license challenges [5]
