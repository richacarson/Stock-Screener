Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
PACS Group, Inc. is one of the largest post-acute healthcare platforms in the United States, operating 344+ skilled nursing, assisted living, and post-acute care facilities across 17 states. The company employs a 'facility maturation playbook' that acquires underperforming skilled nursing facilities and implements operational improvements to increase quality metrics, occupancy, and skilled patient mix. Q2 2026 revenue grew 9.1% YoY to $1.43B with net income up 50% to $76.4M. Full-year 2026 guidance raised to $5.75-5.85B revenue and $640-660M adjusted EBITDA. IPO'd April 2024. Founded in 2013, headquartered in Salt Lake City, Utah.

Investment Thesis
PACS Group is executing a distinctive facility maturation playbook at scale, delivering Q2 2026 revenue of $1.43B (up 9.1% YoY) and net income of $76.4M (up 50% YoY) across 344+ facilities with industry-leading occupancy of 90.4% and 83.6% of SNFs earning 4-5 star CMS quality ratings [1]. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was raised to $640-660M (~29% growth over 2025), and Forward P/E of 15.3x with PEG of 1.06x reflects reasonable valuation for the growth profile [5]. The Eduro acquisition of 34 facilities extends the growth runway, and the 'Silver Tsunami' demographic trend drives secular demand for skilled nursing capacity [1]. ROE of 27.9% demonstrates efficient capital deployment through the maturation model.
The bear case centers on unresolved legal and governance risk. The DOJ investigation into reimbursement and referral practices — including False Claims Act (PDPM billing), Anti-Kickback Statute (patient referrals), and COVID Hospital Stay Waiver abuse inquiries — remains active following Hindenburg Research's November 2024 report alleging PACS 'systematically scammed taxpayer-funded healthcare programs' [3][4]. Any adverse finding could result in substantial fines, Medicare exclusion, or mandated operational changes fundamentally altering the company's economics. D/E of 305% (including operating leases for 344+ facilities) represents meaningful leverage risk, and profit margins of 4.85% leave minimal cushion against regulatory changes, labor cost inflation, or Medicaid reimbursement rate adjustments. The stock has declined from $48.44 to $41.55 since August, reflecting continued investor uncertainty around the investigation overhang [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Eduro Healthcare acquisition of 34 facilities (3,633 beds) provides immediate growth runway with PACS's proven facility maturation playbook targeting occupancy and skilled mix improvements [1]

Key Risks
  1. Ongoing DOJ investigation into False Claims Act (PDPM billing), Anti-Kickback Statute (referrals), and COVID waiver abuse could result in substantial financial penalties, Medicare exclusion, or mandated operational changes that fundamentally alter the business model [3][4]
  2. Total D/E of 305% (including operating lease obligations for 344+ facilities) and 4.85% profit margin leave minimal financial cushion against labor cost inflation, reimbursement rate cuts, or occupancy declines [5]
  3. Hindenburg Research allegations that unnecessary billing drove 'more than 100% of operating and net income from 2020-2023' — if substantiated, historical financial performance was materially inflated and current run-rates may not be sustainable [3]
