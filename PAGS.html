Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure (Payments & Digital Banking) ·NYSE ·Brazil
PagSeguro Digital, operating as PagBank, provides payment acquiring, POS terminals, digital banking, credit (payroll, FGTS, working capital), insurance and investment products to consumers, micro-merchants and SMBs in Brazil. Founded in 2006 within the UOL group and headquartered in São Paulo, it reached 34 million customers by 4Q25. FY2025 revenue was R$19.4B (+5.6%) with net income of R$2.08B; Q2 2026 total revenue was flat at ~R$5.08B (+0.4%) with non-GAAP net income of R$576M (+1.9%) and ROAE of 14.9%. Carlos Mauad succeeded Alexandre Magnani as CEO in January 2026.

PagBank has a real inclusion story — taking micro-merchants and the underbanked from card terminals into full banking — but its recent behavior is that of a mature company managing for returns: flat revenue, a newly initiated dividend (R$1.4B planned for 2026) and buybacks, with leadership turnover at the top. Investment Thesis
PagBank trades at ~4.2x forward earnings and ~0.8x book value [7][1] despite FY2025 net income of R$2.08B, a 23.7% five-year revenue CAGR [1], and ROAE of 14.9% in Q2 2026 [2]. The business has shifted toward higher-value banking: 34 million customers and a loan book growing 36% in 1Q26 [3][2], while AI-driven service automation has cut support costs by 27% [4]. A newly established dividend (R$1.4B planned for 2026, ~9.7% TTM yield) plus buybacks return substantial capital at a deep-value multiple [2][1].
The bear case is that growth has stalled and the macro is hostile. Q2 2026 total revenue rose only 0.4% and non-GAAP net income only 1.9% [2], as high Brazilian interest rates (FY2025 interest expense of R$5.1B against R$7.3B operating income) squeeze funding costs [1]. Competition from Stone, Mercado Pago, Nubank and PIX-based instant payments compresses acquiring take rates, free cash flow was negative on a TTM basis [1], and BRL currency risk amplifies volatility for USD holders. The low multiple may reflect these structural headwinds rather than mispricing.
Key Catalysts
  1. Brazilian Selic rate cuts lowering PagBank's R$5B+ annual funding cost and re-accelerating earnings growth [1]
  2. Continued 30%+ loan-book growth in lower-risk payroll and FGTS credit lifting banking revenue mix [2]
  3. Q3 2026 results (mid-November 2026) and execution of the remaining 2026 dividend installments under new CEO Carlos Mauad [2][5]
Key Risks
  1. Stalled top line: Q2 2026 revenue +0.4% YoY suggests share loss or take-rate compression in acquiring [2]
  2. Credit-quality deterioration as the loan book grows 36% into a high-rate Brazilian economy [2]
  3. Leverage and funding: FMP TTM D/E of 310% including bank funding, negative TTM free cash flow, and BRL depreciation risk for USD investors [1]
