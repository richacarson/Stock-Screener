Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
Par Pacific Holdings, Inc. owns and operates energy, infrastructure, and retail businesses across the western United States. The company operates refineries in Hawaii (89.8 Mbpd), Montana (56.9 Mbpd), Washington (23.0 Mbpd), and Wyoming (14.6 Mbpd) for total throughput of 184 Mbpd, plus convenience stores under the Hele, 76, and nomnom brands and logistics infrastructure including pipelines and storage terminals. The Hawaii renewable fuels facility achieved commercial operations in April 2026.

Investment Thesis
Par Pacific delivered Q1 2026 net income of $54.5M ($1.10/diluted share) on revenue of $1.82B (up 4.5% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $91.5M, demonstrating resilient refining margins across its four-state platform [1]. At $83.63 with a forward P/E of 15.65 and trailing P/E of 9.34, the stock offers reasonable valuation for a refiner generating 33.3% ROE with $937.7M total liquidity [2]. The Hawaii renewable fuels facility achieving commercial operations in April 2026 diversifies revenue into sustainable aviation fuel, a growing market driven by airline decarbonization mandates. Geographic monopoly in Hawaii refining (89.8 Mbpd, sole refinery on the islands) and dominant intermountain positions provide structural pricing power unmatched by mainland peers [1].
The primary risk is cyclical exposure — Par Pacific's 6.02% profit margin reflects the thin economics of downstream refining, and crack spread compression would significantly impact earnings [2]. The stock has rallied 217% from its 52-week low of $26.83, leaving limited margin of safety at current levels. TTM revenue declined 2.5% and projected annual revenue decline of 1.1% suggests structural headwinds for traditional refining as EV adoption accelerates [5]. D/E of 87.47% with $637.9M in gross debt limits financial flexibility during industry downturns [1][2]. Concentration in Hawaii operations (89.8 Mbpd out of 184 Mbpd total, ~49% of capacity) creates geographic risk — natural disaster, shipping disruption, or Jones Act reform could materially impact the largest and highest-margin segment.
