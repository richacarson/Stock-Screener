Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
Par Pacific Holdings is an energy company that owns and operates refineries in Hawaii, Montana, Washington, and Wyoming, converting crude oil into gasoline, distillate, asphalt, and other products. The company also operates convenience stores and fuel retail outlets under the Hele, 76, and nomnom brands, and a logistics segment with terminals, pipelines, and marine vessels. Under CEO William Monteleone (appointed April 2024), the company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $2.97B with record refining margins and a 125% system-wide capture rate. Par Pacific recently formed Hawaii Renewables, LLC, a joint venture with Mitsubishi and ENEOS for renewable fuels.

Investment Thesis
Par Pacific delivered a blowout Q2 2026 with revenue of $2.97B (+56.8% YoY), adjusted EPS of $10.10 (+556% YoY), and a system-wide refining capture rate of 125%, driven by record Tacoma refinery throughput of 41,200 bpd at 98.1% utilization [1]. The balance sheet has been significantly strengthened through a $500M refinancing that reduced net debt by $220M+, ending Q2 with approximately $1.4B in liquidity. At 5.56x forward earnings with D/E of just 56.2% and ROE of 53.5%, PARR offers deep cyclical value with a strong operational platform across four refineries in structurally advantaged markets (Hawaii, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountain) [5].
The bear case centers on refining margin cyclicality and structural energy transition risk. The Q2 2026 results were driven by exceptionally favorable crack spreads that are historically mean-reverting — the 125% capture rate is unsustainable as a baseline expectation, and normalized margins would dramatically reduce EPS from the $10.10 adjusted quarterly level [1]. Revenue growth of 56.8% is entirely a function of commodity pricing, not organic volume gains (throughput actually declined 2.8%). The CEO's multi-million dollar stock sale signals potential insider concern about sustainability of current margins [4]. Profit margin of 9.9% reflects the inherently thin economics of refining. The company's concentration in Hawaii creates geographic risk (volcanic activity, tourism dependency), and the long-term energy transition toward electrification and renewables poses existential questions for conventional refiners. Workplace culture scores declining 15% YoY suggest internal execution risk during a critical strategic period.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record refining margins and 125% system-wide capture rate with continued strength in Pacific Basin crack spreads through favorable supply/demand dynamics and limited West Coast refining capacity additions [1]
  2. Hawaii Renewables LLC joint venture with Mitsubishi and ENEOS could unlock renewable fuel revenue and provide structural margin protection against energy transition regulatory pressures [3]
  3. Significant balance sheet deleveraging ($220M+ net debt reduction in Q2 2026) creates capacity for shareholder returns or opportunistic M&A if refining assets become available at distressed valuations [1]
Key Risks
  1. Refining margin cyclicality is the dominant risk — crack spreads are historically mean-reverting, and a normalization from the 125% capture rate to historical averages of 80-90% would reduce EPS by 30-40%, compressing the earnings multiple [1]
  2. Geographic concentration in Hawaii (Jones Act-protected but exposed to tourism cyclicality, volcanic/weather risk, and high operating costs) with limited diversification despite multi-refinery expansion [3]
  3. CEO multi-million dollar stock sale and declining workplace culture ratings (2.7/5, down 15% YoY) signal potential insider concern about earnings sustainability and internal execution risk during the energy transition [4]
