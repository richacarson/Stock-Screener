Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYSE ·United States
Paycom Software provides cloud-based human capital management (HCM) software delivered as SaaS, covering the full employment lifecycle including payroll, talent acquisition, talent management, HR management, time and labor management, and benefits administration. Founded in 1998 by CEO Chad Richison and headquartered in Oklahoma City. Q2 2026 revenue reached $531.2M (+9.8% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA margin of ~46%. Full-year 2026 guidance raised to $2.197B-$2.212B (7-8% growth) with adjusted EBITDA of $1.007B-$1.022B.

Track Record (2/3): Paycom launched BETI (Better Employee Transaction Interface), the industry's first employee-driven payroll system, and IWant, an AI-driven natural language command engine allowing employees to interact with HR data conversationally [1][2]. These represent meaningful product innovation within HCM, though not a transformative business model pivot. AI Deployment (1/2): IWant is shipping to customers as an AI-powered natural language interface within the Paycom platform, enabling conversational access to payroll and HR data, but no specific AI-driven revenue gains or cost savings have been quantified publicly [2]. Market Share (1/2): Paycom is a meaningful player in the $30B+ HCM market serving ~37,000 clients, but competes against much larger incumbents — ADP ($19B+ revenue), Paychex, and Workday. The single-database architecture differentiates but has not achieved category leadership [1]. Product Pipeline (1/2): International expansion via GONE (Global HCM) has been announced but timelines remain vague. IWant continues expanding capabilities. Shane Hadlock's February 2026 promotion to President signals investment in growth leadership [2]. R&D Intensity (1/1): Paycom invests approximately 15% of revenue in R&D, building and maintaining its single-database platform entirely in-house rather than through acquisitions — well above the application software industry average [1].

Investment Thesis
Paycom at 16x forward earnings offers compelling value for a high-quality SaaS compounder — Q2 2026 revenue of $531.2M (+9.8% YoY) with ~46% adjusted EBITDA margin demonstrates the operating leverage of the single-database HCM platform, and the company raised full-year guidance to $2.197B-$2.212B with EBITDA of $1.007B-$1.022B [1]. ROE of 41.1% and profit margin of 22.8% reflect strong capital efficiency, and the stock has rallied 55% from its 2026 lows on margin expansion and guidance raises. The IWant AI command engine positions Paycom as an AI-enabled platform rather than an AI-disrupted one, and the single-database architecture creates genuine competitive differentiation [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 53 (ALIGNED) makes this accessible for faith-based investors. Five-year revenue CAGR of ~16% confirms sustained scaling despite recent deceleration.
The bear case centers on decelerating organic growth, elevated leverage, and cultural warning signs. Revenue growth slowed from a ~16% five-year CAGR to 7-8% FY2026 guidance, putting Paycom in an uncomfortable middle ground — too slow for growth multiples, too small for the stability premium ADP commands [1]. D/E of 172% (up from ~5% a year ago) reflects aggressive debt-funded share repurchases that boost EPS but increase financial risk if growth continues to decelerate. Glassdoor reviews describe waves of layoffs and deteriorating morale as management responds to slowing growth with cost-cutting rather than investment — a finite-game pattern that could accelerate talent attrition [2]. The failed co-CEO transition with Chris Thomas in 2024 creates key-person risk around founder Richison, and international expansion remains nascent with no specific revenue targets. ADP, Workday, and UKG are embedding AI capabilities that could neutralize Paycom's technology differentiation in the mid-market segment.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q2 2026 revenue acceleration to 9.8% YoY (from Q1's 7.8%) with raised full-year guidance signals potential growth inflection, with IWant AI adoption and GONE international expansion as potential re-acceleration drivers [1]
  2. Adjusted EBITDA margin trajectory toward 48-50% as the company scales past the Beti/IWant investment cycle, with operating leverage on a single-database platform providing a clear path to profitability improvement [1]
  3. Shane Hadlock's elevation to President in February 2026 addresses succession risk and signals organizational maturity, potentially enabling Chad Richison to focus on long-term strategy and international expansion [2]
Key Risks
  1. D/E surge from ~5% to 172% reflects aggressive debt-funded buybacks that boost EPS but create financial vulnerability if revenue growth continues decelerating below the 7-8% guidance range [5]
  2. Cultural deterioration evidenced by Glassdoor complaints about layoffs and management style could trigger talent attrition among software engineers, undermining the R&D velocity that sustains product differentiation [2]
  3. Intensifying competition from ADP (AI-powered), Workday (enterprise expansion), and UKG (mid-market focus) threatens to squeeze Paycom's addressable niche and compress pricing power in the 50-10,000 employee segment [1]
