Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYSE ·United States
Paycom Software, Inc. provides cloud-based human capital management (HCM) software solutions delivered as Software-as-a-Service. Its comprehensive platform covers the full employment lifecycle including payroll, talent acquisition, talent management, HR management, time and labor management, and benefits administration. Founded in 1998 by Chad Richison and headquartered in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma.
7,200 Employees ·www.paycom.com

Mindset: MIXED Overall: 5 /10
Paycom under Chad Richison shows a mix of infinite and finite game characteristics. The founder's 28-year tenure and willingness to launch products that cannibalize revenue (BETI, IWant) demonstrate genuine long-term thinking and purpose alignment. However, the company's explicit core value of 'We Win (playing to win)' reflects a finite competitive mindset, the failed co-CEO transition suggests succession planning challenges, and the promotion of Shane Hadlock to President in February 2026 is a positive but unproven step toward organizational durability.

Investment Thesis
Paycom delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $572M (up 8% YoY) with GAAP net income of $156M (27% margin) and Adjusted EBITDA of $275M (48% margin), beating analyst EPS expectations at $3.15 vs. $2.89 consensus [1]. The company's financial profile is exceptional — near-zero debt (D/E 5%), ROE of 28.6%, and Forward P/E of 15.5x offer a rare combination of quality and value in enterprise software. The single-database HCM architecture creates genuine competitive differentiation against multi-module competitors, and the IWant AI product positions Paycom as an AI-enabled platform rather than an AI-disrupted one [2]. At $143.67, Paycom trades at a significant discount to software peers while generating best-in-class margins.
The primary concern is decelerating growth. Revenue growth slowed from a 19.5% five-year CAGR to 9% in FY2025, with 2026 guidance of only 6-7% ($2.175B-$2.195B) suggesting further maturation [1]. This puts Paycom in an uncomfortable middle ground — too slow for growth multiples, too small for the stability premium that ADP commands. The failed co-CEO transition with Chris Thomas in 2024 creates key-person risk around founder Chad Richison, who has been the sole strategic architect for 28 years. Shane Hadlock's February 2026 promotion to President is a positive step but unproven [2]. International expansion remains nascent with no specific revenue targets. The HCM market is intensely competitive, and larger players (ADP, Workday, UKG) are embedding AI capabilities that could neutralize Paycom's technology lead.
Key Catalysts
  1. IWant AI command engine driving measurable client engagement and retention improvements, potentially re-accelerating net revenue retention as clients expand platform usage beyond core payroll [2]
  2. International expansion generating meaningful revenue contribution, opening a new addressable market beyond the U.S. mid-market HCM landscape [1]
  3. Continued margin expansion with Adjusted EBITDA margins already at 48% and potential to reach 50%+ as the platform scales with minimal incremental cost per client [1]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth deceleration to 6-7% guidance for 2026 suggests the company may be approaching a growth ceiling in its core U.S. mid-market HCM segment, with limited near-term catalysts for re-acceleration [1]
  2. Key-person risk around founder/CEO Chad Richison, amplified by the failed co-CEO transition in 2024 — Shane Hadlock's promotion to President in February 2026 is a positive succession signal but remains unproven [2]
  3. Intensifying competition from ADP, Workday, and UKG embedding AI capabilities that could reduce Paycom's single-database technology differentiation in the mid-market segment [1]

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Paycom's core payroll and HCM platform is protected by significant structural moats — payroll processing requires deep regulatory compliance expertise across 50 states, switching costs are extremely high once employee data is embedded, and the single-database architecture creates integration advantages that are difficult to replicate [1]. However, portions of the HCM value chain — particularly HR analytics, employee self-service, and talent management — face competitive pressure from AI-native tools and large platform players embedding AI into their offerings. ADP and Workday are both investing heavily in AI capabilities that could narrow Paycom's differentiation. AI Opportunity (1/1): Paycom's IWant product is a shipping AI-driven natural language command engine that allows employees to interact with payroll and HR data conversationally, representing genuine AI-powered product innovation integrated into the core platform [2].
