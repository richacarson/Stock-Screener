Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·United States
Prosperity Bancshares, Inc. operates as a bank holding company for Prosperity Bank, providing financial products and services to businesses and consumers across Texas and Oklahoma. The company offers deposit products, residential and commercial real estate loans, commercial and industrial lending, agricultural loans, consumer loans, digital banking, trust and wealth management, and treasury services. Founded in 1983 and headquartered in Houston, Texas, the company closed the Stellar Bancorp merger on July 1, 2026, adding 52 banking offices and significantly expanding its Houston and East Texas footprint.

Investment Thesis
Prosperity Bancshares is a deeply valued Texas community bank franchise trading at 11.0x forward earnings with a 3.5% dividend yield backed by 25+ consecutive years of payments [6]. Q2 2026 delivered net income of $168.6M ($1.67 EPS), with 17% revenue growth and 20.4% core net income growth driven by successful acquisition integration and net interest margin expansion [1]. CEO Zalman's 25-year track record of disciplined M&A has built an $85B+ pro forma asset franchise through 30+ acquisitions, and the Stellar Bancorp merger (closed July 1, 2026, adding 52 branches) extends the Houston and East Texas footprint. Analyst consensus of $5.89 EPS and $1.75B revenue for 2026 reflects continued growth momentum.
The bear case centers on acquisition dependence, low returns, and geographic concentration. ROE of 7.21% remains far below the 15% target and likely below cost of capital, reflecting persistent goodwill dilution from serial acquisitions [6]. Organic revenue growth excluding acquisitions is estimated at only 5-8%, raising questions about the core franchise's ability to grow without M&A fuel. The Stellar Bancorp merger adds integration complexity and temporarily elevates expenses. Prosperity's concentrated Texas and Oklahoma footprint exposes the bank to correlated energy sector and real estate cycle risks — the 2014-2016 oil price collapse and 2023 regional banking crisis demonstrated this vulnerability. Consistent insider selling (56 sells, 0 buys over the past year) across the board, while modest in dollar terms, undermines the insider conviction narrative [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Stellar Bancorp integration (closed July 1, 2026) adding 52 branches and significantly expanding the Houston and East Texas footprint, with expected cost synergies and cross-selling across the combined franchise [1]
  2. Net interest margin expansion from improving rate dynamics and asset repricing — Q2 2026 showed strong revenue growth of 17% YoY driven by margin improvement, with further upside as the loan book reprices [1]
  3. Continued Texas banking consolidation creating acquisition opportunities at attractive valuations as smaller community banks seek exit — Prosperity's proven playbook positions it as a preferred acquirer [1]
Key Risks
  1. Serial acquisition dependence — organic revenue growth of 5-8% suggests the core franchise cannot grow meaningfully without M&A, creating execution risk if deal flow slows or integration stumbles with the larger Stellar Bancorp acquisition [1]
  2. Texas and Oklahoma geographic concentration exposes the bank to correlated energy sector and real estate cycle risks — a significant oil price decline or Texas commercial real estate correction could materially increase credit losses
  3. ROE of 7.21% well below cost of capital reflects persistent goodwill dilution from acquisitions — with 56 insider sells and 0 buys over the past year, even leadership appears to be taking chips off the table [5][6]
