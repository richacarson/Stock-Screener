Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·United States
Prosperity Bancshares is a Houston, Texas-based bank holding company for Prosperity Bank, offering deposit accounts, commercial and consumer lending, digital/mobile banking, trust and wealth management, and treasury services across Texas and Oklahoma. The bank has grown substantially through serial in-market acquisitions, completing mergers with American Bank Holding Corporation (January 2026), Southwest Bancshares (February 2026), and Stellar Bancorp (July 2026), materially expanding its scale and Texas footprint [1]. Net income rose 13.2% to $543M in 2025, with Q2 2026 net income up 20.4% year-over-year to $168.6M [1].

Investment Thesis
Prosperity Bancshares is executing a disciplined Texas/Oklahoma bank consolidation strategy, closing three mergers in 18 months (American Bank Holding, Southwest Bancshares, Stellar Bancorp) that drove Q2 2026 net income up 20.4% YoY to $168.6M and full-year 2025 net income up 13.2% to $543M [1]. Trading at a 9.24x forward P/E — well below the 20x target and cheap relative to bank peers — with a 41.3% payout ratio near the 40% target and an estimated 20-year unbroken dividend history growing at a ~4.1% 5-year CAGR, the stock offers reasonable income and valuation support [4]. Conservative underwriting has historically limited credit losses through prior cycles, a durable if unglamorous competitive advantage.
The bear case centers on thin organic profitability and integration risk: ROE of just 7.07% is roughly half the 15% target, reflecting equity dilution from all-stock mergers that has not yet been offset by realized cost synergies, and the 25.9% revenue growth figure is almost entirely acquisition-driven rather than organic [4]. Three overlapping mergers closing within 18 months (most recently Stellar Bancorp in July 2026) create meaningful integration execution risk, including systems conversion, credit mark accuracy on acquired loan books, and retention of acquired customer relationships. Innovation and Inspiration scores are weak because no AI deployment, quantified community investment, or insider buying evidence was found in research, leaving the qualitative 'excellence' case thin relative to the bank's financial conservatism. A regional Texas/Oklahoma concentration also leaves earnings exposed to energy-sector and regional real estate cyclicality.
Key Catalysts
  1. Realization of cost synergies from the Stellar Bancorp, Southwest Bancshares, and American Bank Holding mergers as systems integration completes through 2026-2027, which could lift ROE toward the 15% target [1]
  2. Continued net interest margin expansion if the Fed rate path stabilizes, benefiting a conservatively-funded balance sheet with a low-cost core deposit base
  3. Further in-market Texas/Oklahoma bank acquisitions extending the multi-decade consolidation playbook that has driven long-term book value growth
Key Risks
  1. Integration execution risk across three simultaneous mergers closing within an 18-month window, including credit mark accuracy on acquired loan books and core systems conversion disruption [1]
  2. ROE of 7.07%, roughly half the 15% target, signals that acquisition-driven share issuance has diluted returns on equity faster than earnings have grown to offset it [4]
  3. Regional concentration in Texas and Oklahoma ties earnings to energy-sector health and regional commercial real estate, both of which carry above-average cyclicality
