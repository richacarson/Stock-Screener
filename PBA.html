Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·Canada
Pembina Pipeline provides energy transportation and midstream services across North America. It operates through Pipelines (3.7M boe/day throughput in Q2 2026, up 3% YoY), Facilities (430K bbl/day NGL fractionation, 21M bbl cavern storage), and Marketing & New Ventures segments. Founded in 1954 and headquartered in Calgary, Alberta. CEO Scott Burrows leads the ~3,000-person organization. Q2 2026 reported earnings of CAD 512M (up 23% YoY) with 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance reaffirmed at CAD 4.35B-4.55B.

Investment Thesis
Pembina Pipeline delivered a strong Q2 2026 with reported earnings of CAD 512M (up 23% YoY) and total throughput volumes of 3.7M boe/d (up 3% YoY), with the RFS IV fractionator and Wapiti expansion entering service on time and at or below budget [1]. Management reaffirmed FY2026 EBITDA guidance of CAD 4.35-4.55B with 5-7% annual fee-based EBITDA per share growth through 2030. The ~4.4% dividend yield with dominant WCSB infrastructure provides income and defensive positioning. The Greenlight Electricity Center (up to 1,800 MW) uniquely positions Pembina to capture AI-driven electricity demand growth — a structural tailwind for midstream energy [2]. Revenue growth of 20.1% YoY reflects the improving fundamental trajectory.
The bear case centers on the elevated GAAP payout ratio, valuation compression, and energy transition risk. The trailing GAAP payout ratio of ~103% raises sustainability questions, though management points to distributable cash flow coverage that exceeds reported earnings due to non-cash depreciation charges [5]. Forward P/E has expanded to 20.8x from 18.0x on the prior screen, reducing valuation attractiveness for a midstream company with 10.5% ROE below the 15% target [5]. The 3.0% 5-year dividend growth CAGR in USD terms barely keeps pace with inflation, limiting real income growth. Average daily volume has decreased from 1.53M to 986K shares, though liquidity remains ample at $46.9M/day. Long-term energy transition away from fossil fuels poses structural demand risk to pipeline volumes, and Glassdoor reviews citing nepotism, office politics, and poor communication signal cultural challenges that could affect execution on growth initiatives [2][4].
