Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
PBF Energy Inc., through its subsidiaries, engages in the refining and supplying of petroleum products in the United States. The company operates six refineries with combined throughput capacity of approximately 1 million barrels per day across East Coast, Mid-continent, Gulf Coast, and West Coast regions. Produces gasoline, diesel, heating oil, jet fuel, lubricants, petrochemicals, and renewable diesel through its Saint Bernard Renewables facility. Founded in 2008, headquartered in Parsippany, New Jersey.
3,678 Employees ·www.pbfenergy.com

Investment Thesis
PBF Energy delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $7.9B (beating estimates of $7.51B) with net income of $198.3M, rebounding strongly from the FY2025 net loss as refining margins recovered [1]. The stock's 114.68% 52-week gain reflects the margin recovery, and the Forward P/E of 4.81 prices the stock for continued crack spread compression — creating significant upside if refining margins remain at current levels or improve. D/E of 64% provides balance sheet flexibility, and the 2.30% dividend yield with a conservative 28.81% payout ratio leaves room for capital returns. PBF's six-refinery, 1 million bpd platform provides geographic diversification across East Coast, Mid-continent, Gulf Coast, and West Coast markets.
PBF is a deeply cyclical business with profit margins that oscillate between negative and mid-single digits based on crack spreads it cannot control. FY2025's net loss of -$158.6M on $29.3B revenue (profit margin of -0.5%) illustrates the earnings volatility — refiners can lose money even on massive revenue [1]. The Altman Z-Score of 2.6 signals increased bankruptcy risk. Environmental violations at multiple refineries (Martinez hazardous emissions litigation, Delaware City receiving 50+ violations in a decade) create material regulatory and liability tail risk [3]. Negative free cash flow of -$683.9M in the trailing period suggests capital intensity is consuming cash despite improved earnings. Long-term secular headwinds from EV adoption and decarbonization policy threaten refined product demand, while the renewable diesel venture is too small to offset core business decline. The dividend has only 3 consecutive years of growth following a COVID-era suspension, and no long-term dividend growth track record exists.
