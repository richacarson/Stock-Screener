Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
PBF Energy Inc. is one of the largest independent petroleum refiners in the United States, producing gasoline, ultra-low-sulfur diesel, heating oil, jet fuel, lubricants, petrochemicals and asphalt through its Refining and Logistics segments. Its refineries span the East Coast, Mid-Continent, Gulf Coast and West Coast, including the 157,000 bpd Martinez, California refinery that was rebuilt after a February 2025 fire. Headquartered in Parsippany, New Jersey.

Investment Thesis
PBF is the purest equity play on the 2026 refining-margin spike: Q3 2026 net income attributable to PBF reached $1.53B ($12.54/share) versus $170M ($1.45/share) a year earlier, as Gulf Coast 2-1-1 cracks hit $69.06/bbl versus $24.41 [1]. The company exited Q3 with $2.5B cash against $2.1B debt, raised the dividend 18% to $0.325, and trades at 3.5x trailing and 4.2x forward earnings [1][5]. The completed Martinez rebuild and the $350M RBI cost program add operating leverage if margins stay elevated [3][4].
The bear case is that PBF's earnings are almost entirely a function of crack spreads it does not control: the company lost $534M in 2024 and $159M in 2025, and analysts' forward EPS ($18.52) already sits below trailing ($22.51) [2][5]. Insiders sold heavily throughout 2026 (135 open-market sales across Q1-Q3, zero purchases), signaling that management sees the cycle as near peak [7]. The Martinez fire left 22 Air District notices of violation plus DOJ/EPA investigations, and California's regulatory environment and long-run fuel-demand erosion from electrification threaten the West Coast assets [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained elevated crack spreads ($65-69/bbl 2-1-1 in Q3 2026) driving further cash build and net-cash balance sheet
  2. Full-rate operation of the rebuilt 157,000 bpd Martinez refinery adding West Coast volumes at high margins
  3. Delivery of the $350M annualized RBI cost-savings target lowering through-cycle breakeven
  4. Additional dividend increases or buybacks funded by the 2026 margin windfall
Key Risks
  1. Mean reversion of crack spreads from 2026 highs — PBF lost money in 2024 and 2025 at normal margins
  2. Regulatory penalties from 22 Bay Area Air District NOVs and DOJ/EPA investigations tied to the Martinez fire
  3. California refining regulations and policy hostility to in-state refining threatening West Coast asset value
  4. Process-safety incidents at aging refineries causing outages and community opposition
  5. Long-term gasoline and diesel demand erosion from vehicle electrification
