Company Profile
Industrials ·Farm & Heavy Construction Machinery ·NASDAQ ·United States
PACCAR Inc designs and manufactures premium heavy-duty trucks under the Kenworth, Peterbilt, and DAF nameplates. The company also provides financial services, information technology, and distributes truck parts globally through its PACCAR Parts segment. Founded in 1905 and headquartered in Bellevue, Washington, PACCAR is one of the top three global heavy truck OEMs with operations across the United States, Europe, Australia, and South America.

Investment Thesis
PACCAR is a premium truck OEM with dominant North American Class 8 market share, an 85-year unbroken dividend track record, and a high-margin parts distribution business that hit record Q2 revenue of $1.75B at 29.8% gross margins [1]. Q2 2026 showed meaningful cyclical recovery with $7.55B revenue (beating $7.08B estimates), $1.43 EPS (beating $1.35), deliveries of 38,700 units (+17% QoQ), and gross margin expansion to 14.4% from 13.1% [1]. Build slots are full through Q2 with majority fullness in Q3/Q4, and 2027 EPA emissions standards are expected to trigger a prebuy cycle that could lift production volumes through 2027 [2]. Forward P/E of 19.83 with a PEG of 0.97 suggests fair valuation for the cyclical recovery thesis, while the 2.17% yield and conservative D/E of 73% provide downside protection [3].
The primary risk is the deeply cyclical nature of the truck market — TTM revenue of ~$28B is 21% below the 2023 peak of $35.1B, and any delay in the Class 8 recovery or tariff-induced demand disruption could extend the trough [3][8]. PACCAR's net profit margin of 9% is inherently constrained by capital-intensive manufacturing, leaving limited margin of safety during downturns. R&D intensity of ~1.7% of revenue — roughly half that of Volvo Group and Daimler Truck — raises questions about long-term competitiveness in the transition to EV and autonomous trucking [2]. Net insider selling of $7.2M by CEO Feight and other executives in July-August 2026 signals limited leadership conviction despite the cyclical recovery thesis [5]. The stock's trailing P/E of 27.58 is elevated for a trough-year cyclical, pricing in a recovery that must materialize for the multiple to hold.
Key Catalysts
  1. Class 8 cyclical recovery with Q2 2026 deliveries of 38,700 units (+17% QoQ) and build slots filling through H2 2026, supported by projected U.S./Canada Class 8 market of 230,000-270,000 units [1][2]
  2. 2027 EPA emissions standard prebuy cycle expected to drive elevated truck orders and production volumes beginning in late 2026, potentially lifting revenues toward prior cyclical peaks
  3. PACCAR Parts record Q2 revenue of $1.75B with 29.8% gross margins and 8% Fleet Services growth, providing high-margin counter-cyclical earnings stability [1]
Key Risks
  1. Prolonged freight recession or tariff-induced supply chain disruption could further depress Class 8 demand below the guided 230,000-270,000 U.S./Canada units, extending the cyclical trough beyond 2026
  2. R&D intensity at ~1.7% of revenue — roughly half that of Volvo Group and Daimler Truck — risks falling behind on autonomous driving, battery-electric, and hydrogen fuel cell technology development [2]
  3. Net insider selling of $7.2M (CEO Feight $6.8M exercise-and-sell, VP Bolgar $388K) in July-August 2026 contrasts with the cyclical recovery narrative and suggests leadership uncertainty about near-term upside [5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): PACCAR's core business — designing, manufacturing, and servicing heavy-duty commercial trucks — is fundamentally immune to AI displacement. The business requires massive physical manufacturing capabilities, global supply chains, dealer/service networks, and regulatory compliance (emissions, safety certifications) that AI cannot substitute [1]. Truck demand is structurally tied to freight volumes and fleet replacement cycles. AI-driven automation in logistics actually increases demand for trucking infrastructure by expanding e-commerce fulfillment. Autonomous trucking development would increase truck utilization rates and accelerate fleet turnover, directly benefiting OEMs like PACCAR [3]. No analysts have flagged AI as a substitution risk. AI Opportunity (0/1): While PACCAR references autonomous vehicle platforms and connected vehicle services in its R&D agenda, no quantifiable AI-related revenue has been reported. Employee reviews confirm AI initiatives lack strategic maturity [6].
