Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYSE ·United States
PagerDuty is an AI-first digital operations management platform that ingests signals from over 700 integrations to orchestrate incident response across people, machines, and software. The platform encompasses AIOps (91% alert noise reduction), Automation, Incident Management, and the SRE Agent for autonomous operations. Founded in 2009 and headquartered in San Francisco, PagerDuty completed a CEO transition in May 2026 — John DiLullo replacing Jennifer Tejada (who became Executive Chair). FY2026 (ending Jan 2026) delivered $492.5M revenue (+5.4% YoY) and $172.7M net income, the company's first GAAP profitable year. Q1 FY2027 ARR was flat at $496M.

Investment Thesis
PagerDuty at 4.91x forward earnings with an 82.5% ROE and 35.3% profit margin appears deeply undervalued for an AI-first SaaS platform — FY2026 revenue of $492.5M (+5.4% YoY) and net income of $172.7M marked the company's first GAAP profitable year, driven by 700bps non-GAAP operating margin expansion [1]. The AIOps platform delivers quantified 91% alert noise reduction and the SRE Agent positions PagerDuty at the forefront of autonomous operations. With 700+ integrations creating deep enterprise switching costs and new CEO John DiLullo (ex-Deepwatch) bringing enterprise SaaS transformation experience, the company has both the product moat and leadership to execute on growth re-acceleration [3]. The stock at ~$1.3B market cap trades below 3x revenue — a fraction of SaaS peer multiples — creating significant upside if growth stabilizes above 10%.
The bear case centers on stalling growth and the risk that the favorable financial metrics are transient. FY2026's $172.7M net income (35.3% margin) is almost certainly inflated by a one-time deferred tax asset realization — normalized margins are likely 10-15%, which would put forward PE closer to 15-25x [4]. Q1 FY2027 ARR flat at $496M (0% growth) is an alarming deceleration from the already-slow 5.4% FY2026 pace, suggesting the core business may be approaching zero organic growth [1]. The existential risk is that incident management becomes a feature of larger platforms — ServiceNow's ITSM suite, Datadog's full-stack observability, or CrowdStrike's security operations could absorb PagerDuty's core functionality at scale. CEO transition from Tejada to DiLullo introduces execution risk at a critical juncture; DiLullo must simultaneously re-accelerate growth, maintain profitability, and defend against platform competitors. D/E of 153% signals meaningful leverage for a company with decelerating revenue. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors [5].
