Company Profile
Real Estate ·Hotel & Resort REITs ·NYSE ·United States
Pebblebrook Hotel Trust is a real estate investment trust (REIT) and the largest owner of urban and resort lifestyle hotels in the United States, owning 44 hotels totaling approximately 11,000 guest rooms across 13 urban and resort markets [1]. Founded and led by Chairman and CEO Jon Bortz since 2009, the company acquires, owns, and renovates upper-upscale, full-service hotels operated by third-party management companies under major brands and independent lifestyle collections. Q2 2026 same-property hotel EBITDA rose 7.1% to $123.3M and management raised full-year same-property RevPAR growth guidance to 4.5-5.5% [1].

Investment Thesis
Pebblebrook's Q2 2026 results beat expectations, with same-property hotel EBITDA up 7.1% to $123.3M and management raising full-year same-property RevPAR growth guidance to 4.5-5.5% and adjusted FFO per share to $1.69-$1.76 [1]. CEO Jon Bortz's recent open-market purchases (40,000 shares, ~$678,600, over the past six months, including a $341,800 buy in June 2026) signal insider confidence near a depressed valuation, and the stock's ~0.98x book-adjusted leverage profile against a diversified 44-hotel urban/resort portfolio provides exposure to a continued travel-demand recovery [5]. As a physical real-estate owner, PEB's core asset base is structurally immune to AI disruption, and third-party operators' adoption of AI-driven revenue management tools may improve hotel-level margins at no cost to PEB.
The bear case centers on balance sheet strain and decelerating momentum: PEB carries $2.62B in debt against just $227M in cash (a $2.39B net debt position) and remains GAAP unprofitable, with a -1.63% ROE and -3.11% profit margin reflecting persistent losses (-$78.6M TTM) despite five years of revenue recovery [source]. Q3 2026 guidance calls for RevPAR growth of just 1.0-3.0%, a sharp deceleration from the 4.5-5.5% full-year target, raising questions about the durability of the current upcycle. The token dividend yield (~0.2-0.3%) reflects a payout maintained primarily to satisfy REIT distribution requirements rather than genuine income generation, and the 5-year revenue CAGR is flattered by a depressed 2021 COVID base rather than reflecting steady organic growth. The Infinite Game and Inspiration scores are held back by a shareholder-returns-first mission statement and no evidence of the kind of costly, cause-driven strategic decisions that would mark PEB as a truly infinite-minded operator.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued RevPAR acceleration into H2 2026 toward the raised 4.5-5.5% full-year guidance, driven by resilient leisure demand and recovering group/business-travel bookings [1]
  2. Completion and ramp of ongoing hotel renovations repositioning assets into higher-RevPAR independent lifestyle brands, supporting margin expansion as capex-disrupted properties return to full service
  3. CEO Jon Bortz's continued open-market share purchases, which could attract value-oriented investors to a REIT trading well below net asset value [5]
Key Risks
  1. High leverage — $2.62B in debt against a $2.06B market cap and only $227M in cash — creates refinancing and interest-expense sensitivity if rates remain elevated [source]
  2. Q3 2026 guidance of just 1.0-3.0% RevPAR growth signals a meaningful deceleration from Q2's 7.1% same-property EBITDA growth pace, raising doubt about full-year guidance durability [1]
  3. Persistent GAAP net losses (-$78.6M TTM) despite top-line recovery reflect structurally high interest expense and hotel operating cost inflation that continue to erode profitability [source]
