Company Profile
Utilities ·Electric Utilities ·NYSE ·United States
Public Service Enterprise Group is a diversified energy company primarily operating through its regulated utility subsidiary PSE&G (Public Service Electric and Gas Company) serving approximately 2.3 million electric and 1.9 million gas customers in New Jersey. PSEG also operates a nuclear generation fleet (Hope Creek and Salem stations) through PSEG Power. The company reported 2025 net income of $4.22/share and non-GAAP operating earnings of $4.05/share. 2026 guidance targets $4.28-$4.40 non-GAAP operating EPS (+7%). Data center interconnection requests surged from 400 MW to 4.7 GW. Market cap approximately $37B.

Investment Thesis
PSEG is a premier regulated utility uniquely positioned for the AI infrastructure buildout, now available at a more attractive valuation after pulling back to $73.81. The data center interconnection pipeline at PSE&G surged from 400 MW to 4.7 GW in one year, and CEO LaRossa is actively negotiating nuclear power offtake agreements with data center operators and exploring utility-owned generation for supply needs [3]. The updated $22.5-25.5B capital program (2026-2030) supports rate base CAGR of 6-7.5%, underpinning the updated 6-8% non-GAAP earnings growth outlook through 2030 [1]. At approximately 17.0x forward earnings with a 3.63% dividend yield and 15 consecutive years of dividend increases, PSEG offers compelling total return potential for income-oriented investors. The 2025 non-GAAP operating earnings of $4.05/share with 2026 guidance of $4.28-$4.40 demonstrate steady earnings progression [1]. The MSCI AAA ESG rating and Inspire Impact Score of 49 (ALIGNED) with 18 positive attributions make PSEG highly accessible to faith-based and ESG-conscious investors [2].
The bear case centers on the gap between pipeline interest and contracted revenue, plus inherent utility growth limitations. The 4.7 GW interconnection pipeline represents interest, not binding contracts — actual signed agreements for nuclear offtake have yet to materialize publicly, and if hyperscale demand shifts to regions with cheaper land and power, the AI growth narrative deflates [3]. D/E of 142% is typical for utilities but limits financial flexibility, and the $22.5-25.5B capital plan requires continued access to capital markets at favorable rates — rising interest rates would compress equity returns and increase financing costs [4]. ROE of 13.44% is capped by regulatory rate structures below the 15% target, limiting upside even in bull scenarios and creating vulnerability to unfavorable rate case outcomes in New Jersey. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 5.7% and payout ratio of 56.47% reflect the inherent constraints of the regulated model — even the AI tailwind must flow through the slow regulated capital investment and rate case cycle [1]. Revenue growth of 18.25% in 2025 was partly driven by rate case timing and energy market conditions that may not repeat.
