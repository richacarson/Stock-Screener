Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Penumbra, Inc. designs, develops, manufactures, and markets innovative medical devices for the neurovascular and peripheral vascular markets globally. The company's products include thrombectomy systems (Lightning Flash, Indigo), embolization devices, and access catheters used to treat stroke, pulmonary embolism, and deep vein thrombosis. Founded in 2004 by CEO Adam Elsesser, headquartered in Alameda, California. Boston Scientific announced a $14.5B acquisition agreement in January 2026 at $374/share, expected to close by year-end 2026.

Investment Thesis
Penumbra delivered Q1 2026 revenue of $374.8M (up 15.6% YoY) with global thrombectomy revenue of $253.9M (up 12.1%) and embolization revenue of $120.8M (up 23.8%), continuing the company's strong double-digit growth trajectory [1]. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $1,403.7M (up 17.5%), with ex-China growth of 23.2-23.8% demonstrating the strength of the core franchise [1]. The company has $658.8M in cash, minimal debt (D/E 14%), and $215.9M in free cash flow. Boston Scientific's January 2026 acquisition agreement at $374/share ($14.5B enterprise value) validates the innovation platform and provides a defined price target [3]. The stock trades at $324.66, offering ~15% upside to the deal price if the transaction closes as expected by year-end 2026.
The primary risk is deal uncertainty. The stock trades at a 13% discount to the $374 deal price, reflecting regulatory and antitrust review risk on a $14.5B healthcare acquisition [3]. If the deal breaks, the stock would likely decline significantly from current levels given the 79.8x trailing PE and 58.4x forward PE valuations that reflect acquisition premium rather than standalone valuation. The company's 10.67% profit margin — while improving — remains below MedTech peers, and standalone Penumbra would face intensifying competition from Stryker, Medtronic, and others in thrombectomy [4]. Forward P/E of 58.4 is near the scoring floor of 60, reflecting extreme valuation. China revenue headwinds (responsible for the gap between headline 15.6% and ex-China 23%+ growth) could persist if geopolitical tensions escalate [1].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Penumbra's core business — designing and manufacturing physical medical devices (catheters, aspiration systems, embolization coils) used in life-critical interventional procedures — is structurally protected from AI disruption [1][2]. These products require FDA 510(k)/PMA regulatory approval, physician training, hospital procurement relationships, and physical manufacturing capabilities that AI cannot replicate. Thrombectomy procedures require physical devices inserted into human vasculature — AI cannot substitute for a catheter. Regulatory moats (FDA, CE Mark) and switching costs (physician training, hospital system integration) further protect the business. However, AI-enhanced diagnostic imaging could theoretically reduce some procedure volumes by enabling earlier detection and pharmaceutical treatment of clots, limiting the score below IMMUNE. AI Opportunity (1/1): Penumbra's REAL Intelligence technology represents genuine AI revenue contribution — computer-assisted automated aspiration during thrombectomy procedures that differentiates Penumbra's systems from competitors and is embedded in the company's flagship products [2].
