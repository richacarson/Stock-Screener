Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·Israel
Pagaya Technologies deploys proprietary AI and machine learning technology to evaluate consumer credit applications across personal loans, auto finance, credit cards, BNPL, and real estate. The platform integrates via API with lending partners (SoFi, LendingClub, Prosper, U.S. Bank, Ally, Klarna, Visa) to approve applicants who would otherwise be declined, using non-traditional creditworthiness indicators from trillions of evaluated applications. Q2 2026 delivered record $387M total revenue (+19% YoY), $45M GAAP net income (+$29M YoY), $124M adjusted EBITDA (+43% YoY), and $3.5B network volume (+33% YoY). Raised full-year 2026 GAAP net income guidance to $155-180M. Co-founded in 2016 by CEO Gal Krubiner.

Investment Thesis
Pagaya delivered record Q2 2026 results with $45M GAAP net income (diluted EPS $0.49, up 145% YoY), $387M total revenue (+19% YoY), $124M adjusted EBITDA (+43%), and $3.5B network volume (+33% YoY) — raising full-year GAAP net income guidance to $155-180M [1]. At approximately 11.86x forward earnings with 18.6% revenue growth, six consecutive profitable quarters, PEG of 0.37, and partnerships with SoFi, U.S. Bank, Ally, Klarna, and Visa, Pagaya trades at an extreme discount to its AI-driven growth profile. The first AAA Fitch-rated ABS, record $600M auto securitization, and CEO Krubiner's personal share purchases signal increasing institutional and insider confidence [1][3]. Free cash flow growth of 452% YoY demonstrates operating leverage materializing in the model.
The bear case centers on credit cycle vulnerability and governance overhang. Pagaya's 'second-chance lending' portfolio is inherently composed of borrowers traditional models rejected, making it disproportionately vulnerable to rising consumer delinquencies in a recession [1]. Historical share dilution has eroded per-share value, and prior Iceberg Research allegations about ABS self-dealing remain an unresolved governance concern. The IP lawsuit against Klarna, while asserting proprietary value, could create commercial relationship friction [3]. D/E of 147% reflects meaningful leverage. Profit margin of approximately 10% is improving but remains thin, and FRLPC as a percentage of network volume has shown compression, suggesting pricing pressure on the core fee model [1]. The stock's high beta means it amplifies market drawdowns severely. Revenue growth decelerated from 23% to 18.6% YoY, and any further slowdown combined with credit cycle deterioration could compress the multiple despite the low P/E.

  1. Credit cycle deterioration — rising consumer delinquencies would disproportionately impair Pagaya's portfolio of borrowers rejected by traditional credit models, potentially reversing six consecutive profitable quarters [1]

  3. Governance and litigation overhang — IP lawsuit against Klarna, historical Iceberg Research ABS allegations, and past share dilution create institutional skepticism that may cap valuation re-rating [3]
