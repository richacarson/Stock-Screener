Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·Brazil
PicS N.V. operates PicPay, Brazil's third-largest digital bank serving approximately 42 million quarterly active consumers and over 800,000 active businesses through a comprehensive financial ecosystem. The platform offers Pix instant payments, peer-to-peer transfers, multipurpose cards, personal and payroll loans, digital insurance distribution, and investment products. Founded in 2012, PicPay operates across four segments: Consumer Banking, SMB, Audiences and Ecosystem Integration, and Institutional. IPO'd on NASDAQ in January 2026. Headquartered in Sao Paulo, Brazil.
4,644 Employees ·www.picpay.com

Investment Thesis
PicPay is Brazil's third-largest digital bank with 42M+ quarterly active consumers, delivering explosive growth with Q1 2026 revenue of R$3.5B (up 70% YoY) and adjusted net income of R$169M (up 92% YoY) [1]. The company operates a comprehensive financial ecosystem encompassing payments, credit, insurance, and investments, powered by AI capabilities that rank PicPay among the top 10 global users of OpenAI [2]. At a P/E of 7.7x, forward P/E of 7.0x, and PEG ratio of 0.37, the stock trades at a deep discount to both Brazilian fintech peers and its own growth rate, reflecting its January 2026 NASDAQ IPO and limited U.S. investor awareness [3]. Q2 2026 guidance projects continued acceleration: R$3.6B revenue (13% sequential), R$245M adjusted net income, with ROE expected to recover toward 20%+ as IPO proceeds are deployed [1].
PicPay operates in a fiercely competitive Brazilian fintech market alongside entrenched players like Nu Holdings (valued at 10x+ PicPay's market cap) and Mercado Pago, requiring continuous investment in customer acquisition and product development to maintain relevance [2]. As a newly public company (IPO January 2026), PicPay has limited public financial history, and the stock has declined 33% from its $19.00 IPO price to $13.37, suggesting early investors and pre-IPO shareholders face meaningful losses [3]. Currency risk is material — BRL-denominated revenues translate to USD for investors, and any Brazilian real depreciation directly erodes ADR value. The company's credit expansion strategy carries inherent risk in Brazil's volatile macroeconomic environment, where Selic rate spikes could drive consumer defaults and compress net interest margins from the current 20.7% [1]. Average daily volume of only 187K shares ($2.5M) creates liquidity risk for institutional investors seeking to build or exit positions.
Key Catalysts

  2. Brazilian macroeconomic volatility — high Selic rate environment could accelerate consumer loan defaults in PicPay's growing credit portfolio and compress the 20.7% net interest margin
  3. Recent IPO with 33% decline from $19.00 IPO price creates overhang from pre-IPO shareholders; average daily volume of only 187K shares ($2.5M) poses liquidity risk for institutional position building or exits
