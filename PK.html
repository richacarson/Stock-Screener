Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Hotel & Motel ·NYSE ·United States
Park Hotels & Resorts Inc. is a lodging-focused real estate investment trust (REIT) that owns a portfolio of premium-branded hotels and resorts primarily operated under Hilton and Marriott brands. Spun off from Hilton Worldwide in January 2017, the company owns 41 hotels with approximately 25,000 rooms across major U.S. markets. Headquartered in Tysons, Virginia.

Investment Thesis
Park Hotels returned to profitability in Q1 2026 with $11M net income (vs. ($57M) loss in Q1 2025) on $622M revenue, driven by 5.5% RevPAR growth and strong resort performance (7.6% RevPAR growth) [1]. Hotel adjusted EBITDA of $152M at a 26% margin demonstrates operational improvement, and the company's strategic portfolio repositioning toward premium resort assets is showing results. The 6.8% dividend yield provides meaningful income for a REIT trading at $14.78, and the $2.98B market cap represents a potential discount to net asset value given the quality of the branded hotel portfolio. CEO Baltimore's 9-year tenure provides strategic continuity, and recognition as one of Newsweek's Most Responsible Companies reflects governance quality [4].
The bear case centers on structural financial weakness and modest growth. Revenue declined 1.3% YoY in Q1 2026, the company carries $4.78B in debt against only $278M cash (net debt of -$22.52 per share exceeds the stock price), and the forward P/E of 38.9x is elevated for a slow-growth REIT [1][5]. ROE of -6.53% and profit margin of -8.21% TTM reflect accounting challenges from heavy depreciation, but even FFO-based metrics show a company generating modest returns on a highly leveraged asset base. The company lowered full-year 2026 guidance, signaling cautious expectations for the lodging cycle. Macro risks are significant: a recession would compress RevPAR, business travel remains below pre-COVID levels in many urban markets, and rising interest rates increase the cost of refinancing $4.78B in debt. The San Francisco market exodus (resulting in the strategic default on the Union Square properties) demonstrated how quickly hotel asset values can deteriorate in weak urban markets.
Key Catalysts
  1. Resort portfolio RevPAR growth of 7.6% YoY demonstrates the premium positioning strategy is working, with potential for further upside as leisure travel demand strengthens in 2026-2027
  2. Strategic asset sales and portfolio simplification could unlock net asset value that the market is currently discounting, particularly if proceeds are used to reduce the $4.78B debt burden
  3. Potential for dividend increases as FFO grows from RevPAR recovery, which would attract income-focused REIT investors and support the stock price
Key Risks
  1. Elevated leverage — $4.78B debt with only $278M cash creates significant refinancing risk if interest rates remain high, and net debt of $22.52 per share exceeds the $14.78 stock price, leaving limited equity cushion
  2. Macroeconomic recession risk — a downturn would compress RevPAR, reduce occupancy, and threaten the dividend, with the COVID experience demonstrating that hotel REITs face existential pressure during demand shocks
  3. Structural urban market challenges — business travel remains below pre-COVID levels in many city center markets where PK has exposure, and remote work trends may permanently reduce demand for urban hotel rooms

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Park Hotels' core business — owning physical hotel and resort properties in premium locations — is structurally immune to AI displacement.
