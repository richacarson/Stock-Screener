Company Profile
Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·United States
Packaging Corporation of America is the third-largest producer of containerboard and corrugated packaging products in the United States, with approximately 4 million tons of annual containerboard capacity. The company operates a vertically integrated system of containerboard mills and corrugated products plants, along with a white paper segment. Q2 2026 net sales were $2.5B (+14.7% YoY) with corrugated shipments up 24%, driven by exceptionally strong e-commerce demand. Q3 2026 guidance of $2.91 EPS (ex-items) signals accelerating momentum. CEO Mark Kowlzan has led the company since 2010. Market cap approximately $20.8B with 16,800 employees. Founded 1959, headquartered in Lake Forest, Illinois.

Investment Thesis
Packaging Corporation of America delivered exceptionally strong Q2 2026 results with corrugated shipments up 24% YoY, net sales of $2.5B (+14.7%), and adjusted EPS of $2.35, with Q3 guidance accelerating to $2.91 per share (ex-items) — the strongest quarterly outlook in recent history [1][2]. As the #3 US containerboard producer with vertically integrated operations and 4M tons of annual capacity, PCA benefits from structural demand tailwinds: e-commerce growth, sustainability-driven paper-over-plastic substitution, and rising data center hardware shipments all drive corrugated packaging volumes. At 17.78x forward earnings — below the 20x target and significantly improved from prior periods — the valuation is reasonable for a company guiding toward accelerating profitability. The 23-year dividend payment history, $6.00 annual dividend (2.57% yield), and 6.91% 5-year dividend growth CAGR provide solid income characteristics [3][6].
The bear case centers on margin compression, cyclicality, and balance sheet leverage. GAAP profit margin of 7.26% is below prior year levels (10.4%) due to special items and cost pressures from the $840-870M capacity expansion program, input cost inflation, and labor pressures [1][6]. Trailing ROE of 14.89% has declined below the 15% target, reflecting the impact of special charges on earnings. D/E of 94.5% reflects $4.4B in total debt from capacity investments, creating interest expense headwinds with only $594M in cash and $433M in free cash flow [6]. The payout ratio of 68.2% on trailing earnings limits dividend growth capacity if profitability doesn't recover as Q3 guidance suggests. Corrugated packaging demand is cyclical and correlated with industrial production and consumer spending — the current 24% shipment growth is unsustainably high and a recession would compress both volumes and pricing simultaneously. The PEG of 1.51 suggests the market is pricing in significant but not extraordinary earnings growth that must be delivered to justify the current multiple.

Key Risks
