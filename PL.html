Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Planet Labs PBC designs, builds, and operates the world's largest constellation of Earth-imaging satellites (~200 satellites), capturing daily imagery of the entire planet. The company provides geospatial data, analytics, and AI-enabled solutions to agriculture, defense, forestry, finance, and government customers. FY2026 revenue reached $307.7M (26% YoY growth) with $900M+ backlog, and the company achieved its first full year of adjusted EBITDA profitability. Founded in 2010 as a Public Benefit Corporation, headquartered in San Francisco. Market cap approximately $9B.

Investment Thesis
Planet Labs has reached a commercial inflection, delivering its first full fiscal year of adjusted EBITDA profitability while growing revenue 26% YoY to $307.7M and building a record $900M+ backlog (79% YoY growth) [1]. FY2027 revenue guidance of $415-440M (39% growth at midpoint) with accelerating defense & intelligence demand (50%+ growth) provides exceptional forward visibility [2]. The company's 200+ satellite constellation captures daily imagery of Earth's entire landmass — a proprietary data moat that becomes more valuable as AI/ML applications proliferate and no competitor can replicate without matching the physical constellation. The AI-powered analytics platform is converting raw imagery into actionable intelligence, with specific defense contracts validating the evolution from data vendor to intelligence platform. The PBC structure, ALIGNED faith score (39), and zero negative Inspire attributions make Planet Labs one of the cleanest screens for values-aligned investors in the aerospace sector.
The primary concern is the disconnect between Planet's $9B market cap and its GAAP fundamentals — at $308M revenue with -80% GAAP profit margin (reflecting $247M net loss driven by satellite depreciation and stock-based compensation), the stock trades at approximately 29x TTM revenue with no path to GAAP profitability in the near term [3]. Forward P/E is incalculable given continued expected losses. Net insider selling of $29M over 12 months, while offset by CEO Marshall's $270K purchase, raises questions about management conviction at current valuations [6]. Defense and intelligence revenue concentration exposes Planet to government budget cycles, contract re-competition risk, and shifting geopolitical priorities. Capital-intensive satellite replacement cycles (constellations degrade over 3-5 years) create ongoing cash demands that could consume adjusted EBITDA gains. The stock's 52-week range of $6.10-$51.76 reflects extreme volatility, and the current $23.90 price sits well below the $51.76 peak.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2027 revenue guidance of $415-440M represents 35-43% acceleration from FY2026's 26% growth, supported by $900M+ backlog providing unprecedented execution visibility and potential for upward revisions if defense contracts accelerate
  2. Pelican next-generation constellation achieving full deployment at 50cm resolution, enabling Planet to compete in the high-resolution market currently dominated by Maxar and opening new commercial verticals
  3. AI-powered analytics platform expansion from government use cases into commercial verticals (agriculture, insurance, climate finance) where daily-cadence Earth observation data enables applications impossible with lower-frequency imagery
Key Risks
  1. GAAP profitability remains years away with -80% net margin and $247M net loss; $9B market cap at ~29x TTM revenue leaves no margin of safety if growth decelerates or defense demand normalizes
  2. Defense and intelligence revenue concentration (fastest growth segment at 50%+) exposes Planet to government budget cycles, contract re-competition, and shifting geopolitical priorities
  3. Capital-intensive satellite replacement requirements — constellation degradation over 3-5 years demands continuous capex that could consume adjusted EBITDA gains and delay sustained free cash flow generation

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Planet Labs' core business is physically immune to AI disruption — designing, launching, and operating 200+ satellites in orbit requires hardware engineering, orbital mechanics, and space infrastructure that AI cannot replicate [1]. The satellite constellation itself is a proprietary data moat; no AI model can generate real-time, daily Earth imagery without physical satellites in orbit. AI actually increases demand for Planet's geospatial data as more AI applications require Earth observation training data for agriculture, climate, defense, and insurance models. The daily-cadence global archive dating back years is irreplaceable — unlike synthetic data, it represents ground truth. The analytics layer faces some competitive pressure from AI companies processing publicly available satellite data, but Planet's proprietary daily dataset is a significant barrier [4]. AI Opportunity (1/1): Planet generates quantifiable AI revenue through its AI-powered analytics platform, with AI-enabled solutions driving over 50% revenue growth in the D&I segment, on-satellite ML processing capabilities, and specific AI-powered contracts [1]. AI is integral to the value proposition, not an add-on.
