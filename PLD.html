Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Industrial ·NYSE ·United States
Prologis, Inc. is the world's largest industrial REIT, owning and managing approximately 1.2 billion square feet of logistics and distribution real estate across 19 countries on four continents. The company's properties serve as essential infrastructure for global commerce, housing operations for e-commerce fulfillment, manufacturing, and supply chain logistics. CEO Dan Letter (succeeding co-founder Hamid Moghadam) is executing an 'Intelligent Infrastructure' strategy that includes an $8B data center investment targeting 20 projects to convert logistics facilities near urban centers into AI inference data centers. Prologis reported Q2 2026 revenue of $2.43B with record leasing of 67 million square feet, net earnings of $1.13 per diluted share (+85% YoY), and raised full-year guidance for the second time to $4.40-$4.55 EPS and $6.22-$6.30 Core FFO per share.

Investment Thesis
Prologis at $139 is the world's dominant logistics REIT transforming into an AI infrastructure platform, with record Q2 2026 leasing of 67M sq ft, net earnings up 85% YoY to $1.13 per share, and full-year guidance raised twice to $6.22-$6.30 Core FFO per share [1]. The $8B data center investment across 20 projects targeting AI inference workloads represents one of the most significant value creation opportunities in REIT history — the company's 6,000 buildings near urban centers with existing power infrastructure are uniquely positioned to serve AI data center demand that exceeds available capacity globally [2]. Same-store NOI growth of 6.4% net effective (8.5% cash) demonstrates continued pricing power in the core logistics business. The 3.07% dividend yield with 13 consecutive years of increases and 12.5% 5-year dividend growth CAGR provides reliable income growing well above inflation [4]. The Inspire Impact Score of 64 (ALIGNED) with 21 positive attributions and zero negatives makes PLD among the highest-rated stocks in the IOWN universe for faith-based investors [3].
The bear case centers on elevated valuation, execution risk on the data center pivot, and REIT structural constraints. Forward P/E of 43.71 (Price/FFO of approximately 22.3x) prices in substantial growth that depends on successful execution of the data center strategy — if data center projects are delayed, over-budget, or face permitting challenges, the premium valuation could compress [4]. The GAAP payout ratio of 154% reflects REIT distribution requirements that leave limited retained capital for the $8B data center program, likely requiring debt or equity issuance that could dilute shareholders or increase leverage above the current conservative 61% D/E [4]. Revenue growth CAGR of approximately 8% over 5 years is inflated by the Duke Realty acquisition; organic growth is lower. ROE of 6.84% and Forward P/E of 43.71 are penalized in the Infrastructure calculation, though both metrics are structurally depressed by REIT-specific GAAP accounting (high depreciation on long-lived assets). The logistics market faces potential headwinds from e-commerce growth moderation, reshoring that could shift demand patterns, and rising operating costs. The CEO transition from Moghadam to Letter introduces some execution risk for the data center strategy, though the deliberate succession planning mitigates this concern [2].
