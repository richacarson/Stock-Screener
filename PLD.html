Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Industrial ·NYSE ·United States
Prologis, Inc. is the world's largest industrial REIT, owning, operating, and developing approximately 1.2 billion square feet of logistics and distribution facilities across 20 countries. The company serves ~6,700 customers including Amazon, FedEx, and DHL. Prologis is expanding into data center development with a 5.7-gigawatt power pipeline and operates the Prologis Essentials platform (solar, EV charging, LED lighting). As a REIT, it distributes at least 90% of taxable income. Founded in 1983, headquartered in San Francisco, California.

Investment Thesis
Prologis delivered solid Q1 2026 results with $2,298M revenue (up 7.4% YoY), Core FFO of $1.50/share, and record lease signings of 64 million square feet at 95.3% occupancy [1]. Management raised 2026 guidance to Core FFO of $6.07-$6.23/share with same-store cash NOI growth of 6.25-7.00%. The 5.7-gigawatt data center power pipeline positions Prologis to capture AI infrastructure demand, while the core logistics franchise benefits from e-commerce growth, supply chain reshoring, and net effective rent changes of 31.9% that signal significant embedded growth as in-place rents roll to market [1]. The 3.1% dividend yield backed by 13 consecutive years of growth, 11.2% 5-year CAGR, and an A-rated balance sheet with $6.7B liquidity provides a reliable income profile for dividend investors [5].
The bear case centers on valuation — at 35.9x trailing earnings and forward P/E of ~43, Prologis is priced as a premium growth compounder despite 7.4% revenue growth [5]. ROE of 6.84% reflects the capital-intensive REIT model, and the GAAP payout ratio exceeds 100% (though FFO-based payout is a healthier ~69%). Industrial real estate faces cyclical risks from potential economic slowdown — occupancy peaked at 97.5% in 2022 and has normalized to 95.3%, and further softening could compress both rental rates and multiples [1]. The data center pivot enters a competitive market where specialized REITs (Equinix, Digital Realty) have deeper expertise. Rising interest rates would increase costs on $34.7B of debt (currently 3.3% weighted average) and compress REIT valuations broadly. The CEO transition from 42-year founder Moghadam to Dan Letter introduces execution uncertainty even with Letter's two decades of experience [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Data center pipeline monetization: Converting the 5.7-gigawatt power pipeline into signed hyperscaler leases could add 10-20% to Core FFO and re-rate PLD toward infrastructure REIT multiples
  2. Embedded rent growth realization: 31.9% net effective rent change on Q1 2026 renewals with in-place rents still 20-30% below market provides a multi-year organic NOI growth engine
  3. Supply chain reshoring and nearshoring trends creating sustained demand for Class A logistics space in high-barrier, high-growth markets globally
Key Risks
  1. Premium valuation at 35.9x trailing P/E leaves minimal margin of safety — any deceleration in leasing velocity or occupancy normalization below 95% could trigger significant multiple compression
  2. Rising interest rate environment would increase cost on $34.7B debt and compress REIT valuations broadly, while $6.7B liquidity provides only partial buffer against sustained rate pressure
  3. Data center expansion enters a competitive market where Equinix, Digital Realty, and hyperscalers building their own facilities create leasing, pricing, and development cost uncertainties
