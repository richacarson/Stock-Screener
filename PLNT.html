Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Leisure ·NYSE ·United States
Planet Fitness, Inc. is the largest fitness franchise in the world by number of members and locations, operating and franchising fitness centers under the 'Judgement Free Zone' brand. The company operates through three segments: Franchise, Corporate-Owned Stores, and Equipment. With approximately 2,700+ clubs and 19M+ members across the US, Canada, and international markets, Planet Fitness targets the casual or first-time gym user with $10-25/month memberships. Headquartered in Hampton, New Hampshire.
4,500 Employees ·www.planetfitness.com

Investment Thesis
Planet Fitness delivered strong Q1 2026 results with $337.2M revenue (+21.9% YoY), $51.8M net income (+23%), and diluted EPS of $0.65 (+30%), driven by franchise segment growth (+16.7%), corporate-owned club revenue (+5.2%), and equipment revenue that more than doubled (+123.4%) as franchisees invested in strength-focused upgrades [1]. At $52.36 ($4.16B market cap), the stock trades at 16.1x forward earnings and 1.63x PEG, reasonable for the largest fitness franchise with 2,700+ clubs, 19M+ members, and a capital-efficient model generating $1.29B TTM revenue at 17.8% net margin. The 'Judgement Free Zone' brand commands dominant market share in budget fitness, and 2026 guidance of ~9% revenue growth with ~10% adjusted EBITDA growth signals sustainable expansion [1].
The bear case centers on slowing member growth, cultural controversy fallout, and leverage. Net new member additions declined 36% YoY in Q1 2026 with elevated churn, suggesting the revenue growth is driven by price increases and equipment sales rather than membership expansion — a concerning signal for a business model predicated on volume [1]. The 2024 transgender locker room controversy wiped $400M in market value and generated sustained boycott pressure, with additional incidents in 2025 amplifying reputational risk [3]. The stock has fallen 52% over the past year, reflecting investor concern. Negative book equity (D/E -514%) with $2.94B in debt against $474M cash creates leveraged exposure to any revenue deceleration, and the franchise model's reliance on new club openings for growth faces saturation risk in the US market. CEO Keating is only 13 months into her tenure, creating execution uncertainty during a period requiring decisive leadership [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Equipment revenue surge of 123.4% in Q1 2026 signals a multi-year club refresh cycle that drives near-term franchise segment revenue and positions clubs for improved member experience and retention
  2. International expansion into underpenetrated markets beyond North America represents a long-term growth vector for the franchise model at minimal capital investment
  3. New CEO Colleen Keating's hospitality and franchise expertise could reinvigorate membership growth through improved member value proposition and brand marketing
Key Risks
  1. Net new member additions declined 36% YoY in Q1 2026 with elevated churn — if the membership base stagnates or contracts, the franchise revenue model faces structural growth deceleration [1]

  3. Negative book equity with $2.94B debt against $474M cash creates leveraged downside exposure; 52% stock price decline over the past year reflects market skepticism about the growth trajectory [4]
