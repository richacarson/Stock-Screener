Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
Portland General Electric Company is a vertically integrated electric utility serving approximately 930,000 customers in the Portland, Oregon metropolitan area. PGE generates, transmits, distributes, and sells electricity, operating a diverse fleet including thermal, wind, solar, and hydroelectric resources. The company is a leading beneficiary of AI-driven data center electricity demand, with 400+ MW of data center capacity planned by 2029 through its GridCARE AI partnership. Q2 2026 delivered $814M revenue with 11% industrial load growth from data centers, and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance reaffirmed at $3.33-$3.53.

Investment Thesis
Portland General Electric is a well-positioned regulated utility at the intersection of AI-driven data center electricity demand and Oregon's clean energy transition. Industrial load growth of 11% YoY in Q2 2026 validates the data center demand thesis, with FY2026 adjusted guidance reaffirmed at $3.33-$3.53 EPS and long-term EPS growth of 5-7% [1]. At 13.0x forward P/E with a 4.10% dividend yield, 20+ consecutive years of payments, and the GridCARE AI partnership energizing 400+ MW of data center capacity by 2029, PGE offers reliable income with differentiated growth exposure. The first U.S. utility to join The Climate Pledge, PGE's commitment to 100% clean energy by 2040 positions it favorably for sustainable capital deployment [3]. Weather-normalized load growth of 1.5-2.5% provides baseline visibility beyond the data center opportunity.
The bear case centers on execution risk, regulatory constraints, and wildfire liability. Revenue growth of only 0.9% YoY in Q2 2026 despite strong industrial demand suggests that wholesale volume declines and mix shifts are offsetting retail growth [1]. ROE of 6.45% reflects the regulated return framework that caps upside regardless of data center demand growth — PGE cannot fully capitalize on the AI boom if regulators limit allowed returns. D/E of 136% is elevated even for a utility, and the TTM payout ratio of 84.5% on trailing earnings leaves limited dividend coverage cushion. Wildfire liability remains existential — the $383M mitigation investment may not fully protect against catastrophic scenarios, as PG&E's California experience proved. Oregon consumer advocates have challenged how PGE allocates data center power costs, creating regulatory risk to the growth narrative. Dividend growth of only 3.1% CAGR over five years barely keeps pace with inflation, and profit margin of 7.3% remains well below the 20% infrastructure target [3].
