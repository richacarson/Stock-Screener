Southport Acquisition II Corp. is a blank check company (SPAC) that completed a $200M IPO on October 1, 2026, offering 20 million units at $10 each on the NYSE under the symbol PORT.U. Led by CEO Jeb Spencer (co-founder and Managing Partner of TVC Capital), the company intends to pursue an initial business combination focused on AI, including enterprise AI applications, workflow automation, AI data and governance, AI infrastructure, industrial AI and robotics, and healthcare AI and life-sciences software. No acquisition target has been disclosed.

Investment Thesis
Southport Acquisition II at $10.00 (IPO price) with $200M in trust is a pre-revenue blank check company targeting AI acquisitions across enterprise applications, workflow automation, AI governance, infrastructure, and healthcare AI [1]. The SPAC is led by Jeb Spencer, whose TVC Capital background in software-focused growth equity provides relevant deal-sourcing capability, and whose prior Southport Acquisition I successfully merged with Angel Studios (now NYSE: ANGX). The broad AI focus positions the vehicle to capitalize on the AI spending wave, and the $200M trust size provides meaningful acquisition capacity for mid-market AI companies.
The bear case is fundamental: SPACs have a poor historical track record, with over 60% of 2020-2021 vintage SPACs trading below their IPO price. The entity has no operating business, no revenue, no management team beyond the sponsors, and no disclosed acquisition target [1]. Shareholders face the standard SPAC risks: sponsor dilution through warrants (1/2 warrant per unit), the potential for a deal that benefits sponsors more than public shareholders, and the risk that the AI target is overvalued in a frothy market. The 18-24 month deal timeline means capital is locked in a low-return trust while the AI landscape evolves rapidly. If no suitable target is found, capital is returned at approximately $10/share minus expenses. The IOWN framework is not designed to evaluate blank check companies, and this report should be interpreted accordingly.

Key Risks
  1. Pre-revenue blank check entity with no operating business, no disclosed acquisition target, and no timeline — shareholders are betting entirely on management's ability to identify, negotiate, and close a value-creating AI deal within 18-24 months [1]
  2. Standard SPAC structural risks — sponsor warrants providing 1/2 warrant per unit create meaningful dilution, and the sponsor promote structure misaligns incentives between sponsors who profit from any deal and shareholders who profit only from good deals
  3. AI market froth risk — premium valuations across AI-related companies increase the probability of overpaying for a target, and the rapid pace of AI technology change means today's promising AI company could face obsolescence before the merger closes
