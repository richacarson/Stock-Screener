Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYQ ·United States
PPL Corporation provides electricity and natural gas to approximately 3.6 million customers in the United States through regulated utility segments in Kentucky, Pennsylvania, and Rhode Island. The company generates, transmits, distributes, and sells electricity from coal, gas, hydro, and solar sources. CEO Vincent Sorgi has led the company since 2020, overseeing the $10.4B divestiture of UK operations (WPD) and acquisition of Rhode Island Energy to refocus as a pure-play US regulated utility. Q2 2026 reported EPS of $0.33, with reaffirmed 2026 ongoing EPS guidance of $1.90-$1.98 and 6-8% EPS growth through 2029. Founded 1920, headquartered in Allentown, Pennsylvania.

Investment Thesis
PPL Corporation at $34.25 with a $25.8B market cap is a well-positioned regulated utility offering a compelling total return profile. Forward P/E of 16.16x is attractive for a utility projecting 6-8% EPS growth through 2029, and the 3.33% dividend yield with ongoing increases provides 9-11% total return potential [1][3]. Q2 2026 ongoing EPS of $0.33 and reaffirmed full-year guidance of $1.90-$1.98 demonstrate steady execution. The $23B capital plan through 2029 generates 10.3% average annual rate base growth, with AI data center demand providing a secular tailwind in PPL's Pennsylvania service territory [1][4]. CEO Sorgi's 20-year tenure and the CTO/CIO appointment signal proactive positioning for grid modernization and technology-enabled operations. The balance sheet post-UK divestiture provides a clean platform for disciplined growth.
The bear case centers on regulated utility structural constraints and dividend credibility. D/E of 135.4% reflects heavy leverage required for capital deployment, and negative free cash flow of approximately -$1.9B indicates capex exceeds cash generation, requiring continued capital market access that rising interest rates would make more expensive [3]. ROE of 8.6% is structurally constrained by regulated allowed returns of 9-10%, limiting upside even as AI drives demand. The 2021 dividend cut from $1.66 to $0.90 broke a multi-decade growth streak — only 4 consecutive years of growth since the reset and a 5-year dividend growth CAGR of -7.3% limit credibility versus peers like NEE or SO with unbroken decades-long records [3]. PPL's Kentucky segment includes coal-fired generation facing long-term stranded asset risk as clean energy economics improve [5]. Revenue growth of 4.2% YoY is modest, and organic growth in regulated territories is ultimately capped by rate commission approvals.
