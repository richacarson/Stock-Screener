Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Gold ·NASDAQ ·United States
Perpetua Resources Corp. is a development-stage mining company advancing the Stibnite Gold Project in central Idaho. The project is expected to produce approximately 450,000 ounces of gold annually over its first four years and supply up to 35% of US antimony demand — a critical mineral used in defense ammunition, flame retardants, and semiconductors currently sourced almost entirely from China. Formerly Midas Gold Corp., the company renamed in February 2021 to reflect its broader mission of environmental restoration alongside mineral extraction. Headquartered in Boise, Idaho.

Investment Thesis
Perpetua Resources is a uniquely positioned pre-revenue mining company with $669.5M cash, minimal debt (D/E ~0.5%), and a $6.1B NPV project (at $4,500 gold) that would produce 450,000 oz gold/year and supply 35% of US antimony demand [1][2]. The US EXIM Bank advanced a proposed $2.7B senior secured loan to final board vote in spring 2026, which combined with cash on hand would fully finance the project's $2.576B direct capital costs. The DOD has committed $80M+ to the project, JPMorgan invested through its $1.5T national security fund, and the project received final US Forest Service approval in January 2025 after 8 years of permitting [2]. With gold at $3,300+/oz and antimony prices surging due to China export restrictions, Stibnite's economics are compelling — less than 2-year payback period with 15-year reserve life. At ~$2.3B market cap, the stock trades at roughly 38% of project NPV.
The bear case centers on execution risk, pre-revenue uncertainty, and environmental opposition. Production is still approximately 3 years away (~2029), during which the company generates zero revenue and relies entirely on its cash position and EXIM financing approval [1]. If the $2.7B EXIM loan is not approved or is delayed, the company would need alternative financing at potentially unfavorable terms. Environmental litigation persists — Idaho conservation groups sued over the air quality permit in July 2025, and the Nez Perce Tribe filed suit against the Forest Service in August 2025, creating permitting uncertainty despite the final federal approval [3]. Construction cost overruns are endemic to mining projects; the $2.576B estimate carries meaningful risk of escalation. The single-asset nature of the business means there is no diversification — if Stibnite fails, the company has no fallback. Gold price sensitivity is substantial: NPV drops materially below $3,000/oz gold.
Key Catalysts
  1. US EXIM Board final vote on the proposed $2.7B senior secured loan expected H2 2026 — approval would fully fund the project and remove the primary financing risk overhang [1]
  2. Final investment and construction decision expected H2 2026, which would transition the company from development to construction phase with a 3-year build timeline to production
  3. Antimony price surge driven by China export restrictions creates unprecedented demand for domestic supply — Stibnite would be the only significant US source, serving defense and semiconductor applications [2]
Key Risks
  1. Pre-revenue development-stage company with ~3 years to production and zero revenue — $669.5M cash provides runway but any EXIM loan delay or denial creates critical financing risk [1]
  2. Environmental litigation from Nez Perce Tribe and Idaho conservation groups could delay or restrict operations despite Forest Service approval, with air quality permit challenges ongoing [3]
  3. Single-asset concentration in one project (Stibnite) with no operational diversification — construction cost overruns, geological surprises, or permitting reversals represent existential risk
