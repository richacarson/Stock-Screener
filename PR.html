Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYQ ·United States
Permian Resources Corporation is an independent oil and natural gas company focused on developing crude oil and associated liquids-rich natural gas reserves in the Delaware Basin, a sub-basin of the Permian Basin. Formed in September 2022 through the merger of Centennial Resource Development and Colgate Energy Partners, the company operates primarily in Reeves County, Texas and Lea County, New Mexico. Headquartered in Midland, Texas with 515 employees, Permian Resources generated TTM revenue of approximately $5.7B with a 21.5% profit margin and trades at 9.9x forward earnings.

Investment Thesis
Permian Resources at $22 with an $18.6B market cap offers compelling value in the Permian Basin at 9.9x forward earnings with a 2.9% dividend yield. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.86B beat estimates by $220M with adjusted EPS of $0.69 versus $0.53 expected, reflecting strong operational execution and production growth [1]. The Delaware Basin acreage position provides multi-year drilling inventory, the balance sheet is conservatively managed with D/E of only 26.2%, and the dividend has grown from $0.05 to $0.16 quarterly since inception. Forward consensus EPS of $2.24 implies a 47% increase from trailing, supported by production growth, operational synergies from the 2022 merger integration, and favorable commodity pricing dynamics.
The bear case centers on commodity price exposure and decelerating growth. The 55% YoY revenue growth is substantially driven by merger anniversary effects and production ramp — organic growth rates will normalize to single digits as base production matures. ROE of 11.4% trails the 15% target, and the equity-heavy capital structure constrains capital efficiency. Oil price sensitivity remains the dominant risk: a sustained decline below $60/bbl WTI would pressure margins, potentially forcing dividend cuts in a company with only four years of payout history and no track record through a commodity downcycle [1][2]. Additionally, Permian Basin maturation risks — declining initial production rates per well, increasing water-handling costs, and regulatory tightening on flaring and emissions — could structurally raise operating costs over time. The payout ratio of 40.8% leaves limited cushion for dividend maintenance if commodity prices weaken significantly.
Key Catalysts
  1. Delaware Basin drilling inventory provides multi-year organic production growth runway, with potential for extended lateral wells and co-development optimization to reduce per-BOE costs and sustain capital-efficient returns [1]
  2. AI data center demand driving structural increase in natural gas consumption for power generation, supporting long-term commodity prices and Permian Basin production economics [2]
  3. Balance sheet capacity for accretive bolt-on acquisitions — D/E of 26.2% and strong cash flow generation provide firepower for Permian Basin consolidation as smaller operators seek exits in a challenging capital environment [1]
Key Risks
  1. Direct commodity price exposure with limited hedging visibility — revenues and margins are tied to WTI crude and Henry Hub natural gas prices, with no diversified revenue streams to cushion a sustained downcycle [1]
  2. Permian Basin maturation risk as legacy wells decline and operators face increasing water disposal costs, reduced well productivity from infill drilling, and tighter environmental regulations on flaring and methane emissions [2]
  3. Young dividend with no downcycle track record — the quarterly payout was established in late 2022 and has not been tested through a commodity price crash or sustained capital spending compression, with a 40.8% payout ratio limiting margin of safety [3]
