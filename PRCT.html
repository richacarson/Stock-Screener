Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
PROCEPT BioRobotics develops, manufactures, and sells the HYDROS Robotic System (with FirstAssist AI) for image-guided, minimally invasive urologic surgery. The company's proprietary Aquablation therapy uses waterjet tissue ablation to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), a condition affecting 40M+ men in the U.S. With 765 installed systems in the U.S. and 125,000+ patients treated to date, PROCEPT is pursuing profitability by Q4 2026 under new CEO Larry Wood.

Investment Thesis
PROCEPT BioRobotics is a category-creating surgical robotics company approaching a profitability inflection, with Q1 2026 revenue of $83.1M (+20% YoY), 12,200 U.S. procedures (+30%), and a 765-system installed base growing 40% YoY [1]. The 2026 Investor Day outlined a clear multi-year financial path: $390-410M revenue (27-33% growth), 65% gross margins improving to 68-70% by 2027, EBITDA-positive by Q4 2026, and $25-30M EBITDA in 2027 [2]. The WATER IV prostate cancer trial completing ahead of schedule by mid-2026 could unlock a massive new indication. At $59.21 per share ($3.3B market cap), the stock has recovered significantly from lows, reflecting growing confidence in the profitability path. The low D/E of 21% and $245.6M cash provide balance sheet safety to reach self-funding [1]. TD Cowen raised its price target to $99 based on the AI-integrated HYDROS platform's commercial trajectory.
The bear case focuses on persistent unprofitability and growth deceleration. Revenue guidance was reduced from an initial $410-430M to $390-410M, signaling that the 37% FY2025 growth rate is decelerating toward the mid-20s [1]. The company expects $78.5-91.5M in net losses for 2026, consuming $245.6M cash reserves at a rate that could require dilutive financing if the profitability timeline slips. Q1 2026 net loss widened to $31.6M from $24.7M YoY despite 20% revenue growth, as SG&A and R&D spending outpaced revenue scale [1]. Single-product concentration in Aquablation therapy creates binary risk — any safety signal, reimbursement change, or competitive breakthrough from Olympus or Boston Scientific could be existential. Handpiece ASP of ~$3,500 and handpiece-to-procedure ratio of 95% leave limited consumable pricing upside. The CEO transition from founder Zadno to Wood creates execution risk during a critical commercialization phase.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): PROCEPT's core business — physical robotic surgery in sterile operating rooms with board-certified urologists — is structurally immune to AI substitution. Aquablation therapy requires specialized hardware (the HYDROS system at ~$485K ASP), single-use handpieces (~$3,500 each), surgeon training, and FDA-cleared protocols that AI cannot replicate or displace [1][4]. The regulatory barriers (510(k) clearance, clinical trials, reimbursement coding) create deep moats against AI-driven substitution. AI-enhanced competing treatments (UroLift, GreenLight laser, TURP) represent competitive risk rather than AI disruption risk. The growing counter-UAS defense market is driven by AI-amplified drone proliferation that creates more surgical case volume as aging populations expand the BPH addressable market. AI Opportunity (1/1): The HYDROS system with FirstAssist AI ships to customers as an AI-integrated surgical platform, generating revenue through system sales ($23.4M U.S. system revenue in Q1 2026, +25% YoY) and AI-enhanced procedural consumables [1][4].
