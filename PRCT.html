Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
PROCEPT BioRobotics develops, manufactures, and sells the HYDROS Robotic System (with FirstAssist AI) for image-guided, minimally invasive urologic surgery. The company's proprietary Aquablation therapy uses waterjet tissue ablation to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), a condition affecting 40M+ men in the U.S. With 765+ installed systems and 125,000+ patients treated to date, PROCEPT is pursuing profitability by Q4 2026 and targeting $25-30M adjusted EBITDA in 2027. Q2 2026 revenue reached $94.5M. Stock has declined 67% from prior highs to $19.47.

Investment Thesis
PROCEPT BioRobotics is a category-creating surgical robotics company approaching a profitability inflection at a deeply discounted valuation — Q2 2026 revenue of $94.5M continues 19% YoY growth toward full-year guidance of $390-410M (27-33% growth), with 125,000+ patients treated and 765+ installed HYDROS systems [1][2]. The 2026 Investor Day outlined a clear path: 65% gross margins in 2026 expanding to 68-70% in 2027, EBITDA-positive by Q4 2026, and $25-30M adjusted EBITDA in 2027 [2]. The WATER IV prostate cancer trial completing mid-2026 could unlock a massive new indication with $1B+ TAM potential. At $19.47 ($1.1B market cap), the stock trades at a fraction of its prior valuation, creating asymmetric upside if the profitability crossover materializes and prostate cancer data proves positive. Conservative D/E of 22.8% and cash reserves provide balance sheet runway to reach self-funding.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability, growth deceleration, and single-product concentration. The 67% stock decline reflects market skepticism about the timeline to GAAP profitability — with -32.5% profit margin, -30.2% ROE, and negative forward P/E, PROCEPT is consuming cash to fund commercialization at a pace that could require dilutive financing if targets slip [1][4]. Revenue growth decelerated from 37% in FY2025 to 19.3% TTM, and guidance was reduced from an initial $410-430M to $390-410M, raising questions about the demand trajectory [1]. R&D and SG&A spending continues to outpace revenue scale, widening losses despite top-line growth. Single-product concentration in Aquablation therapy creates binary risk — any safety signal, reimbursement change, or competitive breakthrough from Olympus or Boston Scientific could be existential. The CEO transition from founder Zadno to Wood creates execution risk during the critical profitability pivot.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (8/9): PROCEPT's core business — physical robotic surgery performed by board-certified urologists in sterile operating rooms — is structurally immune to AI substitution [1][4]. Aquablation therapy requires specialized capital equipment (the HYDROS system at ~$485K ASP), single-use handpieces (~$3,500 each), surgeon credentialing, hospital purchasing committees, and FDA-cleared protocols that AI cannot replicate or displace. The regulatory barriers (510(k) clearance, multi-year clinical trials, reimbursement coding through CMS) create deep structural moats. AI actually increases PROCEPT's advantage through FirstAssist AI-enhanced treatment planning while competitors face the same regulatory barriers to AI-enabled alternatives. Physical surgical intervention for BPH cannot be replaced by software. AI Opportunity (1/1): The HYDROS system with FirstAssist AI ships as an AI-integrated surgical platform, with AI-enhanced image guidance generating revenue through both system sales and procedural consumables [4]. The AI component differentiates PROCEPT's platform from competing BPH treatments (TURP, GreenLight laser, UroLift) by enabling consistent outcomes independent of surgeon experience.
