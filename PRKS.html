Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Leisure ·NYSE ·United States
United Parks & Resorts Inc. (formerly SeaWorld Entertainment) operates theme parks and entertainment destinations across the United States, including marine-life parks under the SeaWorld brand in San Diego, Orlando, and San Antonio; Busch Gardens family destination parks in Tampa Bay and Williamsburg; and Aquatica water parks. The company rebranded from SeaWorld Entertainment to United Parks & Resorts in 2025 to broaden its identity beyond the SeaWorld marine-park brand.

Investment Thesis
United Parks & Resorts posted a soft Q2 2026 with revenue of $483.3 million (missing the $485.2 million consensus) and EPS of $1.34 versus a $1.62 estimate, driven by a 3.6% attendance decline tied to an unfavorable Easter calendar shift and weaker international visitation [1]. The one bright spot is record in-park per-capita spending growth of 5.1%, showing the company retains pricing power even as traffic softens. At $44.26 (forward P/E 12.09), the stock trades cheaply against a $47.30 average analyst target, but the discount reflects real balance-sheet fragility.
The balance sheet is the central risk: $2.27 billion of total debt against a $557.2 million stockholders' deficit produces a deeply negative debt-to-equity ratio and a -45.67% ROE, both scoring at the infrastructure floor [2]. Layered on top is unresolved animal-welfare reputational risk (ongoing USDA Animal Welfare Act citations, lingering Blackfish-era brand damage) and a negative Glassdoor culture rating, which together weigh on the Infinite Game and Inspiration scores despite a genuine, well-funded marine-rescue program. AI risk is minimal given the physical, experiential nature of the business, but that is the one clear positive in an otherwise financially strained profile.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record in-park per-capita spending growth (+5.1% in Q2 2026) demonstrates pricing power that could offset soft attendance if guest counts stabilize
  2. Continued new-attraction openings across the SeaWorld, Busch Gardens, and Aquatica portfolio could reverse the attendance decline into 2027
  3. Analyst consensus rating of Buy with a $47.30 price target implies modest upside if leverage concerns ease
Key Risks
  1. Negative stockholders' equity (-$557.2 million deficit) against $2.27 billion in debt leaves minimal balance-sheet cushion if attendance trends do not improve
  2. Attendance declined 3.6% in Q2 2026 with EPS missing consensus by a wide margin, and TTM revenue growth remains negative at -3.54%
  3. Recurring animal-welfare scrutiny (USDA APHIS citations, lingering Blackfish reputational overhang) creates ongoing headline and regulatory risk to the SeaWorld brand
