United Parks & Resorts Inc. (formerly SeaWorld Entertainment, rebranded February 2024) operates theme parks and entertainment destinations across the United States, including marine-life parks under the SeaWorld brand in San Diego, Orlando, and San Antonio; Busch Gardens family destination parks in Tampa Bay and Williamsburg; Aquatica water parks; Discovery Cove; and Sesame Place parks in Philadelphia and San Diego. Founded in 1959 and headquartered in Orlando, the company reported H1 2026 revenue of $761.6M with 9.3M guests and generated $97M in free cash flow on a trailing basis, but carries $2.4B in debt against negative stockholders' equity.

Investment Thesis
United Parks & Resorts at 7.4x forward earnings represents one of the deepest value plays in the leisure sector, pricing in significant pessimism about attendance trends, leverage, and the 32.8% short interest overhang [5]. The underlying business generates $410M in operating cash flow and $97M in free cash flow on $1.65B in revenue, demonstrating that the theme park model remains profitable despite declining traffic [1]. Record in-park per capita spending of $40.62 (+5.3% in Q2 2026) proves pricing power, and H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $253.5M supports debt service. The September 2026 leadership restructuring — promoting 30-year veteran Kyle Miller to President and Scott Maupin to CPOO — signals a more operationally focused management team targeting the back-half growth management has guided [2].
The bear case is substantial and well-funded (32.8% short interest). Negative stockholders' equity of -$435.8M against $2.41B in total debt with only $19.1M cash creates severe refinancing and solvency risk — the company relies entirely on operating cash flow to service its obligations [5]. Revenue has declined from $1.73B (2022) to $1.65B (TTM), attendance is falling (Q2 2026 -2.9%, Q1 -5.1%), and EPS declined 7.6% YoY despite the forward PE compression from $44 to $32 [1]. The stock has fallen 42% from its $55.71 52-week high, and the massive short interest suggests institutional conviction that the leveraged balance sheet is unsustainable if attendance trends do not reverse. Blackfish-era reputational damage continues to linger despite the 2024 rebrand, and competition from Universal's Epic Universe (opened June 2025 in Orlando) threatens market share in the company's core Florida geography [1].
