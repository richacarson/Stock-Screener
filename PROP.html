Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NASDAQ ·United States
Prairie Operating Co. is an independent energy company engaged in the acquisition, development, and production of crude oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids in the oil and liquids-rich Niobrara and Codell formations of Colorado's Denver-Julesburg (DJ) Basin. Scaled rapidly since 2023 through a reverse merger and the transformative Nickel Road Operating acquisition, reaching $311M TTM revenue. In March 2026, CEO Edward Kovalik resigned and President Gary Hanna retired; Richard Frommer was appointed interim CEO, bringing 40+ years of DJ Basin E&P experience from Great Western Petroleum.

Investment Thesis
Prairie Operating has scaled rapidly since its 2023 DJ Basin pivot, posting Q2 2026 revenue of $98.9M (+45% YoY) and H1 2026 operating cash flow of $94.3M, with full-year guidance reaffirmed at $240-260M Adjusted EBITDA [1]. The stock has more than doubled from ~$0.78 to $1.70 since mid-2026, yet still trades at ~2.5x forward earnings on a basic share basis — a fraction of E&P peer multiples — suggesting the market remains skeptical about execution. The core business is structurally immune to AI disruption, and rising data center energy demand could provide secular tailwinds. The Inspire score of 42 (ALIGNED) is notable for an E&P company.
The bear case is governance and financial quality. The March 2026 departure of both CEO Edward Kovalik and President Gary Hanna introduces existential leadership risk for a company this small — key-person dependency is acute when leadership exits months after a transformative acquisition [2]. D/E of 155% and H1 capex of $132.6M against $94.3M operating cash flow creates negative free cash flow, risking further dilutive issuances. ROE of -49% and profit margin of -45.5% (driven by $132M derivative losses) show the company remains unprofitable on a GAAP basis despite strong EBITDA [4]. The massive diluted share count (~843M vs. ~100M basic shares) from warrants and convertible instruments creates severe potential dilution that could prevent meaningful per-share value creation even if EBITDA targets are met [1]. No analyst coverage depth, no ESG data availability, and sub-$200M market cap make this a speculative micro-cap unsuitable for most portfolios.
