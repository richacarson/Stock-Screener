Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NMS ·United States
Paramount Skydance Corporation is a global media and entertainment company formed through Skydance Media's acquisition of Paramount Global in 2025. The company operates CBS Television Network, Paramount+, Pluto TV, Nickelodeon, MTV, BET, Comedy Central, Paramount Pictures, and Showtime across Studios, Direct-to-Consumer, and TV Media segments. Under CEO David Ellison, the company targets $30B in annual revenue and $3.8B in adjusted EBITDA for 2026, with 17,600 employees. The company is pursuing the acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery for an estimated $6B+ in synergies. Headquartered in New York, NY.

Investment Thesis
Paramount Skydance at $9.78 with a $11.0B market cap is a deep-value turnaround play on legacy media restructuring under tech-savvy leadership. The stock trades at just 11.75x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.1, reflecting an expected earnings recovery as Ellison's cost discipline takes hold — Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA surged 59% YoY to $1.16B, and Q2 delivered 27% EBITDA growth with Paramount+ revenue up 16% [1][2]. The company guides to $30B in revenue and $3.8B in adjusted EBITDA for 2026, and the pending WBD acquisition targets $6B+ in synergies that would create the world's second-largest media company. CBS's NFL and live sports rights, Nickelodeon's kids franchise, and Paramount Pictures' film library provide irreplaceable content moats that the streaming wars have undervalued [1].
The bear case is significant: Paramount Skydance is a negative-margin company carrying $16.2B in debt against only $1.6B in cash, with a D/E ratio of 126% that would worsen substantially if the WBD acquisition closes [5]. Revenue growth is essentially zero at 0.9% YoY as streaming gains merely offset accelerating linear TV declines, and the company has yet to demonstrate it can grow the consolidated top line [1][2]. The WBD deal adds enormous integration risk and regulatory uncertainty on top of an already complex Skydance-Paramount integration. AI poses a meaningful structural risk to scripted content production — the company's largest cost center — and the 2023 Hollywood strikes demonstrated that AI displacement anxiety is already disrupting talent relationships. The recently resumed $0.05 quarterly dividend yields 2.1% but consumes 93% of earnings, suggesting it could be cut if profitability falters [5]. Ellison's track record running a major media conglomerate is exactly zero — he is a first-time CEO of a public company with 17,600 employees and $29B in revenue.
Key Catalysts
  1. Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition closing Q3 2026, targeting $6B+ in synergies and creating the world's second-largest media company with combined content libraries spanning HBO, CNN, Warner Bros., and Paramount brands [4]
  2. Paramount+ and Pluto TV technology platform integration within 18 months, reducing streaming infrastructure costs and enabling unified ad targeting across premium and FAST content, expected to improve direct-to-consumer margins significantly [3]
  3. CBS live sports rights (NFL, March Madness, PGA Tour) providing stable, high-value advertising revenue immune to cord-cutting acceleration, anchoring the TV Media segment's cash generation [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Execution risk on dual integrations — the Skydance-Paramount merger is still underway while the WBD acquisition adds a second massive restructuring, risking management distraction, talent attrition, and cultural clashes across three legacy organizations [3][4]
  2. AI-driven disruption of scripted content production — the company's largest cost center — as generative AI tools increasingly replicate writing, visual effects, and localization at a fraction of human talent costs, potentially compressing content budgets industry-wide [3]
  3. Unsustainable capital structure with $16.2B in debt, negative profit margins, and a 93% payout ratio on the recently resumed dividend, leaving minimal financial cushion if the earnings recovery stalls or WBD integration costs exceed projections [5]
