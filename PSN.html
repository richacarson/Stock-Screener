Company Profile
Technology ·Information Technology Services ·NYSE ·United States
Parsons Corporation provides technology-driven defense, intelligence, and critical infrastructure solutions for the US federal government and allied nations worldwide. It operates through Federal Solutions (cyber, missile defense, intelligence, space, electronic warfare) and Critical Infrastructure (transportation, environmental, digital engineering) segments. Founded in 1944 and headquartered in Chantilly, Virginia, Parsons has evolved from a traditional engineering firm into a tech-enabled defense contractor with embedded AI capabilities. CEO Carey Smith has led since 2021. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.6B (-1% YoY, +8% ex-confidential contracts), with record $9.3B backlog (71% funded) and $1.9B in new bookings (+24% YoY). Full-year 2026 revenue guidance is $6.2-$6.5B with adjusted EBITDA of $500-$560M.

Investment Thesis
Parsons Corporation at 12.4x forward earnings represents a compelling value entry point for a technology-enabled defense contractor with embedded AI capabilities and a record $9.3B backlog (71% funded) [1]. The stock has declined 53% from its 52-week high of $89.50 to $42.44, creating a valuation disconnect between the market price and the underlying business quality. Q2 2026 bookings of $1.9B grew 24% YoY with a 1.2x quarterly book-to-bill ratio, and 8 of the last 10 $100M+ wins included AI differentiators [3]. The 5yr revenue CAGR of ~12% demonstrates sustained growth, the D/E of 59.9% provides balance sheet safety, and the Glassdoor #27 Best-Led ranking signals organizational health [4]. Adjusted EBITDA margins expanded to a record 10.1% in both Q1 and Q2 2026, demonstrating improving profitability despite revenue headwinds [2]. An additional $11 billion in contract awards have not yet been booked into backlog, providing multi-year revenue visibility.
The bear case centers on topline pressure, margin fragility, and the gap between strong bookings and reported revenue. Revenue declined 0.5% YoY in Q2 and fell 4% in Q1, with organic revenue down 5-8% when normalizing for confidential contracts [1][2]. The $300M revenue guidance reduction (to $6.2-6.5B midpoint) reflects portfolio divestitures, timing delays, and a one-time JV charge — raising questions about the conversion rate from the $9.3B backlog to recognized revenue. Profit margin of 2.5% is thin even by government services standards, and ROE of 8.2% is well below the 15% target, limiting return on capital [5]. The trailing P/E of 29.3x suggests that trailing earnings are depressed relative to forward expectations (forward EPS of $3.42 vs trailing $1.45), creating risk if the EPS recovery does not materialize. Defense budget uncertainty and potential DOGE-driven government efficiency cuts could pressure contract volumes. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors.
