Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Pershing Square USA, Ltd. is a closed-end equity fund launched in April 2026 via a $5 billion IPO on the NYSE, managed by Pershing Square Capital Management, L.P. ($26.6B total AUM as of March 2026). The fund invests in 12-15 high-quality, predominantly North American-listed, large-capitalization growth companies at discounts to intrinsic value. Current holdings include Amazon, Microsoft, Uber, Meta, Brookfield, Restaurant Brands, Fannie Mae, and Freddie Mac, with approximately 55% of the portfolio concentrated in 4 AI-related stocks. The fund trades at a ~21% discount to NAV ($39.67 vs. $50.32 estimated NAV), which Bill Ackman has called 'frankly absurd.' Annual expense ratio of 2.20% with no performance fee.

Investment Thesis
PSUS offers access to Bill Ackman's 16% annualized track record (since 2004) through a permanent capital structure at a ~21% discount to NAV ($39.67 vs. $50.32 estimated NAV) [1]. The portfolio is concentrated in 12 high-quality large-cap names with ~55% in 4 AI stocks (Microsoft, Amazon, Meta), Uber, Brookfield, and Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac, providing significant exposure to the AI infrastructure buildout and financial sector catalysts [2]. Ackman and affiliates have invested $500+ million in personal capital, creating exceptional alignment — the strongest dollar-value insider commitment across the entire screen universe [1]. The permanent capital structure eliminates redemption pressure, enabling the patient, concentrated investing approach that generated 16% annualized returns over 22 years. If the NAV discount narrows from 21% to the 10-15% range typical of mature closed-end funds, shareholders would capture 7-13% in additional return beyond portfolio performance.
The bear case centers on the persistent NAV discount, the 2.20% expense ratio, and key-person risk. PSUS has traded at a 20-21% discount since its April 2026 IPO, and closed-end fund discounts often persist for years — there is no redemption mechanism to force convergence, and Seeking Alpha analysis projects 'further discounts over time' [3]. The 2.20% annual expense ratio is high relative to ETFs and mutual funds offering similar large-cap equity exposure at 0.03-0.10%, creating a ~2% annual headwind that Ackman must overcome just to match passive alternatives [3]. PSH (the London-listed sister fund) was down -4.7% YTD through May 2026, underperforming the S&P 500 during the same period. Key-person risk is extreme — the entire value proposition depends on Ackman's judgment, and his concentrated style has produced both spectacular gains and devastating losses (Valeant -$4B, JC Penney). The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects limited ESG data available for this newly listed closed-end fund. With zero operating history as an independent entity, all performance claims rely on the parent fund PSH, which operates under different fee and capital structures.

  3. Key-person risk is extreme — Ackman's judgment drives all portfolio decisions with concentrated positions that amplify both gains and losses, and his historical record includes both 70%+ annual returns and multi-billion dollar losses on single positions (Valeant, JC Penney) [2]
