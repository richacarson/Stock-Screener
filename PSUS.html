Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NYSE ·United States
Pershing Square USA, Ltd. is a closed-end equity fund launched in April 2026 via a record $5 billion IPO on the NYSE, managed by Pershing Square Capital Management, L.P. The fund invests in 12-15 high-quality, predominantly North American-listed, large-capitalization growth companies at discounts to intrinsic value. Holdings include Amazon, Microsoft, Uber, Meta, Brookfield, Restaurant Brands, Fannie Mae, and Freddie Mac, with approximately 55% concentrated in AI-related stocks [1]. The fund trades at a ~23% discount to NAV ($37.81 vs. $49.06 NAV per CEF Connect), with total assets of approximately $4.9B and an annual expense ratio of 2.20% with no performance fee [2][3].

Investment Thesis
PSUS at $37.81 offers access to Bill Ackman's 16% annualized track record (since 2004) through a permanent capital structure at a ~23% discount to NAV ($37.81 vs. $49.06 NAV per CEF Connect as of September 2026) [1][3]. The portfolio is concentrated in 12 high-quality large-cap names with ~55% in AI beneficiaries (Microsoft, Amazon, Meta), plus Uber, Brookfield, and Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac, providing significant exposure to AI infrastructure buildout and financial sector catalysts [2]. Ackman and affiliates have invested $500+ million in personal capital at $50, creating extraordinary alignment — the strongest dollar-value insider commitment across the screen universe [1]. If the NAV discount narrows from 23% to the 10-15% range typical of mature closed-end funds, shareholders capture 10-15% in additional return beyond portfolio performance, and the permanent capital structure eliminates the redemption pressure that undermines most hedge fund strategies.
The bear case centers on the persistent NAV discount, high expense ratio, and key-person risk. PSUS has traded at a 20-23% discount since its April 2026 IPO — down 24% from the $50 offering price — and closed-end fund discounts often persist for years with no redemption mechanism to force convergence [3]. The 2.20% annual expense ratio is structurally punitive versus ETFs offering similar large-cap equity exposure at 0.03-0.10%, meaning Ackman must outperform the S&P 500 by at least 2.2% annually just to match zero-cost passive alternatives. NAV returned just 0.6% from inception through late June, underperforming the S&P 500's 5.3% gain over the same period, and PSH (the London-listed sister fund) also lagged markets through mid-2026 [3]. Key-person risk is extreme — the entire value proposition depends on Ackman's judgment, and his concentrated style has produced both spectacular gains (2020 COVID hedge) and devastating losses (Valeant -$4B, JC Penney). With zero operating history as an independent entity, all performance claims rely on the parent fund PSH, which operates under different fee and capital structures [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. NAV discount narrowing from ~23% to the 10-15% range typical of mature closed-end funds could generate 10-15% incremental return beyond portfolio performance, with $500M+ in management insider purchases providing a floor and active investor relations efforts to close the gap [1][3]
  2. 55% portfolio concentration in AI stocks (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) positions PSUS to benefit from continued AI infrastructure spending acceleration, with Ackman explicitly shifting strategy toward AI infrastructure and energy plays as secular growth themes [2]
  3. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac recapitalization thesis could unlock significant asymmetric upside if government-sponsored enterprise reform advances under favorable policy conditions, providing event-driven return potential uncorrelated with broader market direction
Key Risks
  1. Persistent NAV discount is structural for closed-end funds — PSUS has no redemption mechanism, the London-listed PSH has historically traded at ~30% discounts, and the 23% PSUS gap may not narrow given the fund's short track record and IPO-related selling pressure [1][3]
  2. 2.20% annual expense ratio creates a permanent headwind — Ackman must outperform the S&P 500 by at least 2.2% annually to justify the fee versus zero-cost passive alternatives, and NAV returned just 0.6% from April-June 2026 while the S&P gained 5.3% [3]
  3. Key-person risk is extreme — Ackman's judgment drives all portfolio decisions with concentrated positions that amplify both gains and losses, and his historical record includes multi-billion dollar losses on single positions (Valeant, JC Penney) alongside spectacular wins [2]
