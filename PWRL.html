Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
Powerlaw Corp. is a Nasdaq-listed, externally managed closed-end fund registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940, offering retail investors access to a concentrated portfolio of roughly 15-18 late-stage private technology companies — including SpaceX, OpenAI, Stripe, Groq, Deel, Kalshi, Perplexity, Figma, Waymo, Shield AI, and Prometheus — via a single publicly traded security. The fund began trading on Nasdaq on May 27, 2026 following a direct listing, and is advised by Powerlaw Fund Adviser, LLC, backed by Akkadian Ventures. It targets long-term capital appreciation from exposure to artificial intelligence, next-generation software, aerospace/defense, and consumer technology companies not otherwise accessible to public-market investors.

Investment Thesis
Powerlaw Corp. offers rare public-market access to a curated basket of the most sought-after late-stage private technology companies — SpaceX (its largest holding at ~19.4% of net assets), OpenAI, Stripe, Groq, Perplexity, Shield AI, and others — through a single Nasdaq-listed security, a structure otherwise unavailable to non-accredited retail investors [1]. The August 2026 market price reflects a steep ~24-28% discount to the fund's most recently disclosed NAV, which could offer a margin of safety if the fund's private holdings continue to appreciate and the discount narrows [3]. Modest insider buying by CIO David Black in June 2026 provides a small signal of management conviction in the portfolio's value [2].
The bear case is substantial. NAV per share fell 9.4% in a single month (June 30 to July 31, 2026), just weeks after the fund's direct listing, and the market discount to NAV has been extremely volatile — swinging from a ~25% premium to a ~28% discount within two months — suggesting the market has little confidence in the fund's private-company valuation marks or has priced in further write-downs (the OpenAI IPO delay to 2027 was cited as one specific catalyst for renewed skepticism) [3]. The 2.50% management fee and 3.63% total expense ratio are a persistent structural drag that must be overcome before any investor return materializes, and as an externally managed fund with no operating business, Powerlaw scores at the bottom of the IOWN framework on Innovation, Inspiration, and Infinite Game — there is no product, culture, or multi-decade cause to evaluate, only a fee-generating wrapper around illiquid private marks. With only a three-month public trading history, there is no track record to validate whether the fund can sustain its NAV, let alone compound it.
Key Catalysts
  1. Narrowing of the current ~24-28% discount to NAV toward par (or the ~25% premium seen in June 2026) would provide meaningful price appreciation independent of underlying portfolio performance [3]
  2. Successful IPOs or up-rounds among top holdings (SpaceX, OpenAI, Stripe, Groq) would mechanically lift NAV and could restore investor confidence in the fund's marks [1]
  3. Continued portfolio diversification into next-generation categories (physical AI, autonomous defense via Shield AI and Prometheus) could differentiate Powerlaw from single-theme private-market access vehicles [1]
Key Risks
  1. NAV declined 9.4% in a single month (June 30 to July 31, 2026) just after listing, and the OpenAI IPO delay to 2027 already triggered renewed price weakness — private-market marks in this portfolio can move sharply against public shareholders [3]
  2. The 2.50% management fee and 3.63% total expense ratio create a persistent performance drag that the fund must overcome every year before generating net investor returns [2]
  3. Extreme premium/discount volatility (from +25% to -28% within two months) signals a thin, unstable market for the shares and limited price discovery, compounding risk for investors who need to exit before a discount narrows [3]
