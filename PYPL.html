Investment Thesis
PayPal at 10.63x forward earnings trades near historically depressed levels for a company generating $34.1B in TTM revenue, 24.7% ROE, and $4.9B in net income — a quality business priced for permanent decline rather than restructuring recovery [1][5]. Q2 2026 delivered a beat ($1.38 adjusted EPS vs $1.28 consensus, revenue $8.68B vs $8.47B expected) and raised full-year guidance to $5.38 adjusted EPS, the first upward revision under CEO Lores. Venmo Debit Card monthly active accounts surging 50%+ YoY and Braintree TPV growing in the mid-teens demonstrate that both consumer and enterprise segments have growth levers [1]. The 3-unit reorganization (PayPal Commerce, Venmo standalone, Braintree) gives each business unit strategic clarity, and the $1.5B cost savings target over 2-3 years provides a clear margin expansion path. D/E of 67.6% with $8.3B in cash provides financial flexibility for buybacks and strategic investments.
The bear case centers on competitive erosion and organizational dysfunction — PayPal's 5% revenue growth is anemic for a fintech platform while Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Stripe embed payments deeper into native ecosystems, reducing the relevance of standalone checkout buttons [2]. Three CEO changes in three years is a red flag for strategic coherence, and the 20% workforce reduction (4,760 jobs, headcount from 30,900 peak to ~19,700 by 2028) creates organizational instability during a critical transformation [3]. The FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.38 represents only 1.3% growth over FY2025's $5.31 — essentially flat, with Q3 guided for a 'low-single-digit percentage' EPS decline. The venture arm wind-down removes future innovation optionality.
