Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductor Equipment & Materials ·NYSE ·United States
Qnity Electronics is a premier semiconductor materials and technology provider, separated from DuPont de Nemours on November 1, 2025 and trading on the NYSE as an S&P 500 member. The company operates through two segments: Semiconductor Technologies (CMP pads and slurries, photoresists, advanced overcoats, post-CMP cleaners) and Interconnect Solutions (copper pillar plating, redistribution layers, solder bump plating, packaging dielectrics, thermal interface materials). With 39 manufacturing sites and 17 R&D facilities serving customers in 80+ countries, Qnity enables advanced semiconductor manufacturing for AI, HPC, and connectivity applications. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.43B (+22% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $1.19 (+53% YoY), and full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $5.55-$5.65B revenue.

Investment Thesis
Qnity Electronics at 38.81x forward earnings is a high-quality semiconductor materials pure-play riding the AI infrastructure buildout — Q2 2026 revenue of $1.43B (+22% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $1.19 (+53% YoY, crushing estimates by 83%) demonstrates the company's direct exposure to AI chip demand [1][2]. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $5.55-$5.65B revenue with $4.40-$4.60 adjusted EPS, reflecting management confidence in sustained AI-driven demand. Qnity's materials (CMP pads, photoresists, advanced packaging) are irreplaceable inputs to semiconductor fabrication with high switching costs and 6-18 month qualification cycles that create sticky customer relationships. The Interconnect Solutions segment is particularly well-positioned as AI chips increasingly adopt advanced packaging architectures (chiplets, 3D stacking) that consume more materials per wafer. As a newly independent S&P 500 member, Qnity benefits from index inclusion flows and pure-play investor interest that was not available as a DuPont division.
The bear case centers on valuation, cyclicality, and execution risk. Forward P/E of 38.81x and PEG of 2.82 price in substantial continued growth, and any deceleration in AI chip spending or semiconductor inventory correction could trigger meaningful multiple compression [4]. The semiconductor industry is historically cyclical, and while AI provides a structural growth overlay, near-term demand fluctuations are inevitable — the nine consecutive quarters of growth create complacency risk. ROE of 7.48% and profit margin of 13.1% are adequate but not exceptional for a materials company, suggesting that premium valuation relies more on growth expectations than current profitability [4]. As a newly independent company (separated November 2025), Qnity is still building standalone corporate infrastructure, and the transition carries execution risk around ERP systems, procurement, and talent retention. Competition from Entegris, JSR, Shin-Etsu, and emerging Chinese materials suppliers creates pricing pressure.
