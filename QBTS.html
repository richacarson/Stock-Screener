Company Profile
Technology ·Computer Hardware ·NMS ·United States
D-Wave Quantum develops and delivers quantum computing systems, software, and services. Its products include the Advantage and Advantage 2 quantum annealing computers, Leap quantum cloud service, and Ocean open-source SDK. Applications span logistics optimization, resource scheduling, drug discovery, portfolio optimization, and AI-related workflows. TTM revenue of $12.4M with a $6.3B market cap — the stock trades at over 500x revenue. Q1 2026 revenue was $2.9M (down 81% YoY) and Q2 was $3.1M (flat YoY). Founded in 1999, headquartered in Palo Alto, California.

Track Record (2/3): D-Wave is the world's first commercial quantum computing company, having shipped successive generations of quantum annealers — D-Wave One (2011), D-Wave Two (2013), D-Wave 2000Q (2017), Advantage (2020), and now Advantage 2 in development — each representing genuine technological advancement in qubit count and connectivity [1]. The Leap quantum cloud service democratized access to quantum hardware and expanded the addressable market beyond on-premises installations. However, commercial scale remains minimal at $12.4M TTM revenue after 27 years. AI Deployment (1/2): D-Wave applies quantum annealing to AI-adjacent workflows including optimization for machine learning, logistics routing, and portfolio construction, and actively markets quantum computing as complementary to AI workloads [3]. Market Share (1/2): D-Wave is the undisputed leader in quantum annealing, but the overall commercial quantum computing market remains tiny and fragmented across IBM (gate-based), Google (Sycamore/Willow), IonQ, and Rigetti [1]. Commercial customer mix improved — 62.4% of Q2 2026 revenue came from commercial customers (up from 45.1% YoY), with Forbes Global 2000 customers rising to 47.7% of revenue [1]. R&D (1/1): R&D spending vastly exceeds revenue — the company invests approximately 9x its annual revenue in R&D and operations, well above any industry benchmark.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)

D-Wave demonstrates genuine long-term commitment to quantum computing through 27 years of persistent investment, but the cause has not been validated by commercial execution — $12.4M in TTM revenue after a quarter-century of operation is not a success story regardless of the technology's promise. The company shows adequate organizational trust (Great Place to Work certified, 75% Glassdoor recommendation) and some existential flexibility in evolving from hardware to cloud services, but heavy insider selling, lack of competitive acknowledgment, and unproven commercial viability suggest a company still searching for sustainable market fit rather than playing an infinite game.

Existential Flexibility

Existential Flexibility (1/2): D-Wave has evolved from selling on-premises quantum hardware to offering the Leap quantum cloud service, democratizing access and shifting toward a recurring revenue model [1]. The company has also adapted its approach to incorporate hybrid quantum-classical solvers, acknowledging that pure quantum solutions alone are insufficient for enterprise adoption. However, all evolution remains within quantum computing — the company has not demonstrated willingness to pivot away from quantum if the market demands it.
Courage to Lead
6
Courage to Lead (1/2): D-Wave has invested in quantum computing for 27 years despite never achieving profitability, and chose the unconventional annealing approach when the rest of the industry pursued gate-based quantum computing — a genuinely contrarian bet [1][3]. The company went public via SPAC in 2022 to fund continued R&D. However, the courage is tempered by the fact that leadership has been a consistent net seller of shares ($1,795 in insider buying over two years), suggesting personal financial hedging against the very bet they're asking shareholders to make [4].

Investment Thesis
D-Wave Quantum at $16.56 is the world's first and most commercially advanced quantum annealing company, with $588M in cash providing 4-5 years of runway, Q1 2026 bookings of $33.4M signaling future revenue, and commercial customer concentration improving to 62.4% of revenue (up from 45.1% YoY) with Forbes Global 2000 clients rising to 47.7% [1]. The Advantage 2 system promises a generational leap in qubit count and connectivity, the Leap cloud service provides scalable distribution, and average booking size increased 87% YoY in Q2 2026, suggesting enterprise customers are expanding quantum workloads. If quantum computing reaches commercial inflection, D-Wave's 27-year head start in real-world quantum applications (logistics, scheduling, drug discovery) positions it as the category leader [1][3].
The bear case is overwhelming: $6.3B market cap on $12.4M TTM revenue means the stock trades at over 500x sales — pricing in a quantum computing future that may never materialize or may arrive too late for D-Wave [1]. H1 2026 revenue collapsed 67% to $5.9M from $18.1M due to lapping a large NTT contract, and Q2 EPS of -$0.13 missed consensus by $0.05, demonstrating both revenue lumpiness and persistent losses. The company burns approximately $111M annually in operating cash flow on $12.4M revenue, and while $546M in cash provides runway, eventual dilutive raises are nearly certain unless revenue accelerates dramatically. Cumulative insider buying of just $1,795 over two years versus massive insider selling raises serious governance concerns about management conviction [4]. Classical computing and AI improvements continue narrowing the problems where quantum provides meaningful advantage, and competitors IBM, Google, IonQ, and Rigetti are attacking the market from gate-based approaches that may prove commercially superior to D-Wave's annealing architecture [3].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): D-Wave's core business is quantum computing hardware and cloud services — a technology complementary to AI rather than threatened by it [3]. Quantum annealing solves optimization problems (logistics routing, portfolio optimization, drug discovery) that classical AI approaches struggle with at scale, positioning D-Wave as a potential accelerator of AI capabilities rather than a victim of AI displacement. However, D-Wave faces indirect risk from classical AI improvements: as classical computing and AI continue advancing rapidly, the urgency and commercial justification for quantum computing may diminish, extending the timeline for quantum adoption. D-Wave is not critical AI infrastructure in the way that GPUs or data center power are — AI advancement does not directly increase demand for quantum hardware. The company scores PROTECTED rather than IMMUNE because quantum computing's commercial viability remains unproven at scale, and the threat is not from AI disruption per se but from classical computing's continued improvement making quantum less necessary [1]. AI Opportunity (1/1): D-Wave generates revenue from quantum computing applied to AI-adjacent workflows — optimization for machine learning, logistics, scheduling, and portfolio construction — though the absolute revenue remains small at $12.4M TTM [1][3].
