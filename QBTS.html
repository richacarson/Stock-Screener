Company Profile
Technology ·Quantum Computing ·NYSE ·Canada
D-Wave Quantum is the world's first commercial supplier of quantum computers and the only company offering dual-platform quantum computing products spanning both annealing and gate-model technologies. The company delivers enterprise-grade quantum computing through on-premises systems and its Leap quantum cloud service (99.9% uptime SLA). D-Wave went public in August 2022 via SPAC merger. H1 2026 bookings surged to $35.5M (+1,120% YoY), though revenue remains small at $3.1M in Q2 2026 as the business transitions from lumpy system sales to recurring QCaaS subscriptions. The $250M acquisition of Quantum Circuits in early 2026 added gate-model capability, making D-Wave the only dual-platform quantum computing company. Market cap is approximately $7.7B.

Investment Thesis
D-Wave presents a speculative but uniquely positioned quantum computing investment — the only company offering both annealing and gate-model quantum systems, with H1 2026 bookings surging to $35.5M (+1,120% YoY) as enterprise adoption of quantum optimization accelerates [2]. The $546.2M cash position provides approximately 7+ years of runway at current burn rates, and the core QCaaS subscription business grew 50% YoY in Q2 to $1.9M, showing the recurring revenue model is gaining traction. The Nasdaq Verafin deal and 1,994% Q1 bookings growth signal that quantum computing is transitioning from science experiment to enterprise procurement decision. At $7.7B market cap, the stock prices in substantial future growth that has not yet materialized in revenue.
The bear case is substantial — D-Wave generates only $12M in TTM revenue against a $7.7B market cap (644x price-to-sales), making this among the most speculative valuations in the technology sector [4]. The company burned $18.4M in Q1 2026 operating losses and $546.2M in cash represents a declining runway from the $819.3M held a year prior, with ~$250M consumed by the Quantum Circuits acquisition [1]. Revenue recognition is lumpy and dependent on infrequent system sales — Q1 2025's $15.0M revenue inflated by a one-time system sale makes YoY comparisons misleading. The bookings surge has yet to convert to meaningful revenue, and quantum computing's practical advantage over classical computing for most enterprise workloads remains unproven at scale. Glassdoor reviews citing abrasive leadership culture present organizational risk for a company with only 385 employees [3]. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): D-Wave operates in quantum computing, a distinct computational paradigm that addresses optimization and simulation problems fundamentally different from classical AI workloads. AI cannot substitute for quantum computing's advantages in combinatorial optimization, materials simulation, and certain machine learning tasks — these are structurally different computational approaches [2]. Moreover, the explosive growth of AI workloads actually increases demand for quantum computing solutions to problems that scale beyond classical compute capacity. D-Wave's proprietary annealing architecture and now dual-platform capability create technology moats that AI development cannot erode. The primary risk is not AI disruption but rather that advances in classical computing and AI algorithms could narrow the practical advantage window for quantum solutions, delaying commercial quantum computing adoption [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): D-Wave does not currently generate quantifiable AI-specific revenue. While quantum computing is increasingly positioned as an AI-adjacent technology (e.g., the Nasdaq Verafin financial crime detection deal), the company's revenue is classified as quantum computing services rather than AI products [2].
