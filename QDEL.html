Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
QuidelOrtho Corporation provides diagnostic testing solutions globally through four business units: Labs (clinical chemistry, immunoassay, hematology), Transfusion Medicine (blood typing, donor screening), Point of Care (rapid diagnostics), and Molecular Diagnostics (PCR, syndromic testing). Formed from the 2022 merger of Quidel Corporation and Ortho Clinical Diagnostics, the company has struggled with post-merger integration, massive goodwill impairments, and declining revenue. CEO Brian Blaser was appointed in May 2024. Q2 2026 revenue was $631M (+3% reported, +6% ex-China), and full-year 2026 revenue guidance was lowered to $2.52-$2.60B due to persistent China headwinds and weak respiratory demand. Market cap has collapsed to $733M from over $5B post-merger.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): QuidelOrtho's core business — manufacturing physical diagnostic instruments, reagents, and consumables for clinical laboratories, blood banks, and point-of-care settings — is protected from AI disruption [1]. Blood typing requires physical reagents and instruments, molecular diagnostics requires physical PCR thermocyclers and sample-to-result systems, and clinical chemistry requires physical analyzers. The installed base of instruments creates switching costs, and lab testing is heavily regulated (FDA, CLIA). AI cannot replace the physical act of running a blood test or a PCR assay. AI may improve diagnostic interpretation and lab workflow efficiency, but this is an opportunity for diagnostics companies rather than a threat — the instruments and consumables remain essential. No analyst has flagged AI as a substitution risk for the diagnostics industry. AI Opportunity (0/1): QuidelOrtho does not generate meaningful AI-related revenue. AI could enhance diagnostic algorithms and lab workflow optimization, but the company has not publicly deployed AI-powered products or reported AI-driven revenue.
