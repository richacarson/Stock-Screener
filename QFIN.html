Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·China
Qfin Holdings (formerly Qifu Technology / 360 Finance) operates an AI-driven credit-tech platform under the Qifu Jietiao brand in China, matching borrowers with financial institution partners for consumer and SME lending. The company provides credit assessment, fund matching, loan facilitation, and post-facilitation services. Qfin is dual-listed on NASDAQ and HKEX (3660) and uses its proprietary TRIDENT multimodal LLM for credit scoring, fraud detection, and customer service. As of Q1 2026, the platform has 64.8 million users with approved credit lines and partners with 167 financial institutions.

Investment Thesis
Qfin Holdings trades at a historically extreme valuation discount — 2.35x trailing P/E, 2.98x forward P/E, and 11.8% dividend yield — reflecting severe China, regulatory, and geopolitical risk priced into the stock after a 67% decline from 52-week highs [5]. Full year 2025 delivered solid results: RMB19.2B revenue (+12% YoY), RMB6.0B net income, and RMB6.16B returned to shareholders through buybacks and dividends [2]. The AI-native business model (TRIDENT MLLM powering credit scoring, fraud detection, and customer service) positions Qfin at the intersection of AI and fintech, with the capital-light platform pivot gaining traction — platform service revenue surged 44.2% sequentially and facilitation reached 48.3% of volume in Q1 2026 [1][3]. Balance sheet strength (12% D/E, RMB10.7B cash, 27.1% payout ratio) provides substantial cushion through the earnings downturn, and analyst consensus of 'Buy' with $21.88 average target implies 66% upside [5].
The bear case has intensified since Q4 2025. Q1 2026 revenue fell 16.7% YoY to RMB3.9B, net income plunged 51% to RMB880M, and loan facilitation volume contracted 26.8% to RMB65B [1]. Q2 2026 guidance projects another 47-51% YoY income decline, confirming the business is contracting under regulatory pressure [1]. Delinquency rates have risen to 3.50% (90+ day) with elevated provisions of RMB1.2B in Q1 alone, suggesting portfolio credit quality is deteriorating [1]. Grizzly Research's fraud allegations — 'fake financials, fraudulent backers, and an imploding business' — remain unresolved [4]. Parent company Qihoo 360's US sanctions and Pentagon military designation create ongoing ADR delisting risk, and the stock's 67% decline from $39.96 to $13.19 reflects this existential concern [4][5]. TTM profit margins have compressed from 31.1% to 27.5%, with Q1 2026 net margin at just 22.5% [1]. The 2.35x P/E may be cheap, or it may accurately reflect the probability-weighted risk of further regulatory destruction, fraud exposure, or delisting.
