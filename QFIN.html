Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·China
Qfin Holdings, Inc. (formerly Qifu Technology / 360 DigiTech) operates an AI-driven credit-tech platform under the Qifu Jietiao brand in China, matching borrowers with financial institution partners through credit assessment, fund matching, and post-facilitation services, alongside a capital-light platform/SaaS model for banks under CEO Haisheng Wu [1][2]. Under China's August 2026 fintech-lending crackdown -- a 24% APR cap and stricter fee-disclosure rules -- Qfin's profit has been squeezed sharply and the shares have fallen more than 74% from their 52-week high, even as the company accelerates a pivot toward capital-light SaaS and overseas expansion [3].

Investment Thesis
Qfin trades at just 1.85x trailing and 2.67x forward earnings after a 74%+ decline from its 52-week high, a valuation that already prices in severe regulatory-driven margin compression following China's August 2026 24% APR cap [3]. The company's underlying AI capability -- TRIDENT, its proprietary multimodal LLM used in production for fraud detection and customer service, plus its role co-drafting China's first financial-LLM industry standard -- is a genuine, evidenced technology moat that most Western investors underappreciate in a name trading at distressed multiples [1][2]. Management's proactive pivot toward capital-light SaaS services for bank partners and accelerated overseas expansion directly addresses the regulatory threat to its legacy fee-based model, and trailing profitability (22.3% margin, 15.3% ROE) remains intact for now, providing optionality if the SaaS transition executes successfully.
The bear case is dominated by regulatory risk that is still actively unfolding: revenue growth YoY of -31.6% and EPS growth YoY of -76.6% reflect the crackdown already biting hard, Q2 2026 non-GAAP EPS missed consensus by 38%, and profit reportedly tumbled nearly 50% as the 24% APR ceiling strips out previously profitable fee structures [1][3][4]. Qfin was also summoned by Chinese authorities over customer complaint volume, alongside peers, indicating the regulatory scrutiny extends beyond pricing into conduct and disclosure practices that could bring further restrictions. The stock's statistically cheap valuation reflects real, unresolved regulatory and geopolitical risk (China ADR delisting/governance concerns broadly, plus sector-specific crackdown) rather than a simple mispricing, and the mechanically strong Infrastructure score of 9 should be read alongside a Just Cause score of just 3 and an Inspiration score of 2 -- this is a name where financial efficiency and mission/governance quality diverge sharply.
