Company Profile
Technology ·Quantum Computing ·NGM ·United States
Quantinuum Inc. is the world's largest integrated quantum computing company, formed in 2021 from the merger of Honeywell Quantum Solutions and Cambridge Quantum Computing. The company designs trapped-ion quantum computing hardware (H-Series systems including Helios and Nexus), quantum software, and middleware, delivering quantum computing as a service via the cloud. Led by CEO Dr. Rajeeb Hazra, Quantinuum IPO'd on NASDAQ in June 2026 at $60/share, raising $1.68B. Q2 2026 revenue surged 279% YoY to $8M with FY2026 guidance of $28-32M. Stock has declined 31% from IPO price to $41.30 with market cap of approximately $11B.

Investment Thesis
Quantinuum represents a pure-play bet on quantum computing with Q2 2026 revenue surging 279% YoY to $8M and full-year guidance of $28-32M in the company's first public earnings report [1]. Year-to-date bookings of $81M (targeting $120M+) suggest a massive inflection in enterprise quantum adoption, with each booking dollar representing approximately 4x the current revenue run rate. The company's trapped-ion approach has achieved the highest quantum volume records in the industry, and the remaining performance obligations of $74M provide strong visibility [1]. With $2.1B in post-IPO cash, Quantinuum has runway through commercialization without near-term dilution risk [2]. Management projects >100% revenue growth in 2027, and the Oracle partnership signals expanding enterprise distribution channels. If fault-tolerant quantum computing arrives on timeline, the $11B market cap could prove deeply discounted relative to the addressable market.
The bear case is that Quantinuum is an $11B company generating $23M in TTM revenue — approximately 480x trailing revenue — pricing in near-perfect execution of a multi-decade technology bet [1]. The $597M GAAP net loss in Q2 (including $447.5M IPO-related stock compensation charge) and $68M adjusted EBITDA loss demonstrate cash burn intensity, with $2.1B cash providing approximately 3-4 years of runway at current rates [1]. The stock has declined 31% from the $60 IPO price to $41.30, suggesting post-IPO enthusiasm has faded. Fault-tolerant quantum computing remains years away from broad commercial viability, and competing approaches from IBM, Google, and Microsoft bring massive R&D budgets that could leapfrog the trapped-ion architecture. Glassdoor reviews reveal physicist-centric cultural elitism and management instability that could impair talent retention during the critical scaling phase [5]. The bookings-to-revenue gap ($81M bookings vs $23M revenue) suggests lengthy fulfillment cycles and potential recognition challenges.
