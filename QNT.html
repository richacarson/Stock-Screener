Company Profile
Technology ·Quantum Computing ·NASDAQ ·United States
Quantinuum Inc. is the world's largest integrated quantum computing company, formed in 2021 from the merger of Honeywell Quantum Solutions and Cambridge Quantum. The company designs trapped-ion quantum computing hardware (H-Series systems), quantum software, and middleware, and delivers quantum computing as a service via the cloud. Led by CEO Dr. Rajeeb Hazra, Quantinuum IPO'd on NASDAQ in June 2026 at $60/share, raising $1.68B. Q2 2026 revenue surged 279% YoY to $8M in the company's first public earnings report, with FY2026 guidance of $28-32M and year-to-date bookings of $81M targeting $120M+ for the full year. Market cap is approximately $16B against annualized revenue of ~$30M.

Investment Thesis
Quantinuum represents a pure-play bet on quantum computing — the most capital-intensive frontier technology of the decade — with Q2 2026 revenue surging 279% YoY to $8M and full-year guidance of $28-32M [1]. The company's trapped-ion approach has achieved the highest quantum volume records in the industry, and year-to-date bookings of $81M (targeting $120M+) suggest a massive inflection in enterprise quantum adoption, with each booking dollar representing approximately 4x the current revenue run rate. With $2.1B in post-IPO cash, Quantinuum has runway through commercialization without dilution risk. The recently announced Oracle partnership and >100% projected 2027 revenue growth signal accelerating customer traction [1]. Analyst consensus targets $97.17 (60%+ upside), and if fault-tolerant quantum computing arrives on the projected timeline, the $16B market cap could prove deeply discounted relative to the addressable market.
The bear case is that Quantinuum is a $16B market cap company generating $23M in annual revenue — a 700x price-to-sales ratio that prices in near-perfect execution of a multi-decade technology bet [3]. The $597M GAAP net loss and $68M adjusted EBITDA loss in Q2 alone demonstrate the cash burn intensity, and the $2.1B cash position provides approximately 3-4 years of runway at current burn rates [1]. Fault-tolerant quantum computing remains years away from commercial viability, and the quantum computing industry has a history of overpromising timelines. Competing approaches from IBM, Google, and Microsoft have massive R&D budgets and could leapfrog Quantinuum's trapped-ion technology. Glassdoor reviews reveal organizational growing pains — physicist-centric cultural elitism and management instability — that could impair execution during the critical scaling phase [5]. The Incransom ransomware group's claim of a pre-IPO data breach (likely fabricated but still headline risk) adds uncertainty [4]. Finally, the bookings-to-revenue gap ($81M bookings vs $23M revenue) suggests lengthy fulfillment cycles and potential revenue recognition challenges.

Quantinuum's trapped-ion hardware and quantum error correction technology operate in a domain where classical AI reaches physical computational limits.
