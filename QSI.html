Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NASDAQ ·United States
Quantum-Si develops semiconductor-chip-based single-molecule detection technology for next-generation protein sequencing (NGPS). Its Platinum and Platinum Pro benchtop instruments, library prep and sequencing kits, and cloud analysis software serve proteomics research and drug discovery. The company is transitioning its installed base to the next-generation Proteus platform, now targeted for commercial launch in Q2 2027.

Investment Thesis
Quantum-Si owns the only commercial single-molecule protein sequencing platform, and Proteus (targeted for Q2 2027) is designed to raise throughput and lower cost per sample, addressing both human proteomics and an identified $4B+ non-human market in agriculture and pathogen surveillance [1][3]. After the 2026 restructuring, management says cash runway now extends into Q4 2028, which would fund the company through the Proteus launch and first adoption year without an immediate raise [3]. The balance sheet is effectively debt-free (D/E 1.8%), and the stock has rallied to $1.65 from a 50-day average near $0.99, signalling renewed speculative interest in the launch [7].
The bear case dominates. Revenue is shrinking toward a ~$1M run-rate (Q1 2026 $258K, Q2 $344K) while net losses run near $100M per year, producing ROE of -49% and an Infrastructure score of 2 [1][2][5]. Proteus has already slipped once, and any further delay or a weak post-launch install rate would likely force dilutive financing; the ~$361M market cap now prices in some success. The 20% layoff and repeated leadership turnover weaken execution capacity, and NIH and academic budget pressure clouds demand for new capital instruments.
Key Catalysts
  1. Proteus commercial launch in Q2 2027: first shipments, early-access data, and order commentary would be the first evidence of product-market fit for the new architecture [3]
  2. Q3 2026 results (expected around November 4, 2026), with updates on Proteus beta-site progress, cash burn after the 20% RIF, and the Q4 2028 runway claim [3][5]
  3. Non-human proteomics partnerships (agricultural, pathogen and viral surveillance) that could broaden demand beyond grant-constrained academic labs [1]
Key Risks
  1. Further Proteus slippage past Q2 2027 would extend the near-zero revenue period and likely require an equity raise, diluting holders at sub-$2 share prices [3]
  2. Commercial-adoption risk: the prior Platinum platform never exceeded about $3M of annual revenue, and Proteus must prove that protein sequencing can become a routine lab workflow rather than a niche tool [1][2]
  3. Academic capital-equipment budgets under pressure from NIH funding uncertainty, compounded by competition from improving mass-spectrometry proteomics from Thermo Fisher and Bruker
