Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NYSE ·United States
Restaurant Brands International is a quick-service restaurant company that owns and franchises four iconic brands: Tim Hortons, Burger King, Popeyes Louisiana Kitchen, and Firehouse Subs. The company operates over 30,000 restaurants in more than 100 countries, primarily through a franchise model. Controlled by 3G Capital. CEO Josh Kobza targets 40,000 restaurants and $60B system-wide sales by 2028.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 6 /10
RBI shows mixed infinite game characteristics. The company's brands have endured for decades (Burger King since 1954, Tim Hortons since 1964), suggesting infinite game DNA at the brand level. Under CEO Josh Kobza, RBI has shifted from 3G Capital's aggressive cost-cutting playbook toward investment-led growth — the $400M+ 'Reclaim the Flame' bet on Burger King U.S. is now delivering strong Q1 2026 results, demonstrating courage to invest for the long term. However, the franchise holding company structure, 304% D/E ratio, and high payout ratio reflect finite financial engineering.

Investment Thesis
Restaurant Brands International delivered a strong Q1 2026 with revenue of $2.26B (+7.4% YoY), net income surging to $338M (+113% from $159M), and adjusted EBITDA of $706M (+10%) [1]. System-wide sales reached $11.51B (+6.2% constant currency) with comp sales up 3.2%, driven by especially strong Burger King U.S. performance validating the $400M+ 'Reclaim the Flame' turnaround [1]. FY2025 delivered $9.43B revenue (+12.2% YoY) with ROE of 24% and a 3.40% dividend yield. At 19.1x forward P/E, the stock trades at a reasonable valuation for a global franchise platform targeting 40,000 restaurants and $60B system-wide sales by 2028.
The bear case centers on excessive leverage and uneven brand performance. D/E of 304% sits at the Infrastructure scoring floor, reflecting $15.7B in acquisition-driven debt that constrains financial flexibility [5]. While the payout ratio has improved to ~85% with Q1 2026's earnings surge, it remains elevated and leaves limited dividend cushion if earnings decline. Popeyes U.S. same-store sales fell 6.5% in Q1 2026, demonstrating the multi-brand portfolio's uneven execution [1]. Profit margin of 8.2% is thin for a franchise model and reflects the company's supply chain revenue mix. Deforestation-linked supply chain controversies (beef, soy, palm oil) have attracted shareholder activism, and Sustainalytics flagged weak ESG disclosure despite management linking ESG metrics to executive compensation [4]. The 3G Capital ownership overhang creates governance concerns about balancing cost discipline with the brand investment needed for long-term health.

AI Risk Protection (7/9): QSR restaurants are physical-world operations — AI cannot replace the cooking, serving, and in-store experience that drives 30,000+ restaurant locations [1]. The franchise model creates structural protection as franchisees own physical assets and local customer relationships. Food preparation, real estate management, and supply chain logistics are AI-resistant activities. Drive-thru ordering and kitchen operations see AI augmentation but not displacement [3]. Some exposure to AI-driven delivery platforms that could shift power dynamics away from restaurant brands toward platform aggregators. AI Opportunity (1/1): RBI is actively deploying AI in operations — predictive ordering, digital menu optimization, self-ordering kiosks, digitized drive-thru lanes, and a unified technology platform connecting ordering, kitchen, and supply data generate measurable efficiency gains across its restaurant network [3].
