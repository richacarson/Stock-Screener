Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Netherlands
uniQure N.V. is an AAV gene therapy company that developed HEMGENIX, the hemophilia B gene therapy commercialized by CSL Behring, and is now focused on AMT-130 for Huntington's disease plus AMT-260 (refractory epilepsy) and AMT-191 (Fabry disease) [1][5][8]. After the FDA initially steered AMT-130 toward a lengthy sham-controlled trial, a June 2026 Type B meeting aligned on an accelerated-approval filing; the BLA was announced around September 2, 2026, but four-year data on September 29 triggered a ~37% one-day selloff [2]. The company held $810.3M of cash and investments at June 30, 2026, which it says funds operations into 2030 [1].

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 5 /10
uniQure has pursued a genuinely hard problem — a one-time therapy for Huntington's disease — for years and has continued after a public FDA reversal, which Sinek would see as a cause worth sacrificing for [2][7]. But its posture is that of a company fighting to survive regulatory binary events: it sold part of its HEMGENIX royalty for cash, faces a securities class action and a reported FDA rebuke over data presentation, and its long-term identity depends on a single program [6][7].

Investment Thesis
uniQure could own the first disease-modifying therapy for Huntington's disease: AMT-130's BLA was submitted in Q3 2026 after a June Type B meeting aligned on an accelerated-approval path, an MHRA filing is planned, and a widely reported October 28 headline cites FDA acceptance with priority review [1][2]. With $810.3M of cash and investments funding operations into 2030, a HEMGENIX royalty stream, and follow-on shots in epilepsy (AMT-260 data H1 2027) and Fabry disease, the company is financed through a potential launch, and the stock at ~$24.8 sits far below its $51.72 high [1][2].
The bear case is regulatory and credibility risk: the stock fell ~37% on September 29 after four-year data, the FDA wants a randomized standard-of-care confirmatory study well underway before any accelerated approval, and an advisory committee is expected with no timing guidance [2]. A securities class action follows reports the FDA accused uniQure of presenting 'distorted and manipulated' data, revenue is only ~$18.7M of license income against a FY2025 net loss of $199M, and $552M of debt plus likely dilution weigh on equity holders [7][8][9].
Key Catalysts
  1. FDA acceptance/priority review and an eventual PDUFA decision on the AMT-130 BLA for Huntington's disease, plus the planned UK MHRA marketing application [1][2]
  2. Q3 2026 results on November 4 — consensus expects a loss of about $0.84 per share on ~$7.2M revenue, with updates on BLA review timing and advisory committee plans [2]
  3. AMT-260 higher-dose refractory epilepsy data in H1 2027 and clarity on restarting AMT-191 Fabry dose cohorts could diversify value beyond AMT-130 [2]
Key Risks
  1. Regulatory: the FDA may require a substantially enrolled randomized confirmatory study or an advisory committee could vote negatively, delaying or denying accelerated approval for AMT-130 [2]
  2. Litigation and credibility: an ongoing securities class action tied to reports that the FDA rebuked uniQure over AMT-130 data presentation [7]
  3. Financial: FY2025 net loss of $199M on $16.1M revenue, $552M of debt and a slower-than-expected HEMGENIX royalty ramp leave the company reliant on capital markets if launch timing slips [5][8][9]
