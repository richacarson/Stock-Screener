Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·Netherlands
uniQure N.V. is a gene therapy company focused on transformative single-administration therapies for severe genetic diseases. Lead commercial product HEMGENIX is the only FDA-approved AAV gene therapy for hemophilia B, with 75+ patients treated across 8 countries. Lead pipeline candidate AMT-130 for Huntington's disease received FDA agreement on accelerated approval pathway in June 2026, with BLA submission planned Q3 2026. AMT-191 for Fabry disease is also in development. Headquartered in Amsterdam, Netherlands with operations in Lexington, Massachusetts.

Investment Thesis
uniQure sits at a pivotal inflection point: on June 17, 2026, the FDA agreed that three-year Phase I/II data from AMT-130 would support a BLA seeking accelerated approval for Huntington's disease, with submission planned Q3 2026 [2]. AMT-130 demonstrated statistically significant slowing of disease progression versus external controls and holds both Breakthrough Therapy and RMAT designations. If approved, AMT-130 would be the first-ever gene therapy for Huntington's disease — addressing a devastating neurodegenerative condition with no disease-modifying treatments available today. The company's cash position of approximately $811M (post-$225M offering) provides runway into H2 2029, de-risking the regulatory and commercialization timeline [3][4]. HEMGENIX continues real-world validation with 75+ hemophilia B patients treated across 8 countries [1].
The bear case remains formidable despite the regulatory milestone. HEMGENIX revenue of just $3.6M in Q1 2026 (up from $1.6M) demonstrates that even an approved gene therapy faces slow commercial adoption due to pricing ($3.5M per treatment), payer resistance, and small addressable patient populations [3]. The $199M FY2025 net loss and D/E of 270% underscore continued capital market dependence. AMT-130 faces significant execution risk: the FDA agreed on accelerated approval but requires a confirmatory study with concurrent control (not sham surgery), adding time and cost [2]. Huntington's disease has historically been extremely difficult therapeutically. At $42/share ($2.8B market cap), much of the AMT-130 de-risking is already priced in, and any regulatory or clinical setback would severely compress the stock given no near-term path to profitability [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. AMT-130 BLA submission to FDA in Q3 2026 for accelerated approval in Huntington's disease, with Breakthrough Therapy and RMAT designations potentially accelerating review timeline [2]
  2. Four-year AMT-130 durability data readout in Q3 2026 and UK MAA submission providing additional regulatory milestones and de-risking events [2]

Key Risks
  1. AMT-130 regulatory risk — FDA requires a confirmatory study with concurrent standard-of-care control rather than sham surgery, adding years and significant cost to the approval pathway [2]
  2. Gene therapy commercialization challenge — HEMGENIX generates only $3.6M/quarter despite FDA approval, highlighting severe pricing, payer, and adoption headwinds for $3.5M one-time treatments [3]
  3. Capital markets dependence — with $199M annual net loss and no path to near-term profitability, the $811M cash position (H2 2029 runway) may still require additional dilutive financing if AMT-130 commercialization costs exceed expectations [3][4]
