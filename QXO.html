Company Profile
Industrials ·Industrial Distribution ·NYSE ·United States
QXO distributes roofing, waterproofing, and complementary building products across the United States and Canada under brands including GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed, and James Hardie. Formed when serial acquirer Brad Jacobs renamed SilverSun Technologies in June 2024, QXO closed the $11B acquisition of Beacon Roofing Supply in February 2025 and the $2.2B Kodiak Building Partners deal shortly after. Q2 2026 net sales reached $3.25B (up 70% YoY including $595M from Kodiak), with adjusted EBITDA of $272M but a GAAP net loss of $0.14 per share [1]. First half 2026 net sales totaled $4.98B versus $1.92B in the prior year. Management targets $50B in annual revenue within the next decade and more than doubling EBITDA by 2030 through acquisitions and organic growth [3].

Investment Thesis
QXO at $11.18 is Brad Jacobs' fourth serial roll-up, now a top-three building products distributor in North America with a $3.25B quarterly revenue run rate following the $11B Beacon and $2.2B Kodiak acquisitions [1]. The bull case rests on Jacobs' proven operational playbook — he created $37B+ in enterprise value across United Waste, United Rentals, and XPO — applied to a $800B+ fragmented market where the top 10 distributors hold less than 30% share [3]. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $272M (up 33% YoY) suggests early synergies are materializing, the Forward PEG of 0.81 prices in rapid earnings normalization, and management targets more than doubling EBITDA by 2030 through continued acquisitions and technology-driven margin improvement [1][4]. D/E of 57% provides balance sheet capacity for further M&A. Consensus forward estimates project $14.3B full-year revenue and $0.30 EPS as the platform scales [6].
The bear case centers on execution risk and structural profitability concerns. QXO remains GAAP-unprofitable with a -5.2% net margin and negative ROE (-4.6%), and the 70% revenue growth is entirely acquisition-driven with no disclosed organic growth metrics [1][6]. Integration risk is significant — flattening Beacon from 9 to 4 management layers while simultaneously absorbing Kodiak creates operational disruption risk in a relationship-driven distribution business where local contractor relationships drive revenue [4]. The $50B revenue target requires roughly $35B+ in additional acquisitions at increasingly competitive multiples, and the building products market is cyclically exposed to housing starts and construction activity. Jacobs' public statements about AI eliminating human roles, combined with aggressive restructuring, risk talent attrition in a business where relationships matter [5]. The stock has declined from $13.76 to $11.18 since August, and at a $11.6B market cap, prices in substantial future value creation that has yet to materialize [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Beacon/Kodiak synergy realization — management targets more than doubling EBITDA by 2030 through procurement consolidation, technology platform deployment, and SG&A rationalization across the distribution network [1][3]
  2. Continued M&A in fragmented building products distribution where the top 10 players hold less than 30% of an $800B+ market, with QXO's balance sheet capacity (57% D/E) and Jacobs' track record attracting acquisition targets [3]
  3. Technology-driven margin expansion through AI pricing engines, predictive inventory, and dynamic ordering portals that differentiate QXO's platform from traditional distributor competitors running on legacy systems [4]
Key Risks
  1. Integration execution risk — simultaneously restructuring Beacon and absorbing Kodiak while flattening management layers could disrupt local contractor relationships that drive the majority of distribution revenue in this relationship-intensive industry [4]
  2. Cyclical exposure to housing starts and commercial construction activity, with building products demand highly correlated to new residential construction and re-roofing cycles sensitive to interest rates and economic conditions [6]
  3. Acquisition dependency — the $50B revenue target requires $35B+ in additional acquisitions at increasingly competitive multiples, and capital markets access at favorable terms is not guaranteed through multiple economic cycles [3]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Physical distribution of building products — roofing, waterproofing, siding — requires local warehouses, truck fleets, and contractor relationships that AI structurally cannot replace [3][4]. The value-add in building products distribution (job-site delivery, credit extension, technical product knowledge) depends on physical infrastructure and human relationships. Analysts have not flagged AI as a substitution risk for building products distributors. However, AI-enabled procurement platforms could eventually streamline the contractor-manufacturer interface, compressing distributor margins. Jacobs himself acknowledged that 'every single industry will be completely disrupted' by AI, though he positioned distribution as a relatively safe sector compared to AI-native businesses [5]. AI Opportunity (0/1): QXO uses AI internally for pricing optimization and sales lead prioritization but does not generate revenue from AI products or services sold to external customers [4].
