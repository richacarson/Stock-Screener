Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYSE ·United States
Ralliant Corporation designs, develops, manufactures, and services precision instruments and engineered products across two segments: Test & Measurement (Tektronix oscilloscopes, Keithley source measure units, EA Electro-Automatik power supplies) and Sensors & Safety Systems (Qualitrol power grid monitoring, Gems Sensors, Pacific Scientific Energetic Materials for defense/space). Spun off from Fortive Corporation in June 2025, headquartered in Raleigh, NC. Q2 2026 revenue of $568M grew 13% YoY with raised full-year guidance of $2.25-$2.3B revenue and $2.76-$2.90 adjusted EPS.

Investment Thesis
Ralliant delivered accelerating growth through its first full year of independence — Q2 2026 revenue of $568M grew 13% YoY (vs. Q1's 11%), adjusted EPS was $0.68, and full-year guidance was raised to $2.25-$2.3B revenue and $2.76-$2.90 adjusted EPS [1]. The Sensors & Safety segment achieved 28% operating margins while Test & Measurement returned to positive profitability, and adjusted EBITDA margin guidance of 20-21% reflects the underlying earnings power obscured by one-time spinoff charges [1]. At 19.6x forward adjusted earnings with 50.9% gross margins and $334M free cash flow, valuation is reasonable for a precision technology leader positioned in data center infrastructure testing, AI workload monitoring, defense sensing, and power grid resilience [3]. Tektronix's 75+ year brand heritage and category leadership in test and measurement provide deep customer trust and switching costs.
The bear case centers on Ralliant's limited track record and financial complexity as a recent spinoff. Trailing GAAP metrics are deeply distorted — ROE of -53.9% and net margin of -56.4% from spinoff charges — creating uncertainty about the normalized earnings profile and making it difficult for investors to assess intrinsic value [3]. D/E of 79.8% with $1.22B debt against $271M cash is moderate but leaves limited financial flexibility if semiconductor or industrial end markets soften cyclically [3]. The company has no 5-year financial history, PEG ratio, or long-term track record for investors to analyze. The 0.3% dividend yield provides no income support, and the stock lacks the institutional following of larger T&M competitors like Keysight Technologies ($5.5B revenue). Any deceleration from the 13% growth rate could compress the multiple quickly, and the Enterprise Productivity Program's $50-60M savings target remains unproven [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Accelerating organic revenue growth — Q2 2026 grew 13% vs. Q1's 11% — with raised guidance suggesting sustained momentum across semiconductor test, power grid monitoring, and defense/space applications [1]
  2. Margin expansion as spinoff charges roll off and Enterprise Productivity Program delivers $50-60M annualized savings by 2028, driving adjusted EBITDA margins above the guided 20-21% range [1]
  3. Data center and AI infrastructure investment cycle driving demand for Tektronix test platforms and Qualitrol power grid monitoring as hyperscalers expand capacity through 2028 [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. Limited 15-month operating history as an independent company with no proven ability to navigate cyclical downturns in semiconductor capital spending or defense budgets without Fortive's diversification and capital resources [2][3]
  2. Trailing GAAP metrics severely distorted by spinoff charges obscure the true financial profile; normalized D/E of 79.8% with $1.22B debt and $271M cash provides moderate but not conservative leverage [3]
  3. Test & Measurement segment faces indirect AI disruption risk as competitors like Keysight may incorporate AI into competing platforms more rapidly, and AI-driven semiconductor design tools could shift testing paradigms [2]
