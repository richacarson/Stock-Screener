Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NasdaqGS ·United States
Ultragenyx Pharmaceutical Inc. is a biopharmaceutical company founded in 2010 by CEO Emil D. Kakkis, M.D., Ph.D., focused on the development and commercialization of novel products for serious rare and ultra-rare genetic diseases. The company markets four approved therapies — Crysvita (burosumab) for X-linked hypophosphatemia, Dojolvi (triheptanoin) for long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders, Evkeeza (evinacumab) for homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, and Mepsevii (vestronidase alfa) for MPS VII — and has a robust gene therapy pipeline targeting glycogen storage disease type Ia (DTX401), Sanfilippo syndrome type A (UX111), and Angelman syndrome (GTX-102). Headquartered in Novato, California.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Rare disease drug development is exceptionally well-protected from AI disruption. The core competencies — conducting clinical trials in ultra-small patient populations (sometimes fewer than 100 patients globally), navigating orphan drug regulatory pathways, manufacturing complex biologics and gene therapy vectors (AAV production, cell processing, quality control), and maintaining physician relationships for ultra-rare disease diagnosis — are irreducibly human and physical. The ultra-rare disease focus creates additional protection: AI-driven drug discovery is least effective in diseases with minimal existing data and tiny patient populations, which is precisely Ultragenyx's domain. Orphan drug exclusivity provides regulatory barriers that AI-enabled competitors cannot easily overcome. The primary AI-related risk is that AI could enable competitors to more efficiently identify and target rare disease drug candidates, potentially compressing Ultragenyx's first-mover advantage in untreated conditions. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI has meaningful potential to benefit Ultragenyx's business — particularly in rare disease patient identification (accelerating diagnosis for diseases that average 5-7 years to diagnose), gene therapy vector optimization, and clinical trial endpoint analysis in small populations where traditional statistical methods are limited. The company's deep clinical datasets across multiple rare diseases could become increasingly valuable as AI tools for rare disease drug development mature.
