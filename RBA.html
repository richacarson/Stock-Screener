Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NYSE ·Canada
RB Global, Inc. (formerly Ritchie Bros. Auctioneers) operates the world's largest commercial asset marketplace, facilitating the buying and selling of heavy equipment, trucks, and other industrial assets through live and online auctions. Following its transformative 2023 merger with IAA (Insurance Auto Auctions), the company expanded into the insurance salvage vehicle market. Also operates SmartEquip (parts procurement), Rouse Services (data analytics), and Big Iron (agriculture equipment, acquired May 2026). Headquartered in Burnaby, British Columbia. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.3B (up 11% YoY) with net income of $132M (up 33%).

Investment Thesis
RB Global's 17% stock decline to $83.20 has compressed the Forward P/E from 23.0x to 17.1x against accelerating earnings growth (Q2 net income up 33% YoY to $132M), creating an increasingly attractive entry point for the world's largest commercial asset marketplace [1]. TTM revenue of $4.85B with consistent 11% YoY growth, combined with FY2026 adjusted EBIT guidance of $1.49-1.55B, demonstrates the IAA merger synergies are delivering [1][3]. The Big Iron acquisition (May 2026) opens the agriculture equipment market, and deployed machine vision AI for asset valuations provides competitive differentiation [2][3]. The circular economy business model — extending asset lifecycles through trusted marketplace transactions — has structural tailwinds as businesses increasingly prioritize used equipment over new purchases during uncertain economic conditions.
The bear case centers on cyclical exposure and post-merger execution risk. The stock's decline from $99.90 to $83.20 (below the $94.76 SMA-50) reflects market concern about used equipment market softening and the macroeconomic cycle. ROE of 8.1% remains below the 15% target, depressed by $5B+ in goodwill from the IAA acquisition, and profit margin of 10.0% is modest for a marketplace business — suggesting full operating leverage from the merger has not yet materialized [5]. Trailing P/E of 35.9x vs forward of 17.1x implies the market expects significant earnings acceleration that could disappoint if construction or transportation activity weakens. The insurance salvage business (IAA) is inherently countercyclical (accidents create supply regardless of economic conditions), which provides balance but doesn't fully offset the procyclical heavy equipment business. The company's reliance on cyclical end-markets exposes it to simultaneous volume and margin compression in a downturn.

  2. ROE of 8.1% reflects $5B+ in acquisition-related goodwill that may face impairment risk if the IAA integration underdelivers on expected synergies, and profit margin of 10.0% has not yet reached marketplace-model efficiency
  3. Post-merger organizational transformation under CEO Kessler with ongoing leadership restructuring creates execution risk during a critical integration period where cultural stability is needed [2]
