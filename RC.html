Company Profile
Financials ·Mortgage REITs ·NYSE ·United States
Ready Capital Corporation is a multi-strategy real estate finance company that originates, acquires, finances, and services lower-to-middle-market commercial real estate loans. The company specializes in agency multifamily, investor and bridge loans, and SBA 7(a) business loans. Ready Capital is externally managed by Waterfall Asset Management and has been repositioning its balance sheet toward a lower-leverage, more capital-efficient platform since late 2025.

Investment Thesis
Ready Capital trades at $1.68 per share against a book value of $7.43, representing a 77% discount to book — a steep discount even for distressed mortgage REITs [1][3]. If the balance sheet repositioning succeeds and the company stabilizes earnings on a lower-leverage platform, the stock could re-rate meaningfully from current levels. The 12-year dividend payment track record suggests management's intent to maintain distributions, and the commercial real estate lending market should eventually recover from current credit stress.
The bear case overwhelms the bull case: Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $1.25 per share consumed 14% of beginning book value in a single quarter, the dividend was slashed 92% to $0.01 per share, and the company is selling loan portfolios at losses to reduce leverage [1]. Debt-to-equity of 381% remains far above the 300% danger threshold even after deleveraging, and the negative forward P/E of -3.32 indicates analysts expect continued losses [3]. The CLO collapse dynamics that triggered the crisis may not be fully resolved, and further credit deterioration in commercial real estate could accelerate book value erosion. At $1.68 per share and a $277 million market cap, the stock reflects near-terminal distress pricing.
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful completion of balance sheet repositioning toward a lower-leverage, fee-based income model that stabilizes distributable earnings and halts book value erosion
  2. Commercial real estate credit conditions improving with Fed rate cuts, reducing CECL reserve charges and enabling loan portfolio recovery
  3. Re-rating of the stock from its 77% discount to book value as the company demonstrates stabilized earnings and restores investor confidence in the dividend
Key Risks
  1. Book value erosion continuing beyond Q1 2026 levels, with additional CLO collapses, loan sales at losses, and CECL reserve charges further destroying equity — book value fell $1.36 per share in Q1 alone
  2. Debt-to-equity of 381% remains dangerously elevated, and inability to refinance or repay maturing obligations could force additional distressed asset sales or a capital raise at deeply dilutive prices
  3. Permanent impairment of the dividend franchise — the 92% cut from $0.125 to $0.01 per quarter may signal the beginning of a complete suspension, destroying the income-investor base that historically supported the stock
