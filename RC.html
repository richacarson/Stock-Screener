Company Profile
Financials ·Mortgage REITs ·NYSE ·United States
Ready Capital is a multi-strategy real estate finance company that originates, acquires, finances, and services lower-to-middle-market commercial real estate loans and SBA 7(a) small business loans. The company is externally managed by Waterfall Asset Management. FY2025 estimated revenue of $541M masks severe underlying deterioration — TTM EPS of -$3.48 reflects massive credit losses and portfolio writedowns. The dividend was slashed 97% from $0.40/quarter (2023) to $0.013/quarter, the stock has declined 68% over the past year to $1.99, and market cap has shrunk to $280M. CEO Tom Capasse has led the company since 2016 through Waterfall Asset Management.

Investment Thesis
Ready Capital at $1.99 is a deep-value or deep-distress play depending on whether the credit cycle turns. The bull case rests on the company's SBA 7(a) lending franchise, which provides government-guaranteed loan origination fees and recurring servicing income with lower credit risk than the commercial real estate portfolio [2]. Analysts forecast revenue growth of 86.9% and earnings growth of 111.3% per annum as the portfolio stabilizes and mark-to-market losses reverse — if these estimates materialize, the stock could re-rate significantly from its sub-$300M market cap [1]. The D/E ratio improved from 378% to 247% between Q1 and Q2 2026, suggesting active deleveraging or asset value recovery. The 'democratizing capital for SMBs' mission aligns with favorable regulatory tailwinds for community-oriented lending.
The bear case is overwhelming. The stock has lost 68% of its value in one year, the dividend was cut 97% (from $1.60/year to $0.052/year), TTM EPS is -$3.48 (1.7x the current stock price in losses), and net margin is -86.57% [1][3]. These are not cyclical adjustments — they reflect structural portfolio impairment from commercial real estate credit deterioration. The external management structure through Waterfall Asset Management creates a permanent principal-agent conflict where the manager earns fees on AUM regardless of shareholder returns. Market cap of $280M against a book value that may be significantly written down suggests the market is pricing in further equity erosion. ROE of -13.30% means every dollar of shareholder equity is being destroyed. The company trades near $2/share, approaching levels where institutional holders face mandate-driven forced selling, delisting risk, and liquidity spirals. Inspire Impact Score of 25 (MIXED) and minimal positive attributions offer no offset for faith-based investors.
