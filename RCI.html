Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·Canada
Rogers Communications Inc. is one of Canada's Big Three telecommunications companies, providing wireless, cable internet, television, and media services. The company operates Canada's largest cable network and a leading wireless network, along with a Sports and Media segment that includes the Toronto Blue Jays (MLB), Sportsnet, and other media properties. Rogers acquired Shaw Communications for C$26B in 2023, transforming it into Canada's largest cable provider. Founded in 1960 by Ted Rogers, headquartered in Toronto, Ontario.

Investment Thesis
Rogers Communications delivered solid Q2 2026 results with total revenue of C$5.62B (up 7.7% YoY), service revenue growth of 8% to C$5.06B, and free cash flow up 6% to C$1B while reducing capex 16% — evidence that the post-Shaw integration is yielding operational leverage [1]. The Sports and Media segment surged 53% driven by Blue Jays revenue and new channel launches. At a forward P/E of just 11.22x with 3.59% dividend yield, Rogers offers compelling value relative to telecom peers. The company's position as one of Canada's Big Three telecoms with dominant wireless and cable positions, government-protected spectrum licenses, and essential infrastructure provides a durable competitive moat. Revenue synergies from the Shaw integration continue to accrue, and debt reduction from improving free cash flow generation should gradually de-leverage the balance sheet.
The bear case centers on governance risk and financial leverage. Rogers carries $25B+ CAD in long-term debt from the Shaw acquisition, resulting in a 182% debt-to-equity ratio that limits financial flexibility and creates meaningful interest expense headwinds in an elevated rate environment [4]. The 2021 boardroom battle — a hostile takeover of the board by Edward Rogers to install his preferred CEO — demonstrated that family-controlled governance can override institutional decision-making, creating ongoing governance risk that is difficult to price [2]. Glassdoor reviews consistently describe deteriorating culture and disconnected leadership, suggesting the organizational foundation may be eroding beneath strong financial results. The trailing PE of 4.3x reflects one-time gains that inflate TTM earnings; normalized earnings power (implied by the 11.22x forward PE) are more modest. Organic revenue growth of 5-6% excluding Shaw is below the innovation leaders in the IOWN universe, and Canadian telecom regulatory risk remains a persistent overhang.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued Shaw integration synergies driving service revenue growth above 8% with declining capex, expanding free cash flow generation for accelerated debt reduction below 4x net debt/EBITDA
  2. Sports and Media segment momentum with 53% Q2 revenue growth from Blue Jays, Sportsnet expansion, and new channel launches creating a differentiated media asset within the telecom portfolio
  3. 5G network buildout completion enabling premium wireless pricing and enterprise connectivity services as AI-driven data demand accelerates across Canadian businesses
Key Risks
  1. Debt-to-equity of 182% with $25B+ CAD in long-term obligations creates severe interest expense headwinds and limits strategic flexibility — any deterioration in free cash flow could force dividend cuts or asset sales
  2. Family-controlled governance under Edward Rogers creates ongoing risk of boardroom disruption — the 2021 board coup demonstrated that personal power dynamics can override institutional governance at any time
  3. Canadian telecom regulatory risk including potential government mandates on wireless pricing, MVNO access requirements, or spectrum policy changes that could compress margins across the Big Three oligopoly
