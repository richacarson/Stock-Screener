Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NYSE ·United States
Arcus Biosciences is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company developing cancer therapies that harness the immune system. The company's pipeline centers on immuno-oncology combination therapies including casdatifan (HIF-2a inhibitor for kidney cancer, in collaboration with BMS and AstraZeneca), quemliclustat (CD73 inhibitor for pancreatic and lung cancer), and earlier-stage programs targeting CD39, AXL, and inflammatory diseases. Co-founded in 2015 by CEO Terry Rosen following the sale of Flexus Biosciences to Bristol Myers Squibb. The company restructured its 10-year Gilead collaboration after the Phase 3 STAR-121 trial (domvanalimab) was terminated for futility in first-line NSCLC. Q2 2026 revenue was $41M (vs $160M YoY, reflecting Gilead partnership restructuring), net loss was $91M (-$0.72/share), and cash was $775M with runway through H2 2028.

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Arcus' core business — developing immuno-oncology therapies through clinical trials — is heavily protected from AI disruption. Drug development requires physical clinical trials with real cancer patients, FDA regulatory approval that takes years, and specialized manufacturing of biological therapeutics (antibodies, small molecules) that AI cannot substitute [3]. The immuno-oncology field has additional protection: combination therapy approaches require complex clinical trial designs that are inherently human-judgment-intensive, and regulatory pathways for oncology drugs involve safety monitoring that cannot be automated away. No analyst has flagged AI as a threat to clinical-stage oncology biotechs. AI could accelerate target identification and trial design, but cannot replace the clinical development and regulatory process. AI Opportunity (0/1): Arcus does not generate AI-related revenue and does not appear to use AI as a primary tool in its drug discovery process. The company's approach is grounded in traditional medicinal chemistry and immunology expertise rather than AI-driven drug design.
