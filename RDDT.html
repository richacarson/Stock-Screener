Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NYSE ·United States
Reddit, Inc. operates a digital community platform organized around interest-based communities where users share content, hold discussions, and exchange goods and services across roughly 100,000 active communities. Founded in 2005 and headquartered in San Francisco, Reddit monetizes through advertising and, increasingly, data-licensing agreements with AI companies (including Google and OpenAI) for training and retrieval access to its user-generated content. The company delivered its eighth consecutive quarter of 60%+ revenue growth in Q2 2026, with CEO Steve Huffman positioning Reddit as 'the most human place on the internet' as a strategic differentiator in the AI content era.

Investment Thesis
Reddit delivered its eighth consecutive quarter of 60%+ revenue growth in Q2 2026, with revenue of $805M beating estimates of $730M, net income more than doubling to $253M, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 43% [1]. U.S. ARPU grew 51% YoY to $11.85 and Active Advertisers grew over 70% YoY on broader adoption of automated ad tools, while Q3 guidance of $860M-$870M implies continued 47-49% growth [1]. The balance sheet is essentially debt-free (0.64% D/E) with $2.8B in cash, and the forward PEG of 0.99 suggests the stock is not pricing in the full growth trajectory. A new, high-margin data-licensing revenue stream from AI companies (Google, OpenAI) provides monetization optionality beyond advertising that did not exist before 2024 [2].
The bear case centers on AI-driven search disintermediation and thin evidence on culture. Reddit's own CEO has acknowledged organic search traffic has been hurt by Google's AI Overviews, and the sustainability of the $60M+ Google licensing relationship is explicitly in question by Huffman's own comments — if that revenue line erodes or AI search continues to reduce top-of-funnel discovery, growth could decelerate faster than the current 47-49% guided range [2]. Reddit also faces securities fraud litigation tied to its 2024 IPO prospectus (motions to dismiss pending as of February 2026) and ongoing content-moderation and data-privacy regulatory pressure, including a prior UK data privacy fine [3].
