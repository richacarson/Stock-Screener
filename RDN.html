Radian Group Inc. is a global multi-line specialty insurer with core operations in private mortgage insurance and, following the $1.7B acquisition of Inigo (a Lloyd's specialty insurer) that closed in Q1 2026, an expanding specialty insurance platform covering political risk, trade credit, and specialty lines. Investment Thesis
Radian Group trades at a deeply discounted 7.3x forward P/E with a PEG ratio of 0.96, offering compelling value for a specialty insurer generating 40.3% profit margins and 13.2% ROE [4]. The transformative $1.7B Inigo acquisition closed in Q1 2026, immediately driving 58% YoY revenue growth to $466M for the quarter and diversifying Radian from cyclical mortgage insurance into specialty insurance and reinsurance at Lloyd's [1][2]. Full-year 2025 earnings of $562.2M on $1.20B revenue demonstrate the profitability of the core MI business, while CEO-elect Weinbach's $5.77M personal stock purchase signals strong insider conviction from incoming leadership [5]. The 2.74% dividend yield with only a 24.9% payout ratio and 5 consecutive years of growth — supported by an estimated 15.3% 5-year dividend growth CAGR — provides reliable income with substantial room for future increases. At $5.1B market cap, Radian is attractively valued if the Inigo integration delivers mid-teens returns on capital deployed.
The bear case centers on Inigo integration risk and mortgage insurance cyclicality. Deploying $1.7B in specialty insurance — a market where Radian has limited experience — carries significant execution risk that could strain management bandwidth during a simultaneous CEO transition [2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -3.6% reveals that the standalone MI business was in secular decline before the acquisition masked it. Housing market cyclicality remains a structural overhang: rising delinquency rates or a housing downturn would directly stress Radian's MI insurance-in-force portfolio, and the company's stock historically correlates with housing health. Cultural concerns are notable: 4 rounds of layoffs in 3.5 years while reporting record profits suggest talent retention challenges during a period when integration execution matters most [3]. The CEO transition from Thornberry to Weinbach adds leadership uncertainty at a critical inflection point, even with Weinbach's strong JPMorgan and Mr. Cooper pedigree. The negative Inspire score (-49) and MISALIGNED faith alignment label may concern values-based investors.
