Company Profile
Real Estate ·Mortgage Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) ·NASDAQ ·United States
Chicago Atlantic Real Estate Finance is a commercial mortgage REIT specializing in first and second lien loans secured by cannabis industry properties. The company originates senior secured, real-estate-backed loans to state-licensed cannabis cultivation, processing, and retail businesses operating in regulated markets. Founded in 2019, IPO'd December 2021, and headquartered in Miami Beach, Florida, REFI manages a $453.1M loan portfolio across 26 portfolio companies with a gross unlevered weighted average yield to maturity of 15.8%. The company is externally managed by Chicago Atlantic Group and fills a niche created by federal cannabis prohibition that prevents traditional banks from lending to the sector.

Investment Thesis
REFI offers an extraordinary 17.75% dividend yield from a niche cannabis lending operation with $453.1M in loans across 26 portfolio companies at a gross unlevered weighted average yield to maturity of 15.8% [1]. The stock trades at just 5.60x forward earnings and 0.88x book value, reflecting deep value if the cannabis lending market stabilizes. Conservative leverage (46.6% D/E) and 60%+ profit margins demonstrate the structural profitability of being one of the few capital providers in a federally underbanked industry. Q2 2026 distributable earnings of $0.43/share cover the $0.47 quarterly dividend at approximately 91%, within management's 90-100% target range.
The bear case is severe: revenue declined 13.73% YoY and EPS fell 26% as the cannabis lending market contracts under persistent federal prohibition uncertainty [4]. The payout ratio of 138% on a net income basis (vs. distributable earnings) signals that the dividend may require further cuts — REFI already reduced its quarterly dividend from $0.76 to $0.47 in 2024. Book value erosion ($14.39 to $14.15 in one quarter) indicates the company is slowly consuming capital. Total liquidity of only $16M as of August 11, 2026 leaves minimal buffer against credit losses, while the $9.4M credit loss reserve suggests portfolio stress [1]. Federal cannabis rescheduling or legalization — while potentially positive long-term — would eliminate REFI's competitive moat by allowing traditional banks to enter cannabis lending at far lower yields. The stock's 164,945 average daily volume creates meaningful liquidity risk for institutional-sized positions.
Key Catalysts
  1. Federal cannabis rescheduling from Schedule I to Schedule III could dramatically improve borrower creditworthiness, reduce credit losses, and expand the addressable lending market — though it would also invite bank competition at lower yields [2]
  2. New second-lien financing products ($62.5M July 2026 deal) could expand the total addressable portfolio beyond first-lien senior secured, increasing origination volume and net interest income if credit quality holds [1]
  3. Analyst coverage expansion (Citizens initiated at Market Outperform with $15.12 average price target on August 12, 2026) may improve institutional awareness and reduce the deep discount to book value [4]
Key Risks
  1. Cannabis industry credit deterioration as borrower profitability compresses from state-level price deflation and oversupply — the $9.4M credit loss reserve on a $453.1M portfolio may prove inadequate if operator defaults accelerate [1]
  2. Dividend sustainability: the 138% net income payout ratio and prior dividend cut from $0.76 to $0.47/quarter signal that further reductions are possible if distributable earnings continue declining, which would remove the primary investment thesis [4]
  3. Federal legalization risk paradox — while broadly positive for cannabis, full legalization would eliminate the regulatory moat that prevents traditional banks from competing, compressing REFI's lending yields from 15.8% toward conventional commercial real estate rates of 6-8% [2]
