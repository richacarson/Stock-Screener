Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Mortgage ·NASDAQ ·United States
Chicago Atlantic Real Estate Finance, Inc. is a commercial mortgage REIT focused on originating, structuring, and investing in first mortgage loans secured by commercial real estate properties of state-licensed cannabis operators in limited-license states. Externally managed by Chicago Atlantic REIT Manager, LLC, the company holds a $462M lending pipeline and emphasizes senior secured loans at conservative leverage (1-2x EBITDA) with real covenants. FY2025 revenue was $55.6M, up 1.4% from FY2024. On June 17, 2026, REFI agreed to merge into sister entity Chicago Atlantic BDC (LIEN) in an all-stock transaction, with LIEN as the surviving entity; former REFI stockholders expected to own approximately 50.5% of the combined company. Kahn Swick & Foti LLC is investigating the fairness of the merger to REFI shareholders. Co-CEOs Peter Sack and Anthony Cappell lead from offices in Miami Beach, Florida.

Investment Thesis
REFI at $9.35 offers a 20.1% dividend yield with a forward P/E of 4.87 and 60.2% profit margin as the only publicly traded cannabis mortgage REIT, occupying a category-defining niche alongside sister entity LIEN [2]. The company's conservative underwriting — senior secured first-lien loans at 1-2x EBITDA with real covenants — has produced a loan portfolio with strong credit quality, and the $462M lending pipeline provides origination visibility [1]. The pending merger with LIEN would create a combined cannabis lending platform with both direct loans and real estate mortgage exposure, potentially improving liquidity and diversification. Cannabis rescheduling from Schedule I to Schedule III, with recreational rescheduling pending DEA review, is a structural tailwind for the cannabis industry and REFI's borrower base. Forward P/E of 4.87 implies the market deeply discounts the sustainability of earnings.
The bear case centers on the unsustainable payout, regulatory risk, and merger uncertainties. The 137% payout ratio means REFI is distributing more than it earns, requiring either capital market access or portfolio runoff to sustain the dividend — a pattern that cannot continue indefinitely [1]. Revenue declined 7.5% in the most recent quarter with EPS falling 17.1% YoY, suggesting the cannabis lending market may be saturating or REFI's competitive position is eroding. The pending LIEN merger is under investigation by Kahn Swick & Foti LLC regarding fairness to REFI shareholders, and the externally managed structure creates inherent conflicts of interest between the Chicago Atlantic management team and REFI stockholders [3]. Cannabis remains federally illegal, and a shift in DOJ enforcement posture could impair the entire loan portfolio. The 20% dividend yield signals the market perceives significant risk of a dividend cut. If cannabis is fully legalized, traditional mortgage REITs will enter the market with lower costs of capital, compressing the premium yields that justify REFI's existence as a separate entity.
