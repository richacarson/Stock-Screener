Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE American ·United States
Ring Energy, Inc. is an independent oil and natural gas company engaged in the acquisition, exploration, development, and production of conventional and unconventional oil and natural gas properties in the Permian Basin of West Texas and New Mexico. Founded in 2004 and headquartered in The Woodlands, Texas, the company operates approximately 83,550 net developed acres with production of ~19,350 Boe/d.

Investment Thesis
Ring Energy offers deep value exposure to Permian Basin conventional oil at $1.07 per share, trading at 0.36x book value and 4.65x forward earnings, with a PV-10 reserve value of $1.32B against a $272M market cap [1][5]. The company generated record adjusted free cash flow of $50.1M in FY2025 (+15% YoY) and has delivered 26 consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow despite 18% lower realized oil prices [1]. Proved reserves grew 14% YoY to 153.3 MMBoe with a 169% organic reserve replacement ratio. Operating lift costs of $10.41/BOE beat guidance for a fourth consecutive quarter, and drilling efficiency improved 15% in the Northwest Shelf [1]. Strong insider buying by CEO McKinney (50,000 shares) and CFO Johl (231,000 shares) in June 2026 signals management confidence [4].
The bear case is commodity-dependent and structural. Revenue declined 12% in FY2025 to $307.2M, and Q1 2026 featured a $162.1M ceiling test impairment signaling that SEC pricing deems reserves overvalued at current strip prices [1]. The $426M credit facility balance against a $585M borrowing base leaves limited cushion, and the May 2026 dilutive equity offering of 44.4M shares at $1.35 (24% discount) underscores balance sheet stress [5]. Negative ROE of -35.3% and profit margin of -92% (impairment-driven) create a challenging GAAP picture, and the Altman Z-Score of 0.19 places the company in distress territory. At a $272M market cap with $420M+ in debt, equity holders are significantly subordinated to creditors, making this a leveraged commodity bet with asymmetric downside risk if WTI remains below $60/bbl [1][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Oil price recovery above $70/bbl WTI would significantly improve margins, eliminate ceiling test impairments, and restore positive GAAP earnings given sub-$11/BOE operating costs and $65.21/bbl hedge protection on 48% of 2026 oil production [1]
  2. Continued drilling efficiency gains -- 15% faster spud-to-TD in Northwest Shelf and improving horizontal mix to 85% -- could reduce per-well costs and improve capital efficiency across the multi-year conventional inventory [1]
  3. Balance sheet deleveraging from the $60M equity offering plus operating cash flow could reduce leverage ratio from 2.42x toward the 2.0x range, improving equity value per share and reducing borrowing base redetermination risk [1][5]
Key Risks
  1. Sustained oil prices below $60/bbl WTI would trigger additional ceiling test impairments beyond the $270.9M already recorded in FY2025-Q1 2026, and could pressure the $585M borrowing base at the next semi-annual redetermination, potentially forcing additional dilutive capital raises [1]
  2. Micro-cap status ($272M market cap) with $420M+ in debt, Altman Z-Score of 0.19, and a May 2026 dilutive equity offering at 24% discount signal elevated financial distress risk and ongoing equity subordination to creditors [5]
  3. Flat production guidance of ~20,150 Boe/d for 2026 with $115M maintenance capital offers no growth catalyst absent commodity price recovery, while revenue has declined in two of the last three years [1]
