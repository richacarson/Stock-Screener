Investment Thesis
RELX is a high-margin, subscription-heavy analytics compounder whose AI investments are visibly paying off. H1 2026 underlying revenue grew 7%, underlying adjusted operating profit grew 9%, the margin rose to 35.5%, and adjusted EPS rose 11% at constant currency to 68.6p [1]. Legal accelerated to 10% growth on Lexis+ AI and Protégé, and AI-enabled products now drive about 90% of new LexisNexis sales value [2]. At 18.4x forward earnings, a 2.3% yield with 10 straight years of ADR dividend growth, and a 6.9% 5-year dividend CAGR [4], the AI-fear de-rating could be an entry point.
The bear case is that generative AI erodes the pricing power of legal research and scientific publishing over time even if near-term growth holds. The shares remain about 30% below their high on that fear [7]. The balance sheet is levered for buybacks (D/E 710%, $8.9B debt vs. $220M cash) [4], reported revenue growth is only 2.7% after currency effects [4], and Elsevier faces ongoing criticism of its subscription model and data practices. The 2024 greenwashing and retaliation suit adds governance noise [3]. Exhibitions also carries Middle East event uncertainty [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2026 results (February 2027) confirming underlying profit growth above revenue growth, as reaffirmed at H1 [1]
  2. Continued Lexis+ AI / Protégé penetration sustaining Legal's 10% underlying growth and disproving AI-substitution fears [2]
  3. Completion of the remaining £0.5B of the £2.25B 2026 buyback and a FY2026 dividend increase extending the growth streak [1][4]
Key Risks
  1. Frontier-model legal and research agents compressing LexisNexis and Elsevier pricing; the shares already trade about 30% below their high on this fear [7]
  2. High leverage (D/E 710%) from buybacks limits flexibility if AI disruption hits cash flows [4]
  3. Regulatory and reputational pressure on Elsevier's academic-publishing model and on LexisNexis Risk data-broker practices [3]
