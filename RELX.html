Company Profile
Technology ·Information Services ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
RELX PLC is a global provider of information-based analytics and decision tools, serving professional and business customers across four segments: Risk (insurance analytics, identity verification), Scientific, Technical & Medical (Elsevier academic publishing, ScienceDirect), Legal (LexisNexis legal research, Lexis+ AI), and Exhibitions (trade shows). Under CEO Erik Engstrom since 2009, RELX transformed from print publisher Reed Elsevier into a digital analytics powerhouse, reducing print revenue from 27% to below 5%. H1 2026 underlying revenue grew 7% with adjusted operating profit up 9%. Market cap is approximately $63B.

Investment Thesis
RELX at 17.91x forward earnings is a high-quality compounder delivering consistent mid-single-digit organic growth with expanding margins — H1 2026 underlying revenue grew 7% with adjusted operating profit up 9% and margins expanding 70bps to a record 35.5% [1]. The company generates $3.87B in annual free cash flow (6.1% FCF yield), funding both a $1.59B annual dividend (2.50% yield) and GBP 2.25B in 2026 share buybacks while maintaining net debt/EBITDA at a comfortable 2.0x. ROE of 131.86% and profit margin of 23.27% reflect best-in-class capital efficiency among information services companies [4]. RELX's AI strategy is uniquely positioned — rather than competing to build foundation models, the company layers AI on top of irreplaceable proprietary data assets (Elsevier's scientific corpus, LexisNexis legal databases, 78B+ identity records), creating AI-powered products that deepen customer lock-in and expand addressable markets [2].
The bear case centers on valuation premium versus growth rate, succession risk, and AI competitive dynamics. At 17.91x forward earnings, RELX trades at a significant premium to its 7% underlying revenue growth — the PEG ratio of 1.39 suggests the market prices in sustained margin expansion and share count reduction, leaving limited room for deceleration [4]. CEO Erik Engstrom has led the company for 17 years with exceptional results, but no succession plan has been publicly discussed, and his described 'very distant' leadership style raises questions about whether the institutional culture can survive a transition [3]. The D/E of 710% (mechanically driven by thin book equity from acquisitions) overstates risk but highlights that the balance sheet is leveraged with $11.82B in total debt. In AI, RELX faces a two-sided competitive threat: generative AI tools could partially displace traditional legal and research workflows, while open-access publishing models threaten Elsevier's subscription revenues [3]. The 52-week stock price decline of 23.67% from $48.40 suggests the market has partially repriced these risks. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) is neutral for faith-based investors [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-powered product suite (Lexis+ AI, ScienceDirect AI, Risk analytics) gaining customer traction across all four segments, driving operating margin expansion toward 37%+ as AI reduces content acquisition costs and increases customer willingness-to-pay for enhanced analytics [1][2]
  2. GBP 2.25B 2026 share buyback program (GBP 1.75B already completed) reducing share count by ~2.6% annually, compounding EPS growth to 11% versus 7% underlying revenue growth — a mathematical tailwind that amplifies per-share economics [1]

Key Risks

  2. CEO succession risk after 17 years under Erik Engstrom — no public succession plan, and recent insider selling of GBP 2.5M in shares raises questions about leadership transition timing and whether the digital transformation culture can be sustained under new leadership [3]
  3. D/E of 710% with $11.82B in total debt, though mitigated by 2.0x net debt/EBITDA and $3.87B FCF, creates sensitivity to rising interest rates and limits balance sheet flexibility for large-scale M&A that might be needed to compete with AI-native competitors [4]
