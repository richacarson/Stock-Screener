Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure (Digital Cross-Border Payments) ·NASDAQ ·United States
Remitly Global provides digital financial services for immigrants and their families, chiefly mobile-first international money transfers to nearly 150 countries. Founded in 2011 and headquartered in Seattle, the company generated $1.64B of revenue in FY2025 and $495M in Q2 2026 (+20% YoY on +27% send volume). Co-founder Matt Oppenheimer handed the CEO role to former Amazon executive Sebastian Gunningham and moved to board chair. Q3 2026 results are scheduled for November 4, 2026, with guidance of $505-507M revenue (+20-21%) [1][6][8].

Investment Thesis
Remitly is a high-growth, now-profitable leader in digital remittances. Q2 2026 revenue rose 20% to $495M on 27% send-volume growth, FY2026 guidance has been raised four straight times to $1.978-1.988B (+21-22%), and the balance sheet carries essentially no debt (D/E of 3.4%) [1][6]. At $24.15 the stock trades at about 22.6x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.07 and roughly 11.7x TTM free cash flow, with analysts modeling EPS rising to $2.13 by FY2029 [3][4]. A licensed network across nearly 150 countries and a mission-led brand among immigrant customers give it a durable position that AI does not threaten [10].
Revenue is growing about 7 points slower than send volume, a sign of take-rate compression as competition from Wise, legacy players and stablecoin-based rails intensifies [7]. Reported profitability flatters the trend: Q2 2026 net income of $206M included a $141M tax benefit, and pretax margin is only about 9% [2]. Leadership is in transition from founder Matt Oppenheimer to Sebastian Gunningham, insiders have been consistent net sellers with no open-market purchases, and immigration and remittance-tax policy remain exogenous risks to the core customer base [5][8]. Q3 results on November 4, 2026 are a near-term volatility event.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results on November 4, 2026 against guidance of $505-507M revenue (+20-21%); a fifth consecutive guidance raise would reinforce the compounding story
  2. Continued mix shift to high-value senders and broader immigrant financial products lifting revenue per customer
  3. Operating leverage on a nearly debt-free balance sheet, with consensus EPS climbing from $1.07 (FY2027) to $2.13 (FY2029)
Key Risks
  1. Take-rate compression: send volume +27% versus revenue +20% in Q2 2026 as stablecoin and real-time payment rails lower cross-border transfer pricing
  2. US immigration enforcement or remittance excise taxes shrinking the core sender base or raising costs per transaction
  3. Execution risk under a new non-founder CEO, combined with heavy insider selling and earnings quality distorted by a one-time $141M tax benefit

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Cross-border remittances require money-transmitter licenses, AML/KYC compliance and payout partnerships across nearly 150 countries, which AI cannot replicate. Motley Fool/Nasdaq analysis notes there are no signs Remitly is being disrupted by AI and calls it more insulated than many fintech peers [10]. The more credible technology threat is stablecoin and real-time payment rails compressing take rates, flagged in a March 2025 short-seller report. The gap between 27% send-volume growth and 20% revenue growth suggests pricing pressure is already building [7]. AI Opportunity (0/1): AI is used internally for product velocity and customer support, but Remitly reports no AI-attributable revenue [9].
