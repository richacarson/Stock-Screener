Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Replimune Group, Inc. is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing oncolytic immunotherapies to treat cancer. Its proprietary RPx platform uses selectively replicating herpes simplex viruses (HSV-1) engineered to activate the immune system against tumors. Lead candidate RP1 (vusolimogene oderparepvec) targets advanced melanoma with launch readiness underway, while RP2 (encoding anti-CTLA-4) is in a registrational trial for uveal melanoma. Founded in 2015, headquartered in Woburn, Massachusetts, with $195M in cash as of June 2026.

Investment Thesis
Replimune is a high-risk, high-reward clinical-stage biotech with its lead asset RP1 approaching potential commercialization in advanced melanoma, the stock having recovered from a July 2026 crash low of $6.02 to $13.07, suggesting improving FDA regulatory trajectory [1][4]. The RP1 3-year data is genuinely impressive: 47.8% of treated patients alive at 3 years with median overall survival of 32.9 months in anti-PD-1-failed melanoma, and 83.5% 3-year survival among responders [2]. If approved and launched, RP1 would be only the second FDA-approved oncolytic virus therapy (after Amgen's T-VEC) addressing a specific unmet need in checkpoint inhibitor-failed melanoma. RP2's registrational trial in uveal melanoma (enrolling well globally) and new biliary tract cancer cohort provide pipeline diversification beyond the lead asset [1]. At $1.2B market cap, the stock offers significant leverage to successful commercialization.
The bear case centers on financing risk, execution uncertainty, and regulatory history. Cash of $195.3M as of June 2026 (burning ~$74M per quarter) provides runway only into Q1 calendar 2027 — the company will likely need to raise capital to fund commercial launch even if RP1 is approved, and dilutive financing at a biotech struggling with FDA history is a real risk [1]. The securities class action lawsuit alleging concealment of critical risks adds legal liability and management distraction. Replimune has zero product revenue, negative ROE of -134%, and D/E of 106%, making fundamental financial analysis nearly impossible — this is a binary bet on FDA approval and commercial execution [4]. The CEO transition from founder to Patel occurred during the most critical regulatory period, and prior layoffs suggest resource constraints. The pre-commercial biotech model means even approval requires successful commercial launch, payer negotiations, and oncologist adoption against established checkpoint inhibitor combinations, each of which carries meaningful risk.
