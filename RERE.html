Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Internet Retail ·NYSE ·China
ATRenew Inc. is China's leading technology-driven pre-owned consumer electronics transactions and services platform, founded by Kerry Chen in 2011 with a mission to 'give a second life to all idle goods.' The company operates an omni-channel platform including AHS (Ai Hui Shou) stores for device trade-in, online recycling services, and B2B resale markets for refurbished electronics. Q2 2026 revenue grew 32.4% YoY to RMB 6,609M with operating income up 95.7% and net income of RMB 129M [1]. The company invests RMB 240M annually in R&D with 449 R&D personnel, deploying AI for device-model identification and quality grading [3]. CEO Kerry Chen was named to the TIME100 Climate list for advancing large-scale circular economy practices.

Investment Thesis
ATRenew is the dominant platform in China's rapidly growing pre-owned electronics market, compounding revenue at 32.4% YoY with accelerating profitability — Q2 2026 operating income surged 95.7% and net income reached RMB 129M [1]. At just 6.45x forward earnings with a near-debt-free balance sheet (D/E 13.15%), the stock offers deep value for a company growing revenue 30%+ annually in a secular growth category supported by China's national trade-in subsidy programs [1][2]. The circular economy mission earned CEO Kerry Chen a TIME100 Climate recognition, and the RMB 240M annual R&D investment in AI-driven grading and pricing technology creates a widening competitive moat [3]. The company reached its first full year of GAAP profitability in FY2025, with margin expansion continuing as operating leverage kicks in on the growing revenue base.
The bear case centers on structural margin limitations, China risk, and competitive threats. Profit margin remains razor-thin at 2.01%, reflecting the fundamentally low-margin nature of electronics resale where gross margins compress on high-volume, low-value-add transactions [1]. Operating as a Chinese ADR listed on NYSE creates existential regulatory risk under the HFCAA framework — the PCAOB audit access agreement could lapse, potentially forcing delisting that would make shares inaccessible to U.S. investors. Competition from JD.com and Alibaba's recycling platforms, which have vastly larger user bases and engineering resources, could compress margins further and cap market share gains. Chinese consumer spending remains uncertain amid property sector headwinds, youth unemployment, and deflationary pressures. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects the absence of both positive and negative faith-alignment data for this Chinese company [4]. The $3.9M average daily dollar volume, while clearing the liquidity floor, limits position sizing for institutional investors.
