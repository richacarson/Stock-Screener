Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
RPC, Inc. provides diversified oilfield services and equipment to independent and major oil and gas companies. Core services include pressure pumping (fracturing), downhole tools, coiled tubing, nitrogen services, and well control. Founded in 1984 and headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. Investment Thesis
RPC delivered a strong Q1 2026 with revenue of $454.8M (up 36.6% YoY), the fastest growth and largest analyst beat among oilfield services peers, driven by Technical Services strength in pressure pumping and downhole tools [1]. The conservative balance sheet with just 34% debt-to-equity and $160-180M capex guidance provides financial flexibility through oil price volatility. At $1.2B market cap with $1.75B TTM revenue, the stock trades below 1x revenue, offering value if margins recover toward historical levels.
The fundamental challenge is profitability compression: net income collapsed 72.5% to $19.6M on $1.75B revenue, yielding a 1.1% profit margin and 4.4% ROE [1]. The PE ratio of 61x on trailing earnings reflects a business that has lost pricing power despite strong revenue growth — a classic oilfield services squeeze where costs have risen faster than service pricing. The $0.16 annual dividend (2.83% yield) is not covered by earnings with a 107% payout ratio, raising sustainability questions [4]. Chairman Hubbell's insider selling and the company's decade-long resistance to ESG disclosure despite shareholder pressure from Walden Asset Management suggest governance prioritizes existing management interests [3]. The stock is highly cyclical — a decline in US drilling activity or oil prices below $60 would severely compress both revenue and margins.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued Technical Services revenue growth as pressure pumping demand strengthens with potential US energy policy support and increased onshore drilling activity
  2. Margin recovery toward historical mid-teens EBITDA margins (currently 11.8%) as pricing normalizes and dual-fuel fleet efficiency gains materialize
  3. Conservative balance sheet (34% D/E) provides flexibility for fleet expansion or shareholder returns if commodity prices remain supportive
Key Risks
  1. Profit margin of 1.1% and payout ratio of 107% are unsustainable — further earnings deterioration would force a dividend cut and could trigger equity dilution
  2. Oilfield services is deeply cyclical; a decline in US oil prices below $60 or OPEC+ production increases would compress both demand and pricing for pressure pumping services
  3. Chairman insider selling of $101K in January 2026 and decade-long resistance to ESG disclosure signal governance prioritizing insider interests over long-term stakeholder value

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): RPC's oilfield services business is structurally immune to AI disruption. Pressure pumping, hydraulic fracturing, coiled tubing, and well control operations require heavy physical equipment, specialized crews, and on-site execution in remote drilling locations [1]. AI cannot substitute for the deployment of fracturing fleets, the physical pumping of proppant and fluid into wells, or the hands-on maintenance of downhole tools. Regulatory requirements (environmental permits, safety certifications, BOP testing) and the capital intensity of equipment fleets create additional barriers. AI may actually increase demand for oilfield services by enabling E&P companies to identify more productive drilling locations through seismic data analysis. The only minor AI exposure is in back-office operations and pricing optimization where competitors could gain efficiency advantages. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of AI-specific revenue generation. RPC has not disclosed AI product offerings, AI-driven services, or quantifiable AI revenue contributions [2].
