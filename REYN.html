Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Packaging & Containers ·NASDAQ ·United States
Reynolds Consumer Products produces and sells household products across four segments: Reynolds Cooking & Baking (aluminum foil, parchment paper, plastic wrap under Reynolds Wrap, Reynolds Kitchens, and EZ Foil brands), Hefty Waste & Storage (trash and food storage bags), Hefty Tableware (disposable plates, bowls, cups), and Presto Products (store brand bags and wraps). Founded in 1947 and headquartered in Lake Forest, Illinois, the company reported TTM revenue of $3.8B with Q2 2026 EPS up 20% YoY. Reynolds is a subsidiary of Packaging Finance Limited.

Investment Thesis
Reynolds Consumer Products at 12.65x forward earnings with a 4.25% dividend yield offers a defensive consumer staples play anchored by iconic brands (Reynolds Wrap, Hefty) with dominant shelf positions in recession-resistant categories [1][4]. Q2 2026 delivered 20% EPS growth to $0.42 on modest revenue growth, demonstrating effective pricing and productivity execution against $400M in annualized commodity headwinds. Full-year 2026 guidance of $1.57-$1.63 EPS represents continued earnings recovery, and free cash flow of $244M (6.3% FCF yield) supports the dividend and debt reduction. D/E of 71% and current ratio of 1.8 indicate a stable balance sheet. The intelligent factory program and DemandAI+ adoption position the company for longer-term operational improvements [2].
The bear case centers on structural stagnation and cultural dysfunction. Revenue has compounded at approximately 1% over five years, and the core product categories (aluminum foil, trash bags, tableware) face private label pressure and limited growth potential [1]. The $0.92 annual dividend has been frozen since 2021 — zero growth across six years while inflation eroded purchasing power. Profit margin of 9.1% is below packaging industry peers, and the company remains a subsidiary of Packaging Finance Limited, limiting strategic independence. Employee sentiment is deeply negative: 16% CEO approval on Glassdoor with reports of chaos and zero work-life balance suggest organizational dysfunction that could impair execution [2]. Short interest of 11.8% reflects market skepticism. With no next-generation product pipeline and no evidence of strategic transformation, REYN risks being a value trap — cheap on earnings multiples but unable to generate the growth or dividend increases needed to reward long-term holders [4].
