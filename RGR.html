Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Leisure Products ·NYSE ·United States
Sturm, Ruger & Company designs, manufactures, and sells firearms for the commercial sporting market in the United States. Products include pistols, revolvers, rifles, and shotguns marketed under the Ruger brand. The company operates manufacturing facilities in Southport, Connecticut; Newport, New Hampshire; Mayodan, North Carolina; and Prescott, Arizona. Founded in 1949, headquartered in Southport, Connecticut. Virtually debt-free with $105 million cash and $1.65 million in debt.

Investment Thesis
Ruger's Q2 2026 results signal a potential cyclical turning point: net sales of $158.1 million grew 19.3% YoY, adjusted EPS of $0.52 beat estimates by $0.10, and average selling price rose 10% to $384, reflecting improved product mix and disciplined pricing under new CEO Todd Seyfert [1]. The company's fortress balance sheet — $105 million cash, $1.65 million debt, 3.50 current ratio — provides exceptional financial flexibility to invest through the cycle [4]. At approximately 18x forward earnings, valuation is reasonable for a consumer products company with brand loyalty, physical manufacturing moats, and the structural demand tailwind of the Second Amendment. Firearms demand has historically proven resilient across political cycles, and the cyclical trough of 2024-2025 may be over as evidenced by Q2's strong revenue inflection.
The bear case is substantial: TTM ROE of -3.98% and TTM profit margin of -2.18% reflect a business that has been unprofitable on a trailing basis, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of approximately -5.4% (from the 2021 COVID-surge peak of $731M to current levels near $552M) demonstrates that the industry's demand normalization has been severe [4][5]. Glassdoor ratings of 3.0/5 and employee reviews citing layoffs, leadership accountability issues, and limited engineering input raise concerns about organizational health under the new CEO transition [2]. Net insider selling ($712K selling vs. $208K buying) contrasts sharply with companies where management has strong conviction [3]. The firearms industry faces persistent ESG headwinds — Bank of America and Wells Fargo have both declined to provide credit to Ruger due to ESG policies, limiting financial flexibility [7]. The dividend yield of 1.18% with a declining 5-year CAGR of -5.0% offers minimal income support for patient investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q2 2026 revenue inflection to 19.3% YoY growth with 10% ASP increase suggests the firearms demand cycle may have bottomed, with potential for sustained recovery driving margin expansion and positive earnings trajectory
  2. New CEO Todd Seyfert's manufacturing expertise from Dometic Group driving operational improvements in product mix, pricing discipline, and gross margin expansion that could lift ROE back to historical mid-teens levels
  3. Fortress balance sheet with $105M cash and zero effective debt enables opportunistic capital deployment including potential acquisitions, share buybacks, or accelerated new product development
Key Risks
  1. TTM ROE of -3.98% and profit margin of -2.18% demonstrate the business has been unprofitable — the Q2 recovery must prove sustainable rather than a single-quarter anomaly to justify current valuation
  2. ESG-driven financial exclusion by major banks (Bank of America, Wells Fargo declining credit) creates structural financing disadvantages relative to non-firearms manufacturers and limits strategic flexibility
  3. Glassdoor ratings of 3.0/5 with culture at 2.6/5, two rounds of layoffs, and net insider selling signal organizational instability during a critical CEO transition that could undermine operational execution

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Firearms manufacturing is a physical-world business involving precision machining, metallurgy, assembly, quality testing, and distribution through federally licensed dealers (FFLs) — none of which AI can replace or substitute [1]. The industry is deeply regulated by the ATF, requiring physical compliance infrastructure, background check systems, and serialized product tracking that are structurally immune to AI disruption. Consumer demand for firearms is driven by Second Amendment exercise, personal protection, hunting, and sport shooting — fundamental physical activities that AI cannot displace. No analyst coverage has ever identified AI as a risk factor for firearms manufacturers. However, AI does not actively increase demand for firearms (unlike energy or semiconductor sectors where AI is a demand driver), limiting the risk protection score to 8 rather than 9. AI Opportunity (0/1): Ruger generates no revenue from AI products or services. No AI deployment in customer-facing operations has been identified.
