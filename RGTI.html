Company Profile
Technology ·Computer Hardware ·NCM ·United States
Rigetti Computing builds and operates quantum computers and the superconducting quantum processors that power them. Products include the 9-qubit Novera QPU, 36-qubit multi-chip Cepheus system, and 84-qubit Ankaa-3 quantum computer, offered through hardware sales, quantum computing as a service (QCaaS), Rigetti Foundry Services for custom quantum chip fabrication, and professional services. TTM revenue of $13.35M growing 185% YoY in Q2 2026, driven by Novera QPU deliveries and government contracts. Founded in 2013, headquartered in Berkeley, California.
162 Employees ·www.rigetti.com

Investment Thesis
Rigetti Computing at $15.62 is a pure-play quantum computing company experiencing explosive revenue growth — Q2 2026 revenue of $5.14M grew 185% YoY, Q1 grew 193% to $4.4M, and analysts project 220% revenue growth for FY2026 — driven by Novera QPU deliveries and expanding government contracts [1][2]. The vertically integrated fabrication model (in-house Fab-1 in Fremont) provides a competitive moat that most quantum peers lack, and the $393.7M cash position with minimal debt ($7.7M) provides 5-6 years of operational runway. The multi-revenue-stream approach — hardware (Novera), cloud (QCaaS), foundry services, and professional services — diversifies commercialization pathways, and the 84-qubit Ankaa-3 positions Rigetti for the next generation of quantum applications [1].
The bear case is familiar for pre-revenue quantum computing stocks: $5.2B market cap on $13.35M TTM revenue means the stock trades at 390x sales — pricing in a quantum computing future that may arrive years or decades later than investors expect [1][2]. Operating cash burn of $60.7M annually means the company consumes approximately 4.5x its revenue in operations, and while the cash position provides runway, eventual dilutive raises are likely unless revenue scales dramatically. Glassdoor reviews (62% recommend, 58% CEO approval) describing a 'hire and fire culture,' 'egocentric CEO,' and organizational dysfunction raise serious concerns about whether the company can execute on its technological promise with only 162 employees [3]. A pending class action lawsuit adds legal risk. Classical computing and AI improvements continue advancing, potentially extending the timeline before quantum achieves meaningful commercial advantage, and competitors IBM and Google have vastly larger resources to invest in quantum R&D [4]. The stock has fallen 73% from its 52-week high of $58.15, suggesting momentum traders have already exited.
