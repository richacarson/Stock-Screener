Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYSE ·Switzerland
Transocean is the world's largest offshore drilling contractor by fleet value, specializing in ultra-deepwater and harsh-environment semisubmersible drilling rigs. Domiciled in Steinhausen, Switzerland, with operational headquarters in Houston, Texas, the company operates a fleet of approximately 36 rigs serving major oil companies globally. Under CEO Keelan Adamson, Transocean reported Q2 2026 revenue of $966M with 97.3% revenue efficiency and $170M net income — a return to profitability after years of losses. The company guides FY2026 revenue of $3.9-3.975B and maintains a contract backlog of approximately $6.7B, excluding $1B in pending Equinor approvals. Transocean was the owner of the Deepwater Horizon rig at the time of the 2010 Gulf of Mexico oil spill.

Investment Thesis
Transocean offers exposure to a tightening ultra-deepwater drilling market at a cyclical inflection — Q2 2026 revenue of $966M with $170M net income marks a return to profitability, contract backlog of $6.7B provides nearly 7 quarters of revenue visibility, and the $1B+ Equinor harsh-environment contract signals sustained demand for the company's premium rig fleet [1]. Active drillship coverage of 94% for the remainder of 2026 and 81% for 2027, combined with $212M in Q2 free cash flow, positions the company to begin deleveraging its ~$5.5B debt load. The stock at $5.05 trades at a significant discount to book value, and any sustained profitability could trigger a meaningful re-rating as the market recognizes the structural improvement in offshore drilling fundamentals.
The bear case centers on the massive debt burden, cyclicality, and energy transition risk. Approximately $5.5B in debt against a market cap of roughly $4B means equity holders bear significant financial risk — interest expense consumes a large portion of operating cash flow, and any downturn in day rates could quickly push the company back into losses [3]. The forward P/E of 32.07 suggests the market already prices in profitability improvement, limiting upside from current levels. Offshore drilling is inherently cyclical, and the current upcycle could reverse if oil prices decline or E&P capex budgets contract. The TTM net margin of -66.79% reflects the reality that recent profitability may be temporary rather than structural [3]. The Deepwater Horizon legacy (2010) creates permanent ESG and reputational overhang. Long-term energy transition away from fossil fuels threatens the secular demand outlook for ultra-deepwater drilling, though the timeline remains uncertain. The Inspire Impact Score of 41 (ALIGNED) is a moderate positive for faith-based investors despite the fossil fuel business.
