Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NCM ·United States
Riot Platforms is a vertically-integrated digital infrastructure company operating through Bitcoin Mining and Engineering segments. The company mines bitcoin at large-scale facilities in Texas, designs and manufactures power distribution equipment, and is pivoting to AI data center hosting with 241 MW of contracted capacity including a 20-year, $9.1B lease with a frontier AI lab at its Rockdale, Texas campus. Founded in 2000 and headquartered in Castle Rock, Colorado with 816 employees, Riot generated TTM revenue of approximately $674M but remains unprofitable with a -196% profit margin due to bitcoin price volatility and infrastructure investment.

Investment Thesis
Riot Platforms at $18.54 with a $7.0B market cap is a speculative bet on the conversion of bitcoin mining power infrastructure into long-term, contracted AI data center revenue. The $9.1B Rockdale lease (20-year term with a frontier AI lab) and AMD hosting contract bring total contracted capacity to 241 MW representing approximately $9.8B in long-term revenue, fundamentally transforming the business model from volatile bitcoin mining to predictable infrastructure hosting [2]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 14% to $174.2M, the balance sheet holds significant bitcoin reserves, and the multi-gigawatt Texas power position is an asset that is extremely difficult to replicate. The Engineering segment provides vertical integration through in-house power distribution design and manufacturing.
The bear case is that Riot remains deeply unprofitable with a -196% profit margin and -48% ROE, and the AI data center pivot is unproven at scale. The company has never profitably operated data center hosting infrastructure, and delivering 191 MW at Rockdale by June 2028 requires execution capabilities the company has not demonstrated [1][2]. Bitcoin mining revenue is inherently volatile — the all-in cost to mine was $44,629 per bitcoin in Q1 2026, and a sustained bitcoin price decline below $40,000 would create existential cash flow pressure. The company sold 3,778 BTC ($289.5M) in a single quarter to fund the transition, depleting reserves that provide financial cushion. The $573M Morgan Stanley interim financing adds leverage to an already loss-making operation. Massive insider selling by CEO Jason Les and other executives while the company burns cash raises serious governance concerns. The forward P/E is negative (-19.3x) with consensus expecting continued losses through 2027, and the stock trades at 10x TTM revenue on the hope of contracts that won't generate revenue for 12-18 months [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Rockdale AI data center delivery — first 96 MW targeted for December 2027 and full 191 MW by June 2028, with approximately $9.1B in contracted revenue over the 20-year initial term that would transform Riot's financial profile from volatile mining to predictable infrastructure [2]
  2. Bitcoin price appreciation driving mining revenue and reserve value — each $10,000 increase in bitcoin price adds approximately $15M in quarterly mining revenue and hundreds of millions in reserve mark-to-market gains [1]
  3. Additional AI hosting contracts leveraging multi-gigawatt Texas power position — the Corsicana and other facilities could attract further hyperscaler or AI lab tenants, expanding contracted revenue beyond the current 241 MW [2]
Key Risks
  1. Execution risk on unproven AI data center deployment — Riot has never operated hosting infrastructure at scale, and delivering 191 MW at Rockdale on time and budget while simultaneously mining bitcoin requires capabilities the company has not demonstrated [2]
  2. Bitcoin price volatility creates existential cash flow risk — all-in mining cost of $44,629 per BTC leaves minimal margin if bitcoin drops below $45,000, and the company sold 3,778 BTC in a single quarter to fund data center capex, depleting its financial cushion [1]
  3. Massive insider selling by CEO and executives while the company is unprofitable and burning cash to fund a strategic pivot raises governance concerns about leadership conviction and alignment with shareholder interests [3]
