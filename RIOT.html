Company Profile

Riot Platforms is a Castle Rock, Colorado-based Bitcoin mining company operating a 44.4 EH/s deployed hash rate (~4.6% of the global network) that is rapidly diversifying into AI/HPC data center hosting. Q2 2026 revenue was $174.2 million (+14% YoY), split between $113.7M Bitcoin mining and $37.3M data center revenue [1]. On August 11, 2026, Riot signed a 20-year, $9.1 billion compute lease with Anthropic for 191MW of IT capacity at its Rockdale, Texas campus, bringing total contracted long-term data center revenue to roughly $9.8 billion across 241MW [2].

Investment Thesis
Riot Platforms has transformed its investment case in the span of days: the August 11, 2026 announcement of a 20-year, $9.1 billion compute lease with Anthropic (191MW at Rockdale, Texas, extendable to $16.1B) plus an existing AMD lease gives Riot roughly $9.8 billion of contracted long-term revenue across 241MW — a scale and counterparty quality (a frontier AI lab) that few bitcoin-miner-to-datacenter pivots have achieved [2][3]. Combined with industry-leading Bitcoin mining efficiency ($49,912 direct cost per BTC, ~4.6% global hash rate) and a conservative 37% D/E, Riot is executing a genuine strategic reinvention backed by real capital commitments rather than aspirational AI messaging [1].
The bear case centers on profitability and mission credibility: ROE of -32.49% and a profit margin near -133% (an $867M TTM net loss on $653M revenue) reflect a company still burning cash and exposed to Bitcoin price volatility and digital-asset mark-to-market swings, and the new AI data center revenue will not fully ramp until the 191MW buildout completes in June 2028 — a multi-year gap during which execution risk is real [1][2]. Riot also carries a poor ESG track record (Bloomberg ESG score of 1.06/10, no disclosed GHG emissions data, and a defensive public response to New York Times reporting on its energy footprint) that sits uneasily with the newly stated vision of being a 'trusted platform,' and insiders have been exclusively net sellers over the past year with zero buys [4][5]. RIOT is best framed as a high-conviction, high-uncertainty AI-infrastructure transformation story rather than a proven compounder.
Key Catalysts
  1. Staged Anthropic capacity rollout — 96MW online by December 2027, full 191MW by June 2028 — with each milestone providing visibility into the ramp of Riot's highest-quality, longest-duration revenue stream [2]
  2. Potential exercise of the two five-year extension options on the Anthropic lease, which would raise total contract value from $9.1B to $16.1B [2]
  3. Additional AI/HPC hyperscale or enterprise tenant leases at Riot's remaining uncontracted power capacity, following the Chief Data Center Officer's mandate to develop the platform for new tenants [3]
Key Risks
  1. Deeply negative profitability (ROE -32.49%, profit margin approximately -133%, $867M TTM net loss) leaves Riot dependent on capital markets and Bitcoin price appreciation to fund the multi-year AI data center buildout before the Anthropic revenue ramps [1]
  2. Persistent ESG criticism — a Bloomberg ESG score of 1.06/10, no disclosed emissions data, and Greenpeace/NYT reporting characterizing Riot's Texas operations as the most energy-intensive Bitcoin mine in the U.S. — creates reputational and potential regulatory risk as the company positions itself as an AI infrastructure 'trusted platform' [5]
  3. Execution risk on a 20-year, multi-hundred-megawatt buildout is substantial; delays in power delivery, construction, or grid interconnection at the Rockdale campus could push back the 2027-2028 capacity milestones underpinning the $9.8B contracted revenue figure [2]
