Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Mortgage ·NYQ ·United States
Rithm Capital Corp. is an asset manager focused on real estate, credit, and financial services operating through Origination and Servicing, Residential Transitional Lending, and Asset Management segments. Formerly known as New Residential Investment Corp., the company manages approximately $61 billion in assets through subsidiaries including Newrez (mortgage origination and servicing), Sculptor Capital Management (alternatives), and Crestline (direct lending and insurance). Incorporated in 2011 and headquartered in New York with 7,240 employees, Rithm reported Q2 2026 GAAP net income of only $20.2M (down 93% YoY) despite earnings available for distribution of $0.60 per share.

Investment Thesis
Rithm Capital at $8.53 with a $4.8B market cap offers an eye-catching 11.7% dividend yield and trades at just 3.6x forward earnings and 0.69x book value ($12.33/share). The transformation to a diversified asset manager is ambitious — AUM grew 69% YoY to $61B, the Sculptor platform generated $600M in Q1 2026 gross inflows, and earnings available for distribution of $0.60/share in Q2 2026 exceeded the $0.25 quarterly dividend by a comfortable margin [1][2]. CEO Nierenberg has executed a clear strategic vision to evolve from a mortgage REIT to a multi-strategy alternative asset manager, and the book value of $12.33 provides a 38% margin of safety versus the current stock price.
The bear case is severe. GAAP net income collapsed 93% YoY to $20.2M in Q2 2026, revealing the fragility beneath the non-GAAP earnings available for distribution figure. D/E of 388% reflects extreme leverage that amplifies both gains and losses, and the 166.7% GAAP payout ratio means the dividend is not covered by reported earnings [1]. The $0.25 quarterly dividend has been flat since 2022 — there has been no growth for four years despite AUM expansion, because earnings volatility prevents management from raising the payout. The prior dividend cut during COVID (from $0.50 to $0.05 in Q1 2020) demonstrates management's willingness to slash payouts under stress. ROE of 5.6% and profit margin of 10.8% are anemic, and the company's dependence on continued access to debt markets makes it vulnerable to a credit tightening cycle [2]. Insider selling by the CEO and directors while asking shareholders to hold a deeply leveraged financial vehicle raises alignment concerns. The integration of four major acquisitions (Sculptor, Crestline, Paramount, plus existing Newrez operations) creates substantial operational complexity and execution risk [2][3].
