Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NMS ·United States
Rocket Lab is an end-to-end space company providing launch services (Electron small-sat rocket, Neutron medium-lift in development), spacecraft manufacturing, and satellite components. Founded in 2006 by CEO Peter Beck in New Zealand, the company has completed 60+ Electron missions with 100% recent success rate, making Electron the most frequently launched U.S. rocket after SpaceX Falcon 9. Q2 2026 revenue surged 62% YoY to a record $234M with record backlog of $2.36B (+137% YoY). The company has executed a transformative acquisition strategy including Mynaric (laser comms), Motiv (robotics), and the announced Iridium Communications acquisition.

Investment Thesis
Rocket Lab at $72.10 is the most compelling pure-play space infrastructure investment outside SpaceX — Q2 2026 revenue surged 62% YoY to a record $234M with record backlog of $2.36B (+137% YoY) and more than $1B in new contracts already signed in Q3 [1][2]. The transformation from small-sat launch provider to full-stack space platform (launch + spacecraft + satellite operations) through acquisitions of Mynaric, Motiv, and Iridium creates a vertically integrated business model with multiple growth vectors. Revenue has grown from $62M at the 2021 IPO to TTM $769M, a 12x increase in 5 years, with Q3 guidance of $250-265M signaling continued acceleration. The balance sheet is rock-solid with $2.3B cash against just $134M debt (D/E 3.8%), providing ample runway for Neutron development and Iridium integration [1].
The bear case centers on extreme valuation, execution risk, and competition. At $46B market cap against $769M TTM revenue (~60x P/S), Rocket Lab is priced for flawless execution of an ambitious multi-platform strategy — any stumble in Neutron development, Iridium integration, or acquisition digestion could trigger significant multiple compression [1]. The company remains unprofitable with ROE of -7.9% and net margin of -21.5%, though losses are narrowing (Q2 net loss improved to $49.3M from $66.4M YoY). Forward P/E of 1,331x reflects that profitability remains barely visible. The acquisition-heavy strategy (Mynaric, Motiv, SolAero, Sinclair, Iridium) creates integration complexity for a 2,600-person organization with acknowledged growing pains in leadership depth [3]. Neutron development represents both the largest opportunity and largest execution risk — delays or cost overruns would pressure financials and competitive positioning against SpaceX Falcon 9. SpaceX's Starship program, if successful at scale, could commoditize launch pricing and compress Rocket Lab's margins in both the small-sat and medium-lift segments [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record backlog of $2.36B (+137% YoY) with $1B+ in new Q3 2026 contracts provides multi-year revenue visibility, with Q3 guidance of $250-265M signaling continued 60%+ growth trajectory [1][2]

  2. Acquisition integration complexity from Mynaric, Motiv, SolAero, Sinclair, and the pending Iridium deal creates organizational strain for a 2,600-person company, with employee reviews noting uneven leadership depth and shifting priorities as growth challenges [3]
