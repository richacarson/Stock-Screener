Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NASDAQ ·United States
Rocket Lab is an end-to-end space company that provides launch services (Electron small-sat rocket, Neutron medium-lift in development), spacecraft manufacturing, and satellite components. Founded in 2006 by CEO Peter Beck in New Zealand, the company has completed 60+ Electron missions with a recent 100% success rate, making Electron the most frequently launched U.S. rocket after SpaceX's Falcon 9. Q2 2026 revenue surged 62% YoY to a record $234.1M, with product revenue of $181.3M and service revenue of $52.7M. Record backlog of $2.36B (+137% YoY) includes 90+ launch missions. The company has executed a transformative acquisition strategy — closing Mynaric (laser communications) and Motiv (robotic systems), and announcing the acquisition of Iridium Communications to add a satellite constellation and recurring data services. Market cap is approximately $49.5B.

Investment Thesis
Rocket Lab is the most compelling pure-play space infrastructure investment outside SpaceX — Q2 2026 revenue surged 62% YoY to a record $234.1M with record backlog of $2.36B (+137% YoY) and more than $1B in new contracts already signed in Q3 [1]. The company's transformation from small-sat launch provider to full-stack space platform (launch + spacecraft + satellite operations) through the acquisitions of Mynaric, Motiv, and Iridium creates a vertically integrated business model with multiple growth vectors. Revenue has grown from $62M at the 2021 IPO to an annualized ~$900M+ in 2026, a 10x increase in 5 years. Losses are narrowing (Q2 net loss improved to $49.3M from $66.4M YoY) as gross profit surged to $84.6M from $46.4M. Peter Beck's 20-year vision, Electron's 100% recent mission success rate across 60+ launches, and the Neutron medium-lift rocket in development position Rocket Lab to capture a growing share of the $400B+ space economy.
The bear case centers on valuation, execution risk, and competition. At approximately $49.5B market cap against ~$800M TTM revenue (62x P/S), Rocket Lab is priced for flawless execution of an ambitious multi-platform strategy — any stumble in Neutron development, Iridium integration, or acquisition digestion could trigger significant multiple compression [3]. The company remains unprofitable with ROE of -7.92% and net margin of -26.87%, and the acquisition-heavy strategy (Mynaric, Motiv, SolAero, Sinclair, Iridium) creates integration complexity for a 2,600-person company that has grown rapidly [1]. Neutron development represents both the largest opportunity and the largest execution risk — delays or cost overruns would pressure financials and competitive positioning against SpaceX Falcon 9. SpaceX remains the dominant force in launch with massive scale advantages, reusability leadership (Starship), and a captive Starlink demand base. The space economy's growth trajectory, while promising, depends on sustained government and commercial spending that could contract in an economic downturn.
