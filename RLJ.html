Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Hotel & Motel ·NYSE ·United States
RLJ Lodging Trust is a self-advised, publicly traded real estate investment trust that owns premium-branded, focused-service and compact full-service hotels primarily in major urban markets across the United States. The portfolio consists of approximately 96 hotels with ~21,200 rooms operating under Marriott, Hilton, and Hyatt flags. Founded in 2000 by Robert L. Johnson, headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland.

Investment Thesis
RLJ Lodging Trust delivered a solid Q1 2026 with revenue up 3.6% to $340M and RevPAR growth of 4.8%, outperforming the industry by 100bps [1]. Hotel EBITDA grew 7.2% to $89.9M and comparable non-room revenues surged 8.2%, indicating successful ancillary revenue optimization. The 5.5% dividend yield provides attractive current income for a hotel REIT with $1.35B in annual revenue and a portfolio repositioned toward premium-branded urban properties. Full-year 2026 guidance of $1.29-$1.45 adjusted FFO per share reflects cautious optimism amid macroeconomic uncertainty.
However, significant organizational risks overshadow the financial stability. Glassdoor reviews paint a picture of toxic workplace culture with 9 out of 10 employees disapproving of CEO Leslie Hale, and the loss of 20+ employees in 2024 — including the CFO and General Counsel — suggests deep leadership dysfunction at a company with limited corporate staff [4]. GAAP financial metrics are severely distorted by REIT accounting: ROE of 1.54%, profit margin of 2%, and P/E above 400x [2]. The payout ratio of 230% on a GAAP basis (41% on FFO) signals the disconnect between accounting earnings and economic reality. Full-year RevPAR guidance of only 1.5-3.5% reflects a mature hotel market with limited organic growth, while $2.32B in debt against $412.5M cash creates refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment [1][5].

  3. Dividend increase from $0.40 to $0.60 annually (50% increase) signals management confidence in sustainable cash flow generation with potential for further increases as FFO grows
Key Risks
  1. Severe organizational culture dysfunction — 20+ employee departures in 2024 including CFO and General Counsel, with 9/10 employees disapproving of CEO — creates execution risk and potential for further senior leadership turnover [4]
  2. Full-year RevPAR guidance of just 1.5-3.5% reflects a mature hotel market with limited organic growth, making RLJ dependent on capital recycling and acquisitions to grow FFO [1]
  3. Elevated leverage with $2.32B debt against $412.5M cash creates refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment with debt service consuming a significant portion of property-level cash flow [5]
