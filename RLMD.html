Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Relmada Therapeutics is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing transformative therapies for oncology and central nervous system conditions. Following the failure and termination of its lead depression drug REL-1017 (esmethadone) in 2024-2025, the company pivoted to NDV-01 (controlled-release intravesical formulation for non-muscle invasive bladder cancer, Phase 2/3 RESCUE program) and sepranolone (CNS disorders). CEO Sergio Traversa has led the company since 2012. Cash position of $234M (Q1 2026) with runway through 2029. Headquartered in New York, NY.
17 Employees ·www.relmada.com

Investment Thesis
Relmada offers binary clinical trial optionality at a deeply discounted $439M market cap ($4.12/share, down 36% from $6.40 on the prior screen) backed by $234M in cash with runway through 2029 [3]. Analyst consensus price targets of $9-$14 imply 120-240% upside, reflecting the value of two registrational programs: NDV-01 Phase 3 RESCUE for non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (two FDA-confirmed pathways) and sepranolone Phase 2 for Prader-Willi syndrome, both targeting mid-2026 initiation [1]. The company addresses genuine unmet medical needs — BCG-unresponsive bladder cancer has limited approved therapies, and Prader-Willi syndrome has none — creating potential for premium pricing and accelerated regulatory pathways. The appointment of COO Paul Kelly and CBO Bipin Dalmia signals commitment to clinical execution and future commercial readiness [2].
The bear case is substantial for a pre-revenue biotech with a history of clinical failures. REL-1017 failed in two Phase III trials, destroying significant shareholder value (stock declined from $30+ to $4.12) and triggering a securities fraud investigation [1]. Relmada has generated zero revenue in its 20+ year history, with cumulative net losses exceeding $400M, and ROE has worsened to -55.7% reflecting accelerating cash burn [4]. The new pipeline is entirely in-licensed (NDV-01 from Trigone, sepranolone from Asarina Pharma), creating dependency on partners and limited proprietary control. With only 17 employees, execution capacity for two simultaneous registrational programs is questionable. The stock's 36% decline since the prior screen and 16% decline from the $4.89 SMA-50 reflect growing investor skepticism about the pivot's prospects. Even with $234M in cash, Phase 3 oncology trials are expensive and timeline extensions remain a significant risk.
