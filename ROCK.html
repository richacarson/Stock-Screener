Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Gibraltar Industries, Inc. manufactures and provides products and services for the residential, agtech, and infrastructure markets in the United States and internationally. The company operates through Residential (roof ventilation, mail systems, metal roofing, rain dispersion), Agtech (custom greenhouse solutions, controlled environment agriculture, structural canopies), and Infrastructure (expansion joints, structural bearings, bridge cable protection) segments. The January 2026 OmniMax International acquisition transformed the Residential segment, driving Q2 2026 record net sales of $509.5M (+64.6% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $88M. Founded 1972, headquartered in Buffalo, New York.

Investment Thesis
Gibraltar Industries is delivering on its OmniMax integration thesis with record Q2 2026 results — net sales of $509.5M (+64.6% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $88M (+59.7%), and 17.3% adjusted EBITDA margin validate the transformational Residential acquisition [1]. The Residential segment generated $425.9M (+84.9%) with best-in-segment 19.0% EBITDA margins, while Agtech grew 8.7% to $58.8M and Infrastructure delivered 25.4% EBITDA margins. At approximately 12.4x forward P/E and 0.71 PEG, the stock is attractively valued for a building products company guiding $1.76-1.83B in 2026 revenue with $3.65-4.05 adjusted EPS [1][4]. All three segments manufacture physical products structurally immune to AI disruption, and the Inspire Impact Score of 60 (ALIGNED) with 15 positive attributions and zero negatives confirms strong ESG practices [3]. D/E is deleveraging from 156% to 136.75% as the acquisition generates cash flow.
The bear case centers on integration risk, leverage, and organic growth. D/E of 136.75% remains elevated with approximately $1.37B in total debt, and while deleveraging is progressing, any integration stumble or synergy shortfall could pressure the balance sheet [4]. GAAP profit margin remains negative on a TTM basis due to the Renewables discontinuation loss, creating a wide gap between adjusted and reported results. Organic revenue growth excluding OmniMax is guided at only ~5% for 2026, suggesting the legacy Residential business is growing modestly. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -6.5% reflects portfolio disruption from divestitures that distorts historical trends. ROE of 6.6% is well below the 15% target. Housing market cyclicality poses risk to the now-dominant Residential segment ($425.9M quarterly), and the Agtech segment faces headwinds if controlled environment agriculture adoption slows. Forward adjusted EPS of $3.65-4.05 implies the stock needs to deliver on its integration promises — any downward guidance revision could trigger meaningful multiple compression [1].
