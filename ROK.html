Company Profile
Industrials ·Industrial Automation & Digital Transformation ·NYSE ·United States
Rockwell Automation is the world's largest company dedicated to industrial automation and digital transformation. The company provides programmable logic controllers (Logix), industrial software (FactoryTalk), motion control, sensors, and lifecycle services to manufacturers globally. Segments include Intelligent Devices, Software & Control, and Lifecycle Services. Founded in 1903, headquartered in Milwaukee, Wisconsin.

Investment Thesis
Rockwell Automation is inflecting from a cyclical trough into a strong recovery powered by secular automation tailwinds. Q3 FY2026 delivered $2.313B revenue (10% organic growth) with operating margins expanding 280bps to 22.3% and EPS surging 40% to $3.65 [1]. Full-year FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $13.00-$13.30 (~25% growth). The company is the direct beneficiary of three mega-trends: AI-driven data center construction, semiconductor fab onshoring, and e-commerce warehouse automation — all requiring Rockwell's industrial control systems. Software & Control organic sales grew 18%, validating the digital transformation strategy. ROE of 27.18%, $51B market cap, and dominant North American industrial automation position create a quality franchise with cyclical upside [2].
The bear case centers on valuation and cyclical risks. At 33.5x forward earnings and PEG of 2.32, Rockwell trades at a significant premium that prices in sustained high-single-digit organic growth and continued margin expansion [2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of just 5.7% reveals the cyclicality underlying the current growth acceleration — FY2024-2025 delivered only 1% combined growth. Inflation is a growing headwind, with management flagging rising costs that could squeeze Q4 margins [1]. The Lifecycle Services segment declined 2% organically in Q3, constrained by weak food and beverage and process industry spending, exposing the uneven nature of the recovery. D/E of 113% and payout ratio of 55% leave less financial flexibility than peers. Net insider selling of $36M in 12 months, despite CFO Rothe's notable $2M buy, signals mixed conviction from leadership at current valuation [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Secular demand from data center automation, semiconductor fab onshoring (CHIPS Act investments), and e-commerce warehouse buildout creating multi-year growth runway beyond traditional industrial cycles
  2. Software & Control segment growing 18% organically could drive structural margin expansion as Rockwell shifts revenue mix toward higher-margin software and recurring services
  3. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $13.00-$13.30 represents 25% earnings growth, with potential for further upside if Lifecycle Services spending recovers in food/beverage and process industries
Key Risks
  1. Forward PE of 33.5x and PEG of 2.32 price in perfection — any deceleration from 10% organic growth or margin compression from inflation could trigger significant multiple contraction from the $51B market cap
  2. Lifecycle Services organic sales declined 2% in Q3, and inflation is increasing sequentially — management flagged potential Q4 margin squeeze from rising input costs that could disappoint expectations
  3. 5-year revenue CAGR of 5.7% and the FY2024-2025 flat period demonstrate deep cyclicality that current growth rates may mask — a manufacturing recession would expose the premium valuation
