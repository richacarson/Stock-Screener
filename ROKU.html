Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment (TV Streaming Platform) ·NASDAQ ·United States
Roku operates a TV streaming platform made up of the Roku operating system licensed to TV makers, Roku-branded TVs and players, The Roku Channel, and advertising and subscription businesses. Founded in 2002 by CEO Anthony Wood and headquartered in San Jose, it now reports Advertising and Subscriptions as separate segments. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.35B (+22% YoY), with Platform revenue of $1.22B (+25%). Q3 2026 results are scheduled after the close on November 2, 2026 [1][2][6].

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 6 /10
Under founder Anthony Wood, Roku has spent two decades on a single thesis, that all TV will be streamed, and reinvented itself from a hardware company into a licensing and advertising platform to pursue it. That consistency and adaptability are infinite-game traits. A finite, rival-beating competitive posture, consumer-hostile policy moves and no visible insider conviction keep it in the MIXED range.
Just Cause
7
Just Cause (2/3): Wood's long-held vision that TV will move to streaming has outlasted the Netflix player project, the 2017 IPO and the 2022-2023 loss cycle, and it still drives strategy [7][8]. It is a vision of how an industry evolves more than a service-oriented cause that benefits others, which keeps it below a 3.
Trusting Teams
6
Trusting Teams (1/2): A longtime colleague describes Wood as a risk-taker who encourages employees to take risks too, and filings call culture a key retention tool [7][9]. The same filings flag heavy dependence on Wood, the lack of long-term employment agreements and the risk of losing the entrepreneurial culture as the company grows, so trust is adequate rather than exceptional [7].

Existential Flexibility

Existential Flexibility (2/2): Roku began by building a streaming player for Netflix, then moved from selling hardware to licensing its operating system to TV makers and earning most of its revenue from advertising and subscriptions. Platform revenue was $1.22B of $1.35B in Q2 2026 [1][8]. It has kept extending that model with Roku-made TVs and subscription services like Howdy, a reinvention driven by the streaming thesis rather than desperation [2][10].

Investment Thesis
Roku has reached sustained profitability on top of accelerating growth. Q2 2026 revenue rose 22% to $1.35B, Platform revenue rose 25% to $1.22B, and TTM net income reached about $355M after losses of $710M in FY2023 [1][3]. Platform growth accelerated from 17% in Q3 2025 to 25-28% in the first half of 2026, and management expects double-digit Platform growth to continue [1][2][13]. TTM free cash flow of about $826M, D/E of 16.8% and net cash give the company room to keep investing. Consensus EPS climbs from $3.94 in FY2027 to $6.69 in FY2029, and Roku's control of the TV home screen makes it a toll collector on the shift of TV advertising to streaming [4].
Valuation is demanding: 64.6x trailing earnings and about 39x FY2027 consensus EPS, after a rally toward the top of the 52-week range ($78.53-$160.22). Q3 2026 results tonight (November 2) are an immediate volatility event [3][6]. Net margin is only 6.8%, so earnings remain highly sensitive to ad-market cycles. Larger platforms with their own TV operating systems and AI assistants could pressure Roku's home-screen economics [7]. Governance and culture concentrate on founder Anthony Wood with no key-person insurance, insiders are persistent net sellers, and consumer backlash over forced arbitration terms points to brand-trust risk [5][7][12].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results on November 2, 2026, the test of whether Platform revenue can hold 20%+ growth against the 17% Q3 2025 comparison
  2. Scaling the newly separated Subscriptions segment, including Howdy, to diversify beyond advertising
  3. Operating leverage as revenue grows faster than costs, with consensus EPS rising from $3.94 (FY2027) to $6.69 (FY2029)
Key Risks
  1. Competitors' TV operating systems with AI assistants capturing content discovery and ad inventory from Roku's home screen
  2. Cyclical ad-market exposure on a thin 6.8% net margin and a 39x forward multiple, where a growth slowdown would compress both earnings and valuation
  3. Key-person dependence on founder-CEO Anthony Wood, with no key-person insurance or long-term employment agreements for executives

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Roku controls the TV operating system and home screen used by its installed base. Generative AI is more likely to multiply the content and ads flowing through that gatekeeper position than to replace it, and AI-driven ad targeting strengthens platform monetization [1].
