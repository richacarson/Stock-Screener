Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NYSE ·United States
Rollins, Inc. is the world's second-largest pest control company, providing residential, commercial, and termite and ancillary pest control services through its family of brands including Orkin, HomeTeam Pest Defense, Clark Pest Control, Northwest Exterminating, and other regional subsidiaries. The company operates across North America and internationally, delivering recurring-revenue pest management services through a network of company-owned and franchised locations. Founded in 1948 by O. Wayne Rollins, headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia.

Investment Thesis
Rollins is a high-quality compounder delivering its 98th consecutive quarter of revenue growth — Q1 2026 revenue of $906.4M grew 10.2% YoY with 6.6% organic and 3.6% from acquisitions [1]. The pest control industry offers structural advantages: recurring revenue (customers renew annually), essential service (pests don't stop during recessions), and fragmented competition allowing rollup-driven growth. ROE of 38.7% reflects exceptional capital efficiency, D/E of 77% demonstrates balance sheet discipline, and the company targets medium-term organic growth of 7-8% with 2-3% M&A [1][3]. At $20.9B market cap, Rollins trades at a premium but rewards shareholders with consistent double-digit total returns — the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 17.9% signals confidence in earnings trajectory [3]. AI resilience is among the highest possible (9/10) as physical pest control services cannot be digitally substituted.
The valuation is the primary concern. Forward PE of 33.4x and PEG of 2.66 price in near-perfection for a business growing revenue at 10% and earning 13.8% net margins [3]. Q1 2026 operating margin declined 120bps YoY to 16.1%, and EPS was flat despite 10% revenue growth, suggesting margin compression from acquisition integration costs and labor inflation [1]. The pest control industry faces structural challenges: labor shortages drive technician wage inflation, stricter pesticide regulations (EPA 2026 Pesticide General Permit, neonicotinoid bans) increase compliance costs, and the Rentokil-Terminix combination creates a well-capitalized competitor with scale advantages. Any deceleration in organic growth below 7% or sustained margin pressure could trigger multiple compression from the premium valuation, creating meaningful downside risk from current levels.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued organic revenue growth acceleration toward 7-8% target fueled by commercial pest control (+7.7% organic) and termite/ancillary services (+9.8% organic), the two highest-growth segments [1]
  2. M&A pipeline execution at 2-3% annual revenue contribution from acquisitions in a fragmented $25B+ US pest control market, with leverage ratio of 0.9x providing ample acquisition capacity [1]
  3. Technology modernization including Route Optimization AI driving operational efficiency gains that could reverse the Q1 2026 margin compression and expand adjusted operating margin above 17% [2]
Key Risks
  1. Premium valuation at 33.4x forward PE and 2.66 PEG leaves minimal margin of safety — any deceleration below 7% organic growth or sustained margin pressure could trigger significant multiple compression from $20.9B market cap
  2. Q1 2026 operating margin compressed 120bps YoY to 16.1% with flat EPS despite 10% revenue growth, signaling labor cost inflation and acquisition integration expense are outpacing operating leverage
  3. Regulatory headwinds from EPA 2026 Pesticide General Permit and state-level neonicotinoid restrictions increase compliance costs and require continuous formulation innovation to maintain treatment efficacy

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (9/9): Rollins' core business is structurally immune to AI disruption — pest control requires physical human presence at customer premises to inspect, apply chemical treatments, set traps, seal entry points, and manage biological pest populations [1][2]. No AI system can crawl under a house to treat for termites, spray for mosquitoes in a backyard, or inspect a restaurant for rodent activity. The service is inherently local, physical, and relationship-driven. AI actually increases demand indirectly: as data centers proliferate and commercial real estate expands, pest control needs grow. Regulatory requirements for licensed pest control technicians create additional barriers to AI substitution. Rollins' own deployment of Route Optimization AI demonstrates that AI is a pure enabler for this business — reducing costs and improving service delivery rather than threatening the core service model [2]. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI-generated revenue products. AI is deployed operationally for route optimization and scheduling, but Rollins does not sell AI products or services.
