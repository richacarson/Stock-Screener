Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Roper Technologies is a diversified technology company that designs and develops vertical software and technology-enabled products across three segments: Application Software (property insurance, K-12 education, transit, food service), Network Software (life insurance analytics, supply chain, healthcare, church management), and Technology Enabled Products (water meters, medical devices, RFID readers). The company completed a multi-year transformation from an industrial conglomerate (formerly Roper Industries) to a software-centric platform, systematically divesting industrial assets while acquiring niche-dominant vertical software businesses. Q2 2026 revenue grew 9% to $2.11B with backlog up 11% to $3.29B. Market cap is approximately $37B.

Investment Thesis
Roper Technologies at 15.4x forward earnings is a rare high-quality software compounder trading at a reasonable valuation — Q2 2026 revenue grew 9% to $2.11B with 5% organic growth and 3% from acquisitions, while free cash flow increased 11% to $447M and operating cash flow surged 16% to $469M [1]. The company's portfolio of niche-dominant vertical software businesses (Vertafore, Frontline Education, Aderant, Deltek) generates ~75% recurring revenue with high switching costs, creating durable competitive advantages. Adjusted DEPS guidance was raised to $22.15-$22.30 for FY2026, and the $3.2B buyback program (8%+ of shares outstanding) is directly accretive to per-share metrics. Backlog grew 11% to $3.29B, providing strong forward revenue visibility. Profit margin of 30.2% and D/E of 60.5% reflect financial discipline, while the Inspire Impact Score of 65 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes ROP attractive for faith-based portfolios.
The bear case centers on decelerating organic growth and acquisition dependency. Organic revenue growth of 5% in Q2 lags the 9% total growth rate, meaning acquisitions (3%) and the Indicor equity gain ($835M) flatter the headline numbers [1]. ROE of 13.1% is below the 15% target, reflecting the goodwill-heavy balance sheet accumulated through serial acquisitions — a structural feature of the capital-light acquirer model that depresses returns on equity. The PEG ratio of 1.48 suggests moderate but not exceptional growth-adjusted valuation. Roper's decentralized model, while operationally effective, makes it difficult to drive cross-portfolio AI synergies or enterprise-wide innovation at the pace of pure-play software peers. The company's transformation from industrial conglomerate to software platform is largely complete, raising questions about the next phase of value creation — whether through organic innovation, continued acquisitions (with a depleted divestiture pipeline), or shareholder returns. Technology Enabled Products (water meters, medical devices) remain a lower-margin legacy segment that dilutes the software story.

  3. Decentralized operating model limits ability to drive coordinated AI strategy or cross-portfolio innovation at enterprise scale, potentially leaving Roper's vertical software businesses vulnerable to AI-native competitors that can move faster in individual niches [2]
