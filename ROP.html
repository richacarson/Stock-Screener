Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Roper Technologies is a diversified vertical software compounder operating through three segments: Application Software (Vertafore, Deltek, Frontline Education, CentralReach), Network Software (SaaS data and analytics), and Technology Enabled Products (water meters, medical devices, RF technology). The company follows a disciplined acquisition-driven model, deploying $2.6B annual free cash flow into high-quality, asset-light vertical software businesses with deep customer entrenchment. Under CEO Neil Hunn, Roper completed its transformation from industrial conglomerate to software-centric technology company.

Investment Thesis
Roper Technologies is a best-in-class vertical software compounder delivering Q2 2026 revenue of $2.11B (+9% YoY), adjusted EPS of $5.38 (beating consensus by 1.7%), and raised full-year guidance to $22.15-$22.30 DEPS [1]. At 15.6x forward P/E with 30.2% profit margins, 39.3% EBITDA margins, $2.62B TTM free cash flow, and a PEG of 1.44, the stock is attractively valued for a company with 69.4% gross margins and a proven acquisition-driven growth model [4]. The $5.2B total buyback program and robust M&A pipeline provide multiple capital deployment levers. Portfolio companies are deeply embedded in customer workflows (insurance, healthcare, education, government contracting, faith-based organizations via Subsplash) with proprietary data creating high switching costs and durable recurring revenue [3]. The AI strategy (hub-and-spoke model, dedicated leadership, AI-first M&A evaluation) positions Roper to enhance rather than be disrupted by AI [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 65 (ALIGNED) with 19 positive attributions and zero negatives makes this one of the strongest faith-aligned investment opportunities in the screening universe.
The bear case focuses on acquisition integration risk and organic growth limitations. Organic revenue growth of 5% in Q2 2026 — while 3 percentage points came from acquisitions — reveals that Roper's 9% headline growth is significantly acquisition-dependent, creating M&A execution risk as the primary vulnerability [1]. The enterprise value of $47.3B includes ~$11B in net debt from acquisition financing, and rising amortization of acquired assets is compressing reported margins. ROE of 13.1% is suppressed by a goodwill-heavy balance sheet, meaning returns on invested capital may be lower than headline profitability suggests [4]. The Technology Enabled Products segment faces headwinds from water meter technology softness and foreign exchange impact. Revenue growth of ~9% falls below the 10% target, and the diversified vertical software model creates a growth ceiling around 9-10% absent larger acquisitions that carry integration risk. AI disruption risk exists across data analytics, financial software, and estimating businesses in the portfolio, though management is actively mitigating this [2]. At $36.3B market cap, the stock requires sustained M&A execution and organic growth to justify its premium valuation.
