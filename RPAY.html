Investment Thesis
Repay Holdings guided 2026 revenue to $340-346 million (up roughly 10-12% from FY2025's $309.26 million) with Adjusted EBITDA of $136.5-141.5 million and margins near 40%, and Business Payments growth was called out as a bright spot in Q4 2025 results [1]. The stock trades at a low forward P/E of 4.14, reflecting deep investor skepticism after a brutal 2025 in which the company recorded $242.7 million of goodwill impairment charges (mostly in Consumer Payments) and posted a GAAP net loss of $271.1 million for the year.
The impairment-driven losses (ROE of -57.04%, TTM profit margin of -87.28%) reflect real value destruction from prior acquisitions, not just accounting noise, and TTM GAAP revenue growth of only 1.00% shows the core business is barely growing even as management guides to double-digit 2026 growth. Leadership stability is also a concern: co-founder and President Shaler Alias departed in February 2026 with the role left unfilled, and Glassdoor's 51% recommendation rate trails typical fintech peers. With weak Innovation and Inspiration scores and a FINITE Infinite Game mindset, the cheap valuation looks more like a value trap than a bargain absent clear evidence of a turnaround.
Key Catalysts
  1. 2026 guidance of $340-346 million revenue and $136.5-141.5 million Adjusted EBITDA, if achieved, would mark a return to double-digit growth after a flat 2025
  2. Business Payments segment momentum (virtual cards, enhanced ACH) could diversify revenue away from the impaired Consumer Payments segment
  3. Low forward P/E of 4.14 leaves room for a re-rating if 2026 execution restores investor confidence after the 2025 impairment cycle
Key Risks
