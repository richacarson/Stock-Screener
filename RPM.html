Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·United States
RPM International manufactures and markets specialty chemicals for construction, industrial, specialty, and consumer markets through three segments: CPG (Construction Products Group), PCG (Performance Coatings Group), and Consumer. Key brands include Rust-Oleum (#1 in consumer protective coatings), Tremco (commercial waterproofing), DAP (caulks and sealants), Carboline (corrosion control), and Euclid Chemical (concrete admixtures). Founded in 1947 by Frank C. Sullivan, RPM is now led by his grandson Frank Sullivan (CEO since 2002) and has grown to $7.86B in annual revenue through 100+ acquisitions. Headquartered in Medina, Ohio.
17,546 Employees ·www.rpminc.com

Investment Thesis
RPM International is a Dividend King generating $7.86B in FY2026 revenue (+6.7%) with 50+ consecutive years of dividend increases and a forward P/E of just 14.87x [1][4]. The MAP to Growth/CIR operational initiatives have driven record adjusted EBIT margins, and the company holds category-leading positions across specialty coatings niches — Rust-Oleum in consumer coatings, Tremco in commercial waterproofing, DAP in sealants, Carboline in corrosion control. Infrastructure spending tailwinds from the IIJA and data center construction support the Construction Products and Performance Coatings segments. The 2.16% dividend yield with a sustainable 41% payout ratio and ~7% five-year dividend growth CAGR offers reliable income compounding. At 14.87x forward earnings, the stock trades at a discount to the S&P 500 while delivering mid-single-digit organic growth supplemented by proven acquisition capabilities.
The bear case centers on thin margins, decelerating growth, and limited innovation. Profit margin of 8.41% remains well below specialty chemicals peers, and GAAP EPS declined 1.4% in FY2026 despite top-line growth as raw material inflation and restructuring costs compressed earnings [1][4]. FY2027 guidance of only 3-7% revenue growth suggests decelerating momentum from the 6.7% delivered in FY2026. Debt-to-equity of 89% reflects the acquisition-heavy strategy's ongoing leverage requirements. Innovation is essentially absent — no AI strategy, no transformative product pipeline, and R&D intensity below 1% of revenue leaves RPM vulnerable to competitors investing more aggressively in technology-enabled coatings solutions. The business is cyclically exposed to construction and industrial end markets sensitive to interest rates and housing activity, with ~60% of revenue tied to commercial and residential construction. Glassdoor ratings of 3.1/5 suggest cultural challenges that could limit talent retention in a tightening labor market [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act spending acceleration in FY2027-2028 directly benefiting RPM's Construction Products Group through concrete admixtures, waterproofing, and protective coatings demand for bridges, highways, and public infrastructure [1]
  2. FY2027 guidance for 5-10% adjusted EBITDA growth driven by CIR operational improvements and pricing discipline, with potential for margin expansion as titanium dioxide and resin input costs normalize from FY2026 peaks [1]
  3. Continued bolt-on acquisition strategy adding specialized brands and capabilities, with RPM's 75-year track record of successfully integrating 100+ acquisitions providing a repeatable growth engine in the fragmented $150B+ global coatings market [1]
Key Risks
  1. Raw material cost inflation in titanium dioxide, resins, and petrochemical-derived inputs could further compress the already-thin 8.41% profit margin — FY2026 GAAP EPS declined 1.4% despite 6.7% revenue growth, demonstrating margin vulnerability to input cost spikes [1][4]
  2. Cyclical exposure to construction and industrial end markets creates vulnerability if interest rate policy tightens further or housing starts decline, with approximately 60% of revenue tied to commercial and residential construction activity [1]
  3. Acquisition integration risk as RPM pursues growth through M&A in a rising-rate environment — a failed integration or overlevered deal could strain the 89% D/E ratio and threaten the 50+ year dividend growth streak that underpins the income-investor thesis [4]
