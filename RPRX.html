Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Royalty Pharma is the world's largest buyer of biopharmaceutical royalties, with a portfolio of royalties on approximately 35 marketed therapies and 20 development-stage candidates spanning rare disease, oncology, neuroscience, infectious disease, hematology, and diabetes. Founded in 1996 by Pablo Legorreta, the company has deployed $25B+ in capital to advance innovation across the drug development ecosystem. IPO in 2020; headquartered in New York.

Investment Thesis
Royalty Pharma delivered a strong Q1 2026 with revenue of $925M (beating the $882M estimate by 5%), EPS of $1.30 (beating $1.22 by 7%), and net income of $294.7M (+23% YoY from $239.4M) [1]. Royalty Receipts grew 13% and Portfolio Receipts grew 10% to ~$810M, while the company deployed up to $1.25B in new royalty transactions. At a forward P/E of just 9.0x — remarkably cheap for a business with 78% net margin, 34% profit margin, and $2.97B adjusted EBITDA — the market appears to over-discount patent cliff and biosimilar risks [5]. The $186M returned to shareholders in Q1 via dividends and buybacks, plus the $600M social bond for orphan diseases, demonstrate both shareholder-friendly capital allocation and purpose-driven leadership under Legorreta's 30-year stewardship [1][3].
The bear case centers on decelerating growth and portfolio concentration. 2026 guidance of 4-8% Royalty Receipts growth represents a meaningful slowdown from 16% Portfolio Receipts growth in FY2025, driven by loss of exclusivity on key products and biosimilar competition for therapies like Tysabri [1]. The $9.2B total debt load, while manageable against current cash flows, creates refinancing risk if interest rates remain elevated — $350-360M in annual interest expense consumes ~11% of portfolio receipts [1]. GAAP revenue of ~$3.5B TTM significantly diverges from Portfolio Receipts of ~$3.3B, creating accounting confusion that may suppress the valuation multiple. The business model depends entirely on Legorreta's team identifying high-quality royalty streams — with $1.25B deployed in a single quarter, a bad deal at scale could materially impair returns. Medicare drug price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act could reduce future royalty values on negotiated drugs [3].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Royalty Pharma's core business — owning contractual royalty streams on approved and development-stage biopharmaceutical therapies — is structurally immune to AI disruption. The business involves deeply regulated processes (FDA approval, patent law, pharmaceutical regulation) and legal/contractual ownership of cash flows that AI cannot substitute [2]. Drug development requires clinical trials, manufacturing, and regulatory approval that remain firmly in the physical world. Critically, AI-accelerated drug discovery actually INCREASES demand for RPRX's services by expanding the pipeline of drugs reaching approval and creating more royalty acquisition opportunities. The main theoretical risk — that AI could fundamentally change how pharmaceutical royalties are structured — is speculative and long-term. AI Opportunity (0/1): The appointment of Lucas Glass as Head of AI for deal evaluation and sourcing is promising but pre-revenue — no AI products are shipping or generating quantifiable revenue improvements yet [2].
