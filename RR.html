Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NCM ·United States
Richtech Robotics develops, manufactures, and sells AI-powered robotic solutions for the service and industrial sectors. Products include Matradee restaurant service robots, ADAM and Scorpion dual-arm beverage preparation robots, Titan heavy-duty AMRs, DUST-E autonomous cleaning robots, and Dex industrial humanoid robots. The company also offers Robots-as-a-Service (RaaS) and operates the Clouffee & Tea robotic restaurant brand. TTM revenue is $5.39M with 16.7% YoY growth, though the company operates at deep losses (-82% profit margin). Founded in 2016 and headquartered in Las Vegas, Nevada, with approximately 55 employees.

Investment Thesis
Richtech Robotics at $1.71 with a $385M market cap operates in the high-growth AI robotics sector, offering a diversified product lineup spanning restaurant service robots (Matradee), AI-powered beverage preparation (ADAM, Scorpion), industrial AMRs (Titan), cleaning robots (DUST-E), and humanoid robots (Dex) [1][3]. TTM revenue grew 30.6% to $5.39M with RaaS recurring revenue up 31% YoY, and the company holds $339.6M in cash against only $629K debt, providing approximately 12 years of operating runway at current burn rates. The cash-heavy balance sheet means investors are paying only ~$45M above cash value for the operating business and IP, offering downside protection relative to the market cap.
The bear case is severe. Richtech has $5.4M in annual revenue — less than a single restaurant location — with a -82% net margin, negative ROE, and operating cash burn of $27M/year [1]. The company faces a securities class action lawsuit after Microsoft denied a claimed partnership, with Hunterbrook Media reporting the engagement was merely a 'standard customer program with no commercial element,' causing a 29% stock drop [4]. A Nasdaq noncompliance notice for missed 10-Q filing deadlines raises delisting risk. A former partner lawsuit alleged the robots were 'defective and could not operate as promised.' Competition from massively better-funded AI robotics companies — Figure AI ($2.6B+ funding), Tesla Optimus, Boston Dynamics — dwarfs Richtech's capabilities with only 55 employees. Glassdoor reviews warn to 'Run Away' from the company. The trailing P/E of 21.4x on a one-time EPS gain is misleading given the deeply negative operating economics, and the stock faces fundamental questions about management credibility and product quality that no amount of cash can resolve [3][4].
