Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
RTX Corporation is a global aerospace and defense company formed from the 2020 merger of Raytheon Company and United Technologies Corporation, operating through three segments — Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney, and Raytheon. RTX designs, manufactures, and services advanced systems for commercial aerospace and defense customers worldwide. Q2 2026 revenue reached $24.7B (+14.5% YoY, 16% organic), and the company raised full-year 2026 sales guidance to $95-96B on a record $289B backlog. Market cap of approximately $283B.

Investment Thesis
RTX delivered a strong Q2 2026 with $24.7B revenue (+14.5% YoY, 16% organic growth) and adjusted EPS of $1.89, beating consensus by a wide margin, and raised full-year 2026 guidance to $95-96B in sales (8-9% organic growth) with adjusted EPS of $7.10-7.25 [1]. The investment case is anchored by a record $289B backlog — up 22% YoY and split between $170B commercial and $119B defense orders — with the Raytheon defense segment posting a 2.4x book-to-bill in Q2 2026 [2]. Global defense spending tailwinds (NATO budget increases, Indo-Pacific and Middle East air-defense demand) and Pratt & Whitney's sole-source F-35 engine position plus a growing commercial aftermarket provide counter-cyclical ballast. AI Resilience is a genuine strength: RTX's physical, classified, multi-decade-qualification manufacturing base is structurally immune to AI substitution.
The bear case centers on valuation, thin margins, and organizational friction. Forward P/E of 28.46x and a PEG of 2.62 price in sustained double-digit growth that requires flawless backlog conversion. Profit margin of 8.28% remains structurally low for the defense sector and far below the 20% IOWN infrastructure target. The Pratt & Whitney powder-metal remediation continues to create earnings drag years after disclosure. Culturally, RTX scores weakly on Inspiration and Infinite Game — reports of micromanagement and inconsistent communication persist six years post-merger across the combined 180,000-employee Raytheon/UTC workforce, and the company's stated purpose reads as functional rather than a genuine Just Cause. The Inspire MISALIGNED score (-49, driven by LGBT Activism) may also limit the addressable investor base for faith-aligned mandates.
