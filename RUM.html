Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & AI Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Rumble Inc. operates a video-sharing and livestreaming platform positioned as a free-speech alternative to mainstream Big Tech video platforms, serving 56 million monthly active users [1]. In June 2026 the company closed a $970M acquisition of Northern Data AG, adding roughly 200MW of unmonetized energy capacity, 22,000 high-end NVIDIA GPUs, and a European data center network, and restructured into two divisions — Rumble (creator/video platform) and Quake AI (AI compute/cloud infrastructure) [2]. Founded 2013 by Chris Pavlovski, headquartered in Longboat Key, Florida.

Investment Thesis
Rumble is transforming from a pure video platform into a hybrid media-and-AI-infrastructure company. The closed $970M Northern Data acquisition delivers 200MW of power capacity and 22,000 NVIDIA GPUs, and the $270M multiyear Together AI GPU cloud agreement provides a quantified near-term revenue bridge into the AI compute-as-a-service market, with Northern Data's own FY2026 revenue outlook raised to €170-190M [2][3]. The core video platform continues to grow its user base (56M MAUs) with a net-cash balance sheet (D/E near 1%) giving management flexibility to fund the pivot without dilution or leverage risk [1].
The bear case centers on execution risk and profitability. Q1 2026 revenue growth of just 7% missed consensus, the net loss widened to $30.3M, and sales and marketing spend jumped 134% with no clear timeline to profitability on the legacy video business, which posted a -106.9% profit margin and -37.1% ROE [1]. The AI infrastructure pivot is capital-intensive and unproven at scale, introducing new execution, energy-cost, and GPU-depreciation risks that are foreign to Rumble's original media business, and meaningful advertising monetization on the core platform is not expected until late 2026 [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Quake AI/Northern Data ramping toward the raised €170-190M FY2026 revenue outlook as GPU capacity is monetized under the $270M Together AI agreement
  2. Rumble Shorts advertising monetization launching in H2 2026 across the 56M MAU base
  3. Continued data center buildout across the ~250MW of energized and planned power capacity, expanding AI compute-as-a-service capacity
Key Risks
  1. AI infrastructure is capital-intensive and unproven for Rumble; GPU depreciation, energy costs, and data center execution risk are new to the company's cost structure
  2. Core video platform remains deeply unprofitable (-106.9% profit margin, -37.1% ROE) with Q1 2026 revenue missing consensus and losses widening to $30.3M
  3. Integration risk from the Northern Data acquisition, a large cross-border deal (85.2% ownership stake) that could strain management bandwidth during a critical monetization window
