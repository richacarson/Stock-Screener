Company Profile
Energy ·Solar ·NASDAQ ·United States
Sunrun is the largest residential solar, storage, and energy services company in the United States. The company designs, installs, finances, and monitors rooftop solar energy systems and battery storage under long-term customer agreements and direct purchase options. Following its 2020 merger with Vivint Solar, Sunrun serves over 1 million customers and operates a Networked Storage Capacity of 4.6 GWh. The company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $870M (+53% YoY) with a record 74% storage attachment rate and 2026 cash generation guidance of $200M-$375M.

Investment Thesis
Sunrun is executing a powerful revenue acceleration as the largest U.S. residential solar company — Q2 2026 revenue of $870M (+53% YoY) and Q1 of $722M (+43% YoY) demonstrate accelerating demand driven by rising electricity costs and AI data center-driven grid strain [1][2]. The record 74% storage attachment rate and 4.6 GWh Networked Storage Capacity create a distributed energy platform with virtual power plant capabilities positioned to monetize grid services as utility infrastructure strains. Forward P/E of 15.38x values the stock modestly given the 53% revenue growth trajectory. Cash generation guidance of $200M-$375M for 2026 signals a path toward financial sustainability, and the structural tailwind of electrification and AI-driven electricity demand could sustain the residential solar adoption curve for years [2].
The bear case centers on extreme leverage and persistent GAAP unprofitability. D/E of 435.69% reflects $14B+ in project-level debt against a diminished market cap — the stock has declined 56% from its 52-week high of $22.44 to $9.85, suggesting the market is pricing in real execution risk [7]. TTM profit margin of -27.58% and unavailable trailing PE underscore that GAAP profitability remains elusive despite strong revenue growth. Any tightening in credit markets or reduction in solar tax incentives (IRA rollback risk) could impair Sunrun's ability to finance new installations, as the entire business model depends on continued access to tax equity and debt markets. Net insider selling across multiple executives — including CEO Powell ($316K), CFO Abajian ($217K), and founder Jurich (50,000 shares) — signals limited insider confidence in near-term upside [6]. Glassdoor-documented cultural deterioration following the Vivint merger creates organizational risk during a critical scaling period. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors.

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Sunrun's core business — designing, installing, financing, and maintaining physical solar panel and battery storage systems on residential rooftops — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. AI cannot install solar panels, wire electrical systems, manage physical infrastructure, or replace the local permitting and inspection processes required for each installation. The residential solar business requires physical labor, site-specific engineering, and ongoing hardware maintenance that AI structurally cannot displace. Moreover, the explosive growth of AI data centers is driving unprecedented electricity demand, which increases consumer interest in solar energy independence and grid resilience — making Sunrun a potential beneficiary of AI infrastructure buildout [2]. The company's 4.6 GWh Networked Storage Capacity positions it as a virtual power plant operator, a physical-world energy asset that gains value as grid strain from AI workloads intensifies. AI Opportunity (0/1): Sunrun uses AI internally through its CTO (former Head of AI) to optimize customer acquisition, system design, and grid dispatch, but does not generate direct revenue from AI products or services sold to external customers [3].
