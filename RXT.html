Company Profile
Technology ·IT Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Rackspace Technology is a multicloud managed services and IT infrastructure company serving enterprise customers across public cloud, private cloud, and hybrid environments. The company is pivoting toward AI infrastructure, positioning itself as a provider of GPU computing and AI solutions for regulated industries and sovereign markets. Under new CEO Gajen Kandiah (appointed September 2025), Rackspace reported Q2 2026 revenue of $670.1M (+0.6% YoY) with declining Public Cloud revenue (-2.3%) partially offset by Private Cloud growth (+5.5%). The company raised $250M through an at-the-market equity offering for GPU initiatives. Market cap is approximately $1.0B against $3.23B in debt, reflecting the legacy of its Apollo Global Management leveraged buyout.

Investment Thesis
Rackspace at $4.18 per share represents a high-risk speculative bet on an AI infrastructure pivot by a financially distressed company. Q2 2026 revenue of $670.1M (+0.6% YoY) shows the core managed cloud business stabilizing after years of decline, with Private Cloud growing 5.5% partially offsetting Public Cloud contraction of 2.3% [1]. The $250M GPU infrastructure investment targets a genuine market opportunity in AI computing for regulated industries and sovereign markets where data residency requirements limit hyperscaler adoption. New CEO Gajen Kandiah brings proven enterprise technology scaling experience from Hitachi Digital and Cognizant (grew from $368M to $16B+ revenue) [2]. Management raised FY2026 EBITDA guidance despite the revenue revision, suggesting margin improvement in higher-value AI services.
The bear case is severe and multidimensional. The $3.23B debt load from the 2016 Apollo LBO consumes over half of operating cash flow in debt service, leaving minimal capital for the AI transition and creating existential refinancing risk if the pivot fails [4]. FY2026 revenue guidance was cut 7% to $2.45-2.55B, with the company acknowledging a 'strategic shift' that is effectively an admission of core business decline [1]. Forward P/E of 694x reflects near-zero profitability — net losses of $146M TTM on $2.7B revenue demonstrate the company is destroying value, not creating it. The $250M ATM equity offering is dilutive to existing shareholders and insufficient relative to the investment required to compete with hyperscaler AI infrastructure. Three CEO changes in four years (Maletira to Kandiah in September 2025) create strategic discontinuity, and the AI pivot is the fourth strategic repositioning in a decade. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits faith-based investor allocation. Rackspace faces a fundamental question: can a leveraged, loss-making managed hosting company successfully pivot to AI infrastructure while competing against AWS, Azure, and GCP with a fraction of their capital? The historical precedent for such turnarounds is poor.
