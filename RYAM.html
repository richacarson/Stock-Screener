Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals - Cellulose ·NYSE ·United States
Rayonier Advanced Materials is a global manufacturer of cellulose-based products, including high-purity cellulose specialties used in pharmaceuticals, food additives, and industrial applications, as well as commodity viscose and high-yield pulp. Operates manufacturing plants in Florida, Georgia, and Canada. The company is the leading global supplier of high-purity cellulose specialties natural polymers. Market cap ~$525M. Currently exploring strategic alternatives after seven consecutive years of net losses.

Investment Thesis
RYAM holds a niche leadership position as the world's leading supplier of high-purity cellulose specialties — natural polymers used in pharmaceuticals, food, and industrial applications [2]. The strategic alternatives review initiated in April 2026 with Morgan Stanley as financial advisor creates a potential catalyst for value realization — at $525M market cap vs. $1.22B enterprise value, a buyer acquiring RYAM's niche cellulose franchise at a premium to current levels could deliver meaningful returns [3]. The cellulose specialties business serves end markets (pharmaceuticals, food) with relatively stable demand characteristics, and the plant-based product portfolio aligns with sustainability trends favoring natural over petroleum-derived alternatives.
The bear case is overwhelming. Seven consecutive years of net losses — including a Q1 2026 net loss of $81M ($1.22/share) that widened 156% year-over-year — demonstrate a fundamentally broken business model [1]. Gross profit turned negative in Q1 2026, meaning the company cannot cover manufacturing costs with revenue, let alone service its $763M debt burden ($695M net debt, representing 1.3x the market cap) [1][5]. Revenue of $319M in Q1 declined 10.4% YoY, and TTM revenue of $1.43B is flat versus 2021, suggesting zero organic growth over five years. ROE of -100.5% and profit margin of -32.8% place RYAM among the worst-performing public companies in the materials sector. CEO resignation, leadership transition, and strategic alternatives review signal that the current standalone strategy has failed. The most likely outcome is a sale at distressed valuations, a restructuring that dilutes equity holders, or continued value destruction.
