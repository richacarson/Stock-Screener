Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Chemicals ·NYSE ·United States
Rayonier Advanced Materials manufactures cellulose specialty products and biomaterials through five segments: Cellulose Specialties (high-purity cellulose for LCDs, impact-resistant plastics, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, cigarette filters), Biomaterials (biofuels, lignosulfonates, prebiotics), Cellulose Commodities (viscose for textiles and wipes), Paperboard (packaging and printing), and High-Yield Pulp. The company is pursuing a 'BioFuture' strategy to transform renewable materials into high-value biomaterial products. Revenue of ~$1.47B with ongoing turnaround effort after years of financial challenges. Founded 1926, headquartered in Jacksonville, Florida.

Investment Thesis
RYAM at 7.0x forward earnings is a deep-value turnaround bet on cellulose specialty products with Q2 2026 showing meaningful momentum — revenue of $376M (+11% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $40M (up 5x from Q1's $8M), and management targeting positive free cash flow for full-year 2026 [1]. The High Purity Cellulose segment ($301M Q2 sales, $57M EBITDA) serves essential and growing end markets including LCD displays, pharmaceuticals, and food-grade applications. At a $541M market cap, any sustained profitability improvement could drive significant upside. The bear case is existential. D/E of 374% creates severe financial fragility — elevated interest expenses on the heavy debt load consumed a significant portion of operating income even in the improved Q2 [4]. ROE of -49.7% and profit margin of -9.6% indicate the business is not generating adequate returns on its asset base. Revenue has declined from ~$1.8B to ~$1.47B over five years, and Q1 2026's $81.6M net loss (-$1.22/share) demonstrates how quickly results can deteriorate [1]. The dividend has been suspended since 2019 with no resumption timeline. Secular headwinds threaten the Paperboard and Commodity Cellulose segments as digitalization reduces demand for paper products. The $541M market cap limits institutional interest and creates liquidity risk during market stress. Glassdoor reviews citing 'awful leadership' and organizational dysfunction suggest the human capital challenges that frequently derail manufacturing turnarounds [2]. Multiple CEO transitions in recent years introduce strategic inconsistency during a period requiring sustained execution focus.
