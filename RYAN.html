Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance Brokers ·NYSE ·United States
Ryan Specialty Holdings provides specialty insurance distribution, underwriting, product development, administration, and risk management services to insurance brokers, agents, and carriers, operating as a wholesale broker and managing underwriter with delegated carrier authority [1]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 7.2% YoY to $916.6M with adjusted EPS of $0.74 (+12.1%), decelerating from Q1's 15.2% revenue growth as property rate declines and heightened competition pressure the E&S market [2]. Founded in 2010 by Patrick G. Ryan (founder of Aon), headquartered in Chicago; IPO'd on NYSE in July 2021.

Investment Thesis
Ryan Specialty is one of the largest independent wholesale/specialty insurance distributors in the US, having grown revenue from $1.43B in 2021 to $3.16B TTM (21%+ annual growth in each of 2024 and 2025) through a mix of strong organic growth and disciplined bolt-on M&A [1]. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $916.6M (+7.2% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.74 (+12.1%), and Q1 2026 was stronger at $795.2M (+15.2% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $0.47 (+20.5%) [2]. Forward P/E of 19.57 represents a reasonable multiple for a business with 21%+ 5-year revenue CAGR and 22% ROE, and founder Patrick Ryan's $3.9M open-market share purchase at $32.50 signals genuine insider conviction [5][6]. The $300M share repurchase increase reflects management confidence in the business trajectory. The Inspire Impact Score of 36 (ALIGNED) makes RYAN accessible to faith-based investors.
The bear case has intensified since Q2 2026. Management guided full-year 2026 organic growth down to mid-single digits (4-6%), citing 'worsening property rate declines, heightened competition, and slower market conditions,' a sharp deceleration from the 21%+ growth in 2024-2025 [2]. D/E of 375% reflects heavy leverage from the acquisition-fueled growth strategy ($3.81B debt vs. $140M cash), leaving minimal balance sheet flexibility if specialty/E&S pricing conditions continue to soften [6]. GAAP profit margin of 3.13% is wafer-thin, amplified by deal-related amortization but reflecting real capital intensity in the roll-up model. Barclays cut the price target from $52 to $45 specifically on AI disruption concerns for E&S wholesale brokers, and Glassdoor reviews citing large layoffs and lack of transparency raise organizational trust concerns that could impair talent retention during a critical competitive transition [3][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Stabilization or re-acceleration of organic growth back toward historical double-digit rates if E&S property pricing and competitive intensity normalize, with Q1 2026's 15.2% growth suggesting the deceleration may be temporary [2]
  2. Continued bolt-on M&A execution within the fragmented specialty/wholesale brokerage market, sustaining the 21%+ five-year revenue CAGR that has driven the stock's premium multiple [1]
  3. AI-driven operational efficiency gains from underwriting triage and risk flagging tools could expand adjusted margins, offsetting competitive pricing pressure in the E&S market [3]
Key Risks
  1. Full-year 2026 guidance of 4-6% organic growth with margin compression (down 100-150bps) signals a genuine deceleration from 21%+ growth, not just conservative guidance — property rate declines and heightened competition may persist [2]
  2. D/E of 375% ($3.81B debt vs. $140M cash) leaves limited balance sheet flexibility for continued M&A if specialty/E&S pricing conditions soften further and cash generation weakens [6]
  3. AI-driven underwriting and submission automation is compressing quote-to-bind cycle times industry-wide, a structural risk to wholesale broker intermediary economics that Barclays has explicitly flagged with a price target cut to $45 [3]
