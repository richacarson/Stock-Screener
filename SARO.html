Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
StandardAero is the world's largest independent aerospace engine aftermarket services provider, offering MRO, on-wing support, asset management, and component repair for commercial, business, military, and helicopter aircraft. Founded in 1911, the company operates globally with capabilities spanning CFM56, LEAP, PT6, and other major engine platforms. IPO on NYSE in October 2024 after Carlyle private equity ownership. CEO succession underway as Russell Ford retires and Paul McElhinney (30-year GE Aviation veteran) takes over October 1, 2026.

StandardAero is the world's largest independent aerospace engine MRO provider delivering consistent growth with improving profitability — Q2 2026 revenue of $1,599.7M with record 14.4% adjusted EBITDA margin ($230M, up 12.3% YoY), and net income of $97.3M (up 44% YoY) [1]. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $6.375-6.5B revenue and $885-910M adjusted EBITDA while leverage declined to 2.6x [1]. The LEAP program reached profitability in Q2 2026 — a critical milestone with the installed base entering its first major overhaul cycle, targeting $1B annual revenue by decade's end and several billion by mid-2030s [1][6]. Infrastructure scores 9/10 with an attractive forward PEG of 0.92 (Forward P/E 18.62 against 20.24% EPS growth), 12.6% revenue growth, and 15% 5yr revenue CAGR [5]. The bear case centers on thin margins and valuation risk. Profit margin of 5.12% is structurally thin for MRO with material pass-throughs — the primary drag on the infrastructure score [5]. D/E of 93% and $2.57B legacy debt burden from the Carlyle era constrain financial flexibility, though leverage is declining to 2.6x EBITDA [1]. The stock has declined 27% from its $34.48 52-week high to $25.01, reflecting market concerns about growth deceleration (Q2 revenue growth slowed to 4.6% from 13.3% in Q1) [1][5]. CEO transition risk exists as founding CEO Russell Ford hands off to Paul McElhinney effective October 1, 2026, though the structured succession with Ford remaining as Executive Chairman mitigates disruption [3]. Supply chain part delays continue to constrain throughput globally, and the company has zero AI deployment or innovation beyond its core MRO capabilities. No dividend reduces appeal for income-oriented investors.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Aerospace engine MRO is inherently physical — tearing down, inspecting, repairing, and reassembling jet engines requires specialized facilities, FAA/EASA certifications, and hands-on skilled technicians that AI structurally cannot replace [1][3]. Engine overhaul intervals are set by regulators regardless of condition monitoring, ensuring demand is structurally protected. The $110B aerospace MRO market is driven by the growing global aircraft fleet and a trend among airlines to outsource maintenance, creating durable demand [3]. AI-driven predictive maintenance actually increases MRO value by enabling more targeted and efficient shop visits.
