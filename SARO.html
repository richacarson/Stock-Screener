Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
StandardAero is the largest independent pure-play aerospace engine aftermarket services provider, offering engine maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO), on-wing support, component repair and engineering solutions across commercial, business aviation, military and helicopter markets. Founded in 1911, it services platforms including CFM56, LEAP, PT6, and Rolls-Royce engines. It listed on the NYSE in October 2024 after Carlyle ownership; Paul McElhinney succeeded long-time CEO Russell Ford on October 1, 2026.

Investment Thesis
StandardAero is the scale leader in independent engine MRO, a non-discretionary, regulation-driven aftermarket that grows with the global flight-hour base. Q2 2026 revenue rose 4.6% to $1.60B, while adjusted EBITDA rose 12.3% to a record $230M (14.4% margin) and adjusted EPS rose 24% to $0.40 [1]. Management raised 2026 guidance to $885-910M of adjusted EBITDA and $1.50-1.57 of adjusted EPS [1][5]. LEAP has turned profitable and targets about $1B of annual revenue by decade-end as the largest narrowbody fleet enters heavy shop visits [5]. At a 12.8x forward P/E and an implied PEG near 0.6, with Infrastructure scoring 9/10 and AI risk negligible, the risk/reward is attractive for a business with decades of embedded demand [6].
The bear case is margin and leverage. GAAP profit margin is only 5.1% because of material pass-throughs, and D/E of 93% reflects the Carlyle-era debt load, leaving less cushion in a flight-hour downturn [6]. Top-line growth slowed sharply from 13.3% in Q1 to 4.6% in Q2 2026, and military/helicopter revenue fell 3% on input delays [1][2]. The new CEO takes over amid persistent supply-chain part shortages, and Sustainalytics rates ESG risk as severe (42.2), an overhang for some institutional holders [4]. The stock sits well below its 52-week high, showing the market is not yet paying for the LEAP ramp.
Key Catalysts
  1. LEAP heavy-shop-visit ramp: the program turned profitable in 2026, and management targets about $1B of annual LEAP revenue by decade-end [5]
  2. Delivery on the raised 2026 guidance ($885-910M adjusted EBITDA, $1.50-1.57 adjusted EPS) at Q3/Q4 results, which would confirm the margin expansion after the exit from pass-through revenue [1]
  3. Continued deleveraging, plus possible M&A or capital-return optionality under new CEO Paul McElhinney, former head of GE Aviation Services [3]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue growth slowed from 13.3% (Q1) to 4.6% (Q2 2026); a further slowdown or a fall in commercial flight hours could compress the 12.8x forward multiple [1][2]
  2. Supply-chain part shortages and military input delays (military/helicopter revenue down 3% in Q2) constrain shop throughput and turn times [1]
  3. First-year execution risk under a new CEO after Russell Ford's 13-year tenure, combined with 93% D/E that limits flexibility in a downturn [3][6]
