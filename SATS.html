EchoStar Corporation operates a satellite communications and technology platform spanning pay-TV (DISH TV with 4.84M subscribers, Sling TV with 1.79M subscribers), satellite broadband (Hughes), and wireless spectrum assets. The company completed a $23B sale of wireless spectrum licenses to AT&T in 2026 while subsidiaries DISH DBS and Hughes filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection. EchoStar reported Q1 2026 revenue of $3.67B (-5% YoY) with a net loss of $146.89M. The company trades on NASDAQ with approximately $14.8B in trailing twelve-month revenue and $14.4B in TTM losses reflecting significant impairment charges.

Investment Thesis
EchoStar at -1.68x trailing P/E is a distressed restructuring play whose value proposition rests on spectrum asset monetization and post-bankruptcy recovery. The completed $23B spectrum sale to AT&T demonstrates that Ergen's decades-long spectrum accumulation strategy created real value, and the company's physical satellite infrastructure (Hughes broadband, satellite TV) generates recurring revenue from 6.63M pay-TV subscribers and enterprise broadband customers [1]. The Hughes AIOps platform across 32,000+ managed sites represents a technology differentiator in satellite-based enterprise connectivity. If the DISH DBS and Hughes bankruptcy proceedings result in successful debt reduction, the surviving entity could emerge with a cleaner balance sheet, valuable remaining spectrum assets, and a focused satellite broadband business [2].
The bear case is overwhelming: EchoStar carries a 515% debt-to-equity ratio, -112% ROE, and -97% profit margin with $14.4B in TTM losses [4]. Multiple subsidiaries are in Chapter 11 bankruptcy, the pay-TV subscriber base is hemorrhaging (366K lost in Q1 alone), and the 5G wireless network buildout is being abandoned after billions in investment [1][2]. The forward P/E of 399x on projected negligible earnings offers no valuation support. Going-concern warnings have been issued, and the company's ability to service remaining debt depends entirely on the outcome of complex bankruptcy proceedings and spectrum monetization. The DISH pay-TV business faces irreversible secular decline from streaming, and Hughes satellite internet competes directly against SpaceX's rapidly expanding Starlink constellation. The Inspire Impact Score of -62 (MISALIGNED) further limits allocation for faith-based investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Successful resolution of DISH DBS and Hughes Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings could result in significant debt reduction and a cleaner balance sheet, potentially unlocking equity value if the surviving entity emerges with manageable leverage [2]

  1. Multiple bankruptcy proceedings (DISH DBS, Hughes) could result in equity holders being wiped out if restructuring plans prioritize creditors over shareholders, as is typical in Chapter 11 cases where debt far exceeds equity value [2]
  2. Pay-TV subscriber losses of 366,000 per quarter are accelerating the decline of EchoStar's largest revenue stream, with no credible path to stabilization as streaming alternatives proliferate and Sling TV faces intense OTT competition [1]
  3. SpaceX's Starlink constellation is rapidly expanding satellite broadband coverage and capacity, directly threatening Hughes' competitive position with a technologically superior LEO constellation versus Hughes' legacy GEO satellites [3]
