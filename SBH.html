Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Sally Beauty Holdings is the world's largest specialty retailer and distributor of professional beauty products, operating approximately 3,450 stores through two segments: Sally Beauty Supply (consumer/salon professional retail) and Beauty Systems Group (BSG, professional-only distribution). The company carries brands including Wella, L'Oreal, Paul Mitchell, and Schwarzkopf across the US, Canada, Mexico, and Europe.

Investment Thesis
Sally Beauty offers deep value at 6.83x forward P/E with a PEG of 0.68, providing exposure to the world's largest specialty beauty retailer at a significant discount to retail peers [5]. Q2 FY2026 showed improving fundamentals: revenue of $903M (up 2.3% YoY), adjusted gross margin expanding 80bps to 52.8%, adjusted EPS of $0.44 beating $0.41 estimates, and raised FY2026 guidance to $2.02-$2.10 adj. EPS [1]. Color category grew 11% and e-commerce surged 13% to $108M, demonstrating category and channel investment is working [1]. ROE of 23.9%, $229M TTM free cash flow, and disciplined capital allocation ($20M debt paydown + $25M buybacks in Q2) support the improving narrative. Net debt leverage of 1.5x is manageable and declining [1]. The stock's 42% gain over the past year shows the market is beginning to recognize the improving trajectory, but at PEG 0.68 there remains significant upside if comps stabilize above 1%.
The bear case centers on structural retail headwinds, organizational dysfunction, and negligible revenue growth. Revenue has been essentially flat for five years (5yr CAGR of -0.5%) and FY2026 guidance of $3.725-$3.750B implies only low-single-digit growth, suggesting the specialty beauty retail category is mature and shrinking on a per-store basis [1]. D/E of 181% with $1.52B debt creates meaningful leverage risk in a flat-revenue business, and the 4.9% profit margin leaves minimal cushion for revenue declines [5]. Short interest of 21.1% reflects significant bearish conviction from institutional investors who see the improvement as temporary. BSG comparable sales declined 0.3% in Q2, showing the professional distribution segment is weakening even as Sally retail improves [1]. Employee sentiment is deeply negative — Glassdoor 3.3/5 with complaints about leadership dysfunction, poor pay, and high turnover — suggesting the Fuel for Growth savings may be extracting efficiency at the cost of long-term capability [4]. Competitive pressure from Ulta Beauty, Sephora, Amazon, and direct-to-consumer brands continues to structurally erode Sally Beauty's market position.

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Sally Beauty's core business is physical retail distribution of beauty products — customers visit stores to touch, test, and receive expert advice on professional beauty products from licensed cosmetologists [1]. This physical-world retail model with inventory management, real estate, and in-person expertise creates structural barriers to AI substitution. AI cannot replace the tactile experience of testing hair color, mixing custom formulations, or the personalized advice from trained beauty professionals. E-commerce disruption (already priced in over 5+ years) is a greater competitive threat than AI specifically. The professional beauty distribution segment (BSG) requires regulatory knowledge, supplier relationships, and salon-level trust that AI cannot replicate.
