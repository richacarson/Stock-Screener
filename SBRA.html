Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare ·NasdaqGS ·United States
Sabra Health Care REIT, Inc. is a self-administered, self-managed real estate investment trust that owns and invests in skilled nursing facilities, senior housing communities, behavioral health facilities, and other healthcare properties across the United States and Canada. The company is actively pivoting its portfolio toward managed senior housing (SHOP), targeting 30-40% SHOP exposure by end of 2026, while skilled nursing declines to approximately 47% of NOI. Sabra invested approximately $450M in 2025 — its most substantial investment year since before the pandemic — and surpassed 50% private pay concentration for the first time in company history.
58 Employees ·www.sabrahealth.com

Investment Thesis
Sabra Health Care REIT offers a 6.04% dividend yield supported by 77% AFFO payout coverage, positioned to benefit from the most powerful demographic tailwind in U.S. real estate — 10,000 Americans turning 65 daily with limited new senior housing supply [1][2]. The strategic pivot from skilled nursing dominance toward managed SHOP (targeting 30-40% exposure) is well-timed, with same-store SHOP cash NOI growing 14.4% YoY and occupancy up 280 basis points to 88.4% [2]. The $277M in year-to-date 2026 acquisitions at 7.9% initial cash yield and $410M awarded pipeline demonstrate active deal sourcing [2]. Surpassing 50% private pay concentration for the first time reduces government reimbursement risk and improves revenue predictability [3]. Normalized FFO guidance of $1.49-$1.53 per share represents 5% YoY growth, providing a foundation for continued dividend support.
The primary concern is valuation relative to growth. At 26.26x forward earnings and PEG of 5.28, Sabra is priced for growth acceleration that the 5% FFO guidance does not yet support [1]. The GAAP payout ratio of 190.48% — while misleading for a REIT — highlights the gap between reported earnings and distributable cash flow that may concern generalist investors unfamiliar with REIT accounting. D/E of 95.9% is moderate but rising interest rates would pressure refinancing costs and acquisition yields. The SHOP pivot introduces operational risk — unlike triple-net leases where tenants bear operational costs, managed SHOP properties expose Sabra to labor costs, occupancy fluctuations, and operational execution. The $1.20 annual dividend has been frozen since 2021 following a 33% cut during COVID, and the 5-year dividend CAGR of -7.8% penalizes income-growth investors [3]. Skilled nursing tenant concentration remains a risk, with top relationships critical to portfolio NOI, and competition for senior housing acquisitions is intensifying as capital floods into the demographic-driven theme.
Key Catalysts
  1. Baby boomer demographic wave driving sustained demand for senior housing with limited new supply, supporting occupancy gains and rent growth across Sabra's SHOP portfolio through the rest of the decade
  2. SHOP exposure expansion toward 30-40% of portfolio providing higher growth potential and operational upside versus triple-net skilled nursing, with same-store NOI already growing 14.4% YoY
  3. Private pay concentration exceeding 50% for the first time reducing government reimbursement dependency and improving revenue quality and predictability
Key Risks
  1. PEG of 5.28 and Forward P/E of 26.26 reflect a premium valuation relative to the 5% normalized FFO growth guidance — any growth disappointment or interest rate spike could compress the multiple significantly
  2. SHOP operational exposure introduces labor cost, occupancy, and execution risk that triple-net leases insulate against — Sabra is transitioning from a rent collector to a partial operator without deep operational expertise
  3. Healthcare regulatory risk from potential Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement changes and state staffing mandates could pressure skilled nursing tenant coverage ratios and rent affordability

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Healthcare real estate is structurally insulated from AI disruption. Physical buildings that house skilled nursing patients and senior living residents cannot be replaced by software — aging populations require physical care facilities, and the buildings themselves are irreducible physical assets [1][2]. Sabra's triple-net lease model further insulates the REIT, as tenants bear operational risk while Sabra collects contractual rent. The baby boomer demographic wave creates demand that AI cannot substitute [2]. However, AI-enabled remote health monitoring, telemedicine, and predictive analytics could eventually reduce the intensity of facility-based care, potentially dampening demand growth at the margin. AI could also help competing operators optimize facility operations, putting pressure on Sabra's less tech-savvy tenants. The shift toward SHOP gives Sabra more exposure to operational technology but also more operational risk. Interest rate sensitivity and government reimbursement policy are far greater risks than AI disruption. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI tools could modestly benefit Sabra's operations — property analytics for acquisition targeting, predictive modeling for tenant creditworthiness, and operational optimization in managed SHOP properties. AI-powered health monitoring in senior housing could improve care outcomes and occupancy. However, AI is not a material competitive differentiator for healthcare REITs.
