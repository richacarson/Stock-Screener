Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare ·NASDAQ ·United States
Sabra Health Care REIT, Inc. is a self-administered, self-managed REIT that owns and invests in skilled nursing facilities, senior housing communities, behavioral health facilities, and other healthcare properties across the United States and Canada. The company is actively pivoting toward managed senior housing (SHOP), targeting 30-40% SHOP exposure by end of 2026. FY2025 revenue was $775.8M (+10.4% YoY), and Q2 2026 revenue reached $238.1M (+25% YoY). Same-store managed senior housing occupancy is up 280 bps to 88.4%. The company has closed or awarded $400M+ in investments YTD 2026 and raised its 2026 guidance, projecting 7-8% normalized FFO/AFFO growth.
58 Employees ·www.sabrahealth.com

Investment Thesis
Sabra Health Care REIT offers a 5.80% dividend yield supported by ~77% AFFO payout coverage, positioned to benefit from the most powerful demographic tailwind in U.S. real estate — 10,000 Americans turning 65 daily with limited new senior housing supply [1][2]. Q2 2026 revenue of $238.1M (+25% YoY) and same-store SHOP occupancy gains of 280 bps to 88.4% demonstrate the pivot to managed senior housing is delivering results [2]. The company has closed or awarded $400M+ in investments YTD 2026 at attractive yields and raised 2026 guidance to 7-8% normalized FFO/AFFO growth [1]. Surpassing 50% private pay concentration reduces government reimbursement risk [3]. The Inspire Impact Score of 37 (ALIGNED) and Matros' emphasis on culture and quality care add appeal for faith-based investors.
The primary concern is valuation relative to growth. At ~28x forward earnings and PEG of 5.28, Sabra is priced for growth acceleration that the 7-8% FFO guidance only partially supports [1]. The GAAP Q2 2026 FFO loss of $0.021/share (versus $0.45 in Q2 2025) — while likely one-time — raises transparency questions about GAAP vs. normalized earnings. The SHOP pivot introduces operational risk — unlike triple-net leases, managed SHOP properties expose Sabra to labor costs, occupancy fluctuations, and execution challenges. The $1.20 annual dividend has been frozen since 2021 following a 33% COVID cut, with a 5-year dividend CAGR of -7.8% that penalizes income-growth investors [3]. D/E of 95.67% is moderate but rising rates would pressure refinancing costs. Net insider selling of $302K over 90 days suggests limited executive conviction beyond compensation-based equity [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Baby boomer demographic wave driving sustained senior housing demand with limited new supply — same-store SHOP occupancy up 280 bps to 88.4% with runway to pre-pandemic levels above 90% [2]
  2. 2026 investment pipeline of $400M+ already closed or awarded at attractive yields, with management projecting total 2026 investments to exceed 2025's $450M record — accelerating portfolio growth while expanding SHOP exposure toward 30-40% [2]
  3. Raised 2026 guidance targeting 7-8% normalized FFO/AFFO growth, with private pay concentration exceeding 50% for the first time improving revenue quality and reducing government reimbursement dependency [1][3]
Key Risks
  1. PEG of 5.28 and forward P/E of ~28x reflect a premium valuation relative to single-digit FFO growth — any growth disappointment, interest rate spike, or Q2 2026-style GAAP loss could compress the multiple significantly [1]
  2. SHOP operational exposure introduces labor cost, occupancy, and execution risk that triple-net leases insulate against — Sabra is transitioning from rent collector to partial operator without deep operational expertise, and the Q2 GAAP FFO loss highlights this transition risk [2]
  3. Frozen $1.20 annual dividend since 2021 and -7.8% 5-year dividend CAGR penalize income-growth investors — no dividend increase despite rising FFO creates a disconnect between operational improvement and shareholder returns [3]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Healthcare real estate is structurally insulated from AI disruption — physical buildings housing skilled nursing patients and senior living residents cannot be replaced by software, and the baby boomer demographic wave creates irreducible demand [1][2]. Sabra's triple-net lease model further insulates as tenants bear operational risk while Sabra collects contractual rent. CEO Matros is actively adopting AI at the corporate level to speed underwriting and deal analysis, and at the operator level for falls management and risk assessment [3]. However, AI-enabled remote health monitoring and telemedicine could eventually reduce intensity of facility-based care, dampening demand growth at the margin. The SHOP pivot gives Sabra more operational exposure that could benefit from AI optimization but also creates execution risk. Interest rate sensitivity and government reimbursement policy remain far greater risks than AI disruption. AI Opportunity (1/1): Matros has specifically articulated AI benefits — corporate deal analysis compressed from weeks to hours, and operator-level AI for resident outcomes including falls management and risk assessment [3]. While not a revenue driver, AI is being deployed as an operational efficiency tool across the portfolio.
