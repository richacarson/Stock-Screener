Companhia de Saneamento Basico do Estado de Sao Paulo - SABESP is Latin America's largest water and wastewater utility, serving approximately 30 million residents across 375+ municipalities in the state of Sao Paulo, Brazil. Privatized in July 2024 with Equatorial Energia as the strategic investor, the company provides treated water supply and sewage collection/treatment services. Founded in 1954 and headquartered in Sao Paulo, SABESP is listed on NYSE as an ADR and on B3 (SBSP3).

Investment Thesis
SABESP delivered a strong Q1 2026 with R$9.97B net revenue (up 18.3% YoY), R$3.8B adjusted EBITDA (up 25.9% at 62.9% margin), and R$1.5B net income (up 32.2%), driven by tariff increases, operational efficiency from headcount optimization, and power cost savings from free-market migration [1]. The financial profile is compelling for a utility: 30.91% ROE, 27.75% profit margin, forward P/E of 15.4, and 4.84% dividend yield with 22% 5-year dividend growth CAGR [4]. As Latin America's largest water utility with a regulated monopoly serving 30 million people, SABESP benefits from visible long-term demand, a R$3.73B/quarter CapEx program targeting universal sanitation by 2029, and post-privatization margin expansion with operating expenses down 7.7% YoY [1].
The primary risk is Brazil macro exposure and execution complexity during the two-year-old privatization transition. The BRL has depreciated significantly against the USD, eroding returns for ADR holders despite strong BRL-denominated operating performance [4]. D/E of 82% will likely increase as the company funds its ambitious CapEx program through the end of the decade, straining the balance sheet if Brazilian interest rates remain elevated. The 13% headcount reduction creates labor relations risk and potential service quality concerns across 375 municipalities. Regulatory risk persists as the tariff-setting mechanism remains subject to political interference despite privatization, and Sao Paulo's water supply is vulnerable to climate-driven drought, as demonstrated by the severe 2014-2015 water crisis that threatened service to 20+ million residents [3]. The lumpy Brazilian dividend pattern (only 3 consecutive years of growth) creates income uncertainty for dividend-focused investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Universal sanitation coverage target by 2029 driving sustained CapEx of R$3.7B+/quarter, expanding the revenue base through new connections and service upgrades across Sao Paulo state [1]
  2. Post-privatization EBITDA margin expansion trajectory — Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of 62.9% with further upside from headcount optimization, power cost savings, and INTEGRA 4.0 digital efficiencies [1]
  3. EMAE acquisition integrating water and energy infrastructure to create operational synergies and revenue diversification beyond pure water/wastewater services
Key Risks
  1. BRL/USD currency depreciation risk for ADR holders — SABESP's revenues are entirely BRL-denominated, creating FX-driven return volatility independent of strong operating performance [4]
  2. Regulatory and political risk in Brazil's tariff framework could constrain rate increases below inflation or mandate service expansions at uncommercial terms, compressing margins despite privatization
  3. Climate vulnerability to severe drought in the Sao Paulo watershed system, with historical precedent in the 2014-2015 crisis that forced rationing for 20+ million residents [3]
