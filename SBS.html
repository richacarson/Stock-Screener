Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Water ·NYSE ·Brazil
SABESP is Latin America's largest water and sewage utility, serving approximately 28 million customers across 375 municipalities in Sao Paulo State, Brazil. Founded in 1954, the company underwent a transformative privatization in July 2024, with Equatorial Participacoes acquiring a 15% strategic stake and the Sao Paulo state government reducing its ownership while retaining control. Under CEO Carlos Augusto Leone Piani (appointed October 2024), SABESP is executing a R$70 billion five-year investment plan to achieve universal sanitation coverage by 2029, with Q1 2026 EBITDA surging 26% on efficiency gains post-privatization.

Investment Thesis
SABESP at 12.6x forward earnings and a PEG of 0.47 offers compelling value for a transformed utility executing a R$70B infrastructure expansion. Q1 2026 delivered 26% EBITDA growth on 11% revenue growth with a 63% EBITDA margin, demonstrating post-privatization efficiency gains are real [1]. The investment case rests on a visible multi-year growth runway — the concession mandate to achieve universal sanitation by 2029 provides regulatory certainty for sustained CapEx and customer expansion across 375 municipalities serving 28 million people. ROE of 18.8% is exceptional for a utility, Forward P/E of 12.6x reflects significant Brazil country discount, and the institutional validation of LatinFinance's Sustainable Infrastructure Financing award signals credibility with multilateral lenders [3]. Equatorial's strategic 15% stake provides operational expertise from Brazil's leading utility operator.
The bear case centers on Brazil macro risk, regulatory exposure, and capital intensity. Q2 2026 net income declined 31.4% YoY on higher operating expenses and inflation, demonstrating the vulnerability of BRL-denominated earnings to cost pressures [1]. Environmental concerns have emerged that SABESP is exceeding water withdrawal limits from the Cantareira reservoir system — 30% above post-crisis averages in 2025 — which could trigger regulatory penalties or operational restrictions [3]. The R$70B CapEx program strains the balance sheet (D/E of 114%) and requires sustained access to capital markets at reasonable rates, creating refinancing risk in a volatile Brazilian interest rate environment. Currency risk is material for USD ADR holders — the BRL/USD can swing 15-20% annually, amplifying or muting underlying business performance. The post-privatization governance transition remains early (CEO appointed October 2024), and execution risk on the universal coverage mandate is substantial given Brazil's infrastructure challenges.
