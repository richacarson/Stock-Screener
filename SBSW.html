Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Precious Metals & Mining ·NYQ ·South Africa
Sibanye Stillwater is the world's largest primary producer of platinum group metals (PGMs), a significant South African gold miner, and an emerging green metals player through its Keliber lithium project in Finland and recycling operations. The company operates deep-level mines in South Africa's Witwatersrand Basin, PGM operations in Rustenburg and Montana (US). H1 2026 group revenue surged 64% YoY to R90B with adjusted EBITDA of R31.8B, driven by 67% higher 4E PGM basket prices. The company returned to R18.8B profit after prior-year losses and declared an R5.7B interim dividend.

Investment Thesis
Sibanye-Stillwater at $9.95 is a deep-value cyclical play executing a powerful earnings recovery — H1 2026 revenue surged 64% to R90B with adjusted EBITDA more than doubling to R31.8B, driven by a 67% increase in 4E PGM basket prices and steady production output [1]. The balance sheet has improved dramatically with net debt halving to R9.7B (0.18x net-debt-to-EBITDA), enabling an R5.7B interim dividend. At just 2.9x forward earnings with 8.1% dividend yield, the stock prices in significant earnings decline, offering deep value if PGM prices sustain. The Burnstone gold mine expansion adds 130,000 oz/yr of gold production at a time when gold prices exceed $2,600/oz, and the Keliber lithium project enters production to diversify into European EV battery supply chains [1].
The bear case centers on commodity cyclicality and structural PGM risks. The current earnings surge is almost entirely price-driven — the 67% increase in PGM basket prices inflates all profitability metrics, and a price reversion toward 2023-2024 levels would collapse margins given the high fixed-cost base of underground mining [1]. The forward P/E of 2.9x reflects the market's expectation that peak-cycle earnings are unsustainable — the company posted massive losses just two years ago. PGM demand faces long-term structural headwinds from EV adoption reducing catalytic converter demand. South African operations carry sovereign risks including Eskom power instability, above-inflation labor cost escalation (mining unions typically secure 7-9% annual increases), and regulatory uncertainty around the Mining Charter [4]. The CEO transition from Froneman to Stewart introduces execution risk during a critical recovery period, and the battery metals diversification has produced mixed results with the Keliber lithium project facing cost overruns amid lithium market oversupply.
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained PGM price strength driven by autocatalyst demand recovery and structural supply deficits from South African mine closures — the 67% YoY increase in 4E basket prices could persist as above-ground inventories deplete, with H1 2026 SA PGM EBITDA surging 302% to R19.2B [1]
  2. Burnstone gold mine reaching steady-state production of 130,000 oz/yr at gold prices exceeding $2,600/oz, adding a high-margin production stream alongside the existing SA gold business that contributed strong EBITDA in H1 2026 [1]
  3. Balance sheet transformation — net debt halving to R9.7B with 0.18x net-debt-to-EBITDA and gross debt declining 20% enables increased shareholder returns through the R5.7B interim dividend and potential buybacks [1]
Key Risks
  1. PGM price reversion risk — the 64% revenue surge is almost entirely commodity-price-driven, and PGM prices could retrace toward 2023-2024 trough levels if autocatalyst demand weakens or secondary supply increases, collapsing the R31.8B EBITDA base just as it did two years ago [1]
  2. Structural PGM demand headwind from the EV transition — as internal combustion engine production decreases globally, catalytic converter demand for platinum, palladium, and rhodium faces secular decline that no amount of cost optimization can offset [1]
  3. South African sovereign and operational risks including Eskom load-shedding disruptions, above-inflation wage escalation from powerful mining unions, regulatory uncertainty around the Mining Charter, and ZAR currency volatility affecting USD-reported results [4]
