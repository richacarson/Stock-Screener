Company Profile
Materials ·Copper Mining ·NYSE ·United States
Southern Copper Corporation is one of the world's largest integrated copper producers, engaged in mining, smelting, and refining copper, molybdenum, zinc, and silver. Operations are concentrated in Peru and Mexico, with exploration activities in Argentina and Chile. The company is 88.9% owned by Grupo Mexico and holds one of the largest copper reserve bases in the industry. Headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona.

Investment Thesis
Southern Copper delivered record Q1 2026 results with revenue of $4.25B (up 36% YoY) and net income of $1.58B (up 67%), supported by higher copper prices and low-cost operations in Peru and Mexico, prompting the company to raise its 2026 revenue forecast from $15.7B to $16.1B [1][4]. The business sits at the center of the AI-driven electrification supercycle: S&P Global and J.P. Morgan project sharply rising copper demand from AI data centers and grid infrastructure buildout through 2040, positioning SCCO's large reserve base and low-cost Peru/Mexico assets as a structural beneficiary independent of any single company's AI strategy [6]. With ROE above 46%, profit margin above 34%, and D/E of just 62%, the balance sheet and profitability profile are best-in-class for a commodity producer, and the 28-year unbroken dividend record with a 5yr dividend growth CAGR of 7.2% supports the Dividend sleeve classification [4][5].
The primary offsets are cyclicality and execution risk. Revenue growth 5yr CAGR of only ~5.3% reflects the volatility of copper prices rather than structural expansion, and 2026 copper production guidance of 911,400 tons represents a 4.7% decline versus 2025 output, meaning near-term earnings growth leans heavily on price rather than volume [4]. Governance continuity is a live concern following the sudden April 2026 death of CEO Oscar Gonzalez Rocha after 26 years at the helm, with new CEO Leonardo Contreras Lerdo de Tejada untested in the role [3]. Community and environmental friction is a structural risk specific to SCCO's jurisdictions — the Tia Maria project has been stalled since 2011 amid protests that turned fatal in 2011-2015, and the Los Chancas project camp was attacked and set on fire by a rioting mob, underscoring the social-license risk embedded in Peru and Mexico mining operations [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center and grid electrification buildout structurally lifting copper demand, with S&P Global projecting demand growth from 28 million to 42 million metric tons by 2040 and a widening supply deficit
  2. Progress on the pending $10.2B Mexico investment plan and resolution of Tia Maria permitting in Peru, which could unlock meaningful new production capacity
  3. Continued high copper prices sustaining record earnings, with 2026 revenue guidance already raised to $16.1B from $15.7B
Key Risks
  1. 2026 copper production guidance implies a 4.7% year-over-year decline (911,400 tons vs. 956,270 tons in 2025), meaning earnings growth is dependent on price rather than volume
  2. Unplanned CEO transition following Oscar Gonzalez Rocha's sudden death in April 2026 introduces leadership continuity risk after 26 years of stability
  3. Community and regulatory opposition in Peru and Mexico (Tia Maria stalled since 2011, Los Chancas camp attacked and burned) represents a recurring structural risk to project execution and social license to operate
