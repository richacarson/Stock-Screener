Company Profile
Materials ·Copper Mining ·NYSE ·United States
Southern Copper Corporation is one of the world's largest integrated copper producers, engaged in mining, smelting, and refining copper, molybdenum, zinc, and silver. Operations are concentrated in Peru and Mexico, with exploration activities in Argentina and Chile. The company is 88.9% owned by Grupo Mexico and holds one of the largest copper reserve bases in the industry. Following the sudden death of long-tenured CEO Oscar Gonzalez Rocha in April 2026, Leonardo Contreras Lerdo de Tejada was appointed CEO. Q2 2026 revenue reached a record $4.29B (+40.6% YoY) driven by copper prices climbing nearly 40% on the LME.

Investment Thesis
Southern Copper posted record Q2 2026 revenue of $4.29B (+40.6% YoY) and diluted EPS of $2.01 (beating $1.95 consensus), with H1 revenue of $8.54B and EPS of $3.93 (+69% YoY), driven by copper prices climbing nearly 40% on the LME and silver surging 118.6% [1][2]. The company sits at the center of the AI-driven electrification supercycle: S&P Global projects copper demand to surge 50% by 2040 with a widening supply deficit, and SCCO's position as one of the world's largest copper reserve holders makes it a structural beneficiary [6]. ROE of 49.90%, profit margin of 35.87%, and D/E of 68% represent best-in-class financials for a commodity producer. The 28-year unbroken dividend record with 2.23% yield and 7.21% 5yr dividend growth CAGR supports the Dividend sleeve. Citi raised its price target to $108 (likely pre-split basis), reflecting growing institutional recognition of the copper supercycle thesis.
The primary offsets are valuation, cyclicality, and execution risk. Forward P/E of 27.53x is premium for a commodity producer and prices in sustained high copper prices — any cyclical downturn in copper would compress both earnings and the multiple simultaneously [5]. Revenue growth 5yr CAGR of ~9% masks significant commodity-price-driven volatility rather than structural volume growth, and production has been declining (2026 guidance of 911,400 tons, down 4.7% from 2025's 956,270 tons), meaning near-term earnings depend entirely on price rather than volume [1]. The sudden April 2026 death of CEO Oscar Gonzalez Rocha after 26 years introduces leadership continuity risk under untested successor Leonardo Contreras Lerdo de Tejada [3]. Community and environmental friction is structural — the Tia Maria project stalled since 2011 amid fatal protests, and the Los Chancas camp was attacked and burned, underscoring the social-license risk embedded in Peru/Mexico mining [3]. The 88.9% Grupo Mexico ownership concentration limits minority shareholder influence on capital allocation.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center and grid electrification buildout structurally lifting copper demand — S&P Global projects demand from 28M to 42M metric tons by 2040 with a widening supply deficit that benefits low-cost producers like SCCO [6]
  2. Execution on the $20.5B capital investment program including resolution of Tia Maria permitting in Peru, which could unlock meaningful new production capacity and reverse the 2025-2026 production volume decline
  3. Continued copper price strength above $5/lb sustaining record-level earnings and supporting potential dividend increases, with H1 2026 EPS of $3.93 already surpassing full-year 2025 EPS of $5.19 at the current pace
Key Risks
  1. 2026 copper production guidance of 911,400 tons implies a 4.7% YoY decline versus 2025's 956,270 tons — earnings growth is entirely dependent on copper prices staying elevated rather than on operational volume growth [1]
  2. CEO transition following Oscar Gonzalez Rocha's sudden death in April 2026 after 26 years introduces leadership continuity risk under untested successor Leonardo Contreras Lerdo de Tejada, the first CEO change in a generation [3]
  3. Community and regulatory opposition in Peru and Mexico (Tia Maria stalled since 2011, Los Chancas camp attacked) represents recurring structural risk to project execution and social license to operate, threatening the $20.5B capex pipeline [3]
