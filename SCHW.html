Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·United States
The Charles Schwab Corporation provides wealth management, securities brokerage, banking, asset management, custody, and financial advisory services to individual investors and independent investment advisors. Through its subsidiaries including Charles Schwab & Co. and TD Ameritrade, the company manages $13.08 trillion in total client assets across 46.5 million accounts. CEO Rick Wurster (since January 2025) delivered record Q2 2026 revenue of $7.1B (+21% YoY) with $119.8B in core net new assets and raised full-year revenue guidance to 17.5-18.5% growth.

Schwab at 15.3x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.75 is a growth-at-a-reasonable-price franchise delivering accelerating fundamentals — Q2 2026 record revenue of $7.1B (+21% YoY) with net interest revenue of $3.4B (+19%), trading revenue of $1.2B (+28%), and record daily average trades of 11.9M (+57%) demonstrate the post-TD Ameritrade platform is unlocking operating leverage [1]. Core net new assets of $119.8B (+49% YoY) with 1.4M new accounts signal sustained organic growth momentum, while full-year revenue guidance was raised to 17.5-18.5% from the prior 14-15% range. ROE of 20.27%, profit margin of 38.79%, and operating margin of 50.2% reflect best-in-class capital efficiency among diversified financial services firms [4]. CEO Wurster's $1.85M insider purchase in May 2026 underscores executive conviction [3].
The bear case centers on AI disruption risk and interest rate sensitivity. Altruist's AI-powered tax planning tool triggered an 8% SCHW selloff, raising legitimate questions about whether automated financial advice will commoditize advisory fees (~$1.8B quarterly) over the next 3-5 years [2]. Net interest income generates 48% of revenue, making Schwab highly sensitive to Fed rate movements — a rapid cutting cycle would compress the 3.00% NIM that is currently the largest earnings driver [1]. D/E of 151% reflects banking subsidiary leverage that amplifies credit quality deterioration or deposit flight risk, as the 2023 banking stress episode demonstrated. CEO Wurster has been in the role for less than two years, untested through a market crisis or recession. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 8.66% lags the current 21% growth rate, and normalization in trading volumes or client cash sorting could decelerate top-line growth.
