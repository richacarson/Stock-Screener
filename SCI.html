Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Personal Services ·NYSE ·United States
Service Corporation International is the largest provider of deathcare products and services in North America, operating approximately 1,500 funeral service locations, 500 cemeteries, and related crematoria across the United States and Canada. The company offers funeral services, cremation, cemetery property (lots, crypts, mausoleum spaces, niches), and preneed arrangements under the Dignity Memorial, Neptune Society, and National Cremation Society brands. SCI has grown through decades of acquisition-driven consolidation of the fragmented funeral industry. Revenue of ~$4.37B with FY2026 EPS guidance of $4.10-$4.30. Founded 1962, headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Investment Thesis
SCI at 16.71x forward earnings is the monopoly franchise in North American deathcare — the largest funeral services company with ~1,500 funeral homes, 500 cemeteries, and the Dignity Memorial brand serving the most AI-immune business in the economy [1][4]. Demographics provide the ultimate long-term tailwind: aging Baby Boomers will drive a sustained increase in death rates through the 2030s, providing volume growth that SCI is uniquely positioned to capture with the industry's largest footprint. Preneed cemetery sales grew 8% in Q2 2026, and FY2026 EPS guidance of $4.10-$4.30 with double-digit H2 growth signals accelerating profitability [1]. ROE of 34.71% demonstrates exceptional capital efficiency, and the 1.87% dividend yield with a conservative 35.5% payout ratio supports continued dividend growth alongside share repurchases. The preneed model provides decades of locked-in revenue visibility.
The bear case centers on financial leverage, slow growth, and the cremation headwind. D/E of 344.9% creates significant financial vulnerability if credit markets tighten or interest rates remain elevated — the acquisition-driven consolidation model requires continuous debt capacity, and any disruption to capital markets could halt the growth engine [4]. Revenue growth of 3.6% YoY and an estimated 5-year CAGR of ~1.3% are anemic, and the secular shift to cremation (~60% of dispositions) structurally reduces revenue per case from ~$7,000-$9,000 for traditional burial to ~$3,000-$4,000 for cremation, creating a persistent headwind that volume gains can only partially offset [1]. Profit margin of 12.3% is below expectations for a dominant franchise, and EPS growth of only 4.7% suggests limited operating leverage. Cultural challenges are concerning — Comparably's D+ executive team rating and Glassdoor feedback about micromanagement and low pay could impair the compassionate frontline service that differentiates SCI from lower-cost competitors [2]. Price transparency initiatives and online comparison tools could pressure the pricing power that drives margins in an industry with significant information asymmetry.

AI Risk Protection (9/9): Funeral services are among the most AI-immune businesses in the economy. SCI's core operations — conducting funeral services, embalming and preparing remains, cremation, graveside services, cemetery maintenance, and providing compassionate support to grieving families — are irreducibly physical, deeply personal, and heavily regulated [1]. AI cannot conduct a funeral, comfort a bereaved family, embalm a body, maintain a cemetery, or provide the human presence that defines deathcare. State-level licensing requirements for funeral directors create additional regulatory moats. The emotional and sacred nature of the service creates a structural floor on human involvement that no technology can penetrate. No analysts have flagged AI as a risk for funeral services; the industry is considered maximally AI-immune alongside utilities and physical infrastructure. AI Opportunity (0/1): SCI does not generate revenue from AI-related products or services. While AI could eventually assist with back-office operations, scheduling, or digital memorial tools, no quantifiable AI revenue has been disclosed [2].
