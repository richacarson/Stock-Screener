Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Schrodinger develops a physics-based computational platform that enables discovery of novel molecules for drug development and materials science applications. Founded in 1990, the company operates in two segments: Software (computational chemistry tools sold to pharma, biotech, and materials companies) and Drug Discovery (internal and collaborative drug programs). CEO Ramy Farid leads a team of 850. In Q2 2026, ACV surged 27% YoY driven by the launch of Bunsen, an agentic AI product for automated molecular discovery workflows. The company reported its first quarterly net income ($6M) in Q2 2026, signaling a potential profitability inflection after years of investment.

Investment Thesis
Schrodinger at $30.24 offers a rare pure-play on the physics+AI computational drug discovery revolution, with Q2 2026 marking a potential inflection point — first quarterly net income ($6M vs -$43M loss), ACV surging 27% YoY on Bunsen (agentic AI) adoption, and drug discovery revenue reaching $23M including a $10M Ajax Therapeutics milestone [1]. The company's 36-year investment in proprietary force fields and computational chemistry creates a defensible technology moat that pure ML approaches have not replicated, evidenced by NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang publicly citing Schrodinger as a key partner [2]. The addressable market is expanding as pharma companies increasingly adopt computational methods to reduce the $2.6B average cost per approved drug. Full-year 2026 ACV guidance of $218M-$228M (10-15% growth) may prove conservative given Q2's 27% pace.
The bear case centers on sustained unprofitability, competitive intensity, and clinical risk. Despite Q2 2026's first profitable quarter, the company remains loss-making on a trailing basis with -21% profit margin and -16.2% ROE [4]. Forward consensus expects continued losses (forward EPS -$1.35), and the negative forward P/E reflects market skepticism about the profitability path. The competitive landscape is intensifying — DeepMind's AlphaFold, Recursion Pharmaceuticals, and Insilico Medicine are well-capitalized competitors advancing AI-driven drug discovery, and large pharma companies are building internal computational capabilities that could reduce demand for Schrodinger's platform [2]. Drug discovery revenue is lumpy (the $10M Ajax milestone may not recur quarterly), and Schrodinger's internal clinical programs carry binary risk — a clinical failure could impair sentiment significantly. Revenue growth of 7.5% YoY (GAAP basis) understates ACV momentum but reflects the accounting reality investors price against.

Key Risks
