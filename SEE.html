Company Profile
Materials ·Packaging & Containers ·NYSE ·United States
Sealed Air Corporation is a global packaging company operating in two segments: Food (Cryovac food packaging systems) and Protective (Bubble Wrap, Autobag, Instapak protective packaging). The company was acquired by funds affiliated with Clayton, Dubilier & Rice (CD&R) in an all-cash transaction valued at $10.3 billion enterprise value ($42.15 per share), with the acquisition completing on April 9, 2026. Shares have ceased trading on the NYSE.

Investment Thesis
Sealed Air's acquisition by CD&R at $42.15 per share closed on April 9, 2026, effectively making this a completed transaction [2]. For investors who held through the acquisition, the takeout price represented a modest premium. The company's core Food packaging franchise (Cryovac) provides essential food safety and shelf-life extension technology with stable demand dynamics, and the Protective segment benefits from secular e-commerce growth [1]. Return on equity of 47.39% and forward P/E of 12.27 reflected reasonable valuation at the time of acquisition. Under CD&R ownership, operational improvements and debt reduction could position the company for a future public offering or strategic sale at higher value.
The CD&R acquisition renders the stock uninvestable for public market participants — shares have ceased trading on the NYSE as of April 2026 [2]. For analytical purposes, Sealed Air's weaknesses were evident pre-acquisition: revenue was essentially flat (5-year CAGR of 1.6%), debt-to-equity of 331% exceeded the 300% danger threshold, and profit margin of 9.43% was compressed by competitive pricing and input cost pressure [1][3]. CEO instability (Kivits ousted after less than a year) and the board's decision to accept a PE buyout rather than pursue an independent turnaround suggest the company lacked the leadership stability and strategic vision to reignite growth on its own. Private equity ownership typically prioritizes cost reduction and leverage optimization over innovation investment, which could further compress the company's already modest margin profile.
