Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Solaris Energy Infrastructure, Inc. is an energy infrastructure company providing distributed behind-the-meter power solutions for AI data centers, industrial loads, and oil and gas operations. Formerly Solaris Oilfield Infrastructure, the company renamed in September 2024 after acquiring Mobile Energy Rentals (MER), pivoting from frac sand logistics into natural gas-powered turbine leasing for hyperscaler data centers. Q2 2026 revenue of $219.4M grew 47% YoY with adjusted EBITDA of $108.3M. The company operates approximately 3,100 MW of total power generation capacity with over 2 GW under long-term contracts (10-15 year terms) with leading global technology companies. Completed nearly $2B in financing ($1.3B senior unsecured notes + $650M revolving credit facility).

Investment Thesis
Solaris Energy Infrastructure is one of the purest plays on the AI power infrastructure bottleneck, with over 2 GW of natural gas power generation contracted under 10-15 year terms with investment-grade technology companies [1][2]. Q2 2026 delivered $219.4M revenue (+47% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $108.3M (+30% sequential), and Power Solutions now represents 72% of revenue [1]. The expansion of three contracts adding $100M+ annual EBITDA and a turnkey 660 MW power plant for an AI customer demonstrate accelerating demand [1]. PEG of ~0.9 — below 1.0 — is exceptional for a company growing revenue at 47%. Profit margin improved to 11.5% (from 6.69%) as operating leverage materializes. The $2B financing package provides capital to execute the capacity expansion toward 3.1 GW and $1.7B revenue by 2029 [2][3]. Grid capacity constraints structurally favor distributed behind-the-meter power, and Solaris is the scaled leader.
The primary risks are financial leverage and dilution. D/E of ~146% and $2B in new financing create substantial debt burden, with operating cash flow covering only 20% of total debt [5]. The 87.6% increase in shares outstanding over one year represents dramatic dilution — existing shareholders' ownership has been nearly halved to fund the capacity expansion, and further equity issuances may be needed [5]. Insider selling of $2.8M over 3 months is concerning for a founder-led company, suggesting even management is reducing exposure despite bullish operational momentum. Profit margin of 11.5% is improving but still thin for an infrastructure company, and GAAP EPS of $0.26/quarter underperforms the adjusted $0.39 due to non-cash charges. Trailing PE of 77x prices in significant future earnings that must materialize from contracted capacity converting to revenue. Technology risk from nuclear SMRs and grid buildouts could reduce long-term demand for gas distributed power, and the legacy frac sand business ($61.1M Q2) remains exposed to oilfield cyclicality.
Key Catalysts
  1. Expanded contracts adding over $100M of annual adjusted EBITDA, including a turnkey 660 MW power plant for an AI workload customer, with contracted capacity phasing in through 2029 under 10-15 year terms [1]
  2. Margin expansion accelerating as high-margin Power Solutions grows as a percentage of revenue — Q2 profit margin of 11.5% up from 6.69% in Q1, with Power Solutions EBITDA margin of 61% demonstrating the segment's economics [1]
  3. AI data center power demand significantly exceeding grid capacity, creating structural demand for distributed on-site generation that Solaris serves at 3,100 MW scale — the $2B financing provides capital to execute toward 3.1 GW and $1.7B revenue by 2029 [2][3]
Key Risks
  1. Financial leverage risk: D/E ~146% with $2B in new financing and operating cash flow covering only 20% of total debt — any slowdown in AI data center buildout or contract delays would pressure the highly leveraged balance sheet [1][5]
  2. Share dilution: 87.6% increase in shares outstanding over one year and $2.8M in insider selling over 3 months — existing shareholders' ownership has been nearly halved, and further equity issuances may be needed to fund capacity expansion [5]
  3. Technology disruption risk from nuclear SMRs, utility grid modernization, or renewable/battery solutions reducing demand for natural gas distributed power over the 10-15 year contract horizon — plus continued exposure to cyclical oilfield services through the legacy logistics segment [2]
