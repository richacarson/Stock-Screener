Company Profile
Industrials ·Advanced Battery Technology ·NYSE ·United States
SES AI Corporation is a pioneer in lithium-metal battery technology and AI-powered materials discovery, founded in 2012 by Dr. Qichao Hu in the basement of MIT. The company is the only Li-Metal battery maker to enter joint development agreements with three major automotive OEMs (General Motors, Honda, and Hyundai). SES AI operates three revenue-generating business units: energy storage systems (ESS), drones, and advanced materials. The company reaffirmed 2026 revenue guidance of $30-35M with a target of 1M NDAA-compliant cells per year starting Q4 2026. Q2 2026 revenue was $5.1M (+40% YoY) with gross margin improving to 22.3%. Cash position of $177.68M provides a multi-year runway against $7.72M in total debt.

Investment Thesis
SES AI at $1.05 per share ($339M market cap) offers asymmetric upside exposure to lithium-metal battery technology with a strong net cash position of $170M providing multi-year runway. The company is the only Li-Metal battery maker with three major OEM JDAs (GM, Honda, Hyundai), validating the technology's potential [3]. Revenue is growing rapidly (Q2 2026 +40% YoY) with improving gross margins (22.3% vs 11.3% two quarters prior), and management reaffirmed $30-35M FY2026 guidance with three revenue-generating business units now operational [1]. The pivot to NDAA-compliant cells for defense applications (1M units/year target starting Q4 2026) addresses a high-demand, premium-margin market where supply chain security commands pricing power. The AI-powered materials discovery platform represents a potential competitive moat in accelerating battery chemistry iteration [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 56 (ALIGNED) is the highest in this batch.
The bear case centers on commercial execution risk and cash burn trajectory. SES AI lost $72.71M in the trailing twelve months against only $21.92M in revenue, consuming approximately $50M in net cash annually at current rates [4]. At this burn rate, the $170M cash position provides roughly 3 years of runway before additional capital raises would be required, likely diluting existing shareholders (369M shares already outstanding). The $1.05 stock price reflects deep market skepticism about the company's ability to scale from $30M in revenue to commercial relevance. The pivot away from large-format EV batteries — the company's original thesis and the basis for the GM/Honda/Hyundai JDAs — suggests the core EV battery opportunity may be further away than initially promised. Competition from well-capitalized Chinese battery giants (CATL, BYD) and domestic rivals (QuantumScape, Solid Power) with deeper pockets creates significant market risk.
