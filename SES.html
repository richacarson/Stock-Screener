Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Auto Parts ·NYQ ·United States
SES AI Corporation develops AI-enhanced lithium-metal and lithium-ion rechargeable battery technologies for electric vehicles, urban air mobility, drones, robotics, and energy storage systems. Founded in 2012 by Dr. Qichao Hu at MIT, the company is the only Li-Metal battery maker with three major OEM joint development agreements (GM, Honda, Hyundai). SES AI operates three revenue-generating units: ESS, drones, and advanced materials. Q2 2026 revenue was $5.1M (+44% YoY) with gross margin of 22.3%, and management reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guidance of $30-35M.
215 Employees ·www.ses.ai

Investment Thesis
SES AI at $0.88 per share ($328M market cap) offers asymmetric upside exposure to lithium-metal battery technology and AI-driven materials discovery, with FY2026 revenue guidance of $30-35M reaffirmed despite Q2 missing consensus ($5.1M vs $5.84M expected) [1]. The company is the only Li-Metal battery maker with three major OEM JDAs (GM, Honda, Hyundai), validating the technology's potential [3]. Revenue grew 44% YoY in Q2 with gross margin improving to 22.3%, and the pivot to ESS (~65% of FY2026 revenue), drone batteries, and electrolyte materials creates diversified near-term revenue streams while preserving the long-term Li-Metal optionality. The near-zero D/E ratio and cash reserves provide multi-year runway against annual losses. The Inspire Impact Score of 56 (ALIGNED) reflects strong marks across energy management, materials efficiency, and product sustainability.
The bear case centers on commercial execution risk, cash burn, and governance concerns. SES AI is deeply unprofitable with a -289.6% profit margin and TTM losses vastly exceeding $23.5M in TTM revenue [4]. A securities class action covering January 2025 to March 2026 alleges the company overstated business prospects and created an appearance of revenue through related-party Molecular Universe purchases, though these allegations remain unproven [4]. The C-sample step with OEM partners has been put on hold, and management shifted focus toward electrolyte materials 'until the market recovers' — effectively acknowledging that the flagship EV Li-Metal battery business is delayed indefinitely [1]. At $0.88, the stock trades as a penny stock vulnerable to extreme volatility, potential exchange delisting below $1.00, and limited institutional interest. Competition from well-capitalized Chinese battery giants (CATL, BYD) and domestic rivals (QuantumScape, Solid Power) with deeper resources creates significant risk that SES's technology lead erodes before commercialization.
