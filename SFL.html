Company Profile
Industrials ·Marine Shipping ·NYSE ·Bermuda
SFL Corporation is a maritime and offshore asset owning and chartering company operating in oil transportation, dry bulk, container shipping, car carriers, and drilling rigs. As of Q1 2026, the fleet includes approximately 73 vessels across tankers, bulkers, container vessels, car carriers, and energy assets, with a $3.7 billion charter backlog and two-thirds of contracts tied to investment-grade counterparties.

Investment Thesis
SFL Corporation offers a 7.17% dividend yield backed by a $3.7 billion charter backlog with two-thirds contracted to investment-grade counterparties, providing multi-year cash flow visibility [1]. Q1 2026 delivered a 400% EPS surprise ($0.20 vs $0.04 forecast) on $174.5M revenue beating consensus by $6.6M, signaling the beginning of an earnings recovery from the post-supercycle trough [1]. The dividend was raised from $0.20 to $0.22/quarter, restoring growth after the painful 2024 cut, and the new Hercules drilling contract adds $170M to backlog starting Q1 2027 [1]. At approximately $12.28 per share with 22 consecutive years of dividend payments, SFL offers an attractive income vehicle if the charter rate recovery materializes. Maritime shipping remains a critical global infrastructure play structurally immune to AI disruption [3].
The bear case remains significant despite the Q1 beat. SFL is still unprofitable on a trailing basis with negative ROE and profit margins, and the 'Q1 2026 EPS Loss Tests Bullish Profitability Narratives' headline underscores that one good quarter does not establish sustained earnings power [1]. D/E of 279% with $2.77B debt against $281.68M cash creates substantial refinancing and interest rate risk [3]. The 5-year dividend growth CAGR remains negative following the $0.27 to $0.20 cut, and while the recent raise to $0.22 is encouraging, it is still 18.5% below the pre-cut level. Revenue growth is flat as charter rates normalize from the 2022-2024 supercycle, with analyst forecasts of $666.52M for FY2026 reflecting only modest improvement [1]. The Hemen Holding/John Fredriksen control structure means minority shareholders have limited influence over capital allocation decisions, and the stock has declined 23% over the past 52 weeks [3].

  3. Cyclical charter rate normalization after the 2022-2024 shipping supercycle could suppress revenues for multiple years, as demonstrated by the 24.3% annual revenue decline through 2025 [1]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): SFL's business is fundamentally physical — owning and chartering tankers, bulk carriers, container ships, car carriers, and drilling rigs across 73 vessels. AI cannot replace physical maritime transportation or offshore drilling infrastructure [2]. Shipping demand is driven by global trade volumes and energy markets, not information processing vulnerable to AI substitution. AI-driven economic growth and data center expansion actually increase demand for physical goods transportation and energy infrastructure. Regulatory complexity including IMO standards, flag state requirements, and port state controls adds structural barriers that AI cannot circumvent. AI Opportunity (0/1): SFL generates no AI-related revenue. The company could potentially benefit from AI in route optimization, predictive maintenance, and charter pricing analytics, but has not deployed or monetized any AI capabilities [2].
