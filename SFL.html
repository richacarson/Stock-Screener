Company Profile
Industrials ·Marine Shipping ·NYSE ·Bermuda
SFL Corporation is a maritime and offshore asset owning and chartering company that owns and operates a diversified fleet including 17 tankers, 21 container vessels, 7 car carriers, 2 dry bulk carriers, and 2 drilling rigs. The company charters vessels on medium and long-term contracts across oil transportation, dry bulk, containers, car transportation, and offshore drilling. Formerly Ship Finance International, SFL rebranded in 2019 and is incorporated in Bermuda with only 24 corporate employees. Q2 2026 revenue reached $201M with net income of $34M ($0.25/share), driven by Suezmax spot rates surging to $133,000/day. Charter backlog stands at $3.8B with ~65% from investment-grade counterparties. Market cap is approximately $1.9B.

Investment Thesis
SFL Corporation offers a 6.5% dividend yield supported by a $3.8B charter backlog (~65% from investment-grade counterparties), providing multi-year revenue visibility in an otherwise cyclical maritime sector [1]. Q2 2026 showed significant momentum with revenue surging to $201M from $174.5M in Q1, driven by Suezmax spot TCE increasing to $133,000/day from $54,000/day. Net income of $34M ($0.25/share) in Q2 represents a meaningful improvement, and adjusted EBITDA of $130M (+20% QoQ) demonstrates the operating leverage inherent in the asset-heavy model. Full-year 2026 revenue is forecast at $666.5M with $742.5M expected for 2027. The Inspire Impact Score of 48 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions is noteworthy for a shipping company, suggesting responsible operational practices.
The bear case is substantial. SFL's trailing P/E of 57.3x reflects depressed GAAP earnings of just $0.24/share, while the $0.88 annual dividend represents a payout ratio exceeding 360% of trailing earnings — funded from cash flow that includes depreciation, not sustainable earnings [1]. D/E of 230% reflects the asset-heavy model's dependence on debt financing for vessel acquisitions. Revenue declined 19% from $891.6M (2024) to $718.8M (2025) before the current partial recovery, highlighting extreme cyclicality. The company has no innovation capability (score 1/10), no meaningful purpose beyond dividend distribution (inspiration 1/10), and only 24 employees — it is a financial vehicle, not an operating business. The dividend has been cut multiple times (2009, 2014, 2020-2021), offering no consistency for income investors seeking reliability. ROE of 6.2% and profit margin of 9.0% are well below quality thresholds, and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of ~1.5% fails to keep pace with inflation. Maritime shipping is inherently cyclical, and the Q2 2026 Suezmax spot rate surge ($133,000/day) is unsustainable — rates will revert, and earnings will follow.
