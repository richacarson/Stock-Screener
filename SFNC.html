Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NASDAQ ·United States
Simmons First National Corporation is a Mid-South based financial holding company headquartered in Pine Bluff, Arkansas. Its principal subsidiary, Simmons Bank, operates more than 220 branches across Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas. With total assets of $24.8B and total deposits of $19.7B as of Q2 2026, Simmons provides comprehensive financial solutions including commercial and consumer lending, mortgage services, trust, and wealth management. The company has paid cash dividends for 117 consecutive years.

Investment Thesis
Simmons First National offers a compelling value and income proposition: Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.50, revenue of $248.6M (up 16% YoY), and net interest income of $200.6M (up 17% YoY) demonstrate strengthening operating performance [1]. At approximately 11x forward P/E with a 3.8% dividend yield backed by 117 consecutive years of payments — one of the longest dividend streaks in American banking — SFNC provides rare institutional durability [4]. The bank's strong capital position (tangible common equity ratio of 8.91%), 4% annualized deposit growth, and 3% annualized loan growth indicate healthy organic momentum. New CEO Jay Brogdon's focus on organic growth, talent acquisition, and innovation positions Simmons for the next phase of its evolution.
The bear case centers on modest profitability, regional concentration, and leadership transition risk. ROE of 7.69% is below the 15% target and modest for a $24.8B asset bank, reflecting the lingering impact of the Q3 2025 impairment and conservative capital deployment [1]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.50 missed the consensus estimate of $0.52, and total revenue of $248.6M came in below the forecast of $250.7M, suggesting execution is not yet pristine under the new leadership team [1]. The mid-South footprint across Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas leaves Simmons exposed to regional economic volatility in agriculture and energy-dependent markets. D/E of 659% — while standard for banks — creates structural leverage risk. Rising fintech competition from AI-powered lending platforms could pressure margins in the smaller markets where Simmons competes, and the company has shown no visible AI strategy to counter this threat.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued net interest margin expansion through 2026 as fixed-rate assets reprice higher and deposit costs stabilize, with Q2 2026 already showing 17% YoY NII growth to $200.6M [1]
  2. Successful CEO transition to Jay Brogdon, who brings continuity to the growth strategy with new emphasis on organic growth, talent acquisition, and innovation [2]
  3. Potential M&A activity as banking consolidation accelerates — Simmons' strong 8.91% tangible common equity ratio provides balance sheet capacity for accretive deals in the mid-South market
Key Risks
  1. Recurrence of asset quality issues — the Q3 2025 impairment charge of $562.8M that wiped out TTM earnings could signal deeper problems in acquired loan portfolios or mark-to-market exposures [1]
  2. Regional economic concentration across six mid-South states leaves the bank vulnerable to localized downturns in agriculture, energy, and real estate — sectors that disproportionately affect Simmons' markets
  3. No visible AI or digital banking strategy to counter rising fintech competition from SoFi, Upstart, and Chime in consumer lending and deposits — the 220+ branch network could become a cost disadvantage as digital adoption accelerates
