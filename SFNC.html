Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NASDAQ ·United States
Simmons First National Corporation is a Mid-South bank holding company headquartered in Pine Bluff, Arkansas, operating Simmons Bank across Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas. The company offers commercial and consumer banking, mortgage, trust, and wealth-management services through roughly 2,900 employees. Simmons has paid a cash dividend for 117 consecutive years and increased it in 2026 for the 15th consecutive year, even after absorbing a large one-time Q3 FY2025 charge that produced a rare quarterly net loss; Q2 2026 revenue grew 16.4% YoY to $245.6M.

Investment Thesis
Simmons First National offers value and income: Q2 2026 revenue grew 16.4% YoY to $245.6M, diluted EPS came in at $0.46 (Q1 2026: $0.47), and the stock trades around 10x forward earnings with a 3.7% dividend yield backed by 117 consecutive years of dividend payments -- one of the longest streaks in American banking -- plus a 15th consecutive year of dividend increases in 2026 [1][3]. The bank's six-state Mid-South footprint, disciplined underwriting culture, and the orderly transition to new CEO Jay Brogdon in January 2026 provide leadership continuity heading into the next growth phase. Forward P/E of 10.1x versus a 20x target reflects a discount that could close as the market looks past the one-time Q3 FY2025 charge toward the bank's now-recovering quarterly earnings trajectory [4].
The bear case centers on a large, unresolved credibility gap: Simmons recorded a $562.8M net loss on -$572.7M of GAAP revenue in Q3 FY2025 alone, dragging trailing-twelve-month ROE to roughly -9.9% and profit margin to roughly -218% even though the bank has been solidly profitable in every surrounding quarter [1][4]. Whatever combination of securities-portfolio repositioning and/or merger-related credit provisioning drove that charge, it was not transparently explained in the research available, and any recurrence would be a serious red flag for a bank of this size. Simmons also shows no visible AI or digital-banking strategy to counter fintech lenders in consumer credit, and its geographic concentration in six mid-South states leaves it exposed to regional agricultural and energy-sector cycles. The Inspiration and Innovation scores are weak because Simmons discloses little evidence of community investment, employee retention, or insider conviction beyond boilerplate ESG language.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued net interest margin recovery and loan growth following Q2 2026's 16.4% YoY revenue growth to $245.6M, as the one-time Q3 FY2025 charge rolls out of trailing-twelve-month comparisons [1]

  3. 117-year dividend streak with a 15th consecutive year of increases in 2026 supports continued modest annual dividend growth and could attract income-focused capital at the current 3.7% yield [3]
Key Risks
  1. Lack of transparency around the Q3 FY2025 $562.8M net loss / -$572.7M revenue quarter -- without clear disclosure of its drivers, investors cannot rule out recurrence of large one-time charges tied to securities repositioning or acquired-loan credit quality [1][4]
  2. Geographic concentration across Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas leaves earnings exposed to regional agricultural and energy-sector downturns
  3. No visible AI or digital-banking strategy to counter fintech and AI-powered lenders competing for consumer and small-business credit, even as management acknowledges 'ongoing disruption and consolidation' in the industry [2]
