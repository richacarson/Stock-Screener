Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NYSE ·United States
Sweetgreen operates a chain of company-owned, fast-casual restaurants serving salads, warm bowls, and protein plates built around seasonal, locally sourced ingredients, with a large share of sales through its digital channels. Founded in 2007 in Washington, D.C., it is deploying its Infinite Kitchen automated assembly line across new and existing restaurants. Co-founder Jonathan Neman is Chair and CEO.

Investment Thesis
Sweetgreen remains the defining brand in healthy fast casual. Store growth is still adding revenue (Q2 2026 revenue up 3.8% to $192.7M, with net new openings adding $34.8M in H1) despite negative comps [2][3]. The Infinite Kitchen gives a structural path to better labor efficiency and throughput, the balance sheet is largely lease-driven (D/E 76%), and the $186M Spyce sale added cash while Sweetgreen kept deployment rights [5][8]. The shares have rebounded to $10.31 from a 50-day average of $8.16; if the food-safety-driven traffic shock fades and comps turn positive in 2027, operating leverage could rebuild restaurant-level margins toward the high-teens levels of 2025 [2].
The bear case is that the brand has been damaged and unit economics have reset lower. Same-store sales fell 12.8% in Q1 and 6.2% in Q2 2026, restaurant-level margin compressed from 18.9% to 13.1%, and 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut from +$1-6M to a loss of $23-27M [1][2]. Forward earnings remain negative (forward P/E -15), and TTM GAAP profit relies on a one-time divestiture gain [8]. Leadership churn, including the co-founder's departure, weak employee sentiment, and a premium price point in a value-seeking consumer environment add execution risk.   3. Premium salad pricing exposed to consumer trade-down and to faster-growing Mediterranean fast-casual rivals such as CAVA [2]
