Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NASDAQ ·Brazil
Sigma Lithium is a lithium producer operating the Grota do Cirilo mine in Minas Gerais, Brazil — the largest hard-rock lithium operation in the Americas. The company produces high-purity spodumene concentrate using its proprietary 'Triple Zero Green Lithium' process (zero chemicals, zero tailings dams, zero carbon in production). Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $55M with 35,400 tonnes produced (+52% QoQ) at an all-in cash cost of $668/tonne. The company plans to expand capacity from 270,000 to 770,000 tonnes by yearend 2027. Market cap is approximately $1.38B.

Investment Thesis
Sigma Lithium at 12.91x forward earnings ($1.38B market cap) is a rapidly scaling lithium producer with improving unit economics — Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $55M with 47% EBITDA margin (company record), 60% gross margin, and 26% net margin, demonstrating that the Grota do Cirilo mine is reaching profitability as production scales [1]. All-in cash costs fell to $668/tonne with plant costs at $401/tonne and CIF costs at $452/tonne — competitive with Tier 1 global lithium operations. The company exceeded Q2 production guidance by 6% and delivered 35,400 tonnes (+52% QoQ), showing operational momentum [1]. The planned capacity expansion from 270,000 to 770,000 tonnes by yearend 2027 offers near-tripling of production capacity, and analysts maintain a consensus Strong Buy with an average price target of $19.75 (61% upside) [6]. Sigma repaid 25% of total debt over the past year, improving balance sheet health.
The bear case centers on commodity price risk, ESG liabilities, and financial fragility. Lithium spodumene prices remain depressed at ~$1,000-1,200/tonne versus the 2022 peak of $8,000/tonne, and a prolonged lithium price downturn could render the capacity expansion uneconomic and pressure the already-thin equity base (D/E 337%) [6]. TTM metrics are deeply negative — ROE of -31.64% and profit margin of -19.34% — though recent quarters show improvement. A Brazilian court ruling involving $10M in potential legal liability for waste disposal allegations, combined with university researchers documenting 'serious social and environmental harm' including human rights violations and environmental licensing irregularities, creates significant legal and ESG risk [4]. A temporary suspension of mining and processing operations in Minas Gerais (with restart expected within one to two weeks) caused a 4.5% stock decline, demonstrating operational vulnerability [1]. Net insider selling of ~$10M over 90 days, including CEO Ana Cabral's sale of 146,706 shares, weakens the conviction case [5]. The current ratio of 0.34 raises near-term liquidity concerns despite debt reduction progress.
