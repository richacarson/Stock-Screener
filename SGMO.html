Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·OTC (formerly NASDAQ) ·United States
Sangamo Therapeutics was a clinical-stage biotechnology company focused on translating ground-breaking science into genomic medicines using its proprietary zinc finger nuclease (ZFN) gene editing technology. The company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on June 23, 2026, after years of clinical setbacks including the collapse of its Pfizer hemophilia A gene therapy partnership in December 2024. Core technology assets were sold to Eli Lilly for approximately $50M. The stock was delisted from Nasdaq on September 27, 2026, and now trades on OTC markets under the symbol SGMOQ.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)

The collapse of the Pfizer partnership, the inability to secure alternative funding, and the ultimate Chapter 11 filing represent a company that was playing to win a single clinical bet rather than building an enduring enterprise. Sinek would view Sangamo as a cautionary tale: a technically excellent cause undermined by finite execution.

Investment Thesis
There is no bull case for Sangamo Therapeutics. The company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on June 23, 2026, was delisted from Nasdaq on September 27, 2026, and has sold substantially all of its core ZFN gene editing technology to Eli Lilly for approximately $50M [1][3]. FY2025 revenue of $39.6M declined 32% YoY with a net loss of $122.9M, and cash stood at just $20.9M at year-end — insufficient to fund operations for even one quarter. The equity is likely worthless or near-worthless as secured creditors take priority in the bankruptcy estate distribution.
Sangamo's failure traces directly to the collapse of its Pfizer hemophilia A gene therapy partnership in December 2024, which eliminated the company's most promising revenue pathway and sent shares down 50% in a single session [2]. The subsequent inability to secure alternative funding, combined with disappointing clinical results in mucopolysaccharidosis programs and declining collaboration revenues, created an irreversible liquidity crisis. Even the $50M Eli Lilly asset sale merely satisfies creditors; equity holders face near-certain total loss. Any remaining OTC trading volume is purely speculative and carries extreme risk of complete capital destruction.
Key Catalysts
  1. None — the company is in Chapter 11 bankruptcy with core assets already sold to Eli Lilly for approximately $50M; the equity is in liquidation mode with no operational catalysts remaining [3]
  2. Theoretical upside only if bankruptcy estate distributions exceed creditor claims sufficiently to return value to equity holders, which is unlikely given liabilities of $100M-$500M against diminished assets
  3. ZFN technology may continue to generate value through Eli Lilly's development programs, but Sangamo shareholders have no economic participation in that upside post-asset sale
Key Risks
  1. Total equity wipeout — with liabilities between $100M-$500M and core assets sold for ~$50M, secured and unsecured creditors take priority, leaving common shareholders last in the distribution waterfall [1][3]
  2. OTC trading illiquidity — stock trades under SGMOQ on OTC markets with negligible volume, making any position effectively impossible to exit at a meaningful price
  3. Securities litigation risk — a law firm announced an investigation into potential federal securities law violations related to the December 2024 Pfizer exit disclosure, which could further encumber the bankruptcy estate [2]
