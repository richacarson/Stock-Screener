Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Care Facilities ·NASDAQ ·United States
Surgery Partners owns and operates a network of more than 200 surgical facilities across 30 states, including ambulatory surgery centers, surgical hospitals, multi-specialty physician practices, and urgent care facilities. The company provides non-emergency surgical procedures in orthopedics, ophthalmology, gastroenterology, and general surgery. Founded in 2004 and headquartered in Brentwood, Tennessee. Q1 2026 revenue of $810.9M, reaffirmed full-year guidance of $3.35-3.45B.

Investment Thesis
Surgery Partners is one of the largest ambulatory surgery center operators in the U.S. with 200+ facilities across 30 states, delivering Q1 2026 revenue of $810.9M (up 4.5% YoY) with same-facility revenue growth of 4.4% [1]. Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance of $3.35-3.45B revenue and $530M+ adjusted EBITDA, projecting at least 4.2% organic EBITDA growth [1]. The company benefits from the secular tailwind of inpatient-to-outpatient migration driven by cost pressures and payer preferences. FCF of $208M (10.2% FCF yield) provides adequate debt service capacity, and the $15.73 stock price at 0.61x P/S represents a discount to historical averages. The musculoskeletal service line is showing favorable trends that could accelerate same-facility growth in the second half of 2026 [1].
The bear case centers on leverage, profitability, and credibility. Surgery Partners carries $4.04B in total debt against $182M cash, resulting in an Altman Z-Score of 0.86 — flagging increased bankruptcy risk [5]. GAAP net income has been negative every year since 2021 despite revenue growing from $2.2B to $3.3B, with ROE of just 2.75% and profit margin of -2.28% [5]. The securities investigation by Levi & Korsinsky following CEO Evans' misleading FY2025 margin guidance raises governance concerns, and 11.67% short interest reflects market skepticism [4]. Revenue growth has decelerated sharply from 14% (2022) to 6% (2025) to 4.5% (Q1 2026), and management acknowledged $8M in headwinds from Medicaid rate reductions and new provider taxes [1]. Glassdoor reviews (41% recommend, 2.7/5 culture) suggest organizational dysfunction that could impair the operational execution needed to service the $4B debt load. Forward P/E of 31.9x with PEG of 2.34 is expensive for a company that has never generated positive GAAP net income.
Key Catalysts
  1. Musculoskeletal service line showing 'favorable trends' in Q1 2026 that could accelerate same-facility revenue growth above the 4.4% achieved in the quarter [1]
  2. Continued inpatient-to-outpatient migration driven by CMS policy supporting ambulatory procedures and payer cost pressures expanding Surgery Partners' addressable market [2]
  3. Margin expansion path from operational efficiency and favorable payer mix evolution, targeting $530M+ adjusted EBITDA in 2026 versus $508M in 2025 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent GAAP losses despite $3.3B revenue — $4.04B debt load generates interest expense that consumes operating income, with Altman Z-Score of 0.86 flagging elevated bankruptcy risk [5]
  2. Securities investigation by Levi & Korsinsky following CEO Evans' misleading margin expansion guidance for FY2025, combined with 11.67% short interest, creates legal liability and governance overhang [4]
  3. Revenue growth deceleration from 14% (2022) to 4.5% (Q1 2026) coincides with $8M in new headwinds from Medicaid rate cuts and provider taxes, limiting organic growth capacity to service the leveraged balance sheet [1]
