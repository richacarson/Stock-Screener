Company Profile
Technology ·Solar ·NASDAQ ·United States
Shoals Technologies Group designs and manufactures electrical balance-of-system (EBOS) products for utility-scale solar, battery energy storage (BESS), and EV charging, including its patented big-lead-assembly (BLA) cabling, wire harnesses, combiners, and junction boxes. Its products are deployed on more than 20 GW of solar systems globally, with manufacturing in Tennessee. Brandon Moss is CEO.

Investment Thesis
Shoals is in a sharp volume recovery: Q1 2026 revenue rose about 75% to $140.6M and Q2 rose 47% to $163.4M, H1 revenue reached $303.9M (+59%), and management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $600-640M revenue and $118-132M adjusted EBITDA [1][2][3]. Record backlog and awarded orders of $801M (+19% YoY) give more than five quarters of visibility, BESS demand tied to AI-driven power needs diversifies the mix, and the balance sheet is conservative (D/E 38%) [2]. At a 13.6x forward P/E with an implied PEG near 0.4, Infrastructure scores 8/10, and the patented BLA architecture keeps a cost-of-installation advantage for EPCs [8].
The bear case is earnings quality and culture. Despite 59% H1 revenue growth, H1 net profit fell about 13% to $11.8M, and trailing EPS growth is -12.5%, because tariffs, freight, the facility transition, and a class-action settlement absorbed the operating leverage [1][3]. GAAP profit margin is only 5.4% and ROE 5.3% [8]. The 2023 shrinkback defect ($59.5M of warranty charges) and the related securities suit damaged trust, while competition from rival EBOS suppliers, including one led by Shoals' founder, pressures pricing [6][7]. Utility-scale solar remains exposed to tariff, tax-credit, interconnection, and interest-rate policy shifts.
Key Catalysts
  1. Conversion of the record $801M BLAO into 2026-2027 revenue, with Q3 2026 results confirming the $600-640M full-year revenue guide [2][3]
  2. BESS and data-center-linked storage demand broadening Shoals' mix beyond utility-scale solar [2]
  3. Margin normalization once the new facility ramps and tariff and freight headwinds ease, bringing adjusted EBITDA (Q2 +28% to $31.6M) back in line with revenue growth [1][3]
Key Risks
  1. Profit lagging revenue: H1 2026 net income fell about 13% despite 59% revenue growth because of tariffs, freight, facility transition costs, and litigation settlements [1][3]
  2. Product-quality and legal overhang from the 2023 wire-insulation shrinkback defect ($59.5M of warranty charges) and related securities litigation [6][7]
  3. U.S. solar policy and tariff risk (tax-credit changes, permitting, interconnection queues) that could slow backlog conversion, plus price competition from rival EBOS suppliers [4]
