Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NASDAQ ·United States
Shoals Technologies Group provides electrical balance of systems (EBOS) solutions for the energy transition market, including utility-scale solar, battery energy storage systems (BESS), and EV charging infrastructure. Products include cable assemblies, wire harnesses, combiner boxes, junction boxes, and monitoring solutions. The company manufactures at facilities in Portland, Tennessee and serves utility-scale solar developers, EPCs, and asset owners globally.

Investment Thesis
Shoals Technologies is executing a strong revenue recovery in 2026, with Q2 revenue of $163M (up 47% YoY) and Q1 revenue of $140.6M (up 74.9% YoY) driven by robust U.S. utility-scale solar demand and expanding BESS segment [1]. Full-year guidance of $600-640M (+30% at midpoint) with adjusted EBITDA of $118-132M demonstrates improving scale economics. Record backlog and awarded orders of $801.4M with $700M expected to ship by Q2 2027 provides exceptional revenue visibility [1]. Forward PEG of 0.48 and Forward P/E of 17.91 suggest the stock is attractively valued relative to its growth trajectory, and D/E of 37.9% indicates a healthy balance sheet. The energy transition tailwind — utility-scale solar, BESS, and EV charging — provides a multi-decade secular growth driver.
The bear case centers on organizational instability and competitive deterioration. Since the 2021 IPO, Shoals has reportedly lost both margin and market share, with former CEO now heading a directly competing company and recruiting Shoals' best employees [4]. Glassdoor 2.3/5 rating with 29% recommendation rate signals deep cultural problems that new CEO Brandon Moss must address [4][6]. Profit margin of 5.42% remains thin despite 47% revenue growth, raising questions about pricing power and competitive positioning. The solar EBOS market faces potential tariff uncertainty, permitting delays, and financing cost headwinds that could disrupt the current demand cycle. Additionally, Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of 19.3% ($31.6M on $163M revenue) is recovering but still below the company's peak profitability levels, suggesting the margin and share erosion has not fully reversed.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record backlog of $801.4M with $700M expected to ship by Q2 2027 provides strong revenue visibility and supports the FY2026 guidance of $600-640M representing 30% growth at the midpoint [1]
  2. BESS segment expansion as battery energy storage deployment accelerates alongside solar installations, diversifying Shoals' revenue mix beyond utility-scale solar EBOS [1]
  3. New CEO Brandon Moss's operational turnaround — if Moss successfully stabilizes talent retention, restores market share trajectory, and improves profit margins, re-rating potential is significant from a Forward PEG of 0.48 [6]
Key Risks
  1. Continued market share erosion to the former CEO's competing company and other EBOS providers — if talent hemorrhaging continues, Shoals' product quality and delivery capability could deteriorate [4]
  2. Profit margin of 5.42% remains thin despite strong revenue growth, suggesting pricing pressure or cost structure issues that could limit earnings power even as revenue scales past $600M [3]
  3. U.S. solar installation cycle risk — tariff policy changes, permitting delays, or rising financing costs could slow utility-scale solar deployment and compress Shoals' backlog conversion timeline [1]
