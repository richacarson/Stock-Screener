Company Profile
Materials ·Steel ·NYSE ·Brazil
Companhia Siderurgica Nacional (CSN) is Brazil's largest fully integrated steel producer and one of the largest in Latin America. The company operates across five segments: Steel, Mining (via CSN Mineracao), Cement, Logistics, and Energy. Founded in 1941 as a state enterprise and privatized in 1993 under controlling shareholder Benjamin Steinbruch (CEO since 2002). Q2 2026 revenue rose 5.7% YoY to R$11.3B with operating profit surging 53%, but the company swung to a deeper net loss of R$773.1M as BRL/USD exchange-rate driven financial expenses overwhelmed operating gains. Steinbruch has initiated asset sales to reduce leverage from 3.14x EBITDA.
29,000 Employees ·www.csn.com.br

Investment Thesis
CSN faces a convergence of structural challenges that make the current risk-reward profile unfavorable. Q2 2026 showed operational improvement — revenue rose 5.7% YoY to R$11.3B, gross profit surged, and operating profit increased 53% — but the company swung to a deeper net loss of R$773.1M as BRL/USD exchange-rate driven financial expenses overwhelmed operating gains [1]. The ADR trades at just $0.99 with a market cap of approximately $1.3B, reflecting deep investor skepticism about the deleveraging path. D/E of 307% and 3.14-3.36x net debt/EBITDA create severe refinancing risk in an elevated Brazilian interest rate environment [6]. Steinbruch has hired advisers to sell the steel business, logistics stake, and cement unit to reduce leverage — an unprecedented restructuring that signals the severity of the balance sheet crisis [3]. The EBITDA margin of 23.5% shows the operating businesses generate cash, but the debt burden consumes it.
The path to recovery requires simultaneous deleveraging and margin improvement, which is exceptionally difficult in a weak global steel pricing environment with persistent Chinese overcapacity flooding the Brazilian market [1]. CSN's environmental liabilities represent underappreciated risk — 4+ million tons of steel slag stored 50 meters from the Paraiba do Sul River affecting 9 million people's water supply, ongoing daily fines since 2019, and black dust complaints linked to respiratory hospitalizations could escalate into material remediation costs or operational restrictions [5]. Steinbruch's controlling shareholder position means minority ADR holders at $0.99/share have minimal governance influence over capital allocation decisions. The planned asset sales could crystallize value if executed at fair prices, but fire-sale conditions or buyer disputes could further erode equity value. Key Catalysts
  1. Successful execution of planned asset sales (steel business, logistics, cement) at fair valuations, which could reduce leverage below 2.5x net debt/EBITDA and potentially unlock sum-of-the-parts value for remaining mining operations [3]

  2. Environmental liabilities from 4+ million tons of steel slag near the Paraiba do Sul River, ongoing daily fines, and air pollution violations could result in material remediation costs, operational shutdowns, or regulatory penalties that further strain the balance sheet [5]
  3. Asset-sale program under financial duress risks fire-sale pricing — potential buyers of the steel business, logistics, and cement units know Steinbruch is selling from a position of weakness, compressing achievable valuations and leaving equity holders with diminished residual value [3]
