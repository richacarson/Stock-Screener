Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NYSE ·Brazil
Companhia Siderurgica Nacional (CSN) is a vertically integrated Brazilian steel and mining conglomerate operating across steel production, iron ore mining, cement manufacturing, and logistics. The company's Casa de Pedra iron ore mine is one of Brazil's largest, and its Presidente Vargas steelworks is a major flat steel producer. CSN trades as an ADR on NYSE with TTM revenue of approximately $8B (BRL), though it currently reports negative profit margins of -5.8% and negative ROE of -12.3% amid commodity price headwinds and elevated debt.

Investment Thesis
CSN at $1.16 is a deep value play on Brazilian industrial recovery, trading at 9.75x forward earnings and 0.32 PEG with a vertically integrated commodity portfolio spanning steel, iron ore, cement, and logistics. The stock prices in significant distress — negative ROE, negative margins, and 357% D/E — creating asymmetric upside if management successfully deleverages and commodity prices recover [1][2].
The bear case is substantial. D/E of 357% creates existential risk if interest rates remain elevated or commodity prices decline further. Negative ROE of -12.3% and negative profit margins indicate the business is destroying value at current commodity levels. Brazil's macro environment adds currency and political risk. Controlling shareholder Benjamin Steinbruch's financial engineering history raises governance concerns. The ADR at $1.16 reflects single-digit stock price risk, and no dividend is currently paid despite a historical track record [1][2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Iron ore price recovery above $120/ton would dramatically improve profitability given CSN's low-cost Casa de Pedra mine, with operating leverage amplifying earnings through the vertically integrated structure [1]
  2. Deleveraging progress through non-core asset sales and cash flow generation could reduce D/E below 300%, unlocking equity value currently suppressed by balance sheet concerns [1]
  3. Brazilian infrastructure spending under government programs drives domestic steel and cement demand, with CSN positioned as a vertically integrated supplier [1]
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 357% creates existential risk — further iron ore or steel price declines could trigger covenant breaches or require dilutive equity raises that destroy shareholder value [1][2]
  2. Negative ROE of -12.3% and -5.8% profit margin indicate the business is operationally unprofitable at current commodity levels, with no clear timeline for margin recovery [2]
  3. Controlling shareholder governance risk — Benjamin Steinbruch's history of complex related-party transactions and financial engineering raises concerns about minority shareholder alignment [1]
