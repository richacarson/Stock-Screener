Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Steel ·NYSE ·Brazil
Companhia Siderurgica Nacional (CSN) is a Brazilian integrated steel producer and diversified conglomerate operating through Steel, Mining, Logistics, Cement, and Energy segments. The company produces flat and long steel products, extracts and processes iron ore, operates railways and port concessions, manufactures cement, and generates renewable energy. With TTM revenue of approximately R$45.1B and a negative profit margin of -5.8%, CSN is headquartered in Sao Paulo, Brazil. The company reported a net loss of R$773M in Q2 2026 and R$1.3B in H1 2026, primarily due to elevated financial expenses from currency volatility and high leverage at 3.49x net debt/EBITDA [1][2].

Investment Thesis
CSN at $1.16 with a $1.5B market cap trades at just 0.64x book value and 9.75x forward earnings, reflecting deep distress pricing for a diversified Brazilian industrial with R$45.1B in TTM revenue [1]. The new CEO Fabio Schvartsman (former Vale CEO) brings credibility to the deleveraging mandate, and UBS rated the leadership change positively from a governance perspective [3]. The company plans $2.9B in asset divestitures from cement and infrastructure to reduce net debt/EBITDA from 3.49x toward 3.0x by year-end 2026. Adjusted EBITDA of R$2.77B in Q2 2026 with a 23.4% margin suggests the operating businesses generate reasonable cash flow when isolated from financial costs, and free cash flow turned positive at R$808M in Q2 [1][2].
The bear case is severe and multifaceted. CSN reported net losses of R$1.3B in H1 2026 driven by financial expenses from R$54.4B in total debt and Brazilian real depreciation [1][2]. D/E of 357% exceeds every reasonable threshold, ROE is -12.3%, and profit margin is -5.8% — the company is destroying shareholder value on a GAAP basis. The new CEO Schvartsman carries personal legal risk as a reinstated defendant in the Brumadinho dam criminal case, and a separate police inquiry into CSN officials adds governance uncertainty [3]. Brazilian macroeconomic headwinds — high interest rates, currency volatility, and political uncertainty — directly impact CSN's highly leveraged balance sheet. Iron ore prices face downward pressure from slowing Chinese construction demand, while Brazilian steel margins are compressed by imports. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -2.0% reflects structural demand challenges, and the stock's decline from $2.20 to $1.16 (52-week range) suggests the market sees further deterioration ahead [2][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Deleveraging through $2.9B in planned asset divestitures from cement and infrastructure businesses, targeting net debt/EBITDA reduction from 3.49x to 3.0x by year-end 2026, which would meaningfully reduce financial expenses that are driving quarterly losses [1][3]
  2. CEO transition to Fabio Schvartsman professionalizes management and may unlock operational efficiencies — UBS rated the change positively from governance and execution standpoints, and Schvartsman's Vale experience brings large-scale mining operational expertise [3]
  3. Brazilian infrastructure spending cycle — government infrastructure programs and potential housing construction demand could support steel volumes at favorable domestic pricing, with Q2 2026 already showing 5.7% revenue growth [1]
Key Risks
  1. Unsustainable leverage at 3.49x net debt/EBITDA with R$54.4B in total debt — financial expenses are consuming operating cash flow and driving consecutive quarterly net losses, and any delays in the $2.9B divestiture plan would prolong balance sheet distress [1][2]
  2. CEO legal risk — Schvartsman remains a defendant in the Brumadinho dam criminal case after a Brazilian federal appeals court reinstated his status, and a separate police inquiry involves CSN officials, creating governance uncertainty and potential reputational damage [3]
  3. Brazilian macroeconomic exposure — high domestic interest rates, real depreciation, and political uncertainty directly amplify CSN's financial costs on its BRL-denominated debt, while slowing Chinese steel demand and iron ore price weakness compress top-line growth potential [1][2]
