Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·South Korea
SK Telecom is South Korea's dominant wireless carrier (~50% mobile market share) and a subsidiary of the SK Group conglomerate. The company operates through cellular services, fixed-line telecommunications, and a rapidly growing AI/data center business. SKT is aggressively pivoting to an 'AI Native' strategy under CEO Jung Jai-hun, with AI data center revenue surging 92.5% YoY in Q2 2026. Q2 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 4.36 trillion (+0.5% YoY), with operating income surging 67.3% YoY to KRW 566 billion and net income up 459.8% YoY to KRW 466 billion.

Investment Thesis
SK Telecom at 13.34x forward earnings offers a compelling value play on Korea's AI infrastructure build-out, with AI data center revenue surging 92.5% YoY in Q2 2026 and operating income up 67.3% as the company's pivot from traditional telecom to AI infrastructure generates material profitability improvements [1][2]. The conservative balance sheet (D/E 66%) supports continued investment in data centers and submarine cables, while the 2.7% dividend yield provides income during the transition. Forward EPS of $2.69 versus trailing EPS of $1.37 implies near-doubling of earnings, making the PEG ratio of 0.44 one of the most attractive valuations in the telecom-to-AI infrastructure space. Net income surged 459.8% YoY in Q2 2026, suggesting the earnings inflection is already materializing [1].
The bear case centers on structural profitability constraints and execution risk. Profit margin of 4.38% and ROE of 5.19% reflect the capital-intensive, low-return nature of telecom infrastructure that may persist even as AI revenue scales [4]. Revenue growth of just 0.5% YoY demonstrates that AI data center surges have not yet moved the consolidated revenue needle — AI remains a small portion of KRW 4.36 trillion quarterly revenue [1]. The massive earnings growth came off a severely depressed base (trailing P/E of 26.2x vs forward P/E of 13.3x) rather than representing sustainable earnings power. Dividend growth has stalled in USD terms with no consecutive years of growth, and KRW/USD currency risk adds volatility for ADR investors. The competitive landscape in AI data centers features well-capitalized hyperscalers against which SKT has limited differentiation beyond Korea's domestic market.
