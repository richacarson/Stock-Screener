Company Profile
Real Estate ·Retail REIT - Outlet Centers ·NYSE ·United States
Tanger Inc. is the only publicly traded REIT focused exclusively on upscale, open-air outlet and lifestyle shopping destinations. The company owns and operates 39 shopping centers across 22 U.S. states and Canada, encompassing approximately 14 million square feet of gross leasable area. Under CEO Stephen Yalof, the company has repositioned from 'Tanger Factory Outlet Centers' to 'Tanger Inc.,' expanding into open-air lifestyle destinations with food, entertainment, and experiential retail concepts.
700 Employees ·www.tanger.inc

Investment Thesis
Tanger delivered strong Q2 FY2026 results with revenue of $156.4M (up 11.2% YoY), Core FFO of $0.64/share (up 10.3% YoY), and same-center NOI growth of 3.5%, while raising FY2026 FFO guidance to $2.45-$2.52/share [1]. Occupancy of 96.6%, tenant sales of $487/sq ft (up 4.7%), and blended re-tenanting spreads of 28.4% demonstrate pricing power and demand durability in the outlet REIT format. The company's strategic transformation under CEO Yalof — from pure outlet operator to experiential destination curator — has driven the stock up 21% over the past year to a $4.7B market cap. The 3.18% dividend yield with FAD payout of 64%, 7% annual dividend increase, and $1B available liquidity provide income investors with a growing, well-covered distribution [1][3].
The primary risk is valuation. At 34.97x forward GAAP P/E and 16.1x Price/FFO, SKT trades at a premium to the retail REIT sector despite a 4.42 PEG ratio that implies limited growth-adjusted value [3]. The D/E of 278% ($1.95B total debt, 4.7x net debt/EBITDA) is within REIT norms but leaves limited margin for error if interest rates remain elevated — the interest coverage ratio of 4.5x is adequate but not robust [1]. The COVID-era dividend cut means consecutive payment history is only 5 years, and the GAAP payout ratio of 114.77% reflects the structural earnings-versus-cash-flow disconnect in REITs that can spook generalist investors. Rising cremation rates in the retail landscape — specifically e-commerce penetration accelerating via AI-powered shopping assistants — could pressure outlet tenants over time, though the value-driven outlet format has historically been more resilient than enclosed malls [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued same-center NOI growth of 2.75-4.25% (FY2026 guidance) with blended re-tenanting spreads of 28.4% demonstrating pricing power and potential for FFO acceleration beyond $2.52/share guidance
  2. Strategic acquisition pipeline of mixed-use lifestyle centers (Levis Commons at 8.5% cap rate) diversifying beyond pure outlet format and expanding TAM for the 'Tanger Inc.' brand
  3. Demographic tailwind from high consumer demand for value-priced, experiential shopping — tenant sales of $487/sq ft and 96.6% occupancy suggest sustained consumer engagement despite macro uncertainty
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 34.97 and PEG of 4.42 reflect premium valuation that leaves minimal margin of safety — any deceleration in same-center NOI growth below the 2.75% guidance floor could trigger meaningful multiple compression
  2. D/E of 278% with $1.95B total debt and 4.5x interest coverage creates refinancing risk in a sustained high-rate environment — any significant increase in borrowing costs would compress FFO and pressure dividend coverage
  3. Only 5 consecutive years of dividend payments post-COVID cut (down from 29 years pre-COVID) means the dividend track record must be rebuilt, and GAAP payout ratio of 114.77% may concern income-focused investors unfamiliar with REIT FFO-based coverage metrics
