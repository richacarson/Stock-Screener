Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NASDAQ ·United States
SKYX Platforms Corp. provides safe-smart platform technologies enabling light fixtures, ceiling fans, and other electrically wired products to be installed as simple plug-and-play into a ceiling's electrical outlet box. The company holds 100+ U.S. and global patents and operates an e-commerce platform of 60+ websites selling smart home products. Formerly SQL Technologies Corp. (name changed June 2022), SKYX was incorporated in 2004. Revenue reached a record $92M in FY2025 with Q1 2026 at $22M (+10% YoY), though the company remains unprofitable with a market cap of approximately $167M.
73 Employees ·www.skyx.com

Investment Thesis
SKYX Platforms is a speculative bet on a patented safe-smart electrical platform with 100+ patents and a growing e-commerce distribution network of 60+ websites. Revenue reached a record $92M in FY2025 with 8 consecutive quarters of YoY growth, and Q1 2026 continued the streak at $22M (+10% YoY) [1][2]. Gross profit improved to $28M in 2025 (+13% YoY) and operating cash usage declined 27% to $13M, suggesting incremental progress toward break-even [2]. The company has assembled a high-profile advisory board (former CEOs of Home Depot and Disney Parks, former Microsoft AI leader) and launched AI-driven e-commerce software targeting 30% conversion improvement [3]. At $1.23/share and $167M market cap, the stock prices in significant execution risk but offers upside if the platform technology achieves mass adoption in residential and commercial construction.
The bear case is substantial. SKYX has been developing its core technology for over 20 years without achieving profitability — net loss margin of -34.3%, ROE of -271%, and continued cash burn of $13M annually raise questions about viability [4]. The 73-person company has a D/E ratio of 185.7% and relies on high-profile advisors rather than a deep operational bench. Revenue of $92M comes primarily from acquired e-commerce websites selling third-party products, not from the patented plug-and-play ceiling technology that is supposedly the core innovation. Forward P/E is negative (company is unprofitable), and analysts project continued losses through 2026 with estimated revenue of $116.5M [1]. The stock trades at $1.23 — penny stock territory — with a market cap of only $167M and narrow daily liquidity of just $1.3M, making it unsuitable for institutional investors. The Technology has not been adopted by major homebuilders, electrical contractors, or big-box retailers at meaningful scale despite two decades of development.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-driven e-commerce software achieving the projected 30% conversion rate improvement across 60 websites, potentially driving meaningful revenue acceleration from the Q1 2026 pace of $22M quarterly [3]
  2. Adoption of the patented plug-and-play ceiling technology by a major homebuilder, retailer (Home Depot, Lowe's), or electrical contractor chain, which could transform revenue trajectory [3]
  3. Continued operating cash burn reduction from $18M (2024) to $13M (2025) toward cash flow breakeven, which would validate the business model and potentially re-rate the stock [2]
Key Risks
  1. Chronic unprofitability after 20+ years of development — net margin of -34.3% and annual cash burn of $13M with only $167M market cap raises dilution risk if the company needs additional capital raises [4]
  2. Core patented platform technology has failed to achieve mass market adoption despite two decades and 100+ patents, with revenue driven primarily by acquired e-commerce sites rather than the differentiating technology [2][3]
  3. Penny stock status ($1.23) and narrow daily liquidity ($1.3M) create significant price volatility risk and limit institutional ownership, while D/E of 185.7% adds financial fragility [4]
