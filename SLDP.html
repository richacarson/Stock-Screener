Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Auto Parts ·NASDAQ ·United States
Solid Power is an industry-leading developer of sulfide-based all-solid-state battery technology for electric vehicles and other applications. The company's proprietary solid electrolyte replaces the liquid electrolyte in traditional lithium-ion batteries, offering potential advantages in energy density, safety, and cost. Founded from CU Boulder research in 2011 and headquartered in Louisville, Colorado. Key partnerships include BMW, Samsung SDI (joined 2025), SK On, and Ford. The company ended Q2 2026 with $419.3M in liquidity and no debt. CEO John Van Scoter (since June 2023) is targeting electrolyte pilot line commissioning by end-2026 at 75 MT capacity, with a Korean commercial-scale partnership under exploration for up to 500 MT annually.
230 Employees ·www.solidpowerbattery.com

Solid Power holds a differentiated position in the solid-state battery race with proprietary sulfide-based solid electrolyte technology backed by a landmark three-way collaboration with BMW and Samsung SDI (joined 2025) plus partnerships with SK On and Ford [3][6]. Q1 2026 EPS of -$0.06 beat the -$0.13 estimate by 54%, and the SK On site-acceptance milestone validates manufacturing capability. The company ended Q2 2026 with $419.3M in liquidity (bolstered by $121.3M raised via registered direct offering) and no debt, providing a multi-year cash runway [1][2]. The electrolyte pilot line commissioning by end-2026 targeting 75 MT capacity, with Korean commercial-scale partnership exploration for up to 500 MT annually, provides the clearest commercialization pathway in the company's history. If solid-state batteries achieve commercial viability, the TAM exceeds $100B by 2035 as EV adoption accelerates, and SLDP's electrolyte-first strategy positions it as a key supplier rather than a cell manufacturer competing against Samsung SDI and CATL [4].
Revenue declined 49% YoY in Q1 2026 to $3.1M and was negative in Q2 due to a non-cash reversal, underscoring the lumpiness and near-zero nature of milestone-dependent income — full-year consensus is just $13M against approximately $120M in annual operating expenses [1][2]. The stock trades at $2.34, down approximately 73% from its 52-week high of $8.86, reflecting deep market skepticism about commercialization timelines. Solid-state battery technology faces formidable competition from Toyota's sulfide batteries targeted for 2027-2028 production, QuantumScape's lithium-metal approach, and Samsung SDI's own internal solid-state program, any of whom could achieve scale first [4]. The $121.3M equity raise in H1 2026 was dilutive to existing shareholders, and additional capital raises are likely needed if the commercialization timeline extends beyond 2029-2030. The single-technology risk is existential — if sulfide-based solid electrolyte proves commercially unviable due to manufacturing challenges, cycling degradation, or cost competitiveness issues, Solid Power has no fallback business model.
