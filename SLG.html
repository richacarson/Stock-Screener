Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
SL Green Realty Corp. is Manhattan's largest office landlord, a fully integrated REIT focused on acquiring, managing, and maximizing the value of Manhattan commercial properties. As of June 2026, SLG held interests in 54 buildings totaling 30.6 million square feet. The company is led by CEO and Chairman Marc Holliday (CEO since 2004, Chairman since 2019), whose term was recently extended through July 2028. SLG raised 2026 FFO guidance to $5.60-$5.90/share, reflecting strong leasing activity with occupancy trending toward 95% by year-end 2026. One Vanderbilt Avenue serves as the crown jewel of the portfolio, generating incremental fees and income.

Investment Thesis
SL Green at $52.94 offers a contrarian bet on the Manhattan office recovery, with Q2 2026 showing strong operational momentum — revenue of $278.9M (+27.3% YoY), FFO guidance raised 26% to $5.60-$5.90/share, same-store occupancy trending toward 95% by year-end 2026, and same-store NOI growth projected to exceed 10% in 2027 [1]. The 4.67% dividend yield provides income while waiting for the recovery thesis to fully materialize. Marc Holliday's 22-year CEO tenure and commitment to trophy Midtown properties (One Vanderbilt generating incremental fees) position SLG to benefit from the flight-to-quality trend in Manhattan office [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of 60 (ALIGNED) with 17 positive ESG attributions reflects strong environmental stewardship (GRESB 92, ENERGY STAR Partner of the Year) [3].
The bear case is structural: GAAP metrics reflect an impaired business model. Negative ROE (-3.6%), negative profit margin (-16.7%), D/E of 142.6%, and a GAAP payout ratio of 153.75% all score at or near zero in quantitative screens [4]. The dividend has been cut for 4 consecutive years (from $6.27 in 2021 to $2.84 annualized in 2025), a -10.5% 5yr CAGR that signals financial stress rather than growth. While FFO metrics tell a more favorable story ($5.60-$5.90/share), GAAP losses persist because high depreciation and interest expense consume revenue. The 5yr revenue CAGR of ~1% reflects flat-to-declining top-line through the hybrid work disruption, and structural questions about office demand remain unresolved — AI-enabled remote work, AI-driven reduction in knowledge worker headcount, and tenant downsizing trends could limit the occupancy recovery's durability. SLG's leverage (D/E 142.6%) creates vulnerability in a rising rate environment, and the negative forward P/E reflects market skepticism about the earnings trajectory. President Sitomer's $2.3M insider sale in August 2026 does not signal conviction from the executive suite at current prices.

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Physical real estate in Manhattan is inherently protected from direct AI substitution — buildings cannot be digitized, and Class A office space in premier locations retains value through physical scarcity and prestige [2]. Regulatory complexity (NYC zoning, building codes), capital intensity, and location monopoly create structural moats. However, AI-enabled remote and hybrid work tools (video conferencing, AI collaboration platforms, AI-automated knowledge work) represent a real and ongoing headwind for office demand, particularly for non-trophy space. AI may also reduce certain categories of office employment (legal research, financial analysis, content creation), shrinking the tenant base over time. SLG's focus on trophy Midtown properties mitigates this risk, as the highest-quality space has proven most resilient to hybrid work trends, but the structural pressure on office utilization is not fully resolved [1].
