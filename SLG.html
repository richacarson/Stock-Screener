Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
SL Green Realty Corp. is Manhattan's largest office landlord, a fully integrated real estate investment trust (REIT) that acquires, manages, and repositions commercial office properties in New York City. The company holds interests in approximately 28.1 million square feet of wholly owned and joint-venture office space across midtown Manhattan. Founded in 1997 and led by CEO Marc Holliday since 2004.

Investment Thesis
SL Green offers deep value exposure to Manhattan's accelerating office recovery. Q2 2026 revenues grew 9.1% to $264.0M with same-store cash NOI up 4.3% and occupancy reaching 94.7%, while FFO guidance was raised 27% to $5.60-$5.90/share from $4.40-$4.70 [1]. At $52/share and ~9x P/FFO, the stock trades at a substantial discount to the 15-year office REIT average. The 6.25% dividend yield with 5 consecutive years of growth provides attractive income, and management projects occupancy reaching 95% by year-end 2026 with same-store NOI growth exceeding 10% in 2027 [1]. Q2 leasing of 445,161 sq ft with 18% rent mark-to-market increases confirms demand recovery for premium Manhattan office space. The $1.5B Opportunistic Debt Fund provides fee income diversification [2].
The bear case centers on structural headwinds to office demand and financial fragility under GAAP. The company reported a net loss of $26.5M ($0.38/share) in Q2 2026 — GAAP losses persist despite strong FFO due to depreciation, impairment, and interest expense on a highly leveraged 135% D/E balance sheet [1][4]. The dividend was cut 60%+ from its 2019 peak of ~$3.64/share to $2.47, and the GAAP payout ratio suggests limited room for increases without sustained FFO growth. Remote and hybrid work remain structural threats — even as trophy buildings recover, AI-driven productivity improvements could further reduce per-employee office space demand. Interest rate sensitivity is acute: SL Green's leveraged model means higher-for-longer rates directly compress FFO and limit financial flexibility. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -2.6% reflects a shrinking portfolio base from dispositions, and near-total concentration in Manhattan office real estate creates geographic and property-type risk with no diversification [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. FFO guidance raised 27% to $5.60-$5.90/share with management projecting 95% occupancy by year-end 2026 and same-store NOI growth exceeding 10% in 2027, driven by 18% rent mark-to-market increases on replacement leases [1]
  2. 346 Madison Avenue development integrating AI-ready digital infrastructure and carbon-capture technology to capture emerging demand from technology and AI tenants, with a leading AI company signing a 98,420 sq ft 10-year lease [2]
  3. SLG Opportunistic Debt Fund surpassing $1.5B in capital raised (vs $1.0B target), providing fee-based revenue diversification and reducing reliance on cyclical office rental income [2]
Key Risks
  1. Structural decline in office demand from remote/hybrid work trends compounded by AI-driven productivity gains that could permanently reduce per-employee office space needs, undermining the Manhattan recovery narrative [2]
  2. Financial fragility — D/E of 135%, negative GAAP ROE, persistent net losses, and a 60%+ dividend cut since 2019 reflect balance sheet stress that limits flexibility if the recovery stalls or rates remain elevated [4]
  3. Near-total geographic and property-type concentration in Manhattan office real estate with no diversification, making SLG uniquely vulnerable to NYC-specific economic, regulatory (Local Law 97), or tax policy changes [1]
