Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services (Private Education Lending) ·NASDAQ ·United States
SLM Corporation (Sallie Mae Bank) originates and services private education loans for students and families and funds them with retail deposits such as high-yield savings, money market accounts and CDs. It is headquartered in Newark, Delaware. In Q3 2026 it posted a GAAP net loss of $32M (-$0.19 per share) as provisions rose with record originations growth: private education loan originations rose 15% to $3.4B and graduate originations rose 119%. Management cut FY2026 EPS guidance to $1.95-2.05 from $3.10-3.20 [1][2][6].

Investment Thesis
After falling from a 52-week high of $31.06 to $17.80, Sallie Mae trades at about 5.9x FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.00, roughly 1.46x book, and a 2.92% dividend yield covered about 5x by TTM earnings [3][4][7]. Q3 2026 showed accelerating demand rather than shrinking franchise value: private education originations rose 15% to $3.4B, graduate originations rose 119%, and much of the GAAP loss reflects upfront CECL provisions booked on new loans [1][6]. TTM ROE is still 23.6%, and consensus expects EPS to recover to $3.67 by FY2028 if credit normalizes [3][4].
The bear case is credibility and credit. FY2026 EPS guidance was cut 36% to $1.95-2.05 after a second consecutive miss, and net charge-offs of $90M were tied to misaligned third-party debt-resolution practices. That raises questions about collections oversight and potential regulatory scrutiny [2][6]. Leverage is high (D/E of 285%), revenue fell 21% YoY against a quarter that included a loan sale, and rapid expansion into graduate loans brings new underwriting risk at scale [1][3]. The dividend has been flat at $0.13 per quarter since December 2024 [7].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q4 2026 delivery against the reset $1.95-2.05 EPS guide, which would restore management credibility after two consecutive misses
  2. Seasoning of the record 2026 origination cohort, with graduate loans up 119%, converting upfront provisions into net interest income in 2027-2028
  3. Resumption of loan sales or buybacks at about 1.46x book once capital and credit stabilize
Key Risks
  1. Credit deterioration and charge-offs from third-party debt-resolution practices, with potential CFPB or regulatory scrutiny
  2. Underwriting risk from rapid scaling in graduate lending, a newer segment growing 119% YoY
  3. Leverage of 285% D/E and sensitivity of deposit funding costs to rate moves
