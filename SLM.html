Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
SLM Corporation (Sallie Mae) originates and services private education loans to students and their families in the United States. The company also provides retail deposit accounts including high-yield savings, money market accounts, and certificates of deposit. Separated from Navient in 2014, Sallie Mae operates as the nation's leading dedicated private student lender. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $1.54 (+10% YoY) prompted a full-year guidance raise, while Q2 2026 showed GAAP EPS of $0.29 with private education loan originations growing 4.5% YoY. CEO Jon Witter has led the company since April 2020. Market cap ~$4.3B.

Investment Thesis
SLM Corporation trades at a deeply discounted 9.4x forward earnings with elite profitability — 38.1% net margin, 33.8% ROE, and a PEG ratio of 0.62 [4]. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $1.54 (+10% YoY) prompted management to raise full-year guidance to $3.10-$3.20, and private education loan originations grew 4.5% in Q2 2026 with full-year growth guidance of 12-14% [1]. The Parent Loan product launch ahead of the federal Grad PLUS phase-out creates an immediate addressable market expansion, while the Adtalem healthcare financing partnership opens a high-value graduate education segment. The stock has declined from $25.77 to $21.64 since last screen, creating a more attractive entry point with a 2.40% dividend yield and a 20% payout ratio providing massive dividend growth runway from the current $0.52 annual rate.
The bear case centers on competitive disruption from AI-enabled fintech lenders, structural leverage, and weak revenue growth. SoFi Technologies and other digital-native platforms are leveraging AI-driven underwriting, alternative data sources, and superior mobile experiences to capture private student loan market share from Sallie Mae's traditional franchise. D/E of 253% is structurally elevated for a lending institution and leaves limited buffer against credit cycle deterioration — Q2 2026 EPS of $0.29 (down from $0.32 YoY) signals potential earnings softness. Net interest income has been essentially flat ($375.4M vs $374.9M in Q1), and the 2% 5-year revenue CAGR reflects limited organic growth in the mature private student lending niche. Net charge-offs guidance of $345-385M represents meaningful credit losses. Federal student loan policy uncertainty — including potential Grad PLUS reinstatement or new direct lending programs — could reverse the demand tailwind from the current phase-out [1].
