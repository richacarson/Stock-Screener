Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Soleno Therapeutics is a biopharmaceutical company focused on rare diseases. Its lead product VYKAT XR (diazoxide choline controlled-release) is the first FDA-approved treatment for hyperphagia in Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS), a rare genetic condition. VYKAT XR launched in Q2 2025 and generated $190.4M in its first year. In April 2026, Neurocrine Biosciences announced a definitive agreement to acquire Soleno for $53.00/share ($2.9B). Multiple class action lawsuits have been filed alleging safety data misrepresentation. Headquartered in Redwood City, California.

Investment Thesis
Soleno Therapeutics is a rare disease success story now trading as a merger arbitrage at the $53 Neurocrine acquisition price. VYKAT XR's commercial trajectory is exceptional: Q1 2026 delivered $94.6M revenue with $31.4M net income (33.2% margin), accelerating from $190.4M in the first year of sales with only 12% U.S. market penetration [1][3]. Consensus estimates project $467M in 2026 revenue with $4.04 EPS. The 98.6% gross margin and rapidly expanding operating margins validate the rare disease franchise quality. The Neurocrine tender offer at $53/share ($2.9B) provides a defined exit with limited downside to the deal price, offering institutional-quality merger arbitrage return if the acquisition closes as expected [2].
The risks are substantial and concentrated. Multiple class action lawsuits allege Soleno systematically misrepresented DCCR safety data, including concealing evidence of excess fluid retention, pre-diabetes, and potential heart failure in clinical trial participants between March and November 2025 [4]. Sustainalytics rates Soleno's ESG material risk management as 'Weak' [5]. These allegations could affect FDA post-marketing requirements, create product liability exposure, and potentially complicate or threaten the Neurocrine acquisition. If the $53/share deal fails due to litigation, regulatory concerns, or antitrust issues, the stock could decline significantly given the litigation overhang and Soleno's single-product dependency — there are no disclosed pipeline candidates beyond VYKAT XR. The company's agreement to sell after just one year of commercialization, while at a premium, may undervalue the long-term potential of a first-in-class orphan drug with only 12% market penetration [2][4].
