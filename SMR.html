Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYQ ·United States
NuScale Power is the first and only company to receive U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) Standard Design Approval for a small modular reactor (SMR), positioning it as the global leader in advanced nuclear technology. The company's VOYGR SMR power plant design uses a scalable modular approach with individual 77 MWe modules for flexible deployment. Under CEO John Hopkins (since 2012), NuScale is targeting AI data center power supply and international deployment opportunities. Q2 2026 revenue collapsed to $75K (down 99% YoY) as the Fluor FEED Phase 2 contract concluded, but the company maintains $1.9B in liquidity and TTM net losses of approximately $356M [1].

Investment Thesis
NuScale Power at $7.72 ($3.16B market cap) is the highest-conviction speculative bet on nuclear energy for the AI era — the first and only NRC-approved SMR design provides a regulatory moat that competitors cannot replicate in less than 5-7 years [2]. The AI data center buildout creates secular demand for clean baseload electricity that nuclear is uniquely positioned to provide, with NVIDIA's Jensen Huang endorsing nuclear as AI's future and Microsoft, Google, and Amazon announcing nuclear procurement strategies [2]. NuScale is in discussions with a majority of tier-one hyperscalers, and the company's $1.9B liquidity provides 4-5 years of runway to convert regulatory advantage into commercial contracts [1]. CEO Hopkins' 14-year tenure provides leadership continuity rarely seen in pre-revenue companies, and the Inspire Impact Score of 57 (ALIGNED) makes NuScale one of the most faith-compatible clean energy investments available.
The bear case is severe. NuScale generated $75K in Q2 2026 revenue (down 99% YoY) as the Fluor contract concluded, with TTM net losses of $356M — the company is burning approximately $900K per day with no near-term path to profitability [1]. The $3.16B market cap on near-zero revenue implies an extreme speculative premium pricing in decades of growth that may never materialize. The CFPP cancellation in 2023 destroyed the company's first commercial project and remains the primary evidence of execution risk — NRC design approval has not translated into a single operating reactor. Competitors including GE-Hitachi (BWRX-300), X-energy, Oklo, and Rolls-Royce SMR are advancing their own designs, and NuScale's first-mover advantage narrows with each year without commercial deployment. Nuclear energy faces persistent ESG debate — while carbon-free, concerns over uranium mining, waste disposal (86,000 metric tons at 75 U.S. sites), and safety create regulatory and public perception headwinds [3]. The stock has declined 86% from its 52-week high of $57.42, and the $1.9B liquidity runway is finite, potentially requiring dilutive capital raises if the commercial timeline extends.
