Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Furnishings, Fixtures & Appliances ·NYSE ·United States
SharkNinja is a product design and technology company operating two global brands — Shark (cleaning and home environment) and Ninja (cooking, food preparation, and beauty). The company designs, markets, and distributes innovative household appliances across cleaning, cooking, food preparation, beauty, and home environment categories, selling through traditional retail, e-commerce, and direct-to-consumer channels across 38 international markets.

Investment Thesis
SharkNinja is a rare consumer products growth story, delivering Q1 2026 revenue of $1.41B (up 15.6% YoY) with adjusted EPS of $1.09 beating the $0.84 consensus by 30%, leading management to raise full-year guidance to 11.5-12.5% net sales growth and $6.00-$6.10 adjusted EPS [1]. The company combines a 49.0% gross margin (exceptional for appliances) with a conservatively managed balance sheet (D/E 26%, near-net-cash) and strong capital efficiency (29.4% ROE) [5]. At 17.2x forward earnings with a 1.37 PEG ratio, the stock is attractively valued for sustained double-digit growth. SharkNinja is structurally protected from AI disruption as a physical products company while actively deploying AI in product features and operations through the $1M Jailbreak program [3]. International sales surging 31.6% to $497M in Q1 demonstrate the large untapped global growth runway.
The bear case centers on execution sustainability and macro sensitivity. Tariff exposure remains the most immediate risk — SharkNinja manufactures primarily in China, creating direct exposure to trade policy changes that could compress margins on a product line with 10.7% profit margin [1]. Earlier reports of workplace culture pressure and high-intensity expectations raise questions about talent retention at scale, though more recent employee sentiment appears improved [3]. Revenue growth guidance of 11.5-12.5% for 2026 represents deceleration from 15.7% achieved in 2025, suggesting the law of large numbers is beginning to constrain growth. The company has a limited public track record (IPO July 2023) and has not been tested through a significant economic downturn as a public entity. Consumer discretionary spending risk remains elevated in a softening macro environment, and the strategy of competing on value inherently caps margin expansion [5].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): SharkNinja's core business — designing, manufacturing, and selling physical household appliances — is well-protected from AI disruption [1]. Vacuum cleaners, blenders, air fryers, and coffee makers are physical products that AI cannot replace or substitute. The company's competitive advantage lies in product design, supply chain management, and brand positioning — domains where AI serves as a tool rather than a threat. Consumer appliance demand is driven by household formation, replacement cycles, and category expansion rather than information workflows that AI can automate. The physical-world nature of the business creates a structural barrier to AI disruption. AI Opportunity (1/1): SharkNinja is actively investing in AI-enabled product features for robotic vacuums, air purifiers, and coffee makers, with the $1M 'Jailbreak' AI program generating 1,500+ employee ideas and Salesforce Agentforce deployment providing AI-powered customer service [3]. AI product features shipping in H2 2026 represent tangible revenue opportunity.
