Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NYSE ·United States
Snap Inc. operates Snapchat, a camera-first visual communication platform serving 493 million daily active users and 971 million monthly active users globally. The company generates revenue primarily through advertising, supplemented by the rapidly growing Snapchat+ subscription service ($316M quarterly revenue, +85% YoY). Snap also develops augmented reality products including Spectacles smart glasses and the Lens Studio AR development platform. Q2 2026 revenue reached $1.6B (+19% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $250M, though the company remains unprofitable on a GAAP basis with a net loss of $164M.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 4 /10
Snap is a young company (founded 2011) that has not yet demonstrated the multi-decade consistency that defines infinite game players. CEO Evan Spiegel has shown flashes of long-term thinking — rejecting Facebook's $3B acquisition offer in 2013, investing in AR when skeptics doubted it — but the company's frequent strategic pivots, declining employee count, and fear-driven culture suggest a reactive organization playing quarter to quarter. The 9% headcount reduction, Glassdoor ratings trending down, and stock down 28% over 52 weeks reflect an organization under pressure that is optimizing for survival rather than playing to keep playing.

However, this cause reads more as product positioning than a vision of a future state so appealing that people sacrifice to advance it [1]. Spiegel has not consistently used it to drive strategic decisions — the company has pivoted from ephemeral messaging to Stories to Discover to AR to subscriptions to AI chatbots, suggesting the cause is flexible enough to justify anything. Investment Thesis
Snap at 7.72x forward earnings offers a deeply discounted entry into a recovering social media platform with accelerating revenue growth — Q2 2026 revenue of $1.6B (+19% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA surging 505% to $250M demonstrates the company's operating leverage is finally materializing [1]. The Snapchat+ subscription business ($316M quarterly, +85% YoY) diversifies beyond advertising and now represents ~20% of revenue with higher margin and recurring characteristics. Free cash flow turned decisively positive at $407M in H1 2026 (+194% YoY), and the PEG ratio of 0.26 prices in minimal growth despite consensus 3-year EPS growth forecasts of 33.5% [3]. With 493M DAU and 971M MAU, Snap maintains a differentiated reach into the 13-34 demographic that advertisers value, and North America ARPU growth of 23% suggests pricing power remains intact.
The bear case is substantial. Snap remains GAAP unprofitable (-$311M TTM net loss) driven by $1B+ in annual stock-based compensation that dilutes earnings and represents a real economic cost to shareholders [1]. ROE of -15.58% and profit margin of -4.90% reflect a business that has never generated sustained GAAP profitability since its 2017 IPO — nine years of losses erode credibility that 'this quarter is different.' Competitive pressure from TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts continues to fragment advertiser budgets and user attention, with Snap's 5% DAU growth lagging peers. The stock is down 28% over 52 weeks, and the 14-year-old company has cycled through multiple strategic directions without establishing a durable competitive position. The Inspire Impact Score of -88 (MISALIGNED) with negative attributions including Exploitation and Sexually Explicit content severely limits allocation for faith-based investors. Dual-class share structure gives Spiegel and Murphy effective control with ~99% voting power, creating governance risk with no accountability mechanism.

AI Risk Protection (5/9): Snap's core advertising business faces meaningful AI exposure on multiple fronts. AI-powered creative tools from Meta, Google, and emerging startups could reduce the value of Snap's differentiated camera platform by making AR effects and visual content creation ubiquitous across platforms.
