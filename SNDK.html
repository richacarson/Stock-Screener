Company Profile
Technology ·Computer Hardware ·NMS ·United States
Sandisk Corporation is a pure-play NAND flash memory and storage solutions company spun off from Western Digital in February 2025, led by CEO David Goeckeler. The company designs, manufactures, and sells flash memory chips, SSDs, and removable storage products through a vertically integrated model and a joint-venture flash fabrication partnership with Kioxia extending through December 2034. Sandisk has executed a strategic pivot toward high-capacity enterprise and data center storage for AI workloads, with datacenter revenue growing 437% YoY to $5.15B in FY2026 and now representing 38% of the portfolio [1]. FY2026 revenue surged to $20.25B (+175% YoY) with non-GAAP gross margin reaching 84.6% in Q4 FY2026, and Q1 FY2027 guidance of $10.3-10.8B implies continued hypergrowth [1]. Incorporated in 2024 and headquartered in Milpitas, California.

Investment Thesis
Sandisk at $1,692 is the highest-torque pure-play on the AI-driven NAND flash storage supercycle, with FY2026 revenue surging 175% YoY to $20.25B, datacenter revenue jumping 437% to $5.15B, and Q4 non-GAAP gross margin reaching 84.6% [1]. The balance sheet is pristine (D/E 1.3%, $4.8B cash against only $201M debt), profitability is exceptional (91.6% ROE, 56.5% profit margin), and despite the massive stock rally the forward P/E of 6.42 remains deeply attractive because consensus earnings estimates have risen even faster than the share price [1]. The August 2026 Investor Day guided to mid-to-high-teens revenue growth through fiscal 2030 at approximately 80% gross margin and 75% operating margin, with the New Business Model (contract-based supply agreements covering 50% of bits in FY2027) aiming to reduce the historical cyclicality of the NAND business [2]. Q1 FY2027 guidance of $10.3-10.8B implies acceleration from an already massive base.
The bear case is classic memory-cycle reversal risk. NAND is a historically brutal boom-bust commodity business, and the Sandisk flash operation lost money in FY2023-2025 before this AI-driven upswing [1]. Analysts have flagged two failure scenarios: a slowdown in AI infrastructure capex, or oversupply emerging faster than consensus expects as competitors (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) expand capacity, either of which could trigger sharp NAND contract-price declines and multiple compression characteristic of past memory downcycles. The Inspiration score of 2/10 reflects the company's brief 18-month history as an independent entity with no distinctive mission, community programs, retention data, or insider buying identified [3]. The 10/10 Infrastructure score, while mechanically accurate, is a lagging indicator of cyclical peak profitability rather than a structural moat — investors should expect significant volatility if AI capex growth decelerates or the NAND supply-demand balance normalizes. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) is neutral for faith-based investors.
