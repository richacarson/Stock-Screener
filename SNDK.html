Company Profile
Technology ·Computer Hardware ·NASDAQ ·United States
Sandisk Corporation is a NAND flash memory and storage solutions company spun off from Western Digital in February 2025, led by CEO David Goeckeler. The company designs and manufactures flash memory chips, SSDs, and removable storage products, with a strategic pivot toward high-capacity enterprise and data center storage for AI workloads under its 'Flash for AI' initiative. Sandisk operates a joint-venture flash fabrication partnership with Kioxia and reported explosive growth through fiscal 2026 as AI-driven data center demand triggered a structural NAND supply shortage and pricing surge, with FY2026 revenue of roughly $20.3B, up approximately 175% year-over-year.

Investment Thesis
Sandisk is the highest-torque public pure-play on the AI-driven NAND flash shortage, with FY2026 revenue surging roughly 175% YoY to ~$20.3B as Datacenter revenue jumped 233% on hyperscaler AI storage demand [1]. The balance sheet is pristine (D/E of just 5.9%, $10.2B equity), profitability is exceptional (91.64% ROE, 56.46% profit margin), and despite the stock's 550%+ year-to-date rally the forward P/E (~9.5x) and PEG (0.17) remain inexpensive because consensus 2026 EPS estimates have risen even faster than the share price, with one analyst modeling $90-100 in 2026 EPS [4]. Management's August 2026 Investor Day guided to mid-to-high-teens bit and revenue growth through 2030 at ~80% gross margin, and the joint 9th-generation QLC flash platform with Kioxia extends the technology roadmap directly into AI inference workloads [2].
The bear case is straightforward memory-cycle risk: NAND is a historically brutal boom-bust commodity business, and Sandisk itself lost money in FY2023-2025 before this AI-driven upswing. Seeking Alpha analysts have flagged two failure scenarios — a slowdown in AI infrastructure capex, or oversupply emerging faster than consensus expects as competitors (Samsung, SK Hynix) expand capacity, both of which could trigger sharp NAND contract-price declines and multiple compression characteristic of past memory downcycles [1]. Sandisk's Inspiration score is weak (2/10) given the company's brief 18-month history as an independent entity, with no distinctive mission evidence, community programs, retention data, or insider buying identified. The Infrastructure score of 10/10, while mechanically accurate today, is a lagging indicator of a cyclical peak rather than a structural moat, and investors should expect meaningful volatility if AI capex growth decelerates or technological obsolescence risk (flagged for post-2028) materializes.
