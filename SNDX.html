Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Syndax Pharmaceuticals is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on developing and commercializing therapies for patients with cancer. The company has two approved drugs: Revuforj (revumenib) for KMT2A-rearranged acute leukemia and Niktimvo (axatilimab-csfr) for chronic graft-versus-host disease. Both drugs are annualizing above $200M in revenue each as of Q2 2026. Led by CEO Michael Metzger since 2022, Syndax reported Q2 2026 revenue of $72.8M (+92% YoY) and is progressing toward profitability with net losses narrowing significantly. Market cap approximately $1.6B.
298 Employees ·syndax.com

Investment Thesis
Syndax is a rare small-cap biotech success story with two approved cancer drugs both annualizing above $200M, driving explosive revenue growth — Q2 2026 revenue of $72.8M (+92% YoY) with full-year 2026 consensus at $346.4M (+60%) [1][2]. The dual commercial ramp of Revuforj in KMT2A-rearranged leukemia and Niktimvo in chronic GVHD demonstrates strong execution by a lean 298-person organization, and the Glassdoor Best Places to Work culture (4.8/5.0, 97% recommend) supports talent retention critical for sustained performance [3]. Narrowing losses ($(0.55)/share in Q2 2026 vs $(0.83) in Q2 2025) point toward a potential profitability inflection within 12-18 months, which could unlock a significant re-rating from the current $1.6B market cap.
The bear case centers on the fundamental risks facing any small-cap biotech with a narrow product portfolio: concentration risk, balance sheet fragility, and execution uncertainty. Syndax's two approved products serve niche patient populations — if either drug faces safety concerns, competitive entry, or slower-than-expected label expansion, growth could stall with limited diversification [1]. The balance sheet is severely strained with D/E of 3,903% and ongoing losses (profit margin -87.5%), leaving the company vulnerable to capital markets disruption or any delay in reaching profitability [4]. The path from ~$275M TTM revenue to self-sustaining cash flow depends on continued volume ramp and operating leverage that is not guaranteed. At $18.07/share (below the 50-day SMA of $20.10), the market is pricing in uncertainty about whether commercial momentum can translate into durable profitability.
