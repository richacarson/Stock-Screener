Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYSE ·United States
Snowflake Inc. provides a cloud-based AI Data Cloud platform that enables organizations to consolidate data, build data applications, apply AI to business problems, and share data products. Serving financial services, healthcare, retail, technology, government, and other industries, Snowflake operates on a consumption-based revenue model across AWS, Azure, and GCP. With TTM revenue of $5.4B growing 35% YoY and a net revenue retention rate of 125%, Snowflake is headquartered in Menlo Park, California. The company hit $100M in AI revenue run rate ahead of schedule in FY2026 and announced a $200M partnership with OpenAI in February 2026. CEO Sridhar Ramaswamy (appointed February 2024) is repositioning Snowflake from a cloud data warehouse into an enterprise AI platform [1][2][3].
9,394 Employees ·www.snowflake.com

Investment Thesis
Snowflake at $319.61 with a $113B market cap is the leading independent cloud data platform executing a strategic pivot toward enterprise AI. TTM revenue of $5.4B is growing 35% YoY with 125% net revenue retention, and remaining performance obligations of $9.8B (up 42%) provide strong forward visibility [1]. The company reached $100M in AI revenue run rate ahead of schedule, signed a $200M OpenAI partnership, and integrated Anthropic capabilities into the platform [3]. CEO Ramaswamy is repositioning Snowflake from a data warehouse into an AI Data Cloud that enables enterprises to build, deploy, and govern AI applications on their own data — a massive TAM expansion. With $2.3B in cash, $1.7B in annual free cash flow, and 4,900+ customers spending $1M+ annually, Snowflake has the financial foundation to sustain the transformation [1].
The bear case centers on extreme valuation and competitive intensity. At 105.8x forward earnings and a PEG of 5.5, Snowflake prices in years of hyper-growth that must materialize in an increasingly competitive landscape [1]. Databricks (valued at $62B private, growing ~60%) directly challenges Snowflake's data processing moat with a lakehouse architecture that combines warehousing and AI workloads, and hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) are building integrated data and AI platforms that could commoditize Snowflake's core value proposition [3]. The GAAP profit margin of -20.1% reflects heavy stock-based compensation (~35% of revenue) that dilutes shareholders even as adjusted metrics look healthy — ROE of -48.1% confirms the company is not yet earning its cost of capital. Revenue growth decelerated from 35% to 27% in FY2027 guidance, and any further slowdown would severely compress the premium multiple [1]. Leadership instability (CEO replaced in 2024, CRO replaced after one year) during a critical transformation increases execution risk, and the company's consumption-based revenue model creates inherent variability tied to customer cloud spending budgets [3][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI revenue acceleration — the $100M+ AI run rate achieved in FY2026 could scale rapidly as Cortex AI, the OpenAI partnership, and Anthropic integration drive incremental consumption from existing customers and attract new AI-first workloads to the platform [1][3]
  2. Remaining performance obligations of $9.8B (+42% YoY) provide strong revenue visibility for the next 2-3 years, and FY2027 product revenue guidance of $5.66B (+27%) could prove conservative if AI adoption accelerates faster than management's base case [1]
  3. Enterprise AI governance demand — as enterprises move from AI experimentation to production deployment, they need platforms to govern data access, model training, and inference at scale, positioning Snowflake's AI Data Cloud as essential infrastructure for responsible AI adoption [3]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme valuation at 105.8x forward P/E and 5.5x PEG leaves zero margin of safety — the stock must sustain 27-35% revenue growth for years to justify its multiple, and any deceleration below expectations would trigger a severe repricing, as the 2024 decline from $237 to $118 demonstrated [1]
  2. Hyperscaler disintermediation risk — AWS, Azure, and GCP are building integrated data and AI platforms that could reduce the need for a separate data layer, and their ability to bundle data services with compute at lower cost threatens Snowflake's consumption-based pricing model [3]
  3. GAAP profitability absent — the -20.1% profit margin and -48.1% ROE reflect stock-based compensation of approximately 35% of revenue, which is real economic dilution that adjusted metrics obscure, and the path to GAAP profitability requires substantial SBC reduction that could impact talent retention during the AI transformation [1][4]

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Snowflake occupies a complex position relative to AI — it is both a beneficiary and a potential victim of the AI revolution. On the protective side, Snowflake's core data platform benefits from AI adoption as enterprises need to consolidate, govern, and process larger datasets to feed AI models, and the platform's switching costs (data gravity, consumption-based billing, organizational workflows) create meaningful moats [1][3]. However, Snowflake faces genuine competitive risk: hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) are building integrated AI and data platforms that could reduce the need for a separate data layer, and Databricks' lakehouse architecture directly challenges Snowflake's warehouse model. A Stanford GSB case study explicitly flags the question of 'whether Snowflake could reinvent itself as an enterprise AI platform before hyperscalers and AI-native competitors defined the category without it' [3]. The company is PROTECTED but not IMMUNE — the core data business has real moats, but the AI transformation is existential, not optional. AI Opportunity (1/1): Snowflake generates quantifiable AI revenue — the company achieved $100M in AI revenue run rate ahead of schedule by Q3 FY2026, with Cortex AI, AI-powered search, and the $200M OpenAI partnership contributing to a growing AI product portfolio [1][3].
