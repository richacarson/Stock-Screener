Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Regulated Electric ·NYSE ·United States
The Southern Company is the largest regulated electric utility in the southeastern United States, serving approximately 9 million customers across Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, Virginia, Illinois, and Tennessee through its subsidiaries including Georgia Power, Alabama Power, and Southern Company Gas. The company operates a diversified generation fleet anchored by the Vogtle nuclear plant (the largest in the U.S.), complemented by natural gas, solar, and battery storage. TTM revenue is approximately $30.2B with a 15.4% profit margin. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.32 beat consensus by 9%, and full-year 2026 guidance is $4.50-$4.60 [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Southern Company at $90.71 with a $104B market cap is the premier regulated utility play on AI-driven electricity demand, trading at 18.4x forward earnings with a 3.35% dividend yield backed by 25 consecutive years of growth. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.32 beat consensus by 9%, full-year guidance of $4.50-$4.60 implies 15-18% earnings growth, and the $26.5B DOE loan facility materially lowers cost of capital [1][2]. The company has contracted over 17 GW of data center load through the mid-2030s, with hyperscaler partnerships (OpenAI 25-year supply agreement, Google nuclear uprates) converting the Vogtle nuclear fleet from a cost overrun liability into the most valuable utility asset in America [2][3]. Operating cash flow of $10.7B supports the $81.2B capital plan while maintaining the dividend streak, and the regulated utility model provides earnings visibility unmatched by merchant generators.
The bear case centers on capital intensity and regulatory risk. D/E of 182% reflects the enormous capital required to build out transmission and generation for data center load, and the $81.2B capex plan will require continued equity and debt issuance that may dilute existing shareholders [1]. Revenue growth of just 2.1% TTM masks the lag between contracted demand and revenue realization — the 17 GW pipeline is long-dated, and regulatory approval for rate increases to fund infrastructure is not guaranteed. The payout ratio of 71.8% is elevated for a utility undergoing massive capital investment, leaving limited financial flexibility if data center demand slows or construction costs escalate. Profit margin of 15.4% and ROE of 11.5% are below targets, reflecting the capital-intensive regulated model. The dividend growth rate of only 3% annually barely exceeds inflation, and at a PEG of 2.15, the stock prices in substantial future growth that depends on regulatory cooperation and hyperscaler follow-through on long-term contracts [2][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. AI data center demand driving 20% annual commercial electricity sales growth — 17+ GW contracted through the mid-2030s with investment-grade hyperscalers including OpenAI and Google, converting nuclear assets into premium baseload supply [2][3]
  2. $26.5B DOE loan facility lowering cost of capital and enabling $81.2B infrastructure investment plan with 8% projected annual electric sales growth through 2029, creating a multi-decade earnings growth runway [2]

Key Risks
  1. Regulatory risk as Georgia Public Service Commission and other state regulators must approve rate increases to fund massive data center infrastructure buildout — ratepayer advocacy groups may resist passing costs to residential customers, compressing earned returns below authorized levels [1]
  2. Capital intensity with D/E at 182% and $81.2B in planned capex requiring continued equity and debt issuance, which dilutes existing shareholders and increases interest expense sensitivity in a higher-for-longer rate environment [2]
  3. Concentration risk in hyperscaler data center contracts — if AI spending slows, cloud buildout decelerates, or hyperscalers shift demand to other regions, Southern's contracted pipeline may not fully convert to revenue, leaving stranded infrastructure costs [3]
