Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·Canada
South Bow Corporation operates as an energy infrastructure company spun off from TC Energy Corporation in October 2024. The company operates through three segments: Keystone Pipeline System (4,900 km crude oil pipeline connecting Alberta to U.S. Midwest and Gulf Coast refining markets), Marketing (crude oil trading, storage, and logistics), and Intra-Alberta & Other (Grand Rapids and White Spruce pipelines). Incorporated in 2023 and headquartered in Calgary, Canada, SOBO reported TTM revenue of approximately $2.0B with Q2 2026 revenue of $546M (up 4.2% YoY) and raised 2026 normalized EBITDA guidance to $1.04B.

Investment Thesis
South Bow at 16.97x forward earnings with a 6.04% dividend yield offers high-current-income exposure to essential North American crude oil infrastructure, backed by the 4,900 km Keystone Pipeline System connecting Alberta oil sands to U.S. Midwest and Gulf Coast refining markets [1][4]. The company raised 2026 normalized EBITDA guidance to $1.04B after a strong first half, with Q2 revenue up 4.2% to $546M and EPS growing 39% YoY. ROE of 17.4% and profit margin of 23% demonstrate strong profitability for a midstream operator. The pipeline's strategic geography and long-term contracted volumes provide revenue visibility, and increasing AI-driven electricity demand indirectly supports crude oil transportation needs. Free cash flow of $338M funds the $2.00/share dividend with operating cash flow of $839M providing substantial coverage [4].
The bear case centers on structural commodity risk and financial leverage. D/E of 216% reflects the capital-intensive pipeline business model and creates vulnerability to rising interest rates and credit tightening [4]. The 91% payout ratio leaves minimal margin of safety for dividend coverage if operating results weaken — crude oil volume disruptions, tariff-related demand shifts, or extended pipeline shutdowns from integrity incidents could force a dividend cut. South Bow successfully restarted the Keystone Pipeline after an oil release incident, but future spills carry regulatory, environmental, and reputational risk that could impair operations [3]. The energy transition poses a multi-decade existential threat — as global crude oil demand potentially peaks, SOBO's fixed pipeline infrastructure could become a stranded asset with limited repurposing options. Trade tariffs between Canada and the U.S. create additional uncertainty for cross-border crude oil flows, with CEO Wirzba acknowledging tariffs 'could create challenges in the marketing segment' [3]. Short company history (October 2024 spinoff) provides limited track record for evaluating management execution as a standalone entity.
