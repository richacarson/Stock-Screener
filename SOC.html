Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Sable Offshore Corp. acquired the dormant Santa Ynez Unit offshore California from ExxonMobil in 2022, including three offshore platforms (Hondo, Harmony, Heritage) and the pipeline connecting them to onshore processing at Pentland Station in Kern County. The assets had been idle since May 2015 when a pipeline rupture near Refugio Beach released 100,000 gallons of crude oil into the Pacific. Sable restarted pipeline operations in March 2026 after the Trump administration invoked the Defense Production Act. Q2 2026 revenue reached $137.1M with production ramping from 21k to 40k bpd. The company faces an $18M California Coastal Commission penalty and ongoing environmental lawsuits challenging the restart. Market cap approximately $979M.

Investment Thesis
Sable Offshore offers a deeply discounted entry into California offshore oil production at 7.87x forward earnings, with analysts forecasting $539M+ in 2026 revenue and a path to profitability as the Santa Ynez Unit ramps to sustained 40,000+ bpd [1][2]. Q2 2026 marked the first full quarter of revenue ($137.1M) and positive operating cash flow ($9.4M), demonstrating that the restart is generating real economic value. A federal judge ruled in August 2026 that operations can continue under federal oversight, removing a key legal overhang [5]. Three analyst firms rate the stock Strong Buy with price targets of $9-$24, implying 76-370% upside from $5.10. The 149% entry-to-exit oil sales growth rate in Q2 shows strong operational momentum.
The bear case is existential. Sable carries $987.5M in total debt against only $21.6M cash, with an Altman Z-Score of -1.19 indicating significant bankruptcy risk [2]. The company faces the largest penalty ever levied by the California Coastal Commission ($18M), ongoing environmental lawsuits from conservation groups challenging the same pipeline that spilled 100,000 gallons in 2015, and the political risk that a future administration could reverse the Defense Production Act authorization [3][5]. Net loss of $433.8M on $138.4M TTM revenue reflects massive startup costs, and free cash flow is deeply negative at -$558.8M. The stock has declined 81% from its 52-week high, and the company's sole revenue-generating asset faces regulatory, environmental, and operational risk with no diversification. A single adverse court ruling could impair the entire business.
