Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Drilling ·NYQ ·United States
Sable Offshore Corp. acquired the dormant Santa Ynez Unit offshore California from ExxonMobil in 2022, including three offshore platforms (Hondo, Harmony, Heritage) and the pipeline connecting them to onshore processing. The assets had been idle since May 2015 when a pipeline rupture near Refugio Beach released 100,000 gallons of crude oil into the Pacific. Sable restarted pipeline operations in March 2026 under the Trump administration's Defense Production Act authority. Q2 2026 revenue reached $137.1M with production ramping from 21K to 40K bpd, generating first positive operating cash flow of $9.4M. The company completed refinancing in July 2026 including a $675M Term Loan B at 15% and $345M convertible notes.

Investment Thesis
Sable Offshore at $3.32 (forward P/E 2.1x) offers extreme upside if the Santa Ynez production ramp succeeds — Q2 2026 revenue reached $137.1M with first positive operating cash flow of $9.4M, production ramped from 21K to 40K bpd within the quarter, and all 77 wells on Platforms Harmony and Heritage are expected online Q3 2026 with Platform Hondo commencing September 2026 [1]. FY2027 guidance of 45,000 BOE/d represents continued ramp. The federal court ruling allowing operations under federal oversight and the July 2026 refinancing ($675M Term Loan B plus $345M convertible notes) provide near-term operational and financial runway [1][5]. At $637M market cap, the stock trades at a fraction of the estimated $4-5B replacement value of the offshore infrastructure.
The bear case is existential and multi-dimensional. Sable carries crushing debt with a $675M Term Loan B at an extraordinary 15% annual coupon, creating ~$100M in annual interest expense alone against $137M in Q2 revenue [1][4]. The company faces an $18M California Coastal Commission penalty (the Commission's largest ever), five felony criminal charges for discharging pollutants into waterways, sixteen misdemeanor counts, and an Environmental Defense Center federal lawsuit challenging the pipeline permits under which operations resumed [3][5]. The stock has declined from ~$18 to $3.32 (>80% decline), reflecting investor skepticism about the company's ability to sustain operations through persistent legal and regulatory challenges. The Defense Production Act authorization could be revoked by a future administration, instantly imperiling all operations. A single adverse court ruling could shut down the pipeline that connects all three platforms to onshore processing, rendering the entire production base worthless. The 15% coupon on the Term Loan B signals lender perception of extreme credit risk.

  3. Single-asset concentration — the entire business depends on one offshore unit (Santa Ynez) and one pipeline system in the most hostile regulatory jurisdiction in the U.S. for offshore drilling, with zero diversification and no operational fallback [1]
