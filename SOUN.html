Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
SoundHound AI develops voice AI and conversational intelligence platforms for enterprise customers across automotive, healthcare, retail, and financial services verticals. The company's OASYS agentic AI platform enables natural language voice interfaces for businesses. Founded in 2005 by CEO Keyvan Mohajer (Stanford PhD), SoundHound spent 17 years building proprietary voice AI technology before the current AI boom. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $61.9M (+45% YoY) with GAAP gross margin of 45.1%, and management raised full-year guidance to $230-260M. The company has $203M in cash with no debt but remains deeply unprofitable with a -$42.8M net loss in Q2.

Investment Thesis
SoundHound AI at 16x trailing sales offers exposure to the fastest-growing segment of enterprise AI — conversational intelligence — with Q2 2026 revenue of $61.9M (+45% YoY) crushing analyst estimates of $52.4M and management raising full-year guidance to $230-260M [1]. The company's 17 years of proprietary voice AI R&D and 200+ patents create genuine technological differentiation in a market where most competitors rely on general-purpose LLMs. The OASYS agentic AI platform is winning seven-figure enterprise contracts across healthcare, automotive, retail, and financial services, with GAAP gross margin improving to 45.1% (from 39.0% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA loss narrowing to $9.6M [1]. The pristine balance sheet ($203M cash, zero debt) provides runway without dilution risk, and the Inspire Impact Score of 45 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions makes SOUN suitable for faith-aligned portfolios.
The bear case is substantial and reflected in the 38.82% short interest — the highest level of market skepticism of any company in this screen. SoundHound remains deeply unprofitable with -67.45% profit margin, -$136.76M TTM net loss, and -$142M negative free cash flow on only $203M in revenue [3]. The stock has declined 45% over 52 weeks despite strong revenue growth, suggesting the market questions whether voice AI specialization can survive against general-purpose AI from Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and OpenAI — each of which has exponentially more compute, data, and enterprise relationships. At $3.27B market cap on $203M revenue, the valuation assumes massive future scale that may never materialize if LLMs commoditize conversational AI. The company does not report Forward P/E or PEG because there are no earnings to value — investors are buying pure growth with no profitability timeline. Management's adjusted EBITDA loss of $9.6M masks $33.2M in stock-based compensation and other adjustments, and the path from non-GAAP near-breakeven to GAAP profitability remains undefined. Customer concentration risk is also a concern — a few large automotive OEMs likely represent a significant portion of revenue.

  1. Enterprise AI platform expansion across healthcare, retail, and financial services verticals — Q2 2026 saw seven-figure contracts including a major U.S. healthcare system, and each new vertical opens a large addressable market for the OASYS platform [1]
  2. Gross margin expansion trajectory from 39% to 45.1% GAAP (Q2 YoY) suggests the platform is scaling toward software-like economics — continued improvement toward 60%+ non-GAAP margins could accelerate the path to GAAP profitability and re-rate the stock [1]
  3. Agentic AI adoption wave — as enterprises move from basic chatbots to autonomous voice agents that can complete complex multi-step tasks, SoundHound's 17 years of voice AI specialization and OASYS platform position it as a differentiated player in a rapidly growing market [2]
