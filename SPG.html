Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Simon Property Group is the largest retail REIT in the United States, owning and operating premier shopping, dining, entertainment, and mixed-use destinations including malls, Premium Outlets, and The Mills. The company recently completed a generational leadership transition following the passing of longtime CEO David Simon, with Eli Simon now leading the company. Q2 2026 revenue reached $1.79B (+20% YoY) with Real Estate FFO per share growing 7.9% YoY. Full-year 2026 FFO guidance was raised to $13.10-$13.25 per diluted share. The portfolio benefits from broad-based leasing demand, accelerating foot traffic, and strong retailer sales. Market cap is approximately $66B.

Investment Thesis
Simon Property Group at ~15.5x FFO ($13.15 midpoint guidance) offers the largest and highest-quality U.S. retail REIT portfolio at a reasonable valuation, with Q2 2026 revenue growing 20% YoY to $1.79B and Real Estate FFO per share increasing 7.9% driven by broad-based leasing demand, accelerating traffic, and strong retailer sales [1]. The 4.35% dividend yield ($8.90/share) is well-covered by FFO at a 62% payout ratio, and management raised full-year FFO guidance to $13.10-$13.25 per share, signaling confidence in continued momentum. The 32-year consecutive dividend payment history (since 1994) and 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 7.3% provide income quality, while the portfolio of irreplaceable Class A malls and Premium Outlets benefits from a structural shift toward experiential retail. The Inspire Impact Score of 48 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions is attractive for faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on structural threats to physical retail, leadership transition risk, and balance sheet leverage. Despite strong current performance, e-commerce penetration continues to grow, and any macro downturn could accelerate tenant closures and reduce foot traffic — the dividend was cut in both 2009 and 2020 during recessions, undermining reliability for income investors [1]. The generational leadership transition from David Simon (who built the company over decades) to Eli Simon is untested through crisis, and the Comparably executive team rating of 'D' (bottom 15%) suggests organizational challenges that could surface under stress [2]. D/E of 503.5% reflects structural REIT leverage but creates vulnerability to rising interest rates on refinancing — with ~$30B in total debt, even modest rate increases generate significant incremental interest expense. Forward GAAP P/E of 29.75x (distorted by depreciation) and PEG of 4.58 suggest the stock is priced for sustained growth that may be difficult to maintain as the acquisition-driven revenue boost normalizes. Innovation is limited (4/10) — Simon remains fundamentally a commercial landlord without meaningful technology differentiation or AI-driven capabilities.
