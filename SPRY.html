Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
ARS Pharmaceuticals is a biopharmaceutical company that develops and commercializes neffy (epinephrine nasal spray), the first and only FDA-approved needle-free treatment for Type I allergic reactions including anaphylaxis. Neffy is approved for adults and pediatric patients (15 to <30 kg) in the U.S. and the EU. The company is also developing pipeline candidates for chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU). Co-founded and led by CEO Richard Lowenthal, headquartered in San Diego, California.
158 Employees ·www.ars-pharma.com

Investment Thesis
ARS Pharmaceuticals owns a first-of-its-kind product in neffy — the only FDA-approved needle-free intranasal epinephrine spray, addressing a genuine unmet need for millions of patients who delay or avoid EpiPen use due to needle phobia [1]. Q1 2026 revenue of $22.7M tripled year-over-year from $8.0M, with ~120,000 U.S. patients and 28,000+ prescribers demonstrating accelerating commercial adoption [1]. The $2.5M collaboration milestone from EU partner ALK-Abello and neffy 1mg pediatric approval expand the addressable market globally. Management expects growth to accelerate through back-to-school peak season, supported by the new $199 retail cash program and expanded payer access. Cash of $201M is expected to fund operations through cash-flow breakeven, and Phase 2b CSU data mid-2026 offers pipeline optionality [1].
The bear case is dominated by commercial execution risk and governance concerns. On June 24, 2026, the stock dropped 23% when ARS disclosed zero new commercial formulary additions in the July 1 review cycle — despite management's statements projecting CVS Caremark, Anthem, and Aetna coverage by that date [4]. This triggered securities fraud investigations by Levi & Korsinsky, Schall Law Firm, and Pomerantz, creating legal overhang. Neffy U.S. product revenue of $17.5M in Q1 2026 actually declined sequentially from $20.3M in Q4 2025, raising questions about commercial momentum. D/E has deteriorated to 279% with $96.5M in term loans and $74.7M in financing liabilities against only $61.3M equity. The net loss of $60.6M in Q1 2026 alone ($242M annualized) means the $201M cash position provides only ~3.3 quarters of runway at current burn. The entire thesis rests on a single product achieving formulary coverage that management has demonstrably failed to secure on the projected timeline [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Back-to-school peak allergy season in Q3 2026 expected to accelerate neffy prescriptions, supported by 120,000 existing patients and 28,000+ prescribers driving refill momentum [1]
  2. CVS Caremark formulary inclusion and expanded commercial payer coverage — while the July 1 cycle missed, subsequent review cycles could provide coverage that management projects would reach 60-80% of commercially insured patients [4]
  3. Phase 2b CSU (chronic spontaneous urticaria) data expected mid-2026, potentially diversifying beyond single-product epinephrine dependency and validating the intranasal delivery platform for new indications [1]
Key Risks
  1. Securities fraud investigations by multiple law firms following management's misleading formulary expansion projections — legal costs, potential settlements, and reputational damage could distract from commercial execution [4]
  2. Sequential product revenue decline ($17.5M Q1 2026 vs $20.3M Q4 2025) and zero new formulary additions suggest commercial momentum may be stalling, threatening the revenue acceleration needed to reach cash-flow breakeven [1]
  3. Cash runway risk — $201M cash against $60.6M quarterly net loss provides ~3.3 quarters of runway at current burn rates, and any revenue shortfall could necessitate dilutive financing at the current depressed stock price [1]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): ARS Pharmaceuticals' core business — manufacturing and selling a physical FDA-approved drug product (neffy intranasal epinephrine spray) for emergency treatment of anaphylaxis — is structurally immune to AI disruption [2]. The business involves FDA-regulated pharmaceutical manufacturing, physical drug distribution through pharmacies and PBMs, and physician prescribing — none of which AI can replace. The product's value is in the physical delivery mechanism (nasal spray vs injection) requiring chemical engineering, specialized manufacturing, and regulatory compliance. AI cannot substitute for a life-saving emergency medication. If anything, AI-driven allergy diagnosis and patient identification could increase demand for neffy. AI Opportunity (0/1): ARS does not generate revenue from AI products. AI may be used internally for clinical trial optimization and commercial analytics but is not a revenue driver for this single-product pharmaceutical company.
