Company Profile
Technology ·Solar ·NASDAQ ·United States
SunPower Inc. (formerly Complete Solaria) is a residential solar installer operating across 46 states through direct installation, new homes, and dealer channels. The company acquired SunPower Corporation's key assets out of bankruptcy in September 2024, rebranded as SunPower Inc. in October 2025, and targets profitability and cash flow breakeven by Q3 2026 with a long-term vision of a billion-dollar revenue run rate by Q3 2028.

Investment Thesis
SunPower Inc. represents an extreme turnaround bet in residential solar, led by veteran CEO T.J. Rodgers who acquired SunPower's assets out of bankruptcy and built a national 46-state installation platform through three acquisitions [1]. The company targets Q3 2026 revenue above $96M with profitability and cash flow breakeven, which would validate the lean operating model and could dramatically re-rate the stock from its deeply distressed $0.48 level [1]. FY2026 revenue is estimated at $420M (up ~40% from FY2025's $300M), driven by acquisition integration and geographic expansion. The residential solar industry benefits from structural tailwinds: AI-driven data center buildout increasing electricity demand, 30% federal Investment Tax Credit, and rising utility rates driving homeowner adoption. Solar installation is completely immune to AI disruption, and the company operates in a rapidly consolidating market where survivors will benefit from reduced competition.
The bear case is dominated by survival risk. SPWR fails the IOWN liquidity floor test at $758K daily traded value ($0.48 x 1.58M shares), rendering the stock unsuitable for institutional investment [3]. The sub-$0.50 share price creates potential Nasdaq delisting risk, and D/E of -5.27 indicates negative shareholders' equity — the company is technically insolvent [3]. Q1 2026 revenue of $72.8M missed $80M guidance by 9%, and FY2026 estimates have been revised down from $470M to $420M, undermining management credibility [1]. The company recently closed a share exchange to cover $10M in convertible note interest, signaling liquidity stress with only ~$10M cash against $188M total debt [3]. Market cap of $96M represents a 23% decline from April, and the 94% decline from predecessor company highs reflects legitimate structural concerns. The predecessor SunPower Corporation went bankrupt under $2 billion in debt — similar overextension risk persists if the acquisition-driven growth strategy fails to achieve profitability at scale. Potential loss of the 30% Solar ITC under changing federal energy policy remains an existential risk [4].
