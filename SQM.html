Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Specialty Chemicals ·NYSE ·Chile
SQM is one of the world's largest lithium producers and a leading producer of specialty plant nutrition, iodine, and industrial chemicals, operating primarily from Chile's Salar de Atacama. The company trades on NYSE as an ADR and operates through a strategic joint venture with Codelco (Novandino Litio) for lithium extraction. SQM reported record H1 2026 revenue of $4.23B (+103% YoY) and net income of $1.02B (+354%) as lithium prices rebounded and EV demand accelerated globally. Q2 2026 lithium sales volumes hit a record 84,000 metric tons of LCE. Led by CEO Ricardo Ramos since 2018.

Investment Thesis
SQM at 10x forward earnings is a deeply undervalued play on the lithium supercycle rebound — Q2 2026 revenue of $2.47B (+137% YoY) with record lithium volumes of 84,000 mt LCE, H1 net income of $1.02B (+354%), and a PEG ratio of 0.31 suggest the market is not fully pricing in the earnings recovery [1][2]. The Novandino Litio JV provides long-term access to the world's highest-grade lithium deposit in the Salar de Atacama, with production guidance of 280-290K mt in 2026 rising to 300K mt by 2027. Global lithium demand is projected to exceed 2.1M mt in 2026, driven by EV adoption acceleration and AI data center buildout. Conservative balance sheet (D/E 61%, payout ratio 21%) provides a dividend yield of ~3.5% with significant room for payout increases as earnings normalize at higher levels [5].
The bear case centers on lithium price cyclicality, Chilean political risk, and ESG controversies. Lithium prices are inherently volatile — SQM's annual dividends swung from $7.72/share in 2022 to $0.21 in 2024 as prices crashed 80%, and the current recovery could reverse if EV demand slows, new supply comes online from Australia and Argentina, or battery chemistry shifts toward sodium-ion alternatives [2]. The Codelco JV introduces state involvement in SQM's most valuable asset, with governance complexities and potential political interference in production decisions. ESG risks are material: Indigenous community protests over water depletion in the Atacama, controversial CORFO agreements lacking proper consultation, and systematic environmental concerns around brine extraction have drawn sustained criticism from civil society and scientists [4]. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects the lack of coverage but the underlying ESG profile suggests meaningful concerns for faith-based investors. Revenue concentration in lithium (~65%+ of revenue) means SQM's fortunes are tied to a single commodity market.

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): SQM's core business is physically extracting and processing lithium from salt flats in the Atacama Desert — a physical-world operation involving brine evaporation, chemical processing, and logistics that AI structurally cannot displace [1]. Mining operations require on-the-ground infrastructure, environmental management, and government concessions that create insurmountable barriers to AI substitution.
