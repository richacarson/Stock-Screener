Investment Thesis
Sportradar is the dominant global sports technology integrator, delivering Q1 2026 revenue of EUR346.5M (+11% reported, +16% CC) with Adjusted EBITDA of EUR66M (+12%) and free cash flow of EUR44M (+38%) [1]. The 2026 full-year guidance of 23-25% constant-currency revenue growth to EUR1,557-1,582M represents acceleration from 2025's 17% growth, driven by IMG ARENA integration, Betting Technology expansion (+15% in Q1), and AI-powered product innovation across 150+ AI scientists with 100+ use cases [1][2]. A pristine balance sheet (EUR542M liquidity, D/E 7%, no net debt) and $1B total authorized share buyback including a new $250M enhanced program signal capital discipline [1]. Exclusive multi-year data rights with NBA, NFL, NHL, MLB, ATP, IOC, and FIFA create a structural moat that competitors cannot replicate through AI alone.
The bear case centers on valuation compression, FX sensitivity, and gambling dependency. The stock has declined 44.6% over the past 52 weeks to $13.04 despite strong operational execution, reflecting market skepticism about the forward multiple (23x forward P/E, PEG 1.86) in a rising-rate environment [4]. Q1 2026 showed a EUR6.3M net loss driven by EUR9.3M FX headwinds and higher acquisition-related amortization, highlighting sensitivity to EUR/USD movements on a predominantly Euro-denominated revenue base [1]. Net profit margin of 5.27% and ROE of 7.42% remain below IOWN targets despite improving trajectories [4]. The revenue base is concentrated in regulated sports betting, exposing Sportradar to jurisdictional gambling taxes, regulatory crackdowns, and rights-cost inflation as leagues extract more value at renewal. The Inspire negative attribution for 'Gambling' keeps the faith-alignment label MIXED, and the societal concerns around problem gambling create reputational overhang for mission-driven investors.

Key Risks
  1. FX and profitability pressure — EUR9.3M Q1 FX loss drove net loss of EUR6.3M, and net margin of 5.27% / ROE of 7.42% remain well below IOWN targets despite revenue scale
  2. Gambling regulatory risk — new remote gaming duties, US state-by-state taxation, and potential prediction-market crackdowns could compress bookmaker margins and Sportradar's economics
  3. Rights cost inflation — league renewals (NBA, NFL, FIFA) extract increasing economics from data partners, and failure to pass through costs to bookmakers would squeeze gross margins
