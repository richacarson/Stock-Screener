Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Diversified ·NYSE ·United States
Sempra is a California-based energy infrastructure holding company operating regulated utilities SDG&E and SoCalGas in Southern California, a majority stake in Oncor (Texas transmission and distribution), and Sempra Infrastructure (LNG and energy networks). FY2025 revenue was $13.7B with net income of $1.84B. In 2026 Sempra affirmed adjusted EPS guidance of $4.80-$5.30, 2027 EPS guidance of $5.10-$5.70, and a 7-9% long-term EPS growth rate, while advancing the sale of a 45% stake in Sempra Infrastructure Partners to KKR to sharpen its focus on regulated utilities. Q3 2026 results are scheduled for November 5, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Sempra offers regulated-utility durability with above-average growth: management affirmed 2026 adjusted EPS of $4.80-$5.30, 2027 EPS of $5.10-$5.70, and a 7-9% long-term EPS growth rate, and Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.16 beat the $1.01 consensus [2][3]. Oncor gives Sempra direct exposure to ERCOT's data-center and electrification load growth, while SDG&E and SoCalGas provide monopoly franchises in Southern California [1][6]. At $86.06, the stock trades at 15.5x forward earnings with a 3.0% yield, 28 years of uninterrupted dividends, and 23 consecutive years of increases [3][5]. The pending sale of a 45% stake in Sempra Infrastructure Partners to KKR should simplify the story toward regulated earnings and fund capital needs without heavy equity issuance [2].
The bear case starts with fundamentals that lag the narrative: revenue is flat (-0.1% YoY), Q2 2026 revenue of $3.0B missed consensus, ROE is just 6.7%, and FMP shows negative TTM free cash flow (-$8.02/share) as capex of $16.30/share runs at roughly twice operating cash flow [3][4]. Interest coverage of 2.4x and a 72.8% payout ratio leave limited cushion if rates stay high or regulators push back on affordability [4]. California wildfire liability and rate-case risk remain persistent overhangs, the KKR transaction still requires approvals, and LNG projects such as Port Arthur carry construction and cost risk [2].
