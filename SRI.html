Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Auto Parts & Equipment ·NYSE ·United States
Stoneridge designs and manufactures electrical and electronic systems, components, and modules for the automotive, commercial vehicle, off-highway, and agricultural vehicle markets. Its flagship product is MirrorEye, a camera monitor system replacing conventional side mirrors on commercial vehicles, which achieved record Q2 2026 revenue of $37M and surpassed 150,000 units produced globally. The company recently divested non-core operations to focus on technology-driven products, with 2026 continuing operations revenue guided to $645-670M. Headquartered in Novi, Michigan. New CEO Natalia Noblet took the helm in April 2026.

Investment Thesis
Stoneridge's bull case centers on MirrorEye, a camera monitor system replacing traditional side mirrors on commercial vehicles that is inflecting toward $160M+ in 2026 revenue with 45% YoY growth [2]. Q2 2026 continuing operations revenue of $181.4M (+15.1% YoY) and record MirrorEye sales of $37M demonstrate accelerating adoption as European regulatory mandates and North American OEM program ramp-ups expand the addressable market [1]. With 150,000+ cumulative MirrorEye systems produced, the company is approaching scale economics that should improve margins from the current adjusted EBITDA margin of 3.1-3.7%. Management targets $715M+ in 2027 revenue with at least $35M incremental growth from existing OEM take rate increases, suggesting a clear path to profitability as MirrorEye mix increases. D/E of just 66% provides balance sheet flexibility for the transformation.
The bear case is substantial. Stoneridge has been persistently unprofitable — $102.8M net loss in 2025 and continued losses in H1 2026 despite 'best quarterly EBITDA in two years' of just $5.5M [1][4]. ROE of -11.20% and profit margin of -3.39% reflect a company burning cash through a turnaround with no guarantee of reaching sustained profitability. Forward P/E of 30.28 at a $5.36 stock price prices in a breakeven-to-profitable transition that adjusted operating margin guidance of 0-0.5% barely supports. New CEO Natalia Noblet (April 2026) introduces execution risk during a critical transformation period, and Glassdoor reviews suggest cultural challenges beneath the surface [3]. Gross margin compression to 20.3% from 23.1% in Q2 (higher material costs, unfavorable FX, inventory actions) raises questions about MirrorEye unit economics at scale. The $150M market cap makes this a micro-cap with limited institutional support and thin liquidity (~$1.3M daily trading value). MirrorEye concentration risk is high — if OEM adoption slows or competitors (Continental, Ficosa) accelerate their CMS offerings, the entire investment thesis collapses.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Stoneridge's core business — physical camera monitor systems, electronic modules, and electrical components installed on commercial vehicles — has strong structural protection from AI disruption. MirrorEye requires manufactured camera hardware, in-cabin displays, and OEM-integrated wiring harnesses that AI software cannot substitute [1][2]. Vehicle safety regulations (UN R46, FMVSS) mandate physical compliance testing, creating regulatory moats. OEM qualification cycles of 2-3 years and installed-base switching costs add further protection. However, Stoneridge is not fully immune — software-defined vehicle architectures could eventually commoditize some electronic module functions, and larger Tier 1 suppliers with deeper AI/software capabilities could integrate CMS functionality into broader ADAS platforms. AI Opportunity (0/1): Stoneridge does not generate meaningful AI-specific revenue. While MirrorEye incorporates machine vision, the company does not market AI products and has not reported AI-attributable revenue or cost savings.
