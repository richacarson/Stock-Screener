Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Sarepta Therapeutics is a commercial-stage biotechnology company focused on precision genetic medicine for rare diseases, primarily Duchenne muscular dystrophy. Commercial portfolio includes four PMO exon-skipping therapies (EXONDYS 51, VYONDYS 53, AMONDYS 45, CASIMERSEN) and ELEVIDYS, the first FDA-approved gene therapy for DMD. New CEO Michael Severino, M.D. (formerly AbbVie R&D President) appointed in 2026 following Doug Ingram's retirement. Q2 2026 net product revenue of $328.7M; FY2026 guidance narrowed to $1.2-1.3B. Market cap ~$2.3B.

Investment Thesis
Sarepta at 9.87x forward earnings and $2.3B market cap is a rare-disease genetic medicine pioneer showing early profitability inflection — Q2 2026 achieved $13.3M GAAP operating income and $86.5M non-GAAP operating income, the second consecutive profitable quarter after years of losses [1]. The stock has rallied 37% since Q2 earnings, recognizing the operational turnaround. Net product revenue of $328.7M ($230.6M PMO + $98.1M ELEVIDYS) demonstrates a durable PMO franchise and stabilizing ELEVIDYS demand under the narrowed ambulatory-patient label [1]. New CEO Michael Severino, M.D. (formerly AbbVie R&D President) brings deep precision medicine and clinical development expertise, and the siRNA pipeline pivot opens new optionality beyond DMD [2]. Five FDA-approved products, orphan drug exclusivity protections, and the world's largest gene therapy manufacturing capacity provide structural competitive advantages.
The bear case is structural and operational. ELEVIDYS faces lasting commercial damage from two patient deaths, a boxed warning, and label restriction to ambulatory patients only — FY2026 guidance was narrowed to $1.2-1.3B net product revenue, a significant decline from FY2025's $1,864.3M net product revenue [1][2]. ROE of -53.47% and trailing profit margin of -32.45% reflect ongoing capital destruction despite the recent quarterly profitability, and the improving GAAP results depend heavily on collaboration revenue that may be lumpy and unsustainable [5]. The $939.65M cash position against $1.04B debt creates a near-net-debt balance sheet unusual for a company of this commercial stage. The CEO transition from Ingram (deeply embedded in DMD patient community) to Severino (corporate pharma background) tests whether the patient-mission culture survives. Competitive threats from siRNA peers (Alnylam), emerging DMD gene therapies, and novel modalities could compress long-term market share.
