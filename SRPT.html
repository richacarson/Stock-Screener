Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Sarepta Therapeutics is a commercial-stage biotechnology company focused on precision genetic medicine for rare diseases, primarily Duchenne muscular dystrophy. Commercial portfolio includes four PMO exon-skipping therapies (EXONDYS 51, VYONDYS 53, AMONDYS 45, CASIMERSEN) and ELEVIDYS, the first FDA-approved gene therapy for DMD. The company is pivoting toward its siRNA pipeline following ELEVIDYS label restrictions and safety setbacks. CEO Doug Ingram retiring by end of 2026.

Investment Thesis
Sarepta is a rare-disease gene therapy pioneer trading near multi-year lows ($18.98 vs. 52-week high of ~$103) after a traumatic 2025 marked by ELEVIDYS safety setbacks, label restrictions, and strategic restructuring [4]. Q1 2026 showed a dramatic operational turnaround with $730.8M total revenue and $358.4M GAAP operating income, driven by ~$400M in collaboration revenue that signals partner validation of Sarepta's platform [1][2]. The PMO franchise ($228.6M Q1 product revenue) provides a durable revenue base while ELEVIDYS ($102M Q1) stabilizes under the updated ambulatory-patient label. At forward P/E of 5.70 and $2B market cap on a company with five FDA-approved products and 28% 5-year revenue CAGR, the valuation embeds extreme pessimism. The siRNA pipeline pivot under new R&D President Louise Rodino-Klapac opens additional optionality.
The bear case is structural and operational. ELEVIDYS faces lasting commercial damage from two patient deaths, a boxed warning, and label restriction to ambulatory patients only — FY2025 ELEVIDYS revenue of $898.7M is not expected to repeat, with FY2026 net product guidance of only $1.2-1.4B representing a significant portfolio-level contraction [1][2]. ROE of -53.47% and trailing profit margin of -11.2% reflect a company still burning through capital despite improving trajectory [4]. The CEO transition (Ingram retiring by year-end 2026), 500 layoffs, and near-complete C-suite overhaul create execution risk at the worst possible moment. MSCI's BBB ESG rating highlights gaps in supply chain transparency. Competitive threats from siRNA peers (Alnylam) and emerging DMD gene therapies could compress long-term market share. The $2B market cap on $2.41B TTM revenue suggests the market views the revenue base as unsustainable without ELEVIDYS recovery.
Key Catalysts
  1. ELEVIDYS educational push and Gene Therapy Scholarly Exchange initiative could drive H2 2026 physician re-engagement and stabilize the ambulatory-patient market opportunity
  2. siRNA pipeline data catalysts under new R&D leadership (Rodino-Klapac) could validate the strategic pivot and unlock the next growth phase beyond DMD
  3. Q1 2026 collaboration revenue of ~$400M and GAAP operating income of $358.4M demonstrate platform monetization capability that could attract additional partnerships
Key Risks
  1. ELEVIDYS commercial recovery may stall permanently due to the boxed warning and two patient deaths, reducing the addressable market to a fraction of the original DMD population estimate
  2. CEO transition combined with 500 layoffs and near-complete C-suite overhaul creates execution risk during the most critical strategic pivot in the company's history
  3. Trailing ROE of -53.47% and profit margin of -11.2% reflect ongoing capital destruction; collaboration revenue may be lumpy and unsustainable, masking deteriorating product economics
