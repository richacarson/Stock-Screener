Company Profile
Materials ·Diversified Chemicals ·NYSE ·South Africa
Sasol Limited is an integrated chemicals and energy company based in South Africa that develops and commercializes technologies including its proprietary Fischer-Tropsch process for converting gas and coal into liquid fuels and chemicals. Sasol operates across the hydrocarbon value chain with operations in South Africa (Secunda, Sasolburg), Mozambique, and the United States (Lake Charles, Louisiana). FY2026 (ended June 30) delivered turnover up 9% to R272.1B, adjusted EBITDA up 17% to R61B, EBIT up 37% to R25.7B, and basic EPS up 79% to R18.99. Net debt was reduced 11% to $3.3B. Secunda achieved a five-year production high of 7.26 million tonnes.
27,411 Employees ·www.sasol.com

Investment Thesis
Sasol trades at an exceptionally low forward P/E of 4.46 with an estimated PEG of 0.75, offering deep value exposure to a potential commodity and earnings recovery [4]. FY2026 results demonstrated significant operational improvement: turnover up 9% to R272.1B, adjusted EBITDA up 17% to R61B, EBIT up 37%, basic EPS up 79% to R18.99, and Secunda achieved a five-year production high of 7.26 million tonnes with the South African oil breakeven reduced to $49/barrel [1]. Net debt was reduced 11% to $3.3B, tracking toward the sub-$3B threshold linked to dividend resumption in FY2027-2028. International Chemicals generated $604M adjusted EBITDA with improved commercial execution [1]. The D/E of 70% is manageable, and the Fischer-Tropsch technology platform remains globally unique, providing exposure to synthetic fuels and chemicals from multiple feedstocks.
The bear case is dominated by environmental and structural concerns. Sasol operates the world's single largest point-source GHG emitter (Secunda, ~60M tonnes CO2e annually) and has a documented 20-year record of failing to meet emissions reduction targets [3]. Global institutional investors have openly criticized the company's decarbonization timeline, and environmental groups label it a 'carbon criminal.' The Secunda plant released 112,000 tonnes of SO2 in 2024, causing documented health impacts in surrounding communities [3]. GAAP profit margin of 0.97% and ROE of 2.47% reflect a business struggling to generate adequate returns on capital even in a year of improving operations [4]. Dividends remain suspended, and the company continues to carry $6.67B in total debt. The stock's commodity/currency triple exposure (Brent crude, chemicals prices, ZAR/USD) creates unhedgeable macro risk. South African political, regulatory, and energy infrastructure risks (load-shedding disruptions, potential carbon taxes) add further uncertainty. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects the ESG challenges.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Sasol's core business — chemical manufacturing via Fischer-Tropsch synthesis, mining operations, fuel refining, and energy production — is deeply physical and structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. Chemical processes require real catalysts, real feedstocks, and precise temperature and pressure conditions that AI cannot virtualize or substitute. The 27,411 employees operate complex petrochemical plants, coal mines, and gas extraction operations requiring physical presence and engineering expertise. Regulatory permits, environmental compliance, and decades of process engineering knowledge create barriers that AI-native entrants cannot replicate. Increased AI adoption across the economy may drive incremental energy and chemicals demand (AI data centers consume electricity and cooling chemicals), providing indirect tailwind. The energy transition represents a broader structural risk, but this is driven by policy and economics rather than AI specifically.
