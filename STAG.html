Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Industrial ·NYSE ·United States
STAG Industrial is a real estate investment trust focused on the acquisition, development, ownership, and operation of single-tenant industrial properties throughout the United States. The portfolio consists of approximately 601 buildings in 41 states with approximately 120 million rentable square feet, serving logistics, e-commerce, and light manufacturing tenants. The company recently transitioned from monthly to quarterly dividend distributions and achieved Core FFO per diluted share of $0.65 in Q2 2026.

Investment Thesis
STAG Industrial offers a reliable 3.95% dividend yield backed by a 601-building portfolio of single-tenant industrial properties diversified across 41 states with 120M rentable square feet [1]. Revenue grew 10.1% YoY to $845M in 2025 with earnings up 44.6%, and Q2 2026 Core FFO per diluted share of $0.65 grew 3.2% YoY [1][2]. The industrial REIT sector benefits from secular tailwinds in e-commerce fulfillment, reshoring, and AI-driven logistics demand that support occupancy and rental rate growth. STAG's Inspire score of 58 (ALIGNED) with 13 positive attributions and zero negative makes it one of the strongest faith-aligned names in industrial real estate. The recent dividend increase to $1.55/share annual rate signals management confidence [2].
The bear case centers on stagnant dividend growth and REIT structural limitations. The dividend has grown just 0.52% annually over 5 years — effectively zero in real terms — meaning purchasing power erodes over time despite the attractive 3.95% headline yield [5]. The payout ratio near 100% of GAAP earnings (though covered by FFO/AFFO) leaves minimal margin of safety if occupancy declines or cap rates expand in a sustained higher-rate environment. Forward P/E of 32.83 overstates valuation concerns (P/FFO of ~14.9x is more representative), but ROE of 7.17% reflects the capital-intensive nature of the business and limits total return potential [5]. STAG's single-tenant focus creates binary occupancy risk — when a tenant vacates, the entire building produces zero revenue until re-leased. Same-store NOI growth guidance of 3.5% for 2026 (down from 4.0-4.25% in 2025) suggests decelerating organic momentum. Rising interest rates increase borrowing costs for the acquisition-driven growth model that underpins portfolio expansion [5]. Net insider selling by CEO and senior executives is a cautionary signal.
Key Catalysts
  1. Secular demand growth from e-commerce fulfillment automation, data center expansion, and manufacturing reshoring driving occupancy, rental rate increases, and tenant demand across STAG's 120M sq ft portfolio [1][3]
  2. Dividend increase from $1.49 to $1.55/share annual rate alongside the monthly-to-quarterly distribution transition demonstrates management confidence and potential inflection in dividend growth trajectory [2]
  3. New development pipeline including the Dallas project and data center-adjacent opportunities could drive higher-return growth beyond the acquisition-only model, with COO Kimball's operational scalability mandate as a catalyst [3]
Key Risks
  1. Near-zero dividend growth (0.52% 5yr CAGR) erodes real purchasing power for income investors — if same-store NOI growth decelerates to 3.5% and interest rates remain elevated, dividend growth may remain stagnant indefinitely [5]
  2. Single-tenant industrial lease structure creates binary vacancy risk — when a tenant vacates, revenue from that building drops to zero until re-leased, with 601 single-tenant buildings each representing an individual credit risk [1]
  3. Net insider selling by CEO and senior executives following the Q2 2026 earnings beat, combined with 97% payout ratio and $3.1B in debt against $34M cash, raises concerns about balance sheet flexibility in a rising-rate environment [5]
