Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
StepStone Group is a global private-markets investment solutions firm providing advisory, fund management, and data analytics across private equity, infrastructure, real estate, and private debt for institutional and, increasingly, individual investors. Founded in 2007 and headquartered in New York, the firm had $233.3B in assets under management as of March 31, 2026 (up 23% YoY), with fee-related earnings growing 14% to $354.4M and management/advisory fees up 21% to $926.5M for fiscal 2026. StepStone went public in September 2020 and pays a quarterly dividend.

Investment Thesis
StepStone Group's AUM grew 23% YoY to $233.3B and fee-related earnings -- the firm's core recurring profitability metric -- grew 14% to $354.4M in fiscal 2026, while management and advisory fees grew 21% to $926.5M, demonstrating durable, largely recurring revenue growth independent of volatile performance fees [1]. The build-out of a private-wealth distribution channel opens access to a vastly larger pool of individual investors beyond StepStone's traditional institutional client base, and Forward P/E of roughly 13x is inexpensive relative to the firm's underlying fee growth. The 3.88% dividend yield, paid consistently since the firm's September 2020 IPO with 5 consecutive years of increases, adds an income component atop the growth story [4].
The bear case centers on GAAP optics and a challenging internal culture. GAAP ROE of -61.4% and profit margin of -30.5% reflect fund-consolidation accounting and carried-interest revaluations rather than the underlying cash-generative fee business, but they create real headline risk and complicate screening for GAAP-focused investors, and the roughly 167% GAAP payout ratio raises legitimate questions about dividend sustainability if fee-related-earnings growth slows [1][4]. Glassdoor reviews describing talent management as 'random at best, destructive at worst,' with well-functioning teams dismantled under 'zero transparency,' suggest meaningful key-person and retention risk in a business built on relationship-driven deal flow [3]. D/E of 126.5% is elevated for a firm with volatile GAAP earnings, and a sustained downturn in private-markets fundraising -- driven by higher rates or LP liquidity constraints -- could slow the 21% organic fee-earning AUM CAGR that underpins the entire growth thesis [1][2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued fee-earning AUM growth at or near the 21% organic CAGR maintained since FY2021, as institutional allocations to private markets continue rising [1]
  2. Private-wealth/evergreen fund distribution scaling further, opening a large incremental addressable market of individual investors beyond StepStone's traditional institutional base [1][2]
  3. Normalization of GAAP earnings as carried-interest/fund-consolidation items stabilize, which could close the gap between GAAP optics and the underlying 14% fee-related-earnings growth [1]
Key Risks
  1. GAAP payout ratio of roughly 167% alongside negative GAAP ROE/profit margin creates real dividend-sustainability questions if fee-related-earnings growth decelerates, even though the underlying fee business remains healthy [1][4]
  2. Glassdoor evidence of destructive, non-transparent talent management creates key-person and retention risk in a relationship- and deal-flow-driven business [3]
  3. A cyclical slowdown in private-markets fundraising -- from higher rates or LP liquidity constraints -- could slow AUM and fee growth below the 21% organic CAGR baked into the current valuation [1][2]
