Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
StepStone Group is a global private markets investment solutions firm providing customized advisory, data analytics, and fund management services across private equity, real estate, infrastructure, and private debt. As of March 31, 2026, the firm had $233.3 billion in assets under management and $651.8 billion in assets under advisement, bringing total capital responsibility to $885.2 billion. Founded in 2007 and headquartered in New York, StepStone went public in September 2020 and serves institutional investors including pensions, sovereigns, endowments, and family offices.

Investment Thesis
StepStone Group at $41.13 with $885B in total capital responsibility, fee-related earnings growing 14% to $354.4M, and a first-ever quarter exceeding $100M in FRE offers exposure to the secular growth in private markets allocations [1]. Management fees grew 21% to $926.5M in FY2026, demonstrating durable recurring revenue growth independent of volatile performance fees [1]. The 2.72% dividend yield with ~15% 5-year growth CAGR and $5.0M in recent insider buying provide income and insider conviction [4]. The Inspire Impact Score of 49 with 16 positive attributions and zero negative makes StepStone one of the strongest faith-aligned names in financial services. With institutional investors continuing to increase private markets allocations from ~30% toward 40%+ of total portfolios, StepStone's advisory-led model is positioned to capture outsized growth [2].
The bear case centers on GAAP optics and valuation vulnerability. GAAP net losses of $535.8M in FY2026 — 198% worse than the prior year — reflect unrealized carried interest reversals that could persist through volatile markets, creating headline risk and potentially pressuring the stock [1]. D/E of 150% is elevated for a firm with volatile GAAP earnings, and the $382M in debt against $244M in cash leaves negative net cash of -$138M [4]. The private markets fundraising environment faces cyclical headwinds from rising rates and LP liquidity constraints, which could slow AUM growth and compress fee-earning assets. GAAP profit margin of -17.1% and ROE of -12.81% obscure the underlying profitability but create challenges for systematic investors who screen on GAAP metrics. The stock has declined from its highs, and at ~19x adjusted earnings, the valuation assumes continued strong growth in fee-related revenue without performance fee volatility.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued private markets allocation growth toward 40%+ of institutional portfolios, driving organic fee-earning AUM growth at the 21% CAGR maintained since FY2021 and expanding StepStone's management fee base [1][2]

Key Risks

  3. Key-person dependency on CEO Scott Hart and co-founder Monte Brem, combined with Glassdoor reviews citing cultural concerns, creates retention risk for the relationship-driven investment professionals who generate proprietary deal flow [3]
