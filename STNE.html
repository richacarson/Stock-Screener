Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NMS ·Cayman Islands
StoneCo is a Brazilian financial technology company providing payment processing, digital banking, and credit solutions to merchants across in-store, online, and mobile channels. Operating under the Stone and Pagar.me brands, the company serves small and medium businesses throughout Brazil with TTM revenue of approximately R$13.6B and a 24.7% profit margin. Founded in 2012 and headquartered in George Town, Cayman Islands, StoneCo reported Q2 2026 revenue of R$3.59B (up 2.5% YoY) with adjusted net income of R$582.7M [1].

Investment Thesis
StoneCo at $12.07 with a $2.8B market cap is a deeply undervalued Brazilian fintech trading at just 5.1x forward earnings — one of the lowest multiples in global payments — with a 24.7% profit margin, 34.0% ROE, and a credit business doubling annually [1]. Q1 2026 delivered R$3.58B revenue (+6.5% YoY) with credit revenue surging 186% YoY to R$297M, and the credit portfolio reached R$3.8B including R$300M in government-backed loans, demonstrating successful expansion from payments into lending [2]. The integrated platform serving millions of Brazilian SMBs across payments, banking, and credit creates significant cross-selling opportunities and merchant lock-in, while the company's conservative 2026 guidance (adj. gross profit R$4.6-7B, adj. basic EPS R$10.8-11.4) provides a clear earnings floor.
The bear case centers on decelerating topline growth and credit risk in a challenging macro environment. Q2 2026 revenue grew just 2.5% YoY to R$3.59B while adjusted net income declined 2.6%, and the company is guiding toward the lower end of 2026 targets due to higher-for-longer interest rates [1]. The credit expansion, while strategically compelling, exposes StoneCo to material loan-loss provisions in a Brazilian economy with elevated rates, and the credit portfolio's rapid growth (2x YoY) has not been stress-tested through a full credit cycle. The March 2026 CEO transition introduces execution risk during a critical scaling phase, and the shareholder class action related to the 2021 PAX Global raid remains unresolved [3][4]. Competition from Nubank (which has significantly more capital and a larger customer base) and bank-owned acquirers with lower funding costs could compress StoneCo's credit margins. The stock's extreme discount (4.7x trailing PE) may reflect legitimate market concerns about earnings sustainability rather than a simple mispricing opportunity.
Key Catalysts
  1. Credit portfolio scaling from R$3.8B toward R$5B+ as government-backed lending programs and improved underwriting expand the addressable market, with credit revenue already growing 186% YoY and representing the primary topline growth driver [2]
  2. Potential re-rating from 5x forward earnings toward the 10-15x range typical of profitable fintechs if the company demonstrates credit quality through a full rate cycle and revenue growth reaccelerates above mid-single digits [1]
  3. New CEO leadership effective March 2026 could bring fresh strategic direction, with 2027 guidance calling for meaningful acceleration to adj. gross profit of R$7.2-8.3B and adj. EPS of R$11.8-13.4 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Credit loss provisions could spike materially if Brazil's high interest rate environment persists or worsens, with the R$3.8B credit portfolio having doubled in just one year without experiencing a full credit cycle stress test [1][2]
  2. Revenue growth deceleration from 6.5% in Q1 to 2.5% in Q2 2026 suggests the core payments business is near-saturated in current market conditions, with earnings growth now negative at -16.6% YoY [1]
  3. CEO transition in March 2026, unresolved shareholder class action, and Brazil's volatile macroeconomic environment (currency, rates, inflation) create compounding governance and country risk for a Cayman Islands-domiciled entity [3][4]
