StubHub Holdings operates the world's largest ticketing marketplace for live events, enabling buying and selling of tickets through websites and mobile applications under the StubHub and viagogo brands. The company holds approximately 50% North American secondary ticketing market share and raised full-year GMS guidance to $10.1-10.3B following strong Q2 2026 results. Investment Thesis
StubHub is the dominant secondary ticketing marketplace with approximately 50% North American market share and accelerating fundamentals. Q2 2026 was a breakout quarter: revenue surged 33% YoY to $573.1M, adjusted EBITDA expanded to $105.7M (18% margin, up 600bps), and net income flipped to $14.6M from a -$53.8M loss [1]. Management raised GMS guidance to $10.1-10.3B (10-12% growth) and maintained $400-420M adjusted EBITDA guidance, implying H2 margin expansion [1]. The stock at $7.11 — down 70% from its $23.50 IPO price — trades at just 12.3x forward earnings with improving profitability trajectory. New revenue streams from advertising ('tens of millions' expected in 2026) and the OpenAI partnership for agentic commerce provide incremental growth catalysts [1][3]. World Cup demand in Q2 demonstrated the platform's leverage to marquee global events.
The bear case revolves around AI disruption risk, cultural dysfunction, valuation legitimacy, and regulatory headwinds. StubHub's own SEC filing warns of 'Google Zero' risk — AI-driven search evolution and agentic AI could bypass marketplace intermediation, threatening the web traffic model that drives transactions [2]. Glassdoor's 2.5/5 rating with only 22% of employees recommending the company, 'incredibly toxic' culture reviews, and 2.2/5 culture and values scores suggest deep organizational rot that strong Q2 numbers cannot paper over [2]. The 70% decline from IPO price reflects either a dramatically overpriced IPO ($16.5B target valuation) or genuine structural concerns about the business model's durability — likely both. GAAP ROE remains deeply negative at -109% from the $1.4B IPO SBC charge, and the company has yet to demonstrate a full year of GAAP profitability as a public company. The $10M FTC settlement for deceptive fee practices signals regulatory scrutiny of secondary ticketing that could intensify, potentially compressing take rates [5]. Revenue concentration — Q2's 33% surge was heavily driven by one-time World Cup demand, and underlying growth ex-World Cup may be much lower.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $400-420M execution with continued margin expansion would demonstrate operating leverage and justify re-rating from the current 70% IPO discount [1]
  2. Advertising revenue scaling toward 'tens of millions' in 2026 introduces a new high-margin revenue stream that could materially improve unit economics without requiring GMS growth [1]
  3. OpenAI/ChatGPT agentic commerce partnership could transform StubHub from a destination marketplace into an embedded ticketing platform within AI assistants, converting the AI disruption threat into a first-mover advantage [3]
Key Risks
  1. AI-driven search disruption ('Google Zero') — StubHub's reliance on internet search engines for traffic is a self-acknowledged risk factor as AI assistants and agentic commerce could disintermediate traditional marketplace discovery models [2]
  2. Organizational dysfunction — 2.5/5 Glassdoor rating, 22% employee recommendation rate, 2.2/5 culture and values, and 'incredibly toxic' culture reviews create execution risk during a critical post-IPO scaling phase [2]
  3. Revenue concentration in marquee events — Q2 2026's 33% revenue surge was heavily driven by FIFA World Cup demand, and underlying growth trajectory ex-World Cup may be materially lower, making the full-year guidance achievable but not a sustainable growth rate [1]
