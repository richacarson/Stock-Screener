Company Profile
Consumer Staples ·Beverages — Alcoholic ·NYSE ·United States
Constellation Brands, Inc. is a leading international producer and marketer of beer, wine, and spirits with operations in the U.S., Mexico, New Zealand, and Italy. The company's beer portfolio includes high-growth Mexican import brands Modelo Especial (the #1 beer brand in the U.S. by dollar sales) and Corona. The wine and spirits segment includes brands such as Kim Crawford, Meiomi, The Prisoner, and High West. Constellation previously held a significant stake in cannabis company Canopy Growth, which was largely written down. Headquartered in Victor, New York.
9,400 Employees ·www.cbrands.com

Investment Thesis
Constellation Brands trades at a compelling 11.36x forward earnings with a 3.06% dividend yield and 10 consecutive years of dividend growth, offering value characteristics for a consumer staples franchise anchored by the #1 beer brand in the U.S. (Modelo Especial) [1][2]. Q4 FY2026 results beat expectations with adjusted EPS of $1.90 vs. $1.72 expected, and net income of $224.7M recovered from a $370.6M loss in the year-ago quarter. The beer segment's 33.6% operating margin reflects exceptional brand pricing power, and the disciplined 39.1% payout ratio leaves room for continued dividend growth. New CEO Nicholas Fink brings digital transformation experience from Fortune Brands Innovations, potentially catalyzing operational efficiency improvements across the portfolio [3].
The bear case is significant: FY2026 revenue declined 10.5% year-over-year to $9.14B, the worst top-line performance in years, driven by wine and spirits weakness and slowing beer volume momentum [1]. D/E of 123% with $10.53B in debt against only $96.6M in cash creates substantial balance sheet risk, particularly if beer growth stalls. The 5-year revenue CAGR of just 1% reveals that Constellation's growth story is largely a beer-only narrative — the wine and spirits segments are structurally declining. Revenue growth is forecast at only 2.5% annually going forward. The CEO transition creates execution risk, and the failed Canopy Growth investment destroyed over $4B in shareholder value, raising questions about capital allocation discipline. PEG ratio of 3.33 suggests the stock is not cheap relative to its actual growth rate.
Key Catalysts
  1. New CEO Nicholas Fink (April 2026) bringing digital transformation and operational efficiency expertise from Fortune Brands Innovations, potentially driving margin improvement across the $9.14B revenue base
  2. Modelo Especial continuing to gain market share as the #1 U.S. beer brand with potential for further premiumization and expanded distribution in the on-premise channel
  3. Continued dividend growth with a sustainable 39.1% payout ratio and potential for accelerated buybacks as wine/spirits divestitures free up capital
Key Risks
  1. FY2026 revenue decline of 10.5% may signal structural deceleration in the beer segment, not just wine/spirits weakness — if Modelo/Corona volume growth stalls, the premium valuation unwinds
  2. Balance sheet leverage of 123% D/E with $10.53B debt and only $96.6M cash creates refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment and limits strategic flexibility for acquisitions
  3. CEO transition risk as Nicholas Fink takes over a company he did not build, with no beverage alcohol industry experience and questions about his strategic vision for the wine/spirits portfolio

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Constellation Brands' core business — brewing, importing, and distributing beer, wine, and spirits — is fundamentally immune to AI disruption [1]. AI cannot replace the consumption of beer and wine. Brand loyalty in alcoholic beverages — particularly for Modelo and Corona — creates deep consumer moats that are reinforced by cultural identity and social occasions, not technology.
