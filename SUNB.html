Company Profile
Industrials ·Rental & Leasing Services (Capital Goods) ·NYSE (primary) / London Stock Exchange (secondary) ·United States
Sunbelt Rentals is the second-largest construction, industrial, and general equipment rental company in North America, operating over 1,175 locations across the U.S., U.K., and Canada. On February 27, 2026, former parent Ashtead Group completed a scheme of arrangement redomiciling the business as U.S.-listed Sunbelt Rentals Holdings, Inc., with a primary NYSE listing and secondary LSE listing. Fiscal 2026 total revenue reached $11,154M with 3.4% rental revenue growth (specialty rental +5.8%, general tool +2.1%), 41.9% adjusted EBITDA margin, and $2,055M free cash flow, though net income and EPS both declined YoY, partly reflecting redomiciliation-related costs [1].

Investment Thesis
Sunbelt Rentals is the #2 North American equipment rental operator with $11.2B in FY2026 revenue, a 41.9% adjusted EBITDA margin, and $2,055M of free cash flow, now trading as a US-domiciled, NYSE-primary-listed company following the February 2026 Ashtead redomiciliation [1]. Specialty rental — the faster-growing, higher-margin segment — expanded 5.8% in FY2026 versus 2.1% for general tool rental, and management's FY2027 guidance of 5-8% rental revenue growth, combined with the Reliant acquisition and 95% fleet telematics coverage, points to continued share gains and operating efficiency. A Forward P/E of ~17x and PEG of 1.47 are reasonable for a company with 17.46% ROE and a fortress position as the clear #2 player behind United Rentals in a large, fragmented rental market.
The bear case centers on cyclicality, leverage, and moderating growth. FY2026 net income and EPS both declined YoY, partly reflecting one-time redomiciliation costs, and overall rental revenue growth of 3.4% is below the 10% target for a Growth-sleeve stock, signaling a maturing rental cycle after the post-pandemic construction boom [1]. D/E of 102.35% is meaningfully above the 70% Infrastructure target, reflecting the fleet-financing leverage inherent to the rental model, which leaves less balance-sheet cushion if construction activity slows. Equipment rental is highly cyclical and correlated to non-residential construction spending, interest rates, and infrastructure capex — a downturn in any of these would directly pressure utilization rates and pricing. Finally, Sunbelt's mission and public communications skew toward competitive positioning rather than an articulated Just Cause, and no evidence of meaningful insider conviction buying (only routine equity-compensation activity) was found to signal management's own confidence in the stock at current levels.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2027 rental revenue growth guidance of 5-8%, which if delivered would represent meaningful reacceleration from FY2026's 3.4% growth and support further margin expansion [1]
  2. Continued specialty-rental segment outperformance (+5.8% vs. +2.1% general tool in FY2026) as Sunbelt shifts mix toward higher-margin, less commoditized rental categories, aided by the Reliant acquisition [1]
  3. Potential valuation re-rating and improved trading liquidity following the February 2026 US redomiciliation, which moved Sunbelt's primary listing and index eligibility to the deeper, typically higher-multiple US capital markets [1]
Key Risks
  1. Cyclical exposure to non-residential construction activity and infrastructure capex — a slowdown would directly pressure fleet utilization, rental rates, and the 41.9% adjusted EBITDA margin [1]
  2. Elevated leverage (D/E ~102%) relative to the Infrastructure target, driven by fleet-financing debt, which reduces balance-sheet flexibility if a downturn coincides with high interest rates
  3. FY2026 net income and EPS both declined YoY even as revenue grew, partly reflecting one-time redomiciliation costs — a reminder that the structural transition carries real near-term earnings drag that needs to prove transitory [1]
