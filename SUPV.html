Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NYSE ·Argentina
Grupo Supervielle is an Argentine financial services holding company operating the fourth-largest private domestically-owned bank in Argentina by assets. Founded in 1887, it provides banking, insurance, asset management, and digital financial services through a multi-brand, multi-channel platform. Key subsidiaries include Banco Supervielle, invertirOnline (digital investment platform), and Supervielle Seguros (insurance). Headquartered in Buenos Aires.

Investment Thesis
Supervielle offers deep-value exposure to Argentine normalization at ~0.86x book: Q2 2026 returned to profit (AR$12.8B, 4.4% ROAE; 14.4% structural ROAE excluding rightsizing effects), NIM expanded to 20.3% from 17.7% as funding costs fell, and the NPL ratio eased to 5.5% [1]. Management targets ROE approaching 15% in 2027 as severance costs roll off and the leaner cost base (52.3% structural efficiency) shows through [2].
The bear case is that the earnings recovery depends on Argentine macro stability: 2026 reported ROE guidance is only 2-4%, real loan-growth guidance was cut to 10-15%, gross loans fell 1.4% sequentially, and management expects NIM pressure in H2 2026 as corporate assets reprice [1][2]. Cost of risk remains elevated at 5.3-5.8%, ARS devaluation or policy reversal could erase USD returns, and TTM ROE is still negative at -4.8% [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results (late November) showing structural ROE progress toward the 2027 ~15% target as severance costs fade [2]
  2. Continued disinflation and lower funding costs in Argentina supporting NIM above the 17-19% guidance range [1][2]
  3. Growth in IOL digital brokerage and dollar-denominated commercial lending [4][5]
Key Risks
  1. Argentine macro/political shock (peso devaluation, capital controls, rate spikes) that reverses the normalization thesis
  2. Asset-quality deterioration with NPLs at 5.5% and cost of risk guided at 5.3-5.8% [1][2]
  3. H2 2026 NIM compression as corporate assets reprice, combined with cut loan-growth guidance [2]
