Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Paper & Paper Products ·NYSE ·Brazil
Suzano S.A. is the world's largest producer of eucalyptus pulp, also manufacturing paper, tissue products, and lignin-based biomaterials. The company manages approximately 2.7 million hectares of land in Brazil, including eucalyptus plantations and native conservation areas. Q2 2026 net revenue was R$11.6B with adjusted EBITDA of R$4.7B, driven by higher pulp prices and volumes. Suzano sold a record 12.7 million tonnes of pulp in the trailing 12 months. TTM revenue of $9.23B and net income of $1.58B. Founded in 1924, headquartered in Salvador, Bahia, Brazil. Market cap approximately $10.8B.

Investment Thesis
Suzano is the world's dominant eucalyptus pulp producer trading at deep value — forward P/E of 5.97x and trailing P/E of 7.04x on TTM revenue of $9.23B and net income of $1.58B [1][5]. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of R$4.7B was driven by higher pulp prices and record volumes, with the company selling 3.3M tonnes of pulp and paper combined in the quarter. The Cerrado mill completion adds capacity to capture rising global pulp demand, while the bioeconomy strategy (biomaterials, carbon removal, sustainable packaging) provides long-term optionality [2]. The 2.18% dividend yield with an 11% payout ratio offers both current income and significant dividend growth potential — the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of 35.31% is among the highest in the materials sector. ROE of 17.6% and profit margin of 17.1% reflect solid profitability for a commodity producer.
The primary risks are cyclical, structural, and ESG-related. D/E of 199% reflects heavy leverage (3.4x in USD terms) in a cyclical commodity business where pulp prices can swing 30-40% in a cycle — a severe price downturn could stress debt service significantly [1]. Revenue growth YoY of approximately 5% in USD terms masks the impact of BRL appreciation compressing dollar-denominated revenue, and ADR investors carry persistent currency risk that is difficult to hedge. ESG controversies present material risk: the Environmental Paper Network documents displacement of indigenous communities, eucalyptus monocultures depleting water resources in the Cerrado savanna, and 1,000 women from the Landless Workers' Movement occupied Suzano land in March 2025 to protest environmental damage [3][4]. If ESG-linked financing (comprising a substantial portion of total debt) faces scrutiny, refinancing costs could rise. Profit margin of 17.1% is solid but below the 20% target, and the commodity nature of pulp limits pricing power when supply expands globally. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects incomplete coverage rather than strong alignment.
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year Cerrado mill ramp driving continued record pulp volumes and operating leverage, with management expecting sustained demand from packaging and tissue end-markets in H2 2026 [1]
  2. Bioeconomy strategy advancement through Suzano Ventures portfolio companies and Mitsui partnership, potentially unlocking higher-margin lignin-based biomaterial revenue streams beyond commodity pulp [2]
  3. Post-Cerrado CapEx reduction expected to drive significant free cash flow improvement in 2026-2027, supporting deleveraging from 3.4x and potential for increased dividend distributions given the 11% payout ratio
Key Risks
  1. D/E of 199% and 3.4x USD leverage creates vulnerability to pulp price downcycles — a 30% decline in pulp prices could compress margins and stress debt service on the capital-intensive balance sheet [1]
  2. ESG controversies (indigenous community displacement, water depletion from eucalyptus monocultures, Landless Workers' Movement land occupation in 2025) risk reputational damage, regulatory action, and potential loss of ESG-linked financing at favorable rates [3][4]
  3. BRL/USD currency dynamics create a structural headwind for ADR investors — BRL appreciation compresses dollar-denominated revenue while costs remain BRL-denominated, and the company's leverage is partially BRL-denominated, adding currency mismatch risk [1]
