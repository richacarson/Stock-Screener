Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Hotel & Motel ·NASDAQ ·United States
Service Properties Trust is an externally managed REIT with approximately $10 billion invested in hotels and service-focused retail net lease properties. As of Q1 2026, SVC operates a reduced portfolio following the sale of 112 hotels in 2025, retaining approximately 94 hotels with 21,000+ guest rooms and 760 net lease properties with 13.6M+ square feet across the U.S., Puerto Rico, and Canada. Managed by The RMR Group. The company executed a ~5:1 reverse stock split and raised approximately $1.5B through capital markets activity to retire $1.6B of debt, reducing annual interest expense by $59M.

Investment Thesis
SVC is executing a significant balance sheet restructuring that has materially improved its near-term survival prospects since the last screen. The company used $1.5B in capital markets activity to retire $1.6B of debt, cutting annual cash interest expense by $59M [1]. At $8.46 post-reverse-split, the stock trades at 0.58x book value ($14.69/share), implying the market assigns deep distress to the equity. FY2026 guidance of $500-520M adjusted EBITDAre and $124-144M normalized FFO shows the business generates positive operating cash flow. The net lease portfolio provides $380-386M guided NOI as a stable income base, and RevPAR growth of 3.5% is projected to outpace the 1.4% industry forecast, suggesting retained hotels are higher-quality assets following the disposition program [1]. CEO Bilotto's insider purchases align management with shareholders. Analyst consensus is Buy with a $12.19 target (+44% upside) [4].
The bear case centers on persistent leverage, dilution damage, and structural challenges. D/E of 1,030% with approximately $5B remaining debt creates ongoing refinancing risk, and short interest of 17.3% reflects deep market skepticism about the restructuring [4]. Q1 2026 revenue declined 16.3% YoY with hotel revenue down 21%, and net loss deepened to $151M including impairment and debt extinguishment charges [3]. The equity offering more than doubled the share count through massive dilution — with reverse-split-adjusted shares now at 129.53M — making any recovery in per-share metrics mathematically challenging. Normalized FFO of $0.04/share in Q1 2026 ($0.20 annualized post-split) barely covers the $0.20 annual dividend, and full-year guidance implies back-half loaded results that may not materialize [1]. External management by RMR Group creates agency conflicts — base management fees are earned regardless of stock performance, reducing management incentive to prioritize equity holders. The dividend of $0.20/year (2.36% yield) is a token payment that provides no meaningful income return, and further cuts cannot be ruled out if hotel operations deteriorate.
