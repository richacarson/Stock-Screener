Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Silver Mining ·NYSE ·Canada
Silvercorp Metals is a Canadian mid-tier silver mining company with primary operations in China's Henan and Guangdong provinces, and development projects in Ecuador (El Domo copper-gold) and Kyrgyzstan. Founded in 2003 by Dr. Rui Feng, the company reported record fiscal 2026 revenue of $438.1M (+47% YoY) driven by a 72% surge in realized silver prices, with adjusted net income of $150.8M ($0.69/share) and operating cash flow of $310.6M. GAAP net income swung to a $9.9M loss due to a $60M non-cash derivative charge on convertible notes.

Investment Thesis
Silvercorp at $9.76 with a $2.2B market cap is a silver-leveraged play benefiting from surging precious metals prices — FY2026 revenue hit a record $438.1M (+47% YoY) driven by a 72% increase in realized silver prices, adjusted net income reached $150.8M ($0.69/share), and operating cash flow surged to $310.6M [1][2]. The El Domo copper-gold project in Ecuador adds commodity diversification and a growth runway, while FY2027 guidance for 6.8-7.1 million ounces of silver production and ore processing of 1.53-1.61 million tonnes provides near-term visibility. The company's low-cost Chinese operations and expanding multi-jurisdiction portfolio position it to capture further upside if silver prices remain elevated.
The bear case centers on commodity price dependence and geographic concentration risk. The 47% revenue surge was almost entirely driven by higher silver prices rather than volume growth, making earnings highly volatile if silver retreats from current levels [1]. GAAP net income swung to a $9.9M loss due to a $60M non-cash derivative charge on convertible notes, highlighting financial structuring risks from the debt used to fund acquisitions [2]. Operations remain heavily concentrated in China (Henan and Guangdong provinces), exposing the company to Chinese regulatory, environmental, and geopolitical risks. The management team's short average tenure of 2.5 years raises operational continuity concerns, and the El Domo project in Ecuador carries development-stage execution risk in a politically complex jurisdiction [3][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained elevated silver prices — realized silver prices surged 72% in FY2026, and continued monetary policy easing, industrial demand from AI data centers and solar panels, and safe-haven flows could maintain or extend the rally [1]
  2. El Domo copper-gold project advancement — production commencement in Ecuador adds a second commodity stream and reduces dependence on Chinese silver operations [1][2]
  3. FY2027 production growth guidance of 6.8-7.1M silver ounces with ore processing expanding to 1.53-1.61M tonnes signals organic volume growth alongside price-driven revenue gains [2]
Key Risks
  1. Silver price reversal — the 47% revenue surge was driven primarily by a 72% increase in realized silver prices rather than production growth, making earnings extremely sensitive to commodity price declines [1]
  2. China concentration and geopolitical risk — the majority of operations are in Chinese provinces, exposing the company to regulatory changes, environmental enforcement, and US-China geopolitical tensions that could affect capital flows or operations [3]
  3. Acquisition integration and development execution risk — the El Domo project in Ecuador is still in development, and the Adventus Mining acquisition added financial complexity including convertible notes that generated a $60M non-cash derivative charge in Q4 FY2026 [1][2]
