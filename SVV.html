Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Savers Value Village is the largest for-profit thrift store operator in the United States and Canada, also operating in Australia. The company purchases secondhand textiles, shoes, housewares, books, and other goods from nonprofit partners, then processes, prices, and sells them through approximately 315 stores under the Savers, Value Village, Value Village Boutique, Village des Valeurs, Unique, and 2nd Avenue brands. Founded in 1954 and headquartered in Bellevue, Washington, SVV reported TTM revenue of approximately $1.74B with Q2 2026 revenue up 7.4% YoY to $448M. The company IPO'd on the NYSE in June 2023.
24,000 Employees ·www.savers.com

Investment Thesis
Savers Value Village at 15.23x forward earnings offers an affordable entry into the largest for-profit thrift operator in North America, with a genuinely differentiated business model built on 70 years of nonprofit partnerships and growing consumer appetite for secondhand goods [1][3]. Q2 2026 delivered 7.4% revenue growth to $448M with improving profitability — adjusted EBITDA margin of 16.6% and full-year EPS guidance of $0.47-$0.53 represent meaningful progress from the IPO era. The thrift/circular economy tailwind is secular: consumers increasingly value sustainability and value-priced alternatives, and Savers' unique nonprofit supply chain is nearly impossible to replicate. Revenue guidance of $1.77-$1.79B implies continued store expansion and same-store sales growth. The stock's decline from $20+ post-IPO to $9.21 may represent an opportunity if margins continue expanding [4].
The bear case centers on unsustainable leverage and profitability concerns. D/E of 323% with $1.44B total debt against a $1.4B market cap reflects legacy private equity leveraged buyout financing that generates ~$80M+ in annual interest expense, compressing net margins to just 1.4% [4]. Short interest of 23.8% signals significant market skepticism about the sustainability of the current financial structure. Free cash flow of only $37.5M against $1.44B debt implies a 38-year paydown timeline at current rates. ROE of just 5.7% is well below the cost of equity, indicating the business does not currently earn its keep for equity holders. CEO Walsh's 35-43% approval rating on Glassdoor/Indeed suggests workforce engagement challenges in a labor-intensive retail operation with 24,000 employees [2]. Online resale platforms (ThredUp, Poshmark) backed by AI-powered pricing and convenience continue gaining share, and any economic recovery could shift consumer spending away from thrift toward new retail, weakening same-store sales trends [1].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Savers' core business — physical collection, processing, pricing, and in-store retail of secondhand goods — is deeply resistant to AI substitution [1]. The treasure-hunting experience of thrift shopping, the tactile nature of evaluating secondhand items, and the physical supply chain dependent on nonprofit-donated goods create structural barriers that AI cannot replicate. The business requires physical stores, manual sorting of unique items, and local community relationships. However, AI-powered online resale platforms (ThredUp's AI pricing, Poshmark's AI recommendations) represent an emerging competitive threat by making secondhand shopping more convenient and personalized, potentially shifting consumer behavior away from in-store browsing. AI Opportunity (0/1): Savers does not generate revenue from AI products or services and has not publicly disclosed AI deployment for internal operations [2].
