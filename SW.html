Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Packaging & Containers ·NYQ ·Ireland
Smurfit WestRock plc is the world's largest listed integrated packaging company, formed through the July 2024 merger of Smurfit Kappa Group (Ireland) and WestRock Company (United States). The company manufactures and distributes containerboard, corrugated containers, and other paper-based packaging products across 40+ countries through approximately 500 converting operations and 62 paper mills. Headquartered in Dublin, Ireland, Smurfit WestRock generated full-year 2025 net sales of $31.18B with net income of $699M and has surpassed its $400M annual run-rate synergy target from the merger integration. Q2 2026 delivered net sales of $8.03B with adjusted EBITDA of $1.14B.

Investment Thesis
Smurfit WestRock at $41.16 with a $21.6B market cap is the world's largest listed integrated packaging company trading at just 11.7x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.26, offering deep value as merger-distorted trailing metrics normalize toward combined-entity earnings power [1]. The company generated $31.18B in 2025 net sales and has surpassed its $400M annual synergy target from the July 2024 merger, with additional cost savings and revenue synergies expected as integration progresses through 2027. The 4.4% dividend yield provides meaningful current income, and management guided 2026 EBITDA of $5.0B-$5.3B, implying ~16% EBITDA margins as merger charges fade. The global footprint across 40+ countries with 62 paper mills and ~500 converting operations creates an unmatched integrated supply chain for corrugated packaging, and secular growth in e-commerce packaging and sustainable packaging regulation provides structural demand tailwinds.

Revenue growth of only 1.1% YoY indicates the underlying packaging market is soft, with containerboard pricing under pressure from elevated industry capacity. D/E of 78.4% constrains capital allocation flexibility during integration. The cross-continental merger of Irish and American corporate cultures creates ongoing integration risk across 96,000 employees and 40+ country operations, and facility closures may alienate key talent during the critical integration period [2][3].
