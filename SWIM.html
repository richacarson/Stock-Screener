Company Profile
Industrials ·Building Products & Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Latham Group is the largest designer, manufacturer, and marketer of in-ground residential swimming pools in North America, Australia, and New Zealand. The company operates through three product lines: fiberglass and packaged pools, automatic safety and all-season pool covers, and pool liners, sold through a network of dealers and distributors under the Latham, Narellan, CoverStar, Radiant, and GLI brands. Founded in 1956 and headquartered in Latham, New York, SWIM reported TTM revenue of $577M with Q2 2026 revenue up 14.4% YoY to $197.5M, led by 22.5% growth in fiberglass pool revenue. Fiberglass pools are on track to reach approximately 25% of new U.S. pool starts in 2026.

Investment Thesis
Latham Group at $6.23 ($733M market cap) offers leveraged exposure to the structural conversion from concrete to fiberglass in-ground pools — fiberglass now represents ~25% of new U.S. pool starts and is growing, with Latham as the dominant category leader posting 22.5% fiberglass revenue growth in Q2 2026 against a flat overall pool market [1]. Full-year 2026 guidance of $580-610M revenue and $105-120M adjusted EBITDA represents meaningful recovery from the 2023-2024 trough. The company achieved 14% total sales growth despite flat industry conditions, demonstrating clear market share capture. FCF of $51.6M (7.0% yield), current ratio of 2.16, and D/E of 76% provide reasonable financial stability. New CEO Sean Gadd (January 2026) brings 30+ years of building products experience with a mandate for commercial transformation. The Freedom Pools acquisition adds Australian manufacturing capacity and international growth optionality [2].
The bear case centers on cyclical vulnerability and compressed profitability. Trailing profit margin of just 0.9% and ROE of 1.3% demonstrate that Latham is barely profitable despite $577M in TTM revenue — high depreciation, $314M in debt service, and input cost inflation compress bottom-line returns [4]. Trailing P/E of 125x underscores the earnings fragility; any revenue miss could push the company back into losses. The pool market is deeply cyclical and interest-rate sensitive — the 2023-2024 downturn saw revenue fall 20% peak-to-trough, and any sustained period of high mortgage rates or housing weakness could repeat this pattern [1]. Earnings growth was negative 16.5% in the most recent period despite 14.4% revenue growth, suggesting margin expansion is not keeping pace with sales recovery. CEO Sean Gadd is only nine months into his tenure, creating execution uncertainty around the 'commercial transformation' mandate. Short interest at 6.1% is moderate but reflects lingering skepticism. The stock has declined from $30+ post-IPO in 2021 to $6.23, a 79% drawdown that indicates significant market doubt about the long-term earnings power of the business [4].
