Company Profile
Industrials ·Tools & Accessories ·NYSE ·United States
Stanley Black & Decker is a global diversified industrial company and the world's largest pure-play tools and outdoor products company. The Tools & Outdoor segment sells professional and consumer power tools, hand tools, and outdoor products under DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY, BLACK+DECKER, and CUB CADET brands. The Industrial segment manufactures engineered fastening systems and infrastructure solutions for automotive, aerospace, and construction markets. Founded in 1843 in New Britain, Connecticut, SWK is a Dividend King with 58 consecutive years of dividend increases and 149 consecutive years of dividend payments. The company is executing a $2B+ Global Cost Reduction Program following the 2021-2022 overexpansion into outdoor power equipment.

Investment Thesis
SWK at 13.9x forward earnings offers a turnaround value play with Dividend King income — Q2 2026 revenue of $3.96B showed 3% organic growth and net income surged to $351.3M, while management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $5.20-$5.80 representing 90% YoY growth at midpoint [1]. The $2B+ restructuring is bearing fruit with the company targeting 35% adjusted gross margins as cost savings annualize. Three category-defining brands (DEWALT #1 professional, CRAFTSMAN #1 consumer, STANLEY #1 hand tools) provide pricing power and distribution moats through Home Depot and Lowe's. The 3.78% dividend yield backed by 58 consecutive years of increases (Dividend King since 1877) provides income stability, the $250M share repurchase signals management confidence, and the Inspire Impact Score of 44 (ALIGNED) makes this accessible for faith-based investors [1][4].
The bear case centers on margin recovery uncertainty and structural revenue headwinds. Profit margin of 4.1% and ROE of 6.9% remain far below industrial peers despite $2B+ in cost cuts, and the path to management's mid-teens operating margin target requires continued execution in an environment of tariff uncertainty and weakening consumer spending [1][5]. Revenue has declined from the 2022 peak of $16.9B to ~$15.2B with only 0.4% trailing growth, and the 5yr CAGR is essentially flat — organic growth of 3% in recent quarters has not yet reversed the multi-year decline trend [2]. The payout ratio of 81.2% on trailing earnings leaves minimal margin for error on the 58-year dividend streak. Milwaukee Tool (TTI) continues to gain professional market share against DEWALT in the critical cordless category, pressuring SWK's brand premium in its highest-margin segment. Glassdoor reviews describing fear-based culture and high turnover suggest the restructuring may have hollowed out organizational capability needed for the next growth phase [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $5.20-$5.80 implies significant margin recovery in H2 2026, with each 100bps of operating margin expansion adding ~$0.80-1.00 to EPS — achieving the 35% gross margin target could support $7+ EPS and drive re-rating from 13.9x forward P/E [1]
