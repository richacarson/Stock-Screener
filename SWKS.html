Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
Skyworks Solutions designs, develops, manufactures, and markets proprietary analog and mixed-signal semiconductors globally. Products include RF front-end modules, power amplifiers, filters, and integrated systems serving mobile (~65% of revenue), automotive, data center, IoT, and industrial markets. Under CEO Liam Griffin since 2016, Skyworks generates approximately $4B in annual revenue with Apple accounting for roughly 35% of sales. Q3 FY2026 delivered $935M revenue with non-GAAP EPS of $1.08, while Broad Markets achieved double-digit year-over-year growth in automotive and data center. In October 2025, Skyworks announced a definitive merger with Qorvo in a $22B cash-and-stock transaction expected to close early 2027, creating a US-based leader in high-performance RF, analog, and mixed-signal solutions.

Investment Thesis
The Qorvo combination, expected to close early 2027, creates a $22B US-based leader in RF, analog, and mixed-signal solutions with a dramatically diversified product portfolio that reduces Skyworks' historical dependence on Apple from approximately 35% to an estimated 20% of combined revenue [3]. At a forward P/E of 13x with a 4.1% dividend yield, SWKS trades at a meaningful discount to semiconductor peers, pricing in near-term margin compression from merger-related charges while undervaluing the pro-forma entity's improved competitive positioning against Qualcomm and Broadcom [4]. Broad Markets — automotive, data center, and Wi-Fi — delivered double-digit year-over-year growth in Q2-Q3 FY2026, demonstrating organic diversification beyond mobile [1][2]. The pristine pre-merger balance sheet (D/E of 21%, net cash position of $376M) and Q4 FY2026 revenue guidance of $1.01-1.06B indicating sequential acceleration provide near-term financial flexibility to manage integration costs while sustaining the 12-year dividend growth streak.
The bear case centers on merger execution risk and structural revenue challenges. The $3B bridge loan financing, antitrust review, and integration of two complex semiconductor operations could depress earnings through 2028, and semiconductor merger track records are mixed — Qualcomm's failed Broadcom defense and Intel's serial acquisition struggles are cautionary examples [3]. GAAP profitability has deteriorated sharply, with Q3 FY2026 profit margin falling to 3.6% from 11% year-prior and GAAP EPS declining 68% YoY, swelling the GAAP payout ratio to approximately 92% [1][4]. Revenue remains structurally 27% below the $5.5B FY2022 peak as the mobile RF market matures, and Apple's well-documented efforts to develop in-house modem and RF capabilities could further erode Skyworks' largest customer relationship [1]. The PEG ratio of 5.7x and ROE of only 6.49% suggest the market sees limited organic growth independent of merger synergies. The Inspire Impact Score of 54 (ALIGNED) with no negative attributions is a strength for faith-based investors, though it does not offset the operational challenges at the current valuation [6].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Skyworks' core business — designing and manufacturing physical analog and RF semiconductor components — is structurally immune to AI substitution. Mobile phones, automotive systems, IoT devices, and data centers all require physical RF front-end modules, power amplifiers, and filters that no software can replace [1][2]. AI may actually increase demand for Skyworks' products as AI-powered smartphones require enhanced connectivity, AI data centers need power and gate driver semiconductors, and AI edge devices rely on wireless communication. Analysts have not flagged AI as a substitution risk for Skyworks' business lines. However, the company's heavy reliance on the mobile smartphone market (~65% of revenue) introduces indirect exposure: if AI shifts computing paradigms away from traditional smartphone form factors over the long term, mobile RF demand could soften, though this remains speculative [5]. The Qorvo combination further diversifies the product portfolio across defense, infrastructure, and automotive applications that are orthogonal to AI disruption [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): While Skyworks is actively developing power and gate driver technologies for AI data centers and showcased these at PCIM Europe 2026, no specific AI-related revenue figures or percentages have been disclosed [5]. Broad Markets data center growth includes AI-adjacent revenue but has not been quantified separately.
