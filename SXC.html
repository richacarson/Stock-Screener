Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Coking Coal ·NYSE ·United States
SunCoke Energy is the largest independent producer of high-quality metallurgical coke in the Americas, with over 45 years of production experience using proprietary heat recovery cokemaking technology. The company operates through two segments: Domestic Coke (serving blast furnace steelmakers) and Industrial Services (material handling, scrap processing, and logistics). SunCoke acquired Phoenix Global in 2025 to expand industrial services. Q2 2026 revenue rose to $475.3M with net income of $15.6M, and management raised full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $250-265M.

Investment Thesis
SunCoke Energy offers a 5.1% dividend yield at $9.46, supported by the largest independent metallurgical coke operation in the Americas and a diversifying Industrial Services segment bolstered by Phoenix Global [1][5]. Management raised FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $250-265M, with Q2 2026 showing recovery to $15.6M net income ($0.15 EPS) after the weather-disrupted Q1 [1]. Industrial Services now contributes $90-100M EBITDA guidance, meaningfully diversifying beyond Domestic Coke's $162-168M. The dividend has grown at a 14.9% 5-year CAGR ($0.24 to $0.48 annually), and the Inspire Impact Score of 41 (ALIGNED) adds appeal for faith-based investors seeking income in basic materials [5].
The bear case centers on cyclicality, secular decline, and deteriorating financial metrics. SunCoke's core coke business is structurally tied to blast furnace steelmaking, which faces long-term decline as EAF now exceeds 70% of U.S. steel production [1]. Trailing metrics have deteriorated sharply: ROE has turned negative (-7.5%), profit margin is -2.9%, and forward P/E has expanded to 32.6x from 15.8x in July as earnings estimates declined [5]. The payout ratio of ~90% leaves minimal margin of safety for the $0.48 annual dividend, and Q1 2026's $4.4M net loss demonstrates operational fragility [2]. D/E of 107.7% reflects Phoenix acquisition leverage. EPA enforcement actions for Clean Air Act violations and community complaints about odors and black residue near the Middletown plant create environmental liability and reputational risk [4]. The stock is priced for a second-half recovery that must materialize to justify current valuation.
