Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE ·United States
Stryker Corporation is a leading global medical technology company offering innovative products and services in MedSurg and Neurotechnology, and Orthopaedics that help improve patient and hospital outcomes. The company operates through two segments: MedSurg and Neurotechnology (surgical instruments, endoscopy, patient handling, neurovascular devices) and Orthopaedics and Spine (hip and knee implants, trauma and extremities products, spine solutions, and the Mako robotic-arm assisted surgery platform). Stryker impacts more than 150 million patients annually worldwide.

Investment Thesis
Stryker is a best-in-class medtech compounder: FY2025 revenue reached $25.1B (11.8% 5-year CAGR), Q2 2026 delivered ~9.0% volume-driven organic growth with adjusted operating margin up 170bps to 27.4%, and management guides FY2026 adjusted EPS to $14.95-$15.10 [1][3]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E 62%), the dividend has been raised for 30+ years at a 35% payout, and Mako robotics provides a durable installed-base moat that is structurally immune to AI substitution [3][8].
The bear case: at ~31x trailing GAAP earnings and ~20x FY2026 guidance, the stock prices in uninterrupted high-single-digit growth while Q3 2026 faces a tough 9.5% organic comparison, an ongoing ~70-80bps peripheral vascular supply drag and softer hip volumes [5]. The March 2026 Handala cyberattack materially hurt Q1 and showed operational fragility, and a CEO transition to Spencer Stiles on January 1, 2027 adds execution uncertainty around a legacy tied closely to Lobo [2][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 earnings (late October 2026) against consensus of ~$6.68B revenue and $3.60 adjusted EPS; a beat on top of a 9.5% organic comparison would validate the 8.3-9.3% FY2026 organic guide [5]
  2. Resolution of the peripheral vascular supply constraint that cost ~70-80bps of growth in Q2 2026, unlocking the vascular franchise as a growth contributor in 2027 [5]
  3. Smooth handoff to CEO Spencer Stiles on January 1, 2027 with continued Mako platform expansion (Mako RPS and new applications) [4][8]
Key Risks
  1. Repeat of the March 2026 Iran-linked wiper attack that materially disrupted manufacturing, ordering and distribution systems and depressed Q1 2026 revenue (missed estimates by ~4.8%) [2][5][6]
  2. Valuation risk at ~31x trailing GAAP P/E if procedure volumes normalize from post-pandemic highs or hip volumes keep softening [3][5]
  3. Leadership transition risk as 14-year CEO Kevin Lobo, architect of the Mako strategy, moves to executive chair effective January 1, 2027 [4]
