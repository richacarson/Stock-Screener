Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Independent Power Producers ·NYSE ·Canada
TransAlta Corporation is a Canadian clean electricity company that generates and sells electricity through a diversified fleet of wind, hydroelectric, solar, natural gas, and battery energy storage facilities across Canada, the United States, and Australia. The company operates approximately 8,600 MW of generating capacity and has completed its coal phase-out. New CEO Joel Hunter took office April 30, 2026 following John Kousinioris's retirement. Q2 2026 revenue was $487M with a return to profitability ($35M net earnings vs $112M loss year-ago). Full-year 2026 EBITDA guided at CAD 950M-1,050M. Founded in 1909, headquartered in Calgary, Alberta.
1,350 Employees ·www.transalta.com

Investment Thesis
TransAlta is an improving story — the Q2 2026 return to profitability ($35M net earnings vs $112M loss year-ago), revenue growth of 12.5%, and D/E improvement from 302% to 229% signal the operational trough has passed [2][6]. The Keephills data center MOU for up to 1 GW of capacity directly ties TransAlta to the secular AI-driven electricity demand surge, and the company reaffirmed 2026 EBITDA guidance at CAD 950M-1,050M with CAD 350M-450M free cash flow [1][2]. The ~8,600 MW diversified fleet across wind, hydro, gas, solar, and battery storage provides structural protection against AI disruption — electricity is the ultimate AI enabler. At $12.22 per share with a forward P/E of 28.89x, the valuation is more reasonable than the 65x two months ago, and a 1.65% dividend yield provides modest income.
TransAlta's financial profile remains severely challenged despite the improving trajectory. D/E of 229% is still among the highest in the utility sector, ROE is -1.42%, and profit margin is -1.01% on a trailing basis — the company has barely crossed into profitability on a quarterly basis and any setback could push it back into losses [6]. PEG of 6.98 indicates the market is paying a steep premium relative to the modest earnings growth trajectory. H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA fell 20% to CAD 495M and free cash flow declined 22% to CAD 245M versus the prior year, primarily due to lower Alberta power prices and loss of Centralia output [2]. The complete C-suite turnover in April 2026 (new CEO Joel Hunter, new CFO Mike Politeski, new CCO Grant Arnold) creates execution risk during a critical period, compounded by below-average employee morale per Glassdoor data [3][5]. Revenue growth of only 2.1% annually is forecast for the next 3 years, well below the renewable energy industry average of 8.6%, and the Keephills data center revenue contribution remains prospective rather than realized.

AI Risk Protection (8/9): TransAlta's core business — owning and operating physical power generation assets (wind, hydro, gas, solar, battery storage) — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1]. The company's fleet of ~8,600 MW of generating capacity faces zero substitution risk from AI — the electricity that powers AI must come from physical generators.
