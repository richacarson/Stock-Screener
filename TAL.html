Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Education & Training Services ·NYSE ·China
TAL Education Group, founded in 2003 and headquartered in Beijing, is a Chinese education company that rebuilt itself after the 2021 national ban on for-profit K-9 academic tutoring. It now runs enrichment and learning services under brands like Xueersi, First Leap and Izhikang, and sells AI-enabled learning hardware such as the Xueersi Learning Tablet, powered by its MathGPT model [5][6]. FY2026 (ended February 28, 2026) net revenue was $3.02B, up 34% year over year, and revenue rose about 32% again in Q1 FY2027 [2][8].

Investment Thesis
TAL has completed a post-ban recovery. Revenue grew 34% to $3.02B in FY2026 and ~32% again in Q1 FY2027 [2][8]. The balance sheet is net-cash, with D/E of 10%, net debt/EBITDA of -2.1x, and EV of only ~$4.4B on a $6.1B market cap [3]. AI-powered learning devices built on MathGPT give TAL a hardware-plus-content position that pure tutoring rivals lack [6].
The bear case centers on earnings quality and policy risk. FY2027 consensus net income of only ~$235M implies a ~26x forward P/E [4]. The TTM 22% net margin depends heavily on non-operating gains rather than the 16% operating margin [3]. Beijing's education policy remains an ever-present overhang after the 2021 ban, and a dual-class structure concentrates control with the founder. Growth is already decelerating from 39% to the high-20s/low-30s as the device business matures [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Learning-device unit growth and new MathGPT-powered tablet launches re-accelerating revenue above 30%
  2. Operating margin expansion from the 16% TTM level as enrichment and device businesses scale toward consensus FY2029 EBIT of ~$393M
  3. Capital return from the large net-cash balance (EV ~$1.8B below market cap)
Key Risks
  1. Renewed Chinese regulatory tightening of after-school education or education hardware
  2. Commoditization of AI tutoring by free general-purpose chatbots, compressing device and service pricing
  3. Earnings quality: pretax income (33% of revenue) far exceeds operating income (16%), implying reliance on non-operating gains
  4. U.S.-China ADR delisting and audit-access risk
