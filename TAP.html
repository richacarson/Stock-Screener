Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Brewers ·NYSE ·United States
Molson Coors manufactures, markets, and distributes beer and malt beverages across the Americas, Europe, Middle East, Africa, and Asia Pacific. Its portfolio includes Coors Light, Miller Lite, Blue Moon, Peroni Nastro Azzurro, and Staropramen, spanning premium, above-premium, and economy segments. Founded in 1774, the company is headquartered in Golden, Colorado. CEO Rahul Goyal (since October 2025) is executing a $450M three-year cost savings program and reaffirmed FY2026 guidance despite volume headwinds.

Investment Thesis
Molson Coors offers deep value at 8.70x forward P/E with a 4.61% dividend yield backed by 38+ consecutive years of payments and 5 consecutive years of growth [5]. Q2 2026 demonstrated continued cost discipline with adjusted EPS of $1.58 beating estimates by 4.4% and EBITDA of $624.6M beating by 3.8%, while management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance despite macro headwinds [1]. The #2 U.S. beer position with Coors Light and Miller Lite provides defensive brand equity, and the above-premium portfolio expansion (Blue Moon, Peroni, ZOA Energy) positions the company for revenue mix improvement over time. The $450M three-year cost savings program provides earnings visibility through 2028. At current levels, investors pay less than 9x underlying earnings for a 252-year-old, cash-generative beverage franchise with family ownership alignment (Molson family board representation) [3].
The bear case centers on deteriorating top-line fundamentals and structural industry headwinds. Q2 2026 revenue declined 3.3% YoY to $3.10B, FY2025 revenue declined ~4% to ~$11.1B, and the 5-year revenue CAGR of only 1.4% reflects secular U.S. beer volume decline as younger consumers shift toward spirits, cannabis, and moderation trends [1][2]. The $2.1B goodwill impairment in FY2025 wiped out GAAP earnings (net loss of $10.75/share) and signals management's admission that prior acquisitions destroyed value [2]. GAAP ROE of -18.2% and net margin of -20.8% mask underlying profitability but indicate real balance sheet damage. The $6.5B debt load against declining revenue limits financial flexibility, and commodity inflation (aluminum, barley) creates persistent cost pressure. Three CEO changes in a decade raises questions about strategic continuity, and Goyal's 'transform faster' mandate may sacrifice organizational trust for near-term results [3]. The Inspire score of -1 (MIXED) reflects the inherent tension between alcohol production and faith-based investing criteria.
