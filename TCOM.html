Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Travel Services ·NASDAQ ·China
Trip.com Group is China's largest and one of the world's leading online travel agencies, operating a comprehensive travel service platform encompassing accommodation reservations, transportation ticketing, packaged tours, and corporate travel management under the Trip.com, Ctrip, Skyscanner, and Qunar brands. The company is dual-listed on NASDAQ and HKEX. In 2026, the company faced a SAMR anti-monopoly investigation that resulted in RMB 1.66B ($244M) in confiscated gains and compulsory refund of RMB 122M ($18M) to hotel operators. Co-founders abruptly resigned from the board in February 2026. Q1 2026 revenue grew 17% YoY to RMB 16.2B ($2.4B).
43,574 Employees ·www.trip.com

Investment Thesis
Trip.com Group is China's dominant travel platform delivering Q1 2026 revenue of RMB 16.2B ($2.4B, up 17% YoY) with international OTA bookings surging 65% and inbound travel growing 90%, demonstrating the global expansion thesis is working [1]. At 12.30x forward earnings with 21.12% ROE, 48.65% profit margin, and RMB 104B ($15.1B) in cash, the stock trades at roughly half the multiple of Booking Holdings (~25x). R&D investment at 24% of revenue (RMB 15.1B) positions the company as a technology-first travel platform with AI deployed across search, pricing, and customer service [2]. The $60.69 analyst consensus target implies ~39% upside from $43.64, reflecting the deep China regulatory discount that may normalize as SAMR penalties are absorbed.
The bear case has intensified materially in 2026. The SAMR anti-monopoly investigation concluded with RMB 1.66B ($244M) in confiscated gains and a compulsory refund of RMB 122M ($18M) to hotel operators — confirming 'abuse of dominant market position' [3]. A securities class action has been filed over AI pricing controversy. Co-founders James Liang and Neil Shen abruptly resigned from the board in February 2026 with no explanation, creating governance uncertainty. Q2 2026 guidance of only 3-8% revenue growth represents severe deceleration from 17% in Q1, with management citing 'macro headwinds and compliance-related operational adjustments' [1]. The reported profit margin of 48.65% is inflated by non-operating investment income; true operating margin is ~25% and faces compression from compliance costs and the $244M penalty. As a Chinese ADR, TCOM carries VIE structural risk and potential delisting exposure under the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act, amplified by ongoing US-China geopolitical tensions.
