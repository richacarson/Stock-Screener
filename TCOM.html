Company Profile

Trip.com Group is China's largest and one of the world's leading online travel agencies, operating a comprehensive travel service platform encompassing accommodation reservations, transportation ticketing, packaged tours, and corporate travel management. The company operates under the Trip.com, Ctrip, Skyscanner, and Qunar brands, dual-listed on NASDAQ and HKEX. Q1 2026 marked a turning point with a SAMR anti-monopoly investigation into potential abuse of dominant market position and Q2 guidance of only 3-8% growth.

Investment Thesis
Trip.com Group is China's dominant travel platform delivering Q1 2026 revenue of RMB16.2B ($2.4B, up 17% YoY) with international OTA bookings surging 65% and inbound travel growing 90%, demonstrating the global expansion thesis is working [1]. At 10.68x forward earnings with 19.9% ROE and a fortress net cash position of $49.6B CNY, the stock trades at one of the deepest valuation discounts among global travel platforms — less than half the multiple of Booking Holdings (~25x). Every infrastructure metric hits or exceeds target: D/E 18.8%, profit margin 48.7%, revenue growth 17.3%. TripGen AI integration and 43,574 employees across 200+ countries position Trip.com as both the undisputed Chinese OTA leader and an increasingly credible global competitor. The $60.98 analyst consensus target implies 49% upside from the current $41 level.
The bear case has intensified materially since the prior screen. The SAMR anti-monopoly investigation disclosed in Q1 2026 — probing 'potential abuse of a dominant market position' — could result in substantial fines (potentially 1-10% of revenue per Chinese antitrust precedent), behavioral remedies, or forced business model changes [1]. Q2 2026 guidance of only 3-8% revenue growth represents severe deceleration from 17% in Q1, with management explicitly flagging 'macro headwinds and compliance-related operational adjustments' — suggesting the SAMR probe is already constraining operations. The stock has declined 35.5% from its 52-week high of $78.99, pricing in significant regulatory risk. The reported profit margin of 48.7% is inflated by non-operating investment income from equity holdings; true operating margin is approximately 25% and faces compression from compliance costs. As a Chinese ADR, TCOM carries VIE structural risk and potential delisting exposure under the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act, amplified by ongoing US-China geopolitical tensions [1].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Online travel agencies face genuine AI substitution risk as AI travel assistants from Google, OpenAI, and emerging platforms could potentially bypass traditional OTA intermediaries by planning and booking trips directly with suppliers [2]. Trip.com's consumer-facing discovery and planning functions are particularly exposed to AI-powered travel tools that can replicate itinerary creation and price comparison at zero marginal cost. However, Trip.com has meaningful defensive moats: massive bilateral supplier relationships built over 25 years, negotiated exclusive inventory and pricing, complex real-world fulfillment and customer service operations, trust for high-value financial transactions, and dominant regulated market position in China. The SAMR probe itself confirms competitive positioning that AI cannot easily replicate. AI Opportunity (1/1): TripGen AI chatbot is deployed and generating customer engagement with real-time personalized recommendations. Internal AI tools deliver 15-30% coding efficiency gains across the engineering organization. AI-driven personalization across accommodation, transportation, and tour recommendations enhances platform conversion and stickiness [2].
