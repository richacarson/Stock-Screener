Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
BlackRock TCP Capital Corp. is a business development company (BDC) specializing in direct equity and debt investments in middle-market companies. Invests primarily in senior secured loans, mezzanine debt, and junior capital across technology, healthcare, and business services sectors. Externally managed by BlackRock as part of its US Private Capital platform. Currently under federal scrutiny over valuation practices, with CEO Phil Tseng departing in mid-2026.

Investment Thesis
TCPC offers a deeply discounted entry into the BlackRock private credit platform at 0.48x book value ($3.33 vs $6.72 NAV) with a 21.62% current yield, making it a high-risk deep-value candidate if the portfolio stabilizes [5][6]. Q1 2026 NII of $0.22/share covered the $0.17 quarterly dividend with 129% NII coverage, suggesting the current distribution rate may be sustainable at lower levels [1]. The BlackRock platform provides institutional-quality deal flow, risk management infrastructure, and scale advantages that smaller standalone BDCs lack. If credit markets recover and the federal valuation probe resolves without material penalties, the 52% discount to NAV could narrow significantly.
The bear case is severe and has intensified since last review. CEO Phil Tseng is departing during an active federal investigation by SDNY prosecutors into BlackRock's loan valuation practices, and a class action lawsuit has been filed on behalf of investors who purchased between November 2024 and January 2026, alleging materially false statements about the fund's business and prospects [2][4]. NAV has been slashed twice — 19% in January 2026 and 5% in May 2026 — and ROE of -18.74% indicates the portfolio is destroying shareholder value at an accelerating rate [1][5]. The dividend has been cut 61% from $0.44/quarter (Q4 2024) to $0.17/quarter, with further cuts probable if NII continues declining. The Altman Z-Score of -0.2 signals increased bankruptcy risk. In a rising default environment, TCPC's $925M in debt against portfolio companies facing stress — including 30.5% software exposure with AI disruption risk — could drive further writedowns [1][5].
