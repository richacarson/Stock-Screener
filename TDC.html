Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NYSE ·United States
Teradata Corporation provides cloud analytics and data platform solutions to enterprise customers worldwide. The company's Teradata VantageCloud platform enables organizations to connect and manage data across hybrid and multi-cloud environments, build AI and machine learning models, and derive actionable business insights at scale. Teradata serves large enterprises in financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, telecommunications, and government. Founded in 1979, headquartered in San Diego, California.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 3 /10
Teradata exhibits predominantly finite game characteristics. The company's recent strategic decisions -- 9-10% workforce reduction, revenue declining for multiple years, and reactive cloud migration under competitive pressure -- reflect a company playing to survive this quarter rather than playing to keep playing. The leadership is optimizing for short-term profitability (non-GAAP EPS guidance, cost cuts) rather than investing boldly in long-term capability. The revolving door of strategic pivots (on-prem to cloud to AI) without a clear Just Cause connecting them suggests financial optimization rather than purpose-driven transformation.

Investment Thesis
Teradata delivered a strong Q1 2026 with EPS of $0.88 (beating consensus by 20.55%), driven by recurring revenue momentum and a major SAP legal settlement [1]. Public cloud ARR of $686M growing 13% YoY demonstrates the cloud migration is gaining traction, while total ARR of $1.492B grew 3% [1]. The forward P/E of 13.06 and trailing P/E of 7.78 (inflated by the SAP settlement) suggest inexpensive valuation for a software company with 150+ enterprise AI engagements and a pivot toward AI-enabled analytics [3]. With $234M in pro-forma cash and expected full-year non-GAAP EPS of $2.55-$2.65, the company has financial stability to complete its cloud transition. All 9 covering analysts rate the stock Strong Buy with a median $15.50 price target implying 179% upside from current levels [5].
The fundamental challenge is that Teradata is a legacy data analytics company in secular revenue decline -- revenue has fallen from $1.84B in 2020 to $1.66B in 2025, a -2.0% 5-year CAGR [2]. The 2026 guidance of -2% to flat YoY revenue signals the decline has not yet inflected. Cloud-native competitors Snowflake and Databricks are building AI-native platforms that increasingly serve the same large enterprise customers, and public cloud providers are embedding analytics directly into their infrastructure. The 9-10% workforce reduction and multiple restructuring cycles create organizational instability that undermines the company's ability to innovate its way out of decline [5]. While the trailing P/E of 7.78 appears cheap, it is artificially depressed by the one-time SAP settlement; normalized earnings suggest the company is more modestly valued. The high analyst price targets reflect speculative upside from a turnaround thesis that has not yet materialized in revenue growth.
Key Catalysts
  1. Public cloud ARR acceleration from 13% to 15%+ growth driven by AI-enabled analytics demand and large enterprise cloud migrations, which would signal the revenue decline inflection point
  2. Monetization of 150+ enterprise AI engagements into recurring cloud ARR as customers operationalize AI workloads on VantageCloud, potentially validating the 'autonomous enterprise' positioning
  3. Strategic combination or takeout -- at a $2.5B market cap with $1.5B in ARR and deep enterprise relationships, Teradata is a potential acquisition target for a larger cloud/data platform
Key Risks
  1. Secular revenue decline from $1.84B (2020) to $1.66B (2025) has not inflected, and 2026 guidance of -2% to flat suggests the cloud transition is not generating net new growth -- just slowing the decline
  2. Snowflake, Databricks, and cloud-native provider offerings are building AI-integrated analytics platforms that could accelerate customer migration away from Teradata's hybrid approach
  3. Repeated workforce reductions (9-10% in 2025-2026) create organizational instability and brain drain, potentially undermining the company's ability to execute its AI and cloud innovation strategy

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Teradata occupies an exposed position in the AI landscape. The company's core data analytics and warehousing platform faces substitution risk from AI-native competitors: Snowflake and Databricks are building AI-native analytics platforms, and cloud providers' native offerings (AWS, Google, Azure) are integrating AI capabilities directly into their data services, potentially reducing the need for a third-party platform like Teradata [2]. Traditional SQL-based analytics queries -- a core Teradata use case -- are increasingly being automated or augmented by AI-powered analytics tools. However, Teradata's large enterprise customers have deep switching costs (petabyte-scale data migrations are complex and risky), and complex analytical workloads in regulated industries still require the performance and governance capabilities that Teradata's mature platform provides. The company is adapting by integrating AI capabilities into VantageCloud and completing 150+ AI engagements [3], but the competitive outcome remains uncertain. AI Opportunity (1/1): Teradata generates quantifiable AI-related revenue through its 150+ enterprise AI engagements and AI-enabled VantageCloud platform features. While specific AI revenue figures are not broken out, the company's cloud ARR growth of 13% to $686M is partially driven by AI-related demand [1][3].
