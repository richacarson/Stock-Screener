Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·United States
Telephone and Data Systems is a telecommunications company operating through two segments: TDS Telecom (wireline broadband, fiber internet, voice, and video services to rural and suburban communities) and Array (tower infrastructure leased to wireless carriers). Founded in 1968 and headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, TDS transformed in 2024-2025 by selling its majority stake in US Cellular to T-Mobile and pivoting to fiber broadband expansion and tower infrastructure. Controlled by the Carlson family through super-voting shares. TTM revenue of $1.26B with $2.2B in cash from the US Cellular divestiture.

Investment Thesis
TDS at $35.20 is a deep-value transformation play with $2.2B in cash ($20.40/share) against a $4.1B market cap, meaning the market values the combined TDS Telecom and Array businesses at less than $1.9B [1][5]. The company is investing aggressively in fiber broadband (40,000 new fiber addresses and 10,900 residential fiber net additions in Q2 2026), and Array's tower site rental revenues surged up to 92% YoY, validating the post-US Cellular strategy [1]. Q1 2026 revenue grew 7% to $309.5M and H1 2026 generated $408M in net income (albeit driven by spectrum sales). TDS Telecom guidance of $1.015-$1.055B revenue with $310-$350M adjusted EBITDA and Array at $200-$215M revenue provide a combined $1.2B+ revenue base with improving profitability trajectory. The clean balance sheet (D/E 23%) and massive cash reserves provide years of runway for the fiber investment cycle.
The fundamental challenge is that TDS's ongoing businesses generate minimal earnings — the forward P/E of 4,330x reflects near-zero normalized earnings after one-time spectrum sale gains conclude [5]. TDS Telecom reported operating losses in Q2 2026 as legacy wireline service erosion (voice, copper broadband) outpaces fiber subscriber additions, creating a race between fiber ramp and copper decay [1]. The company is spending heavily on fiber capex against approximately $1B in telecom revenue — an elevated investment rate that will suppress returns for years. The Carlson family's super-voting control ensures strategic continuity but limits minority shareholder governance rights. Employee culture rated C- (bottom 30%) on Comparably suggests organizational challenges during the transformation [3]. Array's revenue visibility depends heavily on the T-Mobile Master Lease Agreement, creating single-tenant concentration risk. Until fiber economics inflect and TDS Telecom reaches operating breakeven, this remains a faith-based investment in rural broadband demand and management execution.
Key Catalysts
  1. Fiber service address buildout of 250,000-300,000 in 2026 driving subscriber additions that could inflect TDS Telecom toward operating breakeven as the fiber customer base scales beyond the legacy wireline erosion rate [1]
  2. Array tower business achieving 92% site rental revenue growth through T-Mobile MLA expansion and additional carrier tenants, building a high-margin recurring revenue base [1]
  3. Cash-rich balance sheet ($2.2B cash, $20.40/share) funds multi-year fiber investment cycle without leverage, providing strategic optionality for acquisitions, share repurchases, or dividend restoration [5]
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 4,330x reflects negligible normalized earnings — once spectrum sale gains conclude, TDS must demonstrate a path to profitability from underlying fiber and tower businesses or face significant re-rating risk [5]
  2. TDS Telecom operating at a loss as legacy wireline erosion accelerates faster than fiber subscriber additions scale, creating execution risk if fiber take rates or ARPU fall below management projections [1]
  3. Carlson family dual-class super-voting control limits minority shareholder governance rights and insulates management from accountability, while employee culture rated C- (bottom 30%) creates organizational risk during the transformation [3]
