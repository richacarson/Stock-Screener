Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·United States
Telephone and Data Systems, Inc. is a telecommunications company operating through two segments: TDS Telecom (wireline broadband, voice, and video services to rural and suburban communities) and Array (tower infrastructure leased to wireless carriers). Founded in 1969 by LeRoy T. Carlson and headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, TDS transformed in 2024-2025 by selling its majority stake in US Cellular to T-Mobile and pivoting to fiber broadband expansion and tower infrastructure. Controlled by the Carlson family through super-voting shares.

Investment Thesis
TDS is a rare deep-value transformation story in telecom — the company has divested US Cellular, cleaned the balance sheet to 13.8% D/E, and is investing aggressively in fiber broadband expansion (250,000-300,000 addresses in 2026, guidance raised by 50,000) and tower infrastructure through Array [1][3]. The fiber buildout in underserved rural markets fills a genuine demand gap that larger carriers have abandoned, and Array's tower revenue benefits from T-Mobile's network densification through the Master Lease Agreement. Q2 2026 showed Array's strong tower revenue growth and increased profitability, validating the post-divestiture strategy. The stock at $36 with $4.1B market cap could rerate significantly if fiber subscriber additions accelerate and the telecom segment reaches operating breakeven.
The core challenge is that TDS is in the midst of a multi-year investment cycle with no near-term earnings visibility — the forward P/E of 322.8x reflects negligible normalized earnings after spectrum sale gains conclude [5]. TDS Telecom posted a 6% revenue decline and an operating loss in Q2 2026 as legacy wireline service erosion (voice, copper broadband) outpaces fiber subscriber additions [1]. The company is spending $550-600M in annual capex against approximately $1B in telecom revenue — an unsustainable ratio that requires fiber subscriber economics to scale. The Carlson family's super-voting control ensures strategic continuity but limits minority shareholder governance rights. Array's revenue visibility depends heavily on the T-Mobile MLA, creating concentration risk with a single major tenant [3]. Until fiber economics inflect, TDS is a faith-based investment in rural broadband demand.
Key Catalysts
  1. Fiber service address buildout of 250,000-300,000 in 2026 (raised by 50,000) driving subscriber additions that could inflect TDS Telecom toward operating profitability as the fiber customer base scales
  2. Array tower business achieving standalone profitability growth through T-Mobile MLA expansion and additional carrier tenants, providing stable recurring revenue to offset telecom investment phase
  3. Spectrum sale proceeds generating near-term cash flow that funds fiber capex without incremental leverage, maintaining the pristine 13.8% D/E balance sheet through the investment cycle
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 322.8x reflects negligible normalized earnings — once spectrum sale gains conclude, TDS must demonstrate a path to profitability from the underlying fiber and tower businesses or the stock faces significant rerating risk
  2. TDS Telecom's 6% revenue decline and operating loss reflect accelerating legacy wireline erosion that fiber additions have not yet offset, creating a race between fiber subscriber ramp and copper revenue decay
  3. Carlson family super-voting control limits minority shareholder governance rights, and the T-Mobile MLA concentration in Array creates single-tenant risk for a material portion of post-divestiture revenue
