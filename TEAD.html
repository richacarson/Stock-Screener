Teads Holding Co. operates a two-sided omnichannel advertising technology platform connecting media owners with advertisers across video, display, native, and CTV formats. Formerly Outbrain Inc., the company completed the $1B acquisition of Legacy Teads in Q1 2025, rebranding as Teads Holding Co. Q2 2026 revenue of $284.6M declined 17% YoY with a net loss of $42.5M. CTV revenue surged 67% to ~$40M (13% of total), but management suspended full-year guidance amid deteriorating Direct Response and SME demand. Market cap approximately $85M at $0.50/share.

Investment Thesis
Teads is a deeply distressed ad-tech company where CTV is the sole bright spot — Q2 2026 CTV revenue surged 67% YoY to ~$40M, now 13% of total (up from 7%), demonstrating real traction in the fastest-growing ad format and suggesting the combined platform has genuine value in connected TV [2]. CEO Kostman ($50K) and CCO Spilman ($104K) made open-market purchases at $0.99-1.19, signaling C-suite conviction at prices above the current $0.50 [5]. At $85M market cap against TTM revenue of $1.22B, the stock trades at 0.07x revenue — extreme distressed pricing that could offer significant optionality if CTV growth continues and merger synergies stabilize margins. The platform serves premium publishers including The Wall Street Journal, BBC, and Le Monde, providing a quality positioning advantage.
The bear case is overwhelming: Q2 revenue declined 17% YoY to $284.6M (accelerating from -7% in Q1) with a GAAP net loss of $42.5M, and management suspended full-year guidance amid 'volatility in Direct Response and SME demand' [2]. Adjusted EBITDA collapsed from $27M (Q2 2025) to $7M (Q2 2026), undermining the path to profitability. D/E of ~1,300% with $649M in acquisition debt against a business burning cash creates serious going-concern risk [6]. The $0.50 stock price with only ~$180K daily traded value firmly breaches the IOWN $1M liquidity floor and puts NASDAQ delisting at risk (sub-$1 for extended period). AI disruption threatens the core business — generative AI reducing creative demand, AI search reducing publisher traffic, and AI assistants potentially disintermediating display advertising. Revenue declining 17% despite a $1B acquisition suggests structural competitive displacement by Google, Meta, and The Trade Desk that CTV growth alone cannot offset. SELL reflects unsustainable leverage, accelerating revenue decline, existential competitive threats, and severe illiquidity.
