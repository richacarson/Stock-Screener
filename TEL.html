Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYSE ·Ireland
TE Connectivity plc is a $17 billion global industrial technology leader providing connectivity and sensor solutions that enable a safer, sustainable, productive, and connected future. The company operates through two segments: Transportation Solutions (automotive connectors, sensors, and electrification components) and Industrial Solutions (aerospace, defense, medical, data center, and energy connectors). TE is the world's largest connector company, serving customers across virtually every end market where reliable physical connectivity is essential, from AI data centers to electric vehicles to aerospace systems.

Investment Thesis
TE Connectivity is the world's largest connector company experiencing an AI-driven demand supercycle, with Q3 FY2026 delivering record orders of $5.7B (up 27% YoY), record revenue of $5.16B (up 14%), and record adjusted EPS of $2.94 (up 22%) [2]. DDN orders surged 70% YTD as AI data center buildout drives unprecedented demand for high-performance connectivity products [2][3]. The full-year FY2026 outlook calls for approximately 15% revenue growth and 20%+ EPS growth, with guidance raised on record backlog [1]. Forward P/E of 19.03 is attractive for a company with this growth profile, while the balance sheet remains conservative at 45% D/E with 15.61% profit margins [8]. The dividend has grown consistently for 15 years with a sustainable 25.8% payout ratio, and the 1.34% yield provides income alongside secular growth exposure to AI, electrification, and industrial automation [9].
The bear case centers on cyclicality risk: connector demand is historically correlated with industrial and automotive production cycles, and the current AI-driven order surge may create a boom-bust pattern if data center buildout decelerates or customers over-order [2][3]. Revenue growth of 15% partly reflects the inclusion of acquisitions and currency, with organic growth of 12% in Q3 suggesting somewhat more moderate underlying momentum. ROE of 14.62% is just below the 15% target, and profit margin of 15.61% reflects the structural cost base of physical manufacturing — any input cost inflation or competitive pricing pressure could compress margins [8]. The concentration of growth in DDN/AI data center orders creates end-market concentration risk if cloud capital expenditure cycles shift. CEO Curtin himself acknowledges a 'growing need for shared objectives between engineers and executives' around AI adoption, suggesting internal execution risk in translating the AI opportunity into sustained competitive advantage [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record $5.7B Q3 FY2026 orders (up 27% YoY) with DDN orders surging 70% YTD driven by AI data center connector demand, creating substantial backlog visibility into FY2027 [2][3]
  2. Full-year FY2026 guidance raised to approximately 15% sales growth and 20%+ EPS growth, with Q4 guidance of $5.25B and $3.05 adjusted EPS reflecting continued acceleration [1][2]
  3. Automotive electrification and industrial automation secular trends providing multi-year demand tailwinds beyond the AI data center supercycle, diversifying growth drivers across Transportation and Industrial segments [1]
Key Risks
  1. Cyclical order volatility in connector demand — record orders may reflect double-ordering or inventory building by hyperscaler customers, creating risk of an order correction if AI data center buildout decelerates or absorbs existing inventory [2][3]
  2. End-market concentration risk as DDN/AI data center orders become an increasingly large portion of growth — any slowdown in cloud capital expenditure cycles would disproportionately impact TE's fastest-growing business
  3. Profit margin of 15.61% faces structural pressure from physical manufacturing costs, raw material input prices, and competitive dynamics in a global connector market where lower-cost Asian competitors are scaling capabilities [8]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
10
AI Risk Protection (9/9): TE Connectivity manufactures the physical connectors, cables, and sensors that AI infrastructure literally cannot function without — every AI server, GPU cluster, networking switch, and data center requires TE's high-performance connectivity products [2][3]. Physical connector manufacturing involves precision engineering, materials science, cleanroom production, and customer qualification processes that AI cannot substitute. Regulatory certifications (UL, automotive IATF, aerospace AS9100) create additional barriers to disruption. AI is the single largest demand driver for TE's fastest-growing business: DDN (Data, Devices & Networks) orders surged 70% YTD in Q3 FY2026, and record $5.7B total orders (up 27% YoY) are substantially driven by AI data center buildout [2][3]. The more AI scales globally, the more connectors, cables, and sensors are needed — TE's business is structurally enhanced by AI proliferation. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI data center connector demand is generating quantifiable and rapidly growing revenue. DDN orders up 70% YTD represents direct AI-related revenue growth [2][3]. The company will exit FY2026 with a strong backlog position driven substantially by AI infrastructure demand. CEO Curtin's Industrial Technology Index explicitly tracks AI adoption as a revenue driver [6].
