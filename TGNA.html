Company Profile
Communication Services ·Broadcasting ·NYSE ·United States
TEGNA was a broadcast and digital media company that owned or operated 68 television stations in 54 U.S. markets. Originally part of Gannett Co. (founded 1906), TEGNA was spun off in 2015. On March 19, 2026, Nexstar Media Group completed its $6.2 billion acquisition of TEGNA at $22.00 per share. TEGNA shares were subsequently delisted from the NYSE and the company now operates as a wholly owned subsidiary of Nexstar.

Investment Thesis
TEGNA is no longer publicly investable. Nexstar Media Group completed its $6.2 billion acquisition of TEGNA on March 19, 2026, at $22.00 per share in cash, and TEGNA shares were subsequently delisted from the NYSE [1]. The company now operates as a wholly owned subsidiary of Nexstar, the nation's largest local TV broadcaster of ABC, CBS, FOX, and NBC affiliates. Prior to acquisition, TEGNA generated $2.71B in revenue with $231M in free cash flow, operating 68 stations across 54 markets with the Premion digital advertising platform.
As a historical assessment, TEGNA's trajectory reflects the challenges facing local TV broadcasting. Revenue declined 18.9% in 2025 (exaggerated by the political advertising cycle but masking structural secular decline in linear TV). CEO Steib's aggressive AI automation and journalist layoffs optimized near-term costs but gutted the newsroom capabilities that differentiated local TV from digital competitors. The broadcasting industry faces existential AI disruption as AI-generated news, automated weather forecasting, and AI chatbot-driven news aggregation threaten to disintermediate the local TV audience relationship. The $22/share acquisition by Nexstar represented a premium exit for shareholders but confirmed that independent local TV broadcasting is a declining business model. There is no action for investors — the stock is delisted and the acquisition is complete.
