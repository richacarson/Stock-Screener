Investment Thesis
THM offers leveraged exposure to one of the largest undeveloped gold deposits in the United States — 13.6 million ounces at Livengood — at a time when gold prices are trading near multi-year highs. The $115 million equity raise in January 2026 provides adequate funding for the $20-25 million feasibility work program, and the appointment of experienced leadership (CEO David Wiens with $1 billion in mining finance experience at Asante Gold, COO Shane Parrow with Alaska development expertise) signals a credible push toward feasibility completion and permitting [1][2]. If Livengood advances through feasibility and permitting on favorable economics, THM's ~$1 billion market cap could prove undervalued relative to the project's potential net asset value at current gold prices.
The bear case is severe: THM is a pre-revenue exploration company that has failed to advance Livengood to production in over 15 years of development, with zero revenue, zero earnings, and a business model entirely dependent on continued equity dilution [1]. The $115 million raise diluted existing shareholders significantly (261.64 million shares outstanding), and the project will require billions more in capital to construct a mine at this scale — creating further dilution risk or dependency on streaming/royalty financing. Alaska permitting is complex and politically sensitive with no guaranteed timeline. The feasibility study could reveal unfavorable economics at current gold prices given the deposit's metallurgical complexity and remote location. Infrastructure score of 0 reflects the fundamental reality that this is a speculative pre-revenue investment with no financial metrics to evaluate. With an overall score of 33 (SELL), THM is appropriate only for investors with high risk tolerance and a specific gold thesis who understand that most exploration-stage miners never reach production [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Completion of the Livengood feasibility study establishing definitive project economics, mine design, and capital cost estimates — potentially unlocking a re-rating from exploration-stage to development-stage valuation [2]
  2. Continued gold price appreciation above $2,500/oz strengthening Livengood's economic viability and increasing the theoretical net asset value of the 13.6 million ounce resource base
  3. Federal and state permitting milestones for the Livengood project that would de-risk the development timeline and attract potential strategic partners or construction financing [3]
Key Risks
  1. Capital requirements for mine construction at Livengood's scale could exceed $2-3 billion, requiring massive equity dilution, debt financing, or streaming/royalty agreements that would significantly compress shareholder returns even in a successful scenario [1]
  2. Alaska permitting complexity and timeline uncertainty — federal environmental review, state water permits, and local community engagement could delay development by years, and changing political administrations introduce regulatory risk [3]
  3. Feasibility study could reveal unfavorable metallurgical characteristics, higher-than-expected capital costs, or insufficient economic returns to justify development, potentially stranding the project indefinitely after 15+ years of exploration spending
