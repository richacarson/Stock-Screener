TIC Solutions, Inc. (formerly Acuren Corporation) is a leading provider of tech-enabled Testing, Inspection, Certification and Compliance (TICC), engineering, geospatial, and critical asset integrity services across North America and select international markets. The company was formed through the August 2025 merger of Acuren Corporation and NV5 Global, Inc., creating a $2B+ revenue platform serving industrial, infrastructure, energy, utilities, construction, and commercial real estate end markets. Rebranded from Acuren to TIC Solutions in October 2025.
12,760 Employees ·www.ticsolutions.com

Investment Thesis
TIC Solutions offers a contrarian value opportunity in industrial services. The August 2025 Acuren-NV5 merger created a market-leading North American TICC and engineering services platform with $2B+ combined revenue and 12,760 employees serving critical infrastructure markets [3]. Q1 2026 delivered record revenue of $488M (up 4.3% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $57.7M (11.8% margin), and full-year guidance of $2.15-2.25B revenue at 15-16% adjusted EBITDA margin suggests the underlying business is healthy [1][2]. The stock has declined approximately 50% from its September 2025 high of $14.94 to $7.53, pricing in significant merger integration risk. Secular tailwinds from aging US infrastructure, energy transition compliance requirements, and industrial safety regulation create a durable growth backdrop for the TICC industry. At current levels, the stock could offer significant upside if the integration executes on plan.
The bear case is that TIC Solutions is a newly merged, currently unprofitable entity with significant execution risk. The GAAP net loss of $41.5M in Q1 2026 reflects merger integration costs and purchase accounting amortization that may persist for multiple quarters [1]. D/E of 87.9% indicates meaningful leverage from the merger financing, limiting financial flexibility. The CEO transition from Tal Pizzey (39-year tenure) to Ben Heraud, while orderly, introduces leadership uncertainty during a critical integration period [2]. Revenue growth of 4.3% is modest for a company that just completed a transformative merger, suggesting limited organic momentum. The stock's 50% decline from its September 2025 high reflects market skepticism about whether the Acuren-NV5 combination will deliver promised synergies. The company has no dividend, negative free cash flow, and no clear timeline to GAAP profitability, making it a turnaround story that requires patience.
Key Catalysts
  1. Merger integration milestones: realization of Acuren-NV5 cost synergies and cross-selling opportunities that drive adjusted EBITDA margin toward the 15-16% full-year 2026 target
  2. Infrastructure spending tailwinds from aging US infrastructure, energy transition compliance, and industrial safety regulation driving TICC demand across all end markets
  3. Path to GAAP profitability as merger-related purchase accounting amortization and one-time integration costs roll off, revealing the underlying 15%+ EBITDA margin business
Key Risks
  1. Merger integration execution risk: combining Acuren's inspection heritage with NV5's engineering and geospatial services across 12,760 employees presents significant cultural and operational challenges, with GAAP losses persisting into 2026
  2. Leverage risk: D/E of 87.9% from merger financing constrains financial flexibility, with the company carrying meaningful debt while generating negative free cash flow and no dividend
  3. CEO succession uncertainty: Tal Pizzey's retirement after 39 years and replacement by Ben Heraud occurs during the most critical integration phase, introducing leadership transition risk

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): TIC Solutions' core business — physical inspection, testing, and certification of industrial assets, buildings, and public infrastructure — is structurally immune to AI disruption. The work requires physical presence at industrial sites (pipelines, refineries, power plants, bridges), specialized equipment (ultrasonic testing, radiography, eddy current), and certified human inspectors who must physically assess asset integrity [3]. Regulatory requirements mandate in-person inspection and certification for safety-critical infrastructure across energy, utilities, and construction sectors. AI cannot substitute for a technician performing non-destructive testing on a weld joint at a refinery. The growing infrastructure maintenance backlog (aging US infrastructure, energy transition compliance) actually increases demand for TIC Solutions' services. AI could potentially enhance inspection analysis through computer vision for defect detection, but the physical service delivery and regulatory certification requirements remain human-dependent.
