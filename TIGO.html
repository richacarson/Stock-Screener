Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·Luxembourg
Millicom International Cellular operates under the Tigo brand across Latin America, providing mobile, broadband, pay-TV, and fixed-line services to residential and business customers. The company also offers mobile financial services (Tigo Money) including payments, money transfers, micro-insurance, and real-time loans. Millicom completed the transformative Coltel acquisition in Colombia in 2025, along with operations in Ecuador and Uruguay, driving reported revenue to $7.2B. CEO Marcelo Benitez was appointed in June 2024. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $2.18B (+59.4% YoY reported, +4.3% organic) and EBITDA exceeding $1.01B for the first time. Market cap approximately $15.3B.
14,250 Employees ·www.millicom.com

Investment Thesis
Millicom at 12.91x forward earnings and PEG of 0.32 offers compelling value for a Latin American telecom with record-breaking operational momentum. Q2 2026 revenue of $2.18B (+59.4% reported, +4.3% organic) and EBITDA exceeding $1.01B for the first time demonstrate the Coltel acquisition is scaling effectively [1]. The raised 2026 equity free cash flow guidance to ~$1.1B and leverage target below 2.5x signal improving financial health. ROE of 21.73% reflects efficient capital deployment, and the 3.3% dividend yield (recently reinstated and growing) provides income while investors wait for the integration to mature. Tigo's infrastructure position across nine Latin American countries creates a durable moat — fiber, towers, and spectrum are physical assets that AI disruption cannot replicate. Mobile financial services (Tigo Money) provide a digital growth vector in markets with low banking penetration.
The bear case centers on leverage, execution risk, and earnings quality. D/E of 485% reflects $12.1B in total debt — an aggressive capital structure that leaves minimal margin for error if Latin American currencies depreciate, interest rates rise, or integration costs exceed projections [4]. Q1 2026 EPS of $0.65 massively missed the $1.44 consensus forecast, revealing that the Coltel integration headwinds are real and that reported revenue growth significantly overstates organic business momentum (organic growth only ~5%) [1]. Profit margin of 9.18% is thin for a company carrying this much debt, and the 74.81% payout ratio strains cash flow flexibility. Operating in Bolivia, Honduras, and Nicaragua exposes the company to political instability, currency controls, and regulatory risk that could impair asset values.
