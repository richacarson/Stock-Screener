Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NASDAQ ·Singapore
UP Fintech Holding (Tiger Brokers) is a leading online brokerage platform serving global investors, primarily Chinese-speaking users across Singapore, Hong Kong, the US, Australia, and New Zealand. Through its Tiger Trade app, the company offers trading in stocks, options, futures, and other financial instruments across multiple markets. It also provides wealth management, ESOP management, IPO underwriting, and investor education. Headquartered in Singapore. Named to CNBC's World's Top FinTech Companies 2026 for the second consecutive year. Q2 2026 revenue hit an all-time record of $182.3M.

Investment Thesis
UP Fintech delivered a record-setting Q2 2026 — revenue surged 31.4% YoY to an all-time high of $182.3M with net income of $39.4M, returning to profitability after the Q1 CSRC fine-driven loss [2]. At 7.42x forward P/E (PEG 0.59x) with 18.4% profit margins, 14.73% ROE, and a net cash position of $4.43B ($24.87/share), the stock trades at an extreme discount to its financial quality and growth rate [6]. FY2025 established a $612M revenue base growing 56%+, and the H1 2026 run rate of $337M annualizes to $674M+ [1][2]. The TigerAI platform — now operational in 4 markets with exponential user adoption — positions Tiger Brokers as an AI-differentiated neobroker, validated by CNBC's Top FinTech recognition for the second consecutive year [5]. Singapore and Hong Kong contribute 70%+ of new clients, demonstrating successful geographic diversification away from China risk.
The bear case centers on regulatory credibility damage and structural governance risks that explain the deep valuation discount. The CSRC fined UP Fintech $60M for conducting unlicensed cross-border securities business in mainland China — activities regulators had flagged since 2016 — sending shares down 28% in a single session [4]. Q1 2026 swung to a $26.9M net loss from $30.4M profit a year earlier, demonstrating how regulatory risk can instantly reverse profitability [3]. The People's Daily has described cross-border online brokerages as 'illegal financial activities,' and further regulatory action from Chinese, Singaporean, or Hong Kong authorities remains possible [4]. The VIE corporate structure, ADR listing, and minimal ESG transparency create governance risks that keep institutional investors away. Revenue concentration among Chinese-speaking investors means U.S.-China geopolitical tensions directly threaten the customer base. TTM profit margin of 18.4% is compressed from FY2025's 31.7%, reflecting the regulatory penalty's ongoing impact on trailing metrics.
