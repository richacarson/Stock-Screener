TKO Group Holdings, Inc. is a premium sports entertainment company formed through the 2023 merger of UFC and WWE under Endeavor Group. The company operates three segments: UFC (mixed martial arts promotion), WWE (professional wrestling entertainment), and IMG (sports marketing and events). TKO owns two of the most valuable live sports properties in the world, with combined revenue of $5.3B+ TTM.

Investment Thesis
TKO Group Holdings owns two of the most irreplaceable live sports entertainment properties in the world — UFC and WWE — with combined Q2 2026 revenue of $1.547B growing 18% YoY and adjusted EBITDA of $649.9M at an exceptional 42% margin [1]. The Paramount content distribution deal drove 29% UFC revenue growth, and the recently integrated IMG segment adds diversified sports marketing revenue. Full-year guidance was raised to $5.775-5.825B revenue and $2.275-2.305B adjusted EBITDA, with free cash flow of $1.67B supporting aggressive capital returns — $1.3B+ returned to shareholders in H1 2026 through buybacks and dividends [1]. The balance sheet is healthy at 59% D/E, and the stock's PEG of 1.12 reflects reasonable growth-adjusted valuation. Live sports content commands a scarcity premium in the streaming era, and TKO's dual brands are structurally undervalued relative to comparable sports franchises.
The primary concern is valuation transparency — at 65x trailing earnings and 34.5x forward P/E, TKO is priced for flawless execution despite GAAP profit margin of just 4.33% and ROE of 7.06%, reflecting the complex Endeavor ownership structure with significant non-cash charges and minority interest flows [4]. The $4.66B gross debt, while manageable at 59% D/E, creates interest expense that suppresses net income relative to EBITDA. Governance risk remains elevated — Vince McMahon's January 2024 resignation over sexual misconduct allegations and the $375M UFC antitrust settlement reveal systemic issues in fighter labor relations and corporate oversight [5]. The payout ratio of 109% on the dividend is unsustainable without earnings growth. Additionally, TKO's success is personality-dependent — Dana White's centrality to UFC and specific fighters' drawing power create key-person risks that corporate structures cannot fully mitigate.
Key Catalysts
  1. Paramount content distribution agreement driving 29% UFC revenue growth in Q2, with multi-year escalation clauses providing visibility into sustained media rights revenue acceleration
  2. Cross-platform synergies from UFC-WWE-IMG integration unlocking shared sponsorship, venue, and distribution efficiencies — adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 42% with further improvement potential
  3. Live sports scarcity premium intensifying as streaming platforms compete for must-see real-time content, positioning UFC and WWE as two of the most valuable media rights properties globally
Key Risks
  1. GAAP profit margin of 4.33% and ROE of 7.06% mask significant value leakage through Endeavor ownership complexity, non-cash charges, and minority interest — forward P/E of 34.5x leaves limited margin of safety
  2. Governance and reputational risk from the Vince McMahon sexual misconduct scandal and $375M UFC antitrust fighter pay settlement signal systemic labor and oversight vulnerabilities
  3. Key-person dependence on Dana White (UFC) and specific fighter drawing power creates concentration risk that corporate structures cannot fully mitigate — loss of major stars could impact event revenue
