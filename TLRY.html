Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - Specialty & Generic ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Tilray Brands, Inc. is a consumer packaged goods company with four businesses: cannabis (medical and adult-use in Canada and international medical markets), beverage (U.S. craft brewing including SweetWater, Montauk and Shock Top, plus the BrewDog acquisition), distribution (CC Pharma in Germany), and wellness (Manitoba Harvest hemp foods). Fiscal 2026 (May) net revenue was $915.5M, and Q1 FY2027 revenue reached a record $257.1M, up 23%.

Investment Thesis
Tilray's top line is growing. Q1 FY2027 revenue rose 23% to a record $257.1M, gross margin expanded to 30% from 27%, EMEA revenue rose 71%, and the company ended the quarter net cash after paying down $42M of debt [1][2]. It reaffirmed FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $68-75M, weighted to the seasonally stronger second half [1]. At about $390M market cap and roughly 0.4x sales, any progress toward U.S. cannabis rescheduling or European medical legalization gives significant upside optionality [9].
The bear case is that Tilray is a roll-up that has never earned a GAAP profit. The Q1 GAAP loss of $0.32 per share missed the $0.19 consensus, revenue missed by about $8M, and adjusted EBITDA fell to $9.2M from $10.2M [1][2]. Cannabis revenue is shrinking (down 13% YoY), the federal hemp redefinition effective November 12, 2026 threatens hemp-derived THC products [7], and adjusted free cash flow was negative $86M in FY2026 [5]. The stock has fallen to about $2.64, far below its $3.83 50-day average [9], and a third-party ESG risk score of 36.7 (High) plus a -38 Inspire score add values concerns [10].
Key Catalysts
  1. U.S. federal cannabis rescheduling to Schedule III or banking reform unlocking a U.S. cannabis entry path
  2. Delivery on FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $68-75M in the seasonally stronger H2 [1]
  3. BrewDog integration synergies and continued 70%+ EMEA growth, including German medical cannabis [1]
Key Risks
  1. Federal hemp redefinition effective November 12, 2026 eliminating hemp-derived THC beverage and edible sales [7]
  2. Continued Canadian cannabis price compression (cannabis revenue down 13% YoY) and impairment-driven GAAP losses [1]
  3. Further equity dilution or restructuring to fund acquisitions, given negative free cash flow and a sub-$3 share price [5][9]
