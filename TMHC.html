Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Taylor Morrison Home Corporation is a national homebuilder and land developer founded in 1936 and headquartered in Scottsdale, Arizona. The company designs, builds, and sells single-family and multi-family homes across multiple segments — entry-level, move-up, and resort lifestyle — under the Taylor Morrison and Esplanade brand names. CEO Sheryl Palmer has led since 2007 and holds a 96% Glassdoor CEO approval rating, ranking 19th among all large-company CEOs in the U.S. Q1 2026 EPS was $1.12 (beating estimates of $0.87 by $0.25), with full-year 2026 guidance of approximately 11,000 home closings. The company is named America's Most Trusted Builder for 11 consecutive years. Market cap approximately $5.6B.

Investment Thesis
Taylor Morrison is a well-managed homebuilder trading at an attractive 10.43x forward earnings with strong balance sheet discipline — D/E of 35% and $652.9M cash provide financial flexibility through housing cycles [4]. Q1 2026 EPS of $1.12 beat estimates by 29% ($0.87 consensus), demonstrating execution capability even as the housing market normalizes [1]. CEO Sheryl Palmer's 19-year tenure and 96% Glassdoor approval provide exceptional leadership continuity rare in the homebuilding sector. The 'Most Trusted Builder' brand, maintained for 11 consecutive years, creates customer acquisition advantages that reduce marketing costs and support premium pricing. Full-year 2026 guidance of 11,000 closings at 20% gross margin provides earnings visibility, and the Inspire Impact Score of 56 (ALIGNED) is a positive for faith-based investors [3].
The bear case centers on housing cyclicality, earnings deceleration, and structural growth limitations. Forward PE of 10.43 exceeds the trailing PE of 7-8.5, implying analysts expect earnings to decline as the housing market cools from elevated levels — elevated mortgage rates continue to constrain affordability and reduce demand, particularly in the entry-level segment [1]. Taylor Morrison generates no dividend income and has limited innovation, with the homebuilding business model unchanged for decades. ROE of 11.11% is below the 15% target, and estimated profit margin of ~8.5% is well below the 20% target, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of homebuilding. Any recession or further mortgage rate increases could sharply reduce closings and compress margins, as demonstrated in previous housing downturns. The company's reliance on CEO Palmer (19 years) creates key-person risk — her eventual succession would be the first leadership transition in nearly two decades and could disrupt the trust-based culture that differentiates Taylor Morrison. Some Glassdoor reviews cite cronyism and favoritism, suggesting the culture may be more personality-dependent than system-driven [2].
