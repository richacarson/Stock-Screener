Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NMS ·United States
T-Mobile US is the second-largest U.S. wireless carrier, serving approximately 125 million customers through postpaid, prepaid (Metro by T-Mobile, Mint Mobile), and wholesale channels. Under CEO Srini Gopalan (since November 2025, succeeding Mike Sievert), T-Mobile has transformed from a distant #4 carrier to a #2 powerhouse through the 2020 Sprint merger, 2024 Mint Mobile acquisition, and 2025 UScellular deal. Q1 2026 total revenue reached $23.1B (+10.6% YoY) with service revenue of $18.8B (+11%), while Q2 2026 delivered $22.8B (+8% YoY) with postpaid service revenue of $15.9B (+13%). Full-year 2026 guidance targets ~$77B service revenue and $37.1-37.5B Core Adjusted EBITDA.

Investment Thesis
T-Mobile at $159 trades at 11.0x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.58, representing one of the most attractive growth-at-a-reasonable-price opportunities in large-cap telecom. Q1 2026 service revenue grew 11% to $18.8B and Q2 2026 reached $19.0B (+9% YoY), driven by postpaid service revenue surging 13-15% YoY as UScellular integration adds subscribers and Sprint synergies flow through [1][3]. Management guides full-year service revenue of ~$77B (+8% YoY) and Core Adjusted EBITDA of $37.1-37.5B, while TTM operating cash flow of $28.8B and free cash flow of $11.3B fund the growing dividend ($4.08/year, 2.9% yield), share buybacks, and strategic acquisitions. Fixed wireless broadband adds a structural growth vector beyond traditional wireless, expanding TAM into the $100B+ home internet market.
The bear case centers on elevated leverage and decelerating growth. D/E of 214% reflects $120.4B in debt from Sprint and UScellular acquisitions, and net income fell 15% in Q1 2026 due to $476M in UScellular-related accelerated depreciation charges [1]. Revenue growth decelerated from 10.6% in Q1 to 8% in Q2 2026, and the stock has fallen 31% from its 52-week high of $230.54, suggesting the market has repriced growth expectations. The dividend, while growing rapidly (from $0.65/q to $1.02/q in under three years), has only a 3-year payment history — insufficient for dividend-focused investors. Integration risk from UScellular compounds the still-ongoing Sprint complexity, the CEO transition from Sievert to Gopalan introduces leadership continuity risk during a critical execution period, and T-Mobile's low beta of 0.32 limits upside in risk-on markets while high debt creates vulnerability in rising rate environments [2][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. UScellular integration yielding subscriber additions and network synergy benefits through 2027, with accelerated depreciation charges rolling off and unlocking $476M+ in annual earnings improvement as one-time costs normalize [1]
  2. Fixed wireless broadband expansion into underserved markets, competing directly with cable incumbents for the $100B+ U.S. home internet market leveraging existing 5G infrastructure with a capital-light model
  3. Forward EPS growth of ~50% to $14.42 as merger costs normalize, driving potential P/E re-rating from the current 11.0x forward multiple toward telecom peer averages of 15-18x [1][3]
Key Risks
  1. Elevated leverage of 214% D/E ($120.4B debt) creates refinancing risk and limits financial flexibility — each 100bp interest rate increase adds approximately $1.2B in annual interest expense, compressing free cash flow available for dividends and buybacks [1]
  2. Revenue growth deceleration from 10.6% in Q1 to 8% in Q2 2026 suggests organic growth momentum may be fading as the postpaid market matures and Sprint migration benefits are fully captured [3]
  3. CEO transition risk as Srinivasan Gopalan takes over from Mike Sievert during concurrent UScellular integration, with Gopalan's acknowledged openness to AI disruption risk suggesting strategic uncertainty in the company's competitive positioning [2]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): T-Mobile's core wireless business is structurally immune to AI disruption — mobile connectivity requires physical infrastructure (cell towers, spectrum licenses, fiber backhaul) that AI cannot replace [1]. AI is a net demand driver for wireless carriers: AI applications consume mobile data, AI-powered devices require always-on connectivity, and proliferation of IoT/AI edge computing increases network utilization. The deduction from 9 reflects that AI could automate customer service operations and potentially reduce the differentiation value of Un-carrier customer experience if AI levels the service playing field across carriers. New CEO Gopalan acknowledged in a February 2026 T. Rowe Price interview that T-Mobile 'shouldn't shy away from the fact that we could be disrupted' by AI, though this refers to operational disruption rather than core business substitution [2]. AI Opportunity (0/1): T-Mobile deploys AI extensively in network management, customer experience, and operational optimization, but does not generate separately quantifiable revenue from AI-related products sold to customers [3].
