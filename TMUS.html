Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
T-Mobile US, Inc. is the second-largest wireless carrier in the United States, serving approximately 125 million customers through its postpaid, prepaid (Metro by T-Mobile), and wholesale brands. Under CEO Srini Gopalan (since November 2025, succeeding Mike Sievert), T-Mobile has transformed from a distant #4 carrier to a #2 powerhouse through the 2020 Sprint merger and continued market share gains. Q2 2026 revenue reached $22.8B (+8% YoY) with industry-leading postpaid service revenue growth of 13% and the highest-ever wireless NPS of 46. The company is expanding into broadband via 5G Home Internet and acquired UScellular's wireless business to strengthen rural coverage. T-Mobile cut 4,500+ jobs in H1 2026 as part of a restructuring to become an AI-enabled, digital-first organization.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 6 /10
T-Mobile is a fascinating case for Sinek's framework — the company has demonstrated extraordinary existential flexibility (Sprint merger, broadband entry) and courage to lead (massive capital bets during uncertainty), but its competitive language is often finite ('beating' AT&T and Verizon, 'winning' market share) and its organizational trust has been tested by rapid CEO turnover and significant layoffs. The Un-carrier cause is genuine and has shaped strategic decisions for a decade, but it has not yet been validated through a true existential crisis, and the 4,500+ job cuts in 2026 suggest the cause is subordinate to financial optimization when tested.
Just Cause
7
Just Cause (2/3): T-Mobile's Un-carrier mission — to disrupt the wireless industry's broken norms and champion consumers through transparent pricing, contract elimination, unlimited data, and superior value — is a genuine cause that has been partially lived for 10+ years [2]. The cause has shaped real strategic decisions: eliminating two-year contracts (2013), introducing unlimited data, including Netflix at no charge, deploying T-Mobile Tuesdays. Multiple CEOs (Legere, Sievert, Gopalan) have maintained the Un-carrier identity, suggesting it transcends individual leadership. However, the cause has not been tested through a genuine existential crisis — the company has been winning in the market for a decade, and the 4,500+ job cuts in 2026 suggest the cause may be subordinate to financial performance when margins are pressured [5].
Trusting Teams

Trusting Teams (0/2): T-Mobile's organizational trust is undermined by three factors. First, the company cut 4,500+ jobs in H1 2026, reducing headcount by 6.7% — large-scale layoffs are incompatible with Sinek's Circle of Safety, as employees focus on self-preservation rather than advancing the cause [5]. Second, three CEO transitions in 5 years (Legere to Sievert to Gopalan) creates leadership instability that erodes organizational trust. Third, the restructuring toward an 'AI-enabled, digital-first' organization signals that roles may continue to be automated or eliminated, creating ongoing anxiety about job security [3].
Worthy Rivals
3
Worthy Rivals (0/1): T-Mobile's competitive language is predominantly finite — the Un-carrier positioning is explicitly defined in opposition to AT&T and Verizon as incumbents to be disrupted and surpassed. Management commentary frequently references 'winning' customers, 'taking share,' and outperforming competitors on NPS and net additions [1][2]. While this competitive fire has driven remarkable growth, Sinek would note that the language is about defeating rivals rather than learning from their strengths. T-Mobile's identity is built on being better THAN the competition rather than better because of the competition.
Existential Flexibility
10
Existential Flexibility (2/2): T-Mobile has fundamentally reinvented itself twice. The Sprint merger in 2020 transformed T-Mobile from a distant #4 U.S. carrier struggling to compete with AT&T and Verizon into the #2 carrier with the largest 5G network in the country — a $26B+ bet that required absorbing a failing competitor, integrating redundant networks, and managing massive execution risk [1][2]. The pivot into fixed wireless broadband (5G Home Internet) represents a second reinvention, entering the cable broadband market as a new competitive vector. Both pivots were driven by the Un-carrier cause of challenging industry norms rather than desperation, and both have succeeded, generating meaningful new revenue streams.
Courage to Lead
10
Courage to Lead (2/2): T-Mobile demonstrates a pattern of costly long-term bets. The Sprint merger ($26B+) required years of regulatory battles, network integration, and billions in capital expenditure during uncertain competitive conditions [1][2]. The company invested aggressively in 5G network buildout when ROI was uncertain, deploying mid-band spectrum at scale before competitors. The UScellular acquisition extends this pattern of courageous capital deployment. CEO Gopalan's decision to restructure toward an AI-enabled organization — cutting 4,500+ jobs — shows willingness to make painful short-term decisions for long-term capability, though Sinek would debate whether this is courage or finite cost optimization [3][5].

Investment Thesis
T-Mobile at 13.84x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.78 is the best growth-at-a-reasonable-price opportunity in U.S. telecom — delivering industry-leading 13% postpaid service revenue growth, 12% Core Adjusted EBITDA growth to $9.5B quarterly, and EPS of $2.99 that beat consensus by $0.41 [1][4]. The company's structural advantages — largest 5G network, superior spectrum holdings, and highest wireless NPS (46) — are widening the competitive gap against AT&T and Verizon, translating to consistent postpaid account net additions (277K in Q2 2026) while peers struggle. T-Mobile's free cash flow of $18.4B annually funds a $4.08/share dividend (2.36% yield), aggressive share buybacks, and strategic acquisitions (UScellular). The fixed wireless broadband opportunity adds a second growth vector as T-Mobile leverages 5G to disrupt cable incumbents in underserved markets.
The bear case centers on leverage, competitive sustainability, and execution risk from leadership transitions. T-Mobile carries $120.4B in debt (D/E 214%), a legacy of the Sprint merger that limits financial flexibility and creates refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment [4]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 5% reflects that organic growth ex-Sprint has been modest, and the current 9% YoY growth rate may decelerate as the UScellular integration dilutes near-term margins. The Un-carrier competitive advantage may be narrowing — AT&T and Verizon are improving their networks, matching T-Mobile's value positioning, and deploying their own AI capabilities. Three CEO transitions in 5 years (Legere to Sievert to Gopalan) create strategic continuity risk, and the 4,500+ job cuts in H1 2026 signal that T-Mobile is shifting from growth-at-all-costs to efficiency optimization — a potentially finite game shift [3][5]. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors. A widespread network outage in 2026 that affected 140,000+ users highlights operational reliability concerns that could increase regulatory scrutiny and customer churn.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): T-Mobile's core wireless business is structurally immune to AI disruption — mobile connectivity requires physical infrastructure (cell towers, spectrum licenses, fiber backhaul) that AI cannot replace or replicate [1]. AI is a net demand driver for wireless carriers: AI applications consume mobile data, AI-powered devices require always-on connectivity, and the proliferation of IoT/AI edge computing increases network utilization. T-Mobile's 5G network is the backbone for AI-enabled services, not a victim of them. The company's fixed wireless broadband expansion (5G Home Internet) faces minimal AI disruption risk as it competes with cable on physical infrastructure. The deduction from 9 reflects that AI could automate customer service operations (T-Mobile's call centers) and potentially reduce the differentiation value of Un-carrier customer experience if AI levels the customer service playing field across carriers [3]. AI Opportunity (0/1): T-Mobile deploys AI extensively in network management, customer experience, and operational optimization, but does not generate separately quantifiable revenue from AI-related products or services sold to customers. AI is a cost optimization and experience enhancement tool, not a revenue line [3].
