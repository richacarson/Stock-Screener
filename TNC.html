Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Tennant Company is a 156-year-old manufacturer of commercial and industrial cleaning equipment including floor scrubbers, sweepers, and autonomous cleaning robots. Headquartered in Eden Prairie, Minnesota, Tennant has invested significantly in autonomous cleaning technology through its partnership with Brain Corp, deploying BrainOS-powered machines including the T7AMR, T16AMR, and X6 ROVR. The Robotics segment delivered $31M in Q2 2026 revenue (+37% YoY). Full-year 2026 revenue guidance is $1.27-1.31B, though adjusted EBITDA guidance was lowered to $155-170M due to ERP costs and EMEA margin pressures.

Investment Thesis
Tennant Company combines a 156-year industrial legacy with a genuinely differentiated autonomous cleaning strategy — the Robotics segment grew 37% YoY in Q2 2026 to $31M revenue, powered by Brain Corp's BrainOS platform and the newly launched X6 ROVR for large facilities [1][2]. At a forward P/E of 11.78, the stock trades at a significant discount to industrial peers, reflecting depressed current earnings from ERP implementation costs and EMEA margin pressures — but adjusted EBITDA guidance of $155-170M on $1.27-1.31B revenue implies a path to earnings normalization [1]. Orders grew 6.6% YoY in Q2 and 10% in Q1 2026, outpacing revenue and suggesting a building backlog of demand [1]. The Inspire Impact score of 75 (ALIGNED) with 18 positive ESG attributions and zero negatives reflects one of the strongest faith alignment profiles in the screen universe [3]. The investment in Brain Corp (2024) and recurring autonomy services revenue model provide a structural growth vector within the traditional cleaning equipment market.
The bear case centers on execution challenges and slow organic growth. Full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance was lowered 17.5% at the midpoint to $155-170M, reflecting ERP implementation costs and EMEA operational pressures [1]. Q2 EPS of $0.83 missed the $1.23 consensus by 33%, and the stock dropped 13.8% on the report [1]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 2.5% demonstrates that the core cleaning equipment market is structurally slow-growing — autonomous robotics at $31M quarterly revenue represents only ~10% of total sales and may take years to materially shift the overall growth profile [1]. GAAP profit margin of 1.5% and ROE of 3.1% are deeply depressed, and while management attributes this to temporary ERP costs, the path back to normalized margins is uncertain [4]. D/E of 73% limits financial flexibility for additional autonomous technology investments or acquisitions. The dividend yield of 1.7% falls below the 2% threshold typically sought by income-focused industrial investors.
Key Catalysts
  1. Robotics segment momentum — 37% YoY Q2 2026 growth to $31M revenue, with autonomous cleaning adoption accelerating as labor shortages persist and facilities increasingly demand AI-enabled cleaning solutions [1][2]

Key Risks
  1. EMEA operational challenges and ERP cost overruns could persist beyond management expectations, further compressing margins and delaying the earnings recovery implied by the 11.78x forward P/E [1]
  2. Structurally slow-growth core market — 5-year revenue CAGR of 2.5% in traditional cleaning equipment limits upside even as the Robotics segment grows, and autonomous cleaning is still only ~10% of total revenue [1][4]
  3. Robotics segment competitive risk — well-funded startups and larger industrial players (ABB, iRobot) could accelerate autonomous cleaning capabilities, and Brain Corp could license BrainOS to Tennant competitors [2]
