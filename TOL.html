Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Residential Construction ·NYSE ·United States
Toll Brothers, Inc. is the largest luxury homebuilder in the United States, building single-family detached and attached homes, active-adult communities, and rental apartments (Toll Brothers Apartment Living) in 24 states. Founded in 1967, the company targets roughly $10.5 billion in FY2026 home sales revenue from 471 selling communities. Karl Mistry, who joined in 2004 through the executive training program, succeeded Doug Yearley as CEO on March 30, 2026, and Yearley stayed on as Executive Chairman.

Investment Thesis
Toll Brothers is the dominant U.S. luxury homebuilder and trades at about 10.6x forward earnings and a 0.90 PEG. Its balance sheet is conservative at 34% D/E [5]. Demand is holding up: Q3 FY2026 net signed contract value rose 5% to $2.52B, and the average backlog price climbed to $1.17M, which shows the affluent buyer is still active [1]. Selling communities grew to 471 from 420, setting up volume growth in FY2027 [1]. Meanwhile $700M of FY2026 buybacks, about 5% of the market cap, supports per-share value [1].
The bear case is cyclical compression. Q3 revenue fell 9.7% and EPS fell 20.4% to $2.97. FY2026 deliveries are guided down to 10,500-10,600 from 11,292, and adjusted gross margin has slipped about 190 bps to 25.6% [1]. Backlog value of $6.24B is below last year's $6.38B, so FY2027 revenue depends on spring selling and mortgage rates [1]. A new CEO only seven months into the job adds execution risk if the luxury market weakens further.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q4 FY2026 results in early December: guidance calls for $3.43-3.57B home sales revenue and a 26.1% full-year adjusted gross margin; beating it would confirm the margin trough
  2. Community count up about 12% YoY to 471 selling communities, which should turn into FY2027 delivery growth if absorption pace holds
  3. Falling mortgage rates would widen the buyer pool for $1M+ homes, many of which are bought partly with cash and so are less rate-sensitive
Key Risks
  1. Gross margin erosion: adjusted home sales gross margin fell to 25.6% from 27.5% YoY, and more incentives on spec homes could push it lower
  2. Backlog value of $6.24B is down 2% YoY and homes in backlog are down 3%, which limits FY2027 revenue visibility
  3. Wealth-effect sensitivity: a stock market correction would hit Toll's affluent buyer base directly and could stall contract growth
