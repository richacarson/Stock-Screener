Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NASDAQ ·Canada
Thomson Reuters Corporation is a global content and technology company providing news, information, and AI-powered software solutions to legal, tax, accounting, compliance, and government professionals. The company operates through Legal Professionals, Corporates, Tax & Accounting Professionals, Reuters News, and Global Print segments, with approximately 85% recurring revenue. The Woodbridge Company (Thomson family) holds ~67% controlling interest. Founded through the 2008 merger of Thomson Corporation and Reuters Group, headquartered in Toronto, Canada.

The Woodbridge Company (Thomson family) ~67% controlling stake provides multi-generational patient capital. Investment Thesis
Thomson Reuters delivered strong Q1 2026 results with revenue up 10% to $2,087M, adjusted EBITDA margin of 42.2%, net earnings of $459M, and free cash flow of $332M (+19% YoY), reaffirming full-year guidance of 7.5-8% organic growth and ~$2.1B FCF [1]. The Big 3 segments grew 9% organically, driven by AI-powered products including CoCounsel legal AI and embedded AI across Westlaw and ONESOURCE [1][3]. With a 33-year consecutive dividend increase streak (five straight 10% raises), conservative D/E of 22.9%, and dominant market share in legal and tax professional services, Thomson Reuters offers a compelling combination of income growth visibility and AI-driven business transformation. The Woodbridge Company (Thomson family) ~67% controlling stake provides multi-generational patient capital unique among public companies.
At 25.5x trailing earnings and Forward P/E of 19.7, Thomson Reuters trades at a premium for a company with only 5.5% 5-year revenue CAGR and 12.7% ROE — the AI-driven growth acceleration to 8-10% organic is encouraging but has not yet proven sustainable across economic cycles [5]. The elevated payout ratio of 73.1% limits capital allocation flexibility between dividends, buybacks, and M&A at a time when AI investment demands significant ongoing spending. While the CoCounsel AI strategy differentiates from competitors, RELX (LexisNexis) and Wolters Kluwer are making parallel AI investments, and AI-native legal-tech competitors (Harvey, Hebbia) target the Westlaw workflow layer [3]. CEO Hasker himself has publicly framed the 'expensive content retaining value' question as the central strategic challenge, acknowledging the medium-term risk from generalist AI platforms commoditizing basic professional research.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued AI monetization through CoCounsel and embedded AI features driving organic growth acceleration from historical 5-6% toward the 8-10% range, with AI-enabled products becoming a growing share of annualized contract value [1][3]
  2. Free cash flow generation of ~$2.1B in 2026 supporting the 34th consecutive annual dividend increase alongside share repurchases ($262M in Q1) and bolt-on AI acquisitions [1]
  3. Big 3 segment growth — Legal 8-9%, Corporates 9-11%, Tax/Audit/Accounting 11-13% — demonstrating the AI-enhanced workflow value proposition is gaining traction in enterprise budgets [1]
Key Risks
  1. Payout ratio of 73.1% is elevated and could constrain the company's ability to simultaneously fund AI investment, dividend growth, and share buybacks if organic growth decelerates below the 7.5% guidance floor
  2. AI-native legal-tech competitors (Harvey, Hebbia) and generalist AI platforms (OpenAI, Google Gemini) could commoditize basic legal research functions, pressuring Westlaw's entry-level product tier and forcing pricing concessions [3]
  3. 5-year revenue CAGR of only 5.5% reflects a historically mature growth profile — if the AI-driven acceleration proves temporary, the premium valuation at 25.5x trailing earnings becomes difficult to sustain [5]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Thomson Reuters' core business is structurally protected from AI disruption — proprietary, curated legal and regulatory content (Westlaw, Practical Law, Checkpoint) requires editorial accuracy, citation authority, and regulatory compliance that generalist AI models cannot replicate [1][3]. Legal and tax professionals require authoritative, citable sources with guaranteed accuracy, and the company's deep workflow integration creates prohibitive switching costs. CEO Hasker has explicitly framed the 'expensive content retaining value' question as the core strategic challenge, demonstrating management awareness and proactive response with $500M+ AI investment [3]. However, AI-native legal-tech competitors (Harvey, Hebbia) and generalist platforms (OpenAI, Google) could commoditize basic legal research at the entry-level product tier over the medium term. AI Opportunity (1/1): CoCounsel AI legal assistant generates revenue and drives product differentiation, with AI features embedded across the Big 3 segments contributing to 9% organic growth; the proprietary legal foundation model creates a defensible AI capability moat [1][3].
