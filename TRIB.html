Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NMS ·Ireland
Trinity Biotech is an Irish publicly traded diagnostics company specializing in the development, manufacture, and marketing of point-of-care and laboratory-based diagnostic test kits. The company's core products span rapid HIV testing (TrinScreen HIV), haemoglobin testing (Premier and Destiny instruments), and clinical chemistry, sold globally with particular strength in developing markets. Founded in 1992 and headquartered in Bray, Ireland, Trinity Biotech is executing a Comprehensive Transformation Plan under CEO John Gillard to outsource manufacturing, reduce costs, and focus growth on its TrinScreen HIV platform.

Investment Thesis
Trinity Biotech's Q1 2026 results show encouraging momentum — 43% revenue growth to $10.8M, gross margin expansion from 25.2% to 35.4%, and adjusted EBITDA improving to -$1.1M from -$4.0M — driven by strong TrinScreen HIV demand including 2M+ test orders for Q3 2026 delivery [1]. CEO Gillard's Comprehensive Transformation Plan, centered on outsourced manufacturing for cost-effective scalability, is producing measurable operational improvement. If TrinScreen HIV can sustain its global health screening momentum and the outsourced manufacturing model delivers gross margins above 40%, the company has a narrow path to profitability that could trigger a significant re-rating from today's $8.2M market cap [1][3].
The bear case is severe and dominates the investment profile. Total debt of $138M against an $8.2M market cap and only $5.1M in cash creates existential solvency risk — the company is leveraged nearly 17x relative to its equity value [3]. Profit margins of -85.4% and trailing EPS of -$2.00 reflect a business that is deeply unprofitable despite revenue growth. The stock fails the IOWN liquidity floor with just $529K in daily dollar volume, creating meaningful execution risk for any position. Forward EPS of -$1.03 implies continued losses through the transformation period, and the company's 287-person workforce must execute a complex manufacturing outsourcing while simultaneously scaling TrinScreen HIV production to fulfill the 2M+ test order backlog [1][2]. Any disruption to PEPFAR or Global Fund HIV testing budgets — which faced cuts in 2025 — could eliminate the demand that underpins the entire turnaround thesis.
