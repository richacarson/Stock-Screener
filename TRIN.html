Company Profile
Financials ·Asset Management & Custody Banks ·NASDAQ ·United States
Trinity Capital Inc. is an internally managed business development company (BDC) that provides debt financing, including term loans, equipment financing, and asset-based lending, to growth-stage companies across five verticals: Sponsor Finance, Equipment Finance, Tech Lending, Asset-Based Lending, and Life Sciences. Founded in 2019, IPO'd in 2021, and headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona. The company recently expanded to Europe with a London-based managing director.
150 Employees ·www.trinitycap.com

Investment Thesis
Trinity Capital delivered record FY2025 results with $279.5M revenue (up 23.3% YoY), record quarterly funding of $471M, and NAV growth of 33% YoY, followed by a strong Q1 2026 beating estimates with $90.1M revenue (up 37.8% YoY) [1][3]. The company trades at a compelling 8.39x forward P/E while yielding 11.6%, and its diversification into five lending verticals with European expansion provides multiple growth vectors. ROE of 13.8% and profit margin of 43.5% are strong for the BDC sector, and 23 consecutive quarters of maintained or increased dividends demonstrate distribution reliability. The growth-stage lending niche benefits from AI sector expansion driving new borrower demand.
The primary risk is credit quality through a full market cycle — Trinity has only been public since 2021 and has not been tested through a significant credit downturn, which is particularly relevant for a portfolio concentrated in growth-stage companies that are inherently higher-risk borrowers. The 93% payout ratio leaves minimal cushion for credit losses, though this is structurally required for BDC tax status [4]. At a D/E of 116%, the company operates with leverage typical for BDCs but vulnerable to rising non-accruals if the startup/growth-stage lending environment deteriorates. Additionally, the negative PEG ratio of -0.71 suggests analysts may expect earnings growth deceleration, and the small market cap (~$2B) creates liquidity constraints compared to larger BDC peers. Competition from larger, better-capitalized BDCs like Ares Capital could compress yields and limit Trinity's deal flow advantage.
Key Catalysts
  1. European expansion via London-based managing director creating new origination channels and geographic diversification of the portfolio beyond U.S. growth-stage companies
  2. AI sector lending growth as portfolio companies like TQA scale AI offerings, with Trinity's tech lending vertical positioned to capture increasing demand for growth-stage AI financing
  3. Continued NAV growth (33% YoY in 2025) demonstrating portfolio quality improvement and potential for further stock price appreciation from narrowing NAV discount
Key Risks
  1. Untested through a full credit cycle — portfolio concentrated in growth-stage companies that are inherently higher risk; a recession could trigger elevated non-accruals and reduce NAV
  2. 93% payout ratio provides minimal cushion for credit losses or dividend sustainability during periods of portfolio stress, despite being structurally required for BDC tax status
  3. Small market cap (~$2B) limits institutional ownership and creates wider bid-ask spreads; competition from much larger BDCs (Ares Capital, Blue Owl) could compress lending yields
