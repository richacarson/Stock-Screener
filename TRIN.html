Company Profile
Financial Services ·Asset Management ·NASDAQ ·United States
Trinity Capital Inc. is an internally managed business development company (BDC) that provides debt financing, including term loans, equipment financing, and asset-based lending, to growth-stage companies across five verticals: Sponsor Finance, Equipment Finance, Tech Lending, Asset-Based Lending, and Life Sciences. Founded in 2008, IPO'd in 2021, headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona. The company has deployed $6.2B across 490 investments since inception and recently expanded to Europe with a London-based managing director. Forbes spotlighted Trinity for excelling in company culture and employee benefits.
113 Employees ·www.trinitycapital.com

Investment Thesis
Trinity Capital offers a compelling 11.57% dividend yield at just 8.37x forward P/E, backed by 19.3% revenue growth and a diversified five-vertical lending platform that has deployed $6.2B across 490 investments since inception [1][5]. FY2025 delivered $279.5M revenue (up 23.3% YoY) and $135.6M earnings (up 17.3%), and approximately 25 consecutive quarters of maintained or increased dividends demonstrate distribution reliability [1]. The 5-year dividend growth CAGR of ~10% significantly exceeds inflation. ROE of 12.91% and profit margin of 45.15% are strong for the BDC sector. Key growth themes in AI, robotics, reshoring, and data centers position Trinity's tech lending vertical to capture increasing demand for growth-stage financing. The Inspire score of 34 (ALIGNED) with 7 positive attributions makes Trinity suitable for faith-based portfolios, and Forbes recognition for company culture suggests organizational quality [3].
The primary risk is credit quality through a full market cycle — Trinity has only been public since 2021 and has not been tested through a significant credit downturn, which is particularly relevant for a portfolio concentrated in growth-stage companies that are inherently higher-risk borrowers [1]. GAAP earnings declined 22% YoY even as revenue grew 19.3%, suggesting potential unrealized portfolio losses or credit deterioration [5]. The GAAP payout ratio of 127.01% exceeds 100% — while the distribution is covered by NII and structurally required for BDC tax status, this leaves minimal cushion for credit losses [4]. The PEG ratio of 5.14 reflects consensus expectations of earnings growth deceleration. At D/E of 118.88%, the company operates with leverage typical for BDCs but vulnerable to rising non-accruals if the startup/growth-stage lending environment deteriorates. Competition from larger, better-capitalized BDCs like Ares Capital could compress yields and limit Trinity's deal flow advantage. The small market cap (~$1.7B) and 113-person team create concentration risk in key personnel.
Key Catalysts

  2. GAAP payout ratio of 127.01% and -22% earnings growth signal potential credit deterioration — while NII coverage remains adequate, sustained underperformance could force a dividend cut [4][5]
  3. Small market cap (~$1.7B) and 113-person team create concentration risk in key personnel and limit institutional ownership; competition from much larger BDCs could compress lending yields [1]
