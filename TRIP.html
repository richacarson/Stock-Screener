Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NASDAQ ·United States
Tripadvisor operates an online travel platform spanning hotel meta-search, restaurant reviews, and experiences/tours booking (Viator). Q2 2026 revenue from continuing operations was $441.9M (-7% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $76.4M (17.3% margin, down 21% YoY). The Hotels & Other segment declined 21% while the Experiences segment grew 3% with Viator delivering 10% bookings growth. The company announced the pending sale of TheFork to American Express Travel for $700M, expected to close by year-end 2026. The core meta-search business faces existential pressure from AI-powered travel planning tools that are reducing user reliance on traditional review aggregation. Market cap is approximately $1.1B.

Investment Thesis
Tripadvisor trades at 8.52x forward earnings — a deep value multiple that reflects the market's severe skepticism about the company's long-term viability in an AI-disrupted travel landscape. The bull case hinges on the Experiences segment (Viator), which grew bookings 10% in Q2 2026 and represents a stickier, transaction-based business model less vulnerable to AI disintermediation than meta-search [1]. The pending $700M TheFork sale to American Express Travel would generate substantial cash, reduce complexity, and could fund share buybacks or Viator investment that re-rates the stock from its depressed ~$1.1B market cap. The Tripadvisor brand retains massive global recognition with 1B+ reviews, and if management successfully transitions to an Experiences-first strategy, the stock could rerate from a declining-media multiple toward a travel-commerce multiple.
The bear case is structural and urgent. Tripadvisor's core Hotels & Other segment declined 21% YoY in Q2 2026, and first-half net loss of $10.0M (versus $25.0M net income prior year) shows profitability is collapsing alongside revenue [1]. AI-powered travel planning tools from Google, OpenAI, and Perplexity are directly substituting for Tripadvisor's core user-generated review aggregation — the exact use case that built the company. Employees describe the core product as a 'legacy cash cow' with 'LLMs delivering a severe blow' [3]. D/E of 182.9% reflects $1.25B in debt against a shrinking revenue base, creating financial fragility if the decline accelerates. Repeated rounds of layoffs ('every 6 months' per employees) have drained institutional talent and morale. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors. At $9.46 per share, the stock is down over 80% from its 2014 peak of ~$110, and the trajectory of the core business suggests further erosion absent a strategic transformation that management has not yet demonstrated the ability to execute.
Key Catalysts
  1. TheFork sale closing for $700M by year-end 2026 would generate cash exceeding 60% of current market cap, enabling aggressive share buybacks, debt reduction, or Viator investment that could materially re-rate the stock [1]

  2. Hotels & Other segment revenue declined 21% YoY in Q2 2026 with no sign of stabilization, suggesting the core business is in structural decline rather than a cyclical trough, potentially dragging total revenue negative even as Experiences grows [1]
