Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Chemicals ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Tronox is one of the world's largest vertically integrated producers of titanium dioxide (TiO2) pigment, mining titanium-bearing mineral sands and processing them into pigment, zircon, and rare earth concentrates used in paints, coatings, plastics, ceramics, and paper. Q2 2026 revenue rose 19% YoY to $868M on an 18% TiO2 volume rebound, but adjusted EBITDA fell 22% to $73M (8.4% margin) and net leverage reached 11.4x TTM EBITDA. The shares have fallen from roughly $9 in August to $3.60 after multiple analyst downgrades, giving a market cap near $575M ahead of Q3 results on November 4, 2026. CEO John Romano, a 35-year company veteran, has led as sole CEO since 2024.

Investment Thesis
Tronox is a deep-cyclical, deeply levered option on a TiO2 recovery. Q2 2026 revenue rose 19% YoY to $868M with TiO2 volumes up 18% and zircon revenue up 43%, and management guided Q3 adjusted EBITDA to $95-115M (up 30-58% sequentially) on mid-single-digit TiO2 and mid-to-high single-digit zircon price increases, plus meaningful positive full-year free cash flow [1]. The cost program is tracking toward the high end of its $125-175M run-rate target by year-end 2026, inventory was cut ~$120M in the quarter, and the term loans and bonds carry no financial covenants with no major maturity until 2029 [1]. At $3.60 the equity is valued near $575M against ~$3.1B of annual revenue, so any sustained pricing recovery would have outsized equity leverage [4].
The bear case is the balance sheet. Net debt is $3.0B and net leverage rose to 11.4x TTM adjusted EBITDA from 9.0x at year-end 2025, Q2 adjusted EBITDA fell 22% YoY despite the volume rebound, and the GAAP net loss was $171M including a $103M tax valuation allowance [1]. Yahoo Finance data show D/E near 297%, ROE of -41% and profit margin of -19%, while FY2025 revenue of $2.89B is barely above FY2020's $2.76B [4][5]. The quarterly dividend has been cut from $0.125 to $0.05 and analysts have stepped back — Goldman Sachs cut its target to $4 with a Sell rating and UBS to $4.50 in October 2026 as the shares fell to the low $3s [3]. The Q3 print on November 4 is a binary test of whether price increases are finally sticking.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results on November 4: guidance of $95-115M adjusted EBITDA with sequential TiO2 price gains would mark the first real margin inflection since 2024 [1][3]
  2. Cost improvement program reaching the high end of its $125-175M annual run-rate target by year-end 2026, alongside inventory reduction and a return to positive full-year free cash flow [1]
  3. Zircon supply expansion (furnace restart, Namakwa West Mine restart) and the rare earths cracking-and-leaching definitive feasibility study due by Q3 2027 [1]
Key Risks
  1. Net leverage of 11.4x TTM adjusted EBITDA and $3.0B net debt leave little margin for error if TiO2 pricing fails to recover; further equity dilution or asset sales are possible [1]
  2. Sustained Chinese TiO2 overcapacity and weak coatings demand have kept prices flat even as volumes recovered, compressing Q2 EBITDA margin to 8.4% from 12.7% [1]
  3. Negative analyst momentum — Goldman Sachs Sell ($4 target), UBS cut to $4.50 — and a 60%+ share-price decline since August signal market doubt about the recovery timeline [3]
