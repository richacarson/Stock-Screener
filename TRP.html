Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·Canada
TC Energy operates the largest natural gas pipeline network in North America, spanning approximately 93,300 km of pipelines across Canada, the United States, and Mexico. Following the October 2023 spinoff of its liquids pipelines business as South Bow Energy, the company is focused on natural gas transmission (NGTL, Canadian Mainline, Columbia Gas, ANR), power and energy solutions (including a 48.4% interest in Bruce Power, Canada's largest nuclear generating facility), and natural gas storage. TC Energy reported Q2 2026 comparable EBITDA of C$2.95B (+12% YoY) and guided to the upper end of its 2026 EBITDA outlook of C$11.6-11.8B. CEO Francois Poirier received the 2026 Canadian Business Leader of the Year award. Market cap approximately $65B.

Investment Thesis
TC Energy at 22.48x forward earnings offers exposure to North America's largest natural gas pipeline network with 12% EBITDA growth and improving earnings visibility -- Q2 2026 comparable EBITDA of C$2.95B beat expectations with EBITDA guidance raised to the upper end of the C$11.6-11.8B range [1]. The post-spinoff pure-play natural gas focus simplifies the investment thesis, with regulated and contracted cash flows providing revenue certainty. The 4.02% dividend yield (40-year payment history) and C$10B capital project pipeline (C$3B sanctioned in H1 2026, C$7B pending) provide income and growth. AI-driven power demand represents a secular tailwind for natural gas infrastructure, with CEO Poirier actively positioning TC Energy to serve data center electricity needs through pipeline expansions and power generation assets [3]. Bruce Power's nuclear generating capacity adds clean baseload power exposure.
The bear case centers on leverage, dividend sustainability, and energy transition risk. D/E of 165% and a 96% payout ratio leave minimal margin for error -- any earnings disappointment could pressure the dividend, and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of only 1.3% reflects limited capacity for meaningful distribution increases [5]. The Coastal GasLink project experienced massive cost overruns (final cost C$14.5B vs. original C$6.2B estimate), raising questions about capital allocation discipline on large projects [4]. Long-term energy transition away from natural gas remains a structural risk, though the timeline is measured in decades rather than years. Revenue growth of approximately 6% YoY is moderate for the forward valuation, and the post-spinoff company has a shorter standalone track record. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects a neutral faith alignment profile with no positive or negative attributions identified. Regulatory and Indigenous opposition to pipeline construction could delay or increase costs for the C$7B pending project portfolio.
