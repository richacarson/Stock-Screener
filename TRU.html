Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Business Services ·NYSE ·United States
TransUnion is a global information and insights company and one of the three major consumer credit bureaus in the United States, alongside Equifax and Experian. The company provides credit information, fraud detection, identity verification, and analytical solutions to businesses and consumers across financial services, insurance, healthcare, and government sectors. Serves over 65,000 businesses and approximately one billion consumers worldwide. Launched OneTru AI-powered platform connecting data, identity, and analytics. Expanded into identity and fraud prevention through the $3.1B Neustar acquisition (2021). Q2 2026 revenue $1,310M (+15% reported). Market cap ~$16.3B.

Investment Thesis
TransUnion delivered strong Q2 2026 results with revenue of $1,310M (+15% reported, +10% organic CC) and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.23 (+13%), beating the high end of guidance by $0.08, prompting management to raise full-year 2026 guidance to 12-13% revenue growth and 11-12% adjusted EPS growth [1]. U.S. Financial Services led at 18% growth, validating the Neustar-enhanced platform serving identity, fraud prevention, and credit decisioning. International revenues accelerated to 6% organically with strong performances in Canada, India, and the UK [1]. At Forward P/E of 18.4 and estimated PEG of ~1.4, TransUnion offers reasonable value for a company delivering accelerating growth in an FCRA-regulated oligopoly with structural moats against AI disruption. The OneTru AI-powered platform and deleveraging trajectory (D/E improving from 118% toward target) provide a visible path toward margin expansion and capital return improvement.
The bear case centers on leverage, profitability, and cyclical exposure. D/E of 113% remains elevated from the $3.1B Neustar acquisition, constraining financial flexibility, and any economic downturn compressing lending volumes would pressure both revenue and the deleveraging timeline [5]. GAAP ROE of 9.80% and net profit margin of 9.46% trail the IOWN targets and credit bureau peers, reflecting ongoing Neustar intangible amortization and integration costs that may prove persistent rather than transitional [5]. Revenue carries cyclical exposure to mortgage and auto lending volumes — the company assumes 'conservative mortgage volume declines due to higher rates' in its guidance, meaning any further rate increases would compress growth [1]. AI-native lending platforms (Upstart, Zest AI) represent a long-tail risk to traditional credit bureau dependence, particularly in thin-file and alternative lending segments. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): TransUnion's core credit reporting business operates in a regulated oligopoly under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) — only three national consumer reporting agencies operate at scale, a structural moat AI cannot dissolve [1][3]. Financial institutions are legally required to use credit bureau data for lending decisions, and TransUnion's proprietary consumer credit files on over one billion individuals cannot be replicated by AI models. The deeply embedded integration with lenders' underwriting systems and regulatory infrastructure creates prohibitive switching costs. Identity verification and fraud prevention data from the Neustar acquisition adds another defensible data moat. The Data Risk Committee and AI risk assessment framework demonstrate proactive governance of AI-related risks [3]. However, AI-native lending platforms (Upstart, Zest AI) could gradually reduce traditional credit bureau dependence for certain lending decisions. AI Opportunity (1/1): TransUnion generates revenue from AI-powered fraud detection, identity resolution, and credit decisioning products through the OneTru platform. TruVision credit scoring and OneTru real-time fraud analytics use machine learning trained on proprietary data, with AI capabilities serving as a competitive differentiator across financial services, insurance, and government verticals [3].
