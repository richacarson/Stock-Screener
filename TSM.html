Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NYSE ·Taiwan
Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry. It makes leading-edge logic chips for NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm, and the hyperscalers' custom AI accelerators. Morris Chang founded it in 1987 and invented the pure-play foundry model. Q3 2026 revenue was $46.7B, up 41.2% YoY in USD, at a 66.4% gross margin, and management expects full-year 2026 USD growth above 40%. C.C. Wei is Chairman and CEO.
84,000 Employees ·www.tsmc.com

Track Record (3/3): TSMC has ramped N5 (2020), N3 (2023), and N2 (2025-26) in sequence, and its CoWoS advanced packaging became the bottleneck technology of the AI buildout. Its revenue mix has shifted from smartphone-led to HPC/AI-led [1][3]. AI Deployment (2/2): TSMC manufactures nearly every frontier AI accelerator, and management projects AI accelerator revenue to grow at a 54-56% CAGR from 2024 to 2029, up from a prior 45% estimate [3]. Pipeline (2/2): N2 is in steep volume ramp, which management says will dilute gross margin by 3-4 points. A16 follows, and 2026 capex was raised to $60-64B from $52-56B to meet AI demand [1][2]. R&D Intensity (1/1): R&D is about 7% of revenue, which is above other foundries and the largest absolute process-technology R&D budget in the industry [5].

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: INFINITE Overall: 9 /10
TSMC is one of the clearest infinite players in global industry. Its founding promise to be a trusted partner that never competes with its customers has held for 39 years, through a founder succession and several semiconductor cycles. Leadership keeps spending heavily through downturns and frames success as enabling the ecosystem. The main gap is organizational: the culture is demanding, and the evidence that employees feel psychologically safe is not strong enough for full credit.
Just Cause
10
Just Cause (3/3): The pure-play foundry model, under which TSMC exists to make other people's innovations possible, is service-oriented, inclusive, and has lasted 39 years. Wei has said any successor must share the values of 'technology leadership, capacity expansion, and customer trust', which shows the cause outranks any single leader [3][4].
Trusting Teams
6
Trusting Teams (1/2): Leadership has been very continuous: Wei has been at TSMC for more than 28 years and succeeded founder Morris Chang smoothly. In 2026 the company raised employee profit-sharing by about 30% [3][4]. Offsetting this, there are persistent reports of long hours and a rigid hierarchy, and U.S. fab staff have described cultural friction in Arizona. That points to adequate rather than exceptional trust.
Worthy Rivals
10
Worthy Rivals (1/1): Management describes Samsung and Intel as serious competitors that drive its own execution, and it frames its position around earning customer trust rather than destroying rivals. TSMC has also stuck to its pledge not to compete with its customers [3].
Existential Flexibility
10
Existential Flexibility (2/2): TSMC created an industry by spinning out the foundry model in 1987. It later moved from being a wafer supplier to offering advanced packaging (CoWoS, SoIC) and a geographically spread manufacturing base in Arizona, Japan, and Germany, accepting lower overseas margins to keep its customers' trust [1][2].
Courage to Lead
10
Courage to Lead (2/2): TSMC raised 2026 capex to $60-64B even though the higher spend weighed on sentiment, and it accepted 3-4 points of N2 margin dilution to stay ahead on process technology [1][2]. It has a long pattern of counter-cyclical investment, including record capex through the 2009 and 2023 downturns.

Investment Thesis
TSMC is the only company that can make frontier AI silicon at scale, and the numbers show it. Q3 2026 revenue was $46.7B (up 41.2% YoY), gross margin was 66.4%, and operating margin was 58.7%, all above guidance [1]. Management raised full-year 2026 USD growth to above 40% and expects a 54-56% AI accelerator revenue CAGR through 2029 [2][3]. Even so, the stock trades at 18.5x forward earnings with a 0.80 PEG, a 51% net margin, and 12% D/E [5].
The bear case centers on concentration and capital intensity. 2026 capex of $60-64B, with Morgan Stanley estimating $75-80B for 2027, assumes AI demand lasts; a hyperscaler spending pause would leave TSMC with expensive idle capacity [2]. The N2 ramp and overseas fabs will dilute gross margin by several points, and nearly all leading-edge capacity still sits in Taiwan, about 100 miles from mainland China [1]. Water and power limits are also real: TSMC used about 8% of Taiwan's electricity in 2023, and environmental groups have challenged new Kaohsiung fabs [6].
Key Catalysts
  1. N2 volume ramp and A16 introduction lifting average wafer prices; Q4 2026 guidance is a 65-67% gross margin despite N2 dilution
  2. CoWoS capacity roughly tripling to meet AI accelerator demand growing about 80% a year, which unlocks more AI revenue
  3. Arizona fab ramp diversifying the manufacturing base and lowering U.S. tariff and geopolitical risk for U.S. customers
Key Risks
  1. Taiwan Strait geopolitical risk: most leading-edge capacity is in Taiwan, and any blockade or conflict would halt global AI chip supply
  2. AI capex cycle risk: 2026 capex of $60-64B, possibly rising to $75-80B in 2027, assumes durable hyperscaler demand, and a pause would hurt utilization and margins
  3. Margin dilution from overseas fabs and the N2 ramp (3-4 points), plus U.S. tariff and export-control policy uncertainty
