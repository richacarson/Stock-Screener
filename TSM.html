Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NYSE ·Taiwan
Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, manufacturing chips for fabless design companies including NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, Qualcomm, and Google. TSMC invented the pure-play foundry model in 1987 and holds approximately 60% of global foundry market share and over 90% of advanced nodes (sub-7nm). Q2 2026 revenue of $40.2B (up 33.7% YoY in USD), with management raising full-year revenue growth guidance to above 40% in USD terms. Led by Chairman and CEO C.C. Wei.

Investment Thesis
TSMC is the most important company in the global AI supply chain, manufacturing virtually every cutting-edge AI chip and benefiting from insatiable demand. Q2 2026 delivered $40.2B revenue (up 33.7% YoY in USD) with net income of NT$706.56B (up 77.4% YoY), both exceeding expectations [1][2]. Management raised full-year 2026 USD revenue growth guidance to above 40% (up from above 30%), driven by N2 ramp and AI demand acceleration [2]. HPC/AI now represents 66% of revenue and surged 20% QoQ, confirming TSMC as the primary beneficiary of AI infrastructure buildout [1]. The balance sheet is impregnable — D/E of 17% with ~40% ROE — supporting $18.75-20B in annual capex investment for next-generation manufacturing [5]. Over 30 C-suite executives and directors purchased stock in July-August 2026, totaling nearly $800K in personal conviction [6]. At 27.1x forward P/E with 36% revenue growth and 50% profit margins, the valuation is reasonable for a company with near-monopolistic control over the world's most critical technology supply chain.
The dominant risk is geopolitical — TSMC's concentration in Taiwan creates an existential vulnerability to cross-strait tensions with China [8]. A military conflict, blockade, or sustained tension could disrupt global chip supply and TSMC's operations, which represent 80%+ of the world's advanced chip manufacturing capacity. The $40B+ Arizona fab investment partially mitigates this but represents only a fraction of Taiwan capacity and operates at higher costs. At $417.69 per ADR share and ~$2T market cap with 31.15x trailing P/E, TSMC is priced for continued hypergrowth — any deceleration in AI infrastructure spending (customer capex fatigue, model efficiency improvements reducing compute needs, or macro recession) could trigger significant multiple compression. Forward P/E of 27.1 and PEG of 1.55 suggest limited margin of safety if the above-40% growth rate normalizes. Taiwan's energy infrastructure constraints (LNG import dependency, renewable energy targets lagging schedule) could limit future fab expansion capacity [8]. The cyclical history of semiconductors warns against extrapolating peak growth rates.
