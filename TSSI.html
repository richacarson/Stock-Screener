TSS, Inc. (Total Site Solutions) provides data center infrastructure services including AI rack integration, systems deployment, procurement, and facilities management. Based in Georgetown, Texas, the company builds, integrates, and deploys custom high-performance computing infrastructure for data centers, serving OEM equipment manufacturers and enterprise end users. TSS has emerged as a key enabler of AI infrastructure buildout, with 2025 revenue of $245.7M.

Investment Thesis
TSS is a direct beneficiary of the AI infrastructure buildout, having grown revenue from $30.6M (2022) to $245.7M (FY2025) — a 100%+ 3-year CAGR [1]. While Q1 2026 total revenue declined 44% due to procurement lumpiness, core Systems Integration revenue surged 88% YoY to $14.1M and now represents 25% of the revenue mix versus 8% a year ago, indicating a shift toward higher-margin work [2]. Management reaffirmed 2026 EBITDA guidance of $20-22M targeting the high end, and hired two senior Dell veterans (CSO and CTO) to accelerate AI infrastructure growth [4]. At $9.99 (down 37% from April), trailing P/E of 19.07 with D/E of 51% and $66M cash represents a more attractive entry point. The company's competitive advantages — speed (sub-one-day rack completion), quality, and the Georgetown facility — position it to capture continued AI infrastructure spend.
The bear case centers on margin risk, customer concentration, and revenue volatility. Q1 2026's 44% revenue decline demonstrates the lumpy nature of TSS's revenue stream — a single large procurement order can dramatically swing quarterly results, creating earnings unpredictability that the elevated forward P/E of 53.54 cannot absorb [5]. Profit margin of 7.14% is thin for a company expected to grow EPS, and forward EPS estimates imply near-term earnings compression despite revenue normalization. Customer concentration risk remains significant — growth appears driven by a limited number of OEM relationships where loss of a single contract would be material. Larger IT services companies (Dell, Celestica, Flex) could scale rack integration services competitively, and the 88% SI revenue growth may attract new entrants to the space. At $275M market cap with 350 employees, TSS remains a small company where execution missteps are amplified [5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Systems Integration revenue mix shift from 8% to 25% of total revenue, growing 88% YoY, drives higher margins and more recurring business as AI infrastructure demand accelerates [2]
  2. New CSO Matt Wallace and CTO David Hull (combined 50 years at Dell) bring enterprise relationships and technical depth to accelerate growth in AI-driven infrastructure [4]
  3. 2026 EBITDA guidance targeting high end of $20-22M range with Georgetown facility capacity to double rack volumes, supported by reaffirmed long-term AI demand outlook [2]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue volatility — Q1 2026's 44% decline demonstrates procurement lumpiness that creates earnings unpredictability, and forward P/E of 53.54 leaves no room for further disappointment [2][5]
  2. Customer concentration risk — growth appears driven by limited OEM relationships where loss of a single long-term AI rack integration agreement would be material to a $275M market cap company [1]
  3. Competitive pressure from larger IT infrastructure companies (Dell, Celestica, Flex) that could enter AI rack integration at scale, and the 88% SI growth may attract new entrants [5]
