Company Profile
Industrials ·Tools & Accessories ·NYSE ·United States
The Toro Company designs, manufactures, and markets professional and residential equipment for outdoor environment maintenance through Professional and Residential segments. Products include turf maintenance equipment, autonomous mowers, snow/ice management, landscape equipment, irrigation systems, and underground construction equipment. Q3 FY2026 sales rose 8.4% to $1.23B. Full-year FY2026 guidance raised to $4.60-$4.65 adjusted EPS and 6.3%-6.6% net sales growth. Founded 1914, Bloomington, Minnesota.
9,227 Employees ·www.thetorocompany.com

Investment Thesis
Toro delivered strong Q3 FY2026 results with $1.23B revenue (up 8.4%) and raised full-year guidance to $4.60-$4.65 adjusted EPS and 6.3%-6.6% net sales growth, reflecting broad-based momentum across Professional (up 8.8%) and Residential (up 8.6%) segments [1]. ROE of 26.4% and forward P/E of 18.9 indicate strong capital efficiency at reasonable valuation. The autonomous mowing initiative (GeoLink, robotic platforms from Left Hand Robotics and TURFLYNX) opens a significant growth vector in the $6B+ commercial mowing market, with 75% labor reduction creating compelling customer ROI [2]. CEO-elect Edric Funk — who led the robotics acquisitions across his 30-year Toro career — positions the company to accelerate automation as the central growth strategy. Twenty-three consecutive years of dividend growth and the AMP cost savings program underscore financial discipline [1].
The primary concern is Toro's modest growth profile relative to valuation. The estimated 5-year revenue CAGR of ~7% and net margin of 7.6% reflect structural limitations of a mature industrial manufacturer, and trailing P/E of 25.8 with PEG of 1.88 leaves limited margin of safety if the Professional segment recovery stalls [5]. The CEO transition (Funk effective November 2026) introduces execution risk during a pivotal technology transformation period. Cultural concerns from layoffs and RTO mandates could impair recruitment of the software and AI talent needed for autonomous product development. Net insider selling signals less-than-full management conviction at current prices. Cyclical exposure to construction and residential spending remains a persistent headwind, and the autonomous mowing market faces potential competition from robotics-native entrants unencumbered by legacy equipment businesses.
