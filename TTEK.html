Investment Thesis
Tetra Tech is a high-quality compounder positioned at the intersection of multi-decade secular tailwinds in water infrastructure, environmental remediation, renewable energy, and climate adaptation. The company delivered record FY2025 results with $5.44B revenue and 24% adjusted EPS growth, underpinned by a conservative balance sheet (43% D/E) and strong returns on equity (20%) [1][4]. The planned CEO transition from Dan Batrack to Roger Argus preserves institutional culture while positioning for continued global expansion [2].
The primary near-term headwind is the wind-down of USAID and Department of State programs, which compressed headline FY2026 guidance despite 5-11% core growth in the underlying business [1]. At a forward P/E of 21.4 and PEG of 1.40, the stock is reasonably valued for a company with Tetra Tech's quality profile, though it lacks the deep discount that would create a compelling entry point. The long-term opportunity lies in the trillion-dollar global infrastructure upgrade cycle, aging water systems, and expanding environmental regulatory requirements that should drive sustained mid-to-high single digit organic growth for years to come.
Key Catalysts
  1. Record FY2025 results with $5.44B revenue, $4.62B net revenue, and adjusted EPS of $1.56 (up 24% YoY) demonstrate execution quality and market position strength [1]
  2. Core business growth of 5-11% (ex-USAID/DOS) in FY2026 driven by U.S. water infrastructure investment, PFAS remediation demand, and renewable energy consulting expansion [1]
  3. Smooth CEO transition from Dan Batrack to Roger Argus in February 2026 preserved cultural continuity and maintained strategic direction under proven internal leadership [2]
Key Risks
  1. USAID and Department of State program wind-downs reduce headline FY2026 revenue by an estimated $200-300M, creating near-term earnings headwinds and highlighting government funding concentration risk [1]
  2. Profit margin of 4.6% on total revenue (higher on net revenue basis) is structurally constrained by the pass-through subcontractor model and leaves limited buffer against pricing pressure or cost inflation
  3. Forward P/E of 21.4 and PEG of 1.40 embed growth expectations that could be disrupted by government spending cuts, acquisition integration missteps, or a slowdown in infrastructure spending momentum [4]
