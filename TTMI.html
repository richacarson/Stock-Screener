Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components & PCB Manufacturing ·NASDAQ ·United States
TTM Technologies, Inc. is a leading global manufacturer of technology solutions including mission systems, radio frequency (RF) components, RF microwave/microelectronic assemblies, and technologically advanced printed circuit boards (PCBs). The company crossed $1B in quarterly revenue for the first time in Q2 2026 ($1.0B, +37% YoY), driven by AI data center demand. Two primary end markets: Data Center Computing & Networking (40% of Q2 revenue, surging 91% YoY) and Aerospace & Defense (with $1.7B program backlog and $7B+ strategic pipeline). Full-year 2026 guidance raised to ~$4.4B revenue and ~$5 non-GAAP EPS. CEO Dr. Edwin Roks, Ph.D. (since September 2025).
18,200 Employees ·www.ttm.com

Investment Thesis
TTM Technologies is a premier AI infrastructure beneficiary with strong defense electronics positioning, hitting a major inflection point. Q2 2026 delivered record $1.0B revenue (+37% YoY), crossing the billion-dollar quarterly milestone for the first time, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.99 (+71% YoY) beating consensus [1]. Data center and networking revenue surged 91% YoY to 40% of sales, driven by AI infrastructure demand, and is projected to reach 49% of Q3 revenue [1][2]. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to ~$4.4B revenue and ~$5 EPS, up from the prior $4B target. The defense business provides a stable base with $1.7B A&D program backlog, $7B+ strategic pipeline, and book-to-bill of 1.3 [3]. Overall book-to-bill of 1.49 and record backlog of $901M (+81% YoY) signal strong forward demand [1]. The N+M asymmetrical interconnect boards for AI networking represent a new technology capability in early ramp. D/E of 57% supports continued growth investment.
The bear case centers on valuation premium and cyclical risk. At a trailing P/E of 56.7x and forward P/E of approximately 39x (GAAP), TTM trades at a significant premium that prices in sustained AI infrastructure growth [6]. GAAP profit margin of 7.01% reflects the capital-intensive, low-margin reality of PCB manufacturing — this is a hardware business, not a software franchise, and ROE of 13.26% is below the 15% target. The stock has declined ~13% from its $146 July level despite strong Q2 results, suggesting the market may be questioning whether the AI data center buildout can sustain 91% growth rates [1]. U.S.-China trade friction poses a meaningful risk — CNBC reports highlight national security concerns about Chinese-made PCBs in AI servers, which could benefit domestic TTM but also disrupt its Asian manufacturing operations [2]. Concentration risk is elevated as data center approaches 49% of revenue, creating vulnerability if hyperscaler capex cycles moderate.
