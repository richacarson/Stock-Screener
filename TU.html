Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·Canada
TELUS Corporation is a leading Canadian telecommunications company providing wireless, wireline, internet, and television services to approximately 19 million customer connections. The company also operates TELUS Health (digital healthcare), TELUS Agriculture & Consumer Goods, and TELUS Digital Experience (customer experience/BPO). In 2026, TELUS underwent a leadership transition with Victor Dodig (former CIBC CEO) replacing long-tenured Darren Entwistle. TELUS revised 2026 guidance sharply downward: consolidated service revenue flat to -2%, adjusted EBITDA -2% to -4%, and FCF of approximately $1.8B (down from $2.45B). A $2.1B pre-tax non-cash impairment at TELUS Digital — driven by accelerated AI automation of legacy BPO services — weighed on results, contributing to a TTM net loss of -$647M. The stock declined 41% over the past year. Market cap is approximately $15.4B.

Investment Thesis
TELUS trades at 18.16x forward earnings with a 10.79% dividend yield and 20 consecutive years of dividend payments, offering a deep value entry into Canada's second-largest telecom with $14.23B in TTM revenue [4]. The core mobile business showed three consecutive quarters of network revenue growth with ARPU decline stabilizing under 0.5%, and $3.61B in operating cash flow supports the reduced $1.8B FCF target [1]. The fiber-to-the-home network covering 90%+ of the footprint is a durable infrastructure asset. New CEO Victor Dodig brings a transformation track record from CIBC, and the announced business 'transformation' could catalyze cost rationalization and strategic refocusing. TELUS's 'We give where we live' model has invested C$1.4B+ in communities since 2000, creating genuine brand differentiation in Canadian markets [2]. At the current price, the market appears to price in permanent impairment of the business rather than cyclical recovery.
The bear case is severe. The 41% stock decline over the past year reflects structural concerns beyond cyclical weakness. The $2.1B TELUS Digital impairment signals that AI-driven automation is destroying the BPO business faster than expected, and the revised 2026 guidance (flat to -2% service revenue, -2% to -4% EBITDA) represents a dramatic downgrade from prior growth forecasts [1]. D/E of 224% with $22.2B in total debt and an Altman Z-Score of 0.82 (distress zone) create significant leverage risk — any further earnings deterioration could pressure the dividend, which at 10.79% yield is already pricing in substantial cut risk. ROE of -6.39% and profit margin of -4.55% are both negative due to the impairment, and even excluding the charge, core profitability metrics are thin for a company carrying this much debt [4]. Employee morale is declining (Glassdoor 3.5/5, down 2%), and the CEO transition creates execution risk during the most challenging period in the company's history. The Inspire Impact Score of -62 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors.

This is not a theoretical risk; it is a realized loss. TELUS Health also faces competitive pressure from AI-native healthcare platforms. The core telecom business is protected but TELUS Digital's real, demonstrated losses from AI displacement lower the overall score meaningfully.
