Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NYSE ·Canada
TELUS Corporation is one of Canada's Big Three telecommunications companies, providing wireless, wireline, internet, and TV services alongside diversified digital businesses including TELUS Health, TELUS Agriculture & Consumer Goods, and TELUS Digital (formerly TELUS International). The company generated Q1 2026 operating revenue of approximately C$5.0B (down from C$5.1B YoY), with adjusted EPS of C$0.23 (down from C$0.26). TELUS guided 2026 consolidated service revenue growth of 2-4% and EBITDA growth of 2-4%, though the outlook was subsequently cut after Q2 weakness in the Digital segment.

Investment Thesis
TELUS at $8.46 with a $13.3B market cap offers a deeply discounted entry into one of Canada's three essential telecom franchises, trading at just 15.8x forward earnings with a 13.4% dividend yield that implies the market is pricing in a dividend cut [1][3]. The core wireless business holds approximately 30% market share in a three-player oligopoly with high barriers to entry, TELUS Health is growing at 3%+ with a C$2.1B+ revenue base in digital health, and the PureFibre network reaches 3.5M+ premises as a durable competitive advantage. Management guided 2-4% service revenue and EBITDA growth for 2026, and the forward P/E of 15.8 implies a meaningful earnings recovery from the trailing loss period [1].
The bear case is substantial and explains the depressed valuation. Trailing earnings are negative with a -4.5% profit margin and -6.4% ROE, likely reflecting write-downs or restructuring charges that signal business model stress [1][3]. D/E of 223.8% is dangerously high for a period of negative earnings, and the dividend payout ratio exceeds 100% of trailing adjusted EPS — the current C$1.56 annual dividend is mathematically unsustainable without a significant earnings recovery that has not yet materialized. Q2 2026 saw EBITDA decline 2% with TELUS Digital revenue weakening and the full-year outlook cut, suggesting the 2-4% growth guidance may prove optimistic. The CEO transition from Darren Entwistle (who built TELUS's modern identity over 24 years) to Victor Dodig introduces execution risk during the most challenging operational period in the company's recent history. A dividend cut of 30-50% appears probable within 12 months and would trigger further selling from income-oriented investors who constitute the core shareholder base [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Earnings recovery from one-time charges returning to normalized profitability would justify the 15.8x forward P/E multiple and reduce dividend sustainability concerns — analysts expect C$0.55+ adjusted EPS in coming quarters [1]
  2. TELUS Health scaling toward profitability as a C$2.1B+ digital health platform, with growing employer wellness and pharmacy management services in a structurally growing market [1]
  3. Canadian telecom pricing rationalization as post-5G capex declines allow the Big Three to shift from investment mode to free cash flow harvesting, supporting dividend sustainability [3]
Key Risks
  1. Dividend cut of 30-50% appears probable given unsustainable payout ratio above 100% on trailing earnings — this would trigger forced selling from income funds and retail investors who own TU primarily for yield [1][3]
  2. TELUS Digital AI disruption exposure — the BPO and customer experience outsourcing segment faces direct substitution risk from AI chatbots and automated customer service, compressing margins and revenue in a segment that was a key growth driver [3]
  3. Balance sheet stress with D/E of 223.8% during a negative-earnings period leaves minimal financial flexibility for the C$2.3B annual capex required to maintain competitive network infrastructure, increasing refinancing risk if credit conditions tighten [1]
