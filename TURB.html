Company Profile
Technology ·Solar Energy Equipment ·NASDAQ ·Spain
Turbo Energy, S.A. is a global technology integrator specializing in AI-driven intelligent energy storage and energy management solutions. The company designs, develops, and distributes equipment for the generation, management, and storage of photovoltaic energy, including lithium-ion batteries, inverters, photovoltaic modules, and Sunbox, an AI-based software system for energy monitoring and management. Founded in 2013 and based in Valencia, Spain, the company has pivoted from residential solar distribution to integrated commercial and industrial (C&I) energy solutions. Operates as a subsidiary of Umbrella Global Energy, S.A.

Investment Thesis
Turbo Energy's H1 2026 results mark a potential inflection point, with $17.2M revenue (up 180% YoY), positive EBITDA of $0.89M, and the first positive net income in the company's public history ($10,606) [1]. The strategic pivot to C&I energy solutions is delivering results, with large-scale project execution, international expansion, and growing demand for integrated energy storage across industrial markets driving the revenue surge. The HiTHIUM partnership provides access to advanced energy storage technology for the European and Latin American C&I markets. At a $17M market cap, the stock trades at less than 1x annualized revenue, offering significant upside if the growth trajectory continues and profitability becomes consistent.
The bear case centers on extreme financial fragility and execution risk. Debt-to-equity of 491.91% on a $17M market cap company leaves virtually no margin for error — the company has just $962K in cash against $9.24M in debt [5]. The recent leadership transition (new CEO and CFO) introduces execution uncertainty at a critical growth inflection [3]. Profit margin is effectively zero, meaning any disruption to project execution or delays in C&I contract delivery could push the company back into losses. The solar energy storage market is intensely competitive with well-capitalized players (Tesla Energy, BYD, Fluence), and Turbo Energy has no meaningful competitive moat as a technology integrator. As a Spanish micro-cap trading as ADRs on NASDAQ, liquidity is thin and institutional interest is minimal.
