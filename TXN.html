Company Profile
Technology ·Semiconductors ·NASDAQ ·United States
Texas Instruments Incorporated designs, manufactures, and sells semiconductors to electronics designers and manufacturers globally. The company operates through Analog and Embedded Processing segments. Analog products include power management, signal chain, and high-volume analog & logic chips. Embedded Processing encompasses microcontrollers, processors, and connectivity solutions. Founded in 1951, headquartered in Dallas, Texas. Q2 2026 revenue reached $5.46B (+23% YoY), with data center revenue up roughly 90% YoY on surging AI power-delivery demand.

Investment Thesis
Texas Instruments delivered Q2 2026 revenue of $5.46B, up 23% YoY, with diluted EPS of $2.14 (+52% YoY), as growth accelerated across industrial, data center, and automotive end markets [1]. Data center revenue grew approximately 90% YoY in Q1 2026 as advanced AI servers and GPUs require increasingly complex power delivery systems, and Arete Research projects three years of analog semiconductor shortages that structurally favor TI's expanded 300mm capacity [2]. ROE of 35.18%, profit margin of 30.93%, and the world's largest analog semiconductor market position (~19% share) create a durable competitive moat, while 2026 capex is guided down to $2-3B as the fab buildout completes, driving projected free cash flow per share above $8 versus $3.23 in 2025 [3]. Q3 2026 guidance of $5.65-6.15B implies continued strong acceleration, and 24 consecutive years of dividend growth demonstrate capital return discipline even through severe cyclical downturns.
The bear case centers on valuation normalization and payout sustainability. Even after Forward P/E compressed from 34.98x to 27.32x as earnings caught up with the stock price, TXN still trades at a premium that leaves limited margin of safety if the current AI-driven data center surge proves less durable than expected once early deployment waves pass or customers optimize power designs [2]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of just ~1.3% reveals the underlying cyclicality risk: revenue swung from $20B (FY2022) to $15.6B (FY2024) before the current recovery, and a demand air-pocket remains a structural risk for any single-cycle capex bet. The dividend payout ratio of 86.28% is stretched, leaving limited buffer during the next downturn despite the 24-year growth streak, and prior net insider selling of roughly $55M (including a 35.8% position reduction by the CFO) has not reversed. The Inspiration score of 4/10 reflects an uninspiring corporate mission and previously reported cost-cutting culture concerns, while geopolitical risk from U.S.-China semiconductor tensions could impact TI's significant China revenue exposure.
