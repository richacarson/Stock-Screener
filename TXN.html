Company Profile

Texas Instruments Incorporated is the world's largest analog semiconductor company, designing and manufacturing analog chips, embedded processors, and power management ICs used across industrial, automotive, personal electronics, and data center applications. Q2 2026 revenue reached $5.46B (+23% YoY) with gross margin of 61.4% and operating margin of 42.3%, driven by data center revenue roughly doubling and industrial sales rising ~30%. CEO Haviv Ilan succeeded Rich Templeton as chairman in January 2026. The company is executing a massive $30B+ capex program to build new 300mm wafer fabs in Texas and Utah.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 7 /10
Texas Instruments demonstrates many hallmarks of an infinite-game player. The company's multi-decade commitment to analog semiconductor leadership, orderly CEO succession from Templeton to Ilan, and massive counter-cyclical capex investments in 300mm fabs reflect a long-term orientation that Sinek would admire [1][2]. The exit from mobile processors (OMAP) in service of analog focus represents genuine existential flexibility. TXN's competitive language emphasizes manufacturing cost advantages and catalog breadth rather than beating specific rivals. However, the company's conservative culture and financial-target-driven management style reveal some finite-game tendencies — optimizing for free cash flow per share and capital allocation efficiency rather than articulating a transformative purpose that transcends semiconductor manufacturing.

Investment Thesis
Texas Instruments at $276.48 with a forward P/E of 26.0x is the dominant franchise in analog semiconductors — the essential building blocks of every electronic system — with a multi-decade manufacturing cost advantage being constructed through $30B+ in 300mm fab investments [1]. Q2 2026 revenue of $5.46B (+23% YoY) with 61.4% gross margin and 42.3% operating margin demonstrates the operating leverage as the semiconductor cycle recovers, with data center revenue roughly doubling as AI infrastructure buildout drives insatiable demand for power management ICs. The company's 19% analog market share (2x the next competitor), owned manufacturing model, and pricing power (increases rolling out Q3-Q4) create structural competitive advantages that widen through cycles. ROE of 35.2% and profit margin of 31.1% reflect a capital-efficient business generating substantial free cash flow.
The bear case centers on cyclical peak risk and valuation. The trailing P/E of 42x reflects a stock that has risen ~70% from cycle lows, pricing in significant growth that may not persist as the semiconductor cycle normalizes [1][4]. The 22.8% revenue growth and 51.8% earnings growth are cyclical recovery rates, not sustainable long-term trajectories — TXN's through-cycle revenue CAGR is closer to mid-single digits. The $30B+ capex program will continue to depress free cash flow for years, and if the semiconductor cycle turns before the fabs ramp, TXN could face both overcapacity and elevated fixed costs simultaneously. D/E of 78% is manageable but has been increasing as the company funds fab construction with debt. The forward P/E of 26x is above TXN's historical average and above analog semiconductor peers, creating multiple compression risk if growth decelerates. The data center doubling may reflect one-time AI capex surge rather than sustainable demand growth.
Key Catalysts
  1. Data center revenue roughly doubled in Q2 2026 as AI infrastructure buildout drives sustained demand for TXN's power management ICs, voltage regulators, and signal processing chips — AI hardware cannot function without analog components [1]
  2. $30B+ 300mm fab investment creates a structural manufacturing cost advantage that will compound over decades — Sherman and Lehi fabs are ramping with significant capacity ahead, enabling pricing power and margin expansion [1]
  3. Pricing increases rolling out in Q3 2026 with further hikes expected Q4 and beyond, reflecting TXN's market leadership and customer willingness to pay for analog supply security in a geopolitically uncertain semiconductor landscape
Key Risks
  1. Trailing P/E of 42x and forward P/E of 26x price in substantial growth that is driven by cyclical semiconductor recovery — through-cycle revenue growth is mid-single-digit, and a normalization would compress the multiple significantly [4]
  2. $30B+ capex program depresses free cash flow for years and creates fixed-cost risk — if the semiconductor cycle turns before new fabs reach scale utilization, TXN faces overcapacity during a demand trough [1]
  3. Cyclical peak risk — Q2 revenue +23% and earnings +52% YoY reflect recovery from a deep 2023-2024 downturn, and the data center surge may represent front-loaded AI infrastructure spending rather than sustainable demand growth
