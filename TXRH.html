Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Restaurants ·NASDAQ ·United States
Texas Roadhouse operates casual dining restaurants under the Texas Roadhouse, Bubba's 33, and Jaggers brands, serving hand-cut steaks, ribs, and freshly baked bread across approximately 750 locations in 49 states and 10 international markets. Founded in 1993 by Kent Taylor and headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky. The company's managing partner model gives restaurant-level leaders equity-like incentives and operational autonomy. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1.68B (up 11.1% YoY) with comparable restaurant sales growth of 6.2% including 3% traffic growth, and TTM revenue of $6.2B.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 6 /10
Texas Roadhouse under Kent Taylor was one of the purest infinite game players in the restaurant industry — a founder who returned his salary during COVID to pay employees, refused to raise prices to protect margins, and built a managing partner model specifically to create ownership mentality at the restaurant level. Under CEO Jerry Morgan (29-year company veteran), the financial results have been excellent and the culture appears intact. Sinek would see a company with genuine Just Cause DNA being tested by the transition from a legendary founder to professional management — the true test will come during adversity, not during record sales.
Just Cause

Worthy Rivals

Worthy Rivals (1/1): Kent Taylor was famous for dining at competitors' restaurants — studying their menus, service, execution, and value proposition — and using those insights to improve Texas Roadhouse [3]. The company's competitive DNA is about getting better, not defeating rivals. The response to Outback, LongHorn, and other steakhouse competitors has consistently been 'how do we serve better food at better prices?' rather than 'how do we take their market share?' Investment Thesis
Texas Roadhouse at $156.70 is 27% below its 52-week high, trading at 20.2x forward earnings — the most attractive valuation in over two years for a company delivering category-leading execution [1][5]. Q2 FY2026 generated $1.68B revenue (up 11.1% YoY) with 6.2% comparable restaurant sales growth including 3% positive traffic, while the broader casual dining industry reports negative traffic [1][2]. ROE of 27.7% reflects exceptional capital efficiency from the managing partner model, the 5-year revenue CAGR of 12.5% demonstrates consistent double-digit growth, and the development pipeline of 35 new restaurants annually provides visible expansion through 2029. The dividend has been raised for 6 consecutive years (post-COVID) with a 20% five-year growth CAGR, and the PEG ratio of 1.56 suggests the growth trajectory is now more reasonably priced after the pullback. Free cash flow of $236M supports both investment and shareholder returns.
The bear case centers on margin vulnerability and the valuation premium restaurants still command. Profit margin of 6.6% is structurally constrained by the full-service model, leaving EPS vulnerable to commodity inflation (beef, dairy, produce) and labor cost pressure — management noted 50-66bps of restaurant margin compression from these factors in H1 2026 [1][2]. EPS was essentially flat year-over-year in Q2 2026 despite 11% revenue growth, demonstrating how quickly cost inflation erodes bottom-line leverage. At 20x forward earnings, the stock still prices in sustained outperformance — any deceleration in same-store sales or traffic below the current 3% could trigger significant multiple compression. The DEI discrimination complaint represents an emerging litigation and reputational risk in a politically charged environment [4]. The post-founder leadership question persists — Kent Taylor's death in 2021 removed the passionate cultural architect, and the true test of whether 'Legendary Food, Legendary Service' endures will come during a consumer recession, not during record average weekly sales.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Texas Roadhouse's core business is a physical-world dining experience — hand-cut steaks cooked to order, fresh-baked bread, line dancing, peanuts on the floor, and attentive table service — that AI structurally cannot replicate or substitute [1]. No technology can reproduce the sensory, social, and experiential value of dining in a restaurant. The business requires physical real estate, kitchen equipment, food supply chains, and human service that are entirely beyond AI's displacement capability. The managing partner model depends on human judgment, local market knowledge, and relationship-based leadership. AI-powered meal delivery or ghost kitchens represent only marginal exposure since Texas Roadhouse's value proposition is specifically the dine-in experience. AI Opportunity (0/1): No AI-related revenue or quantified efficiency gains; management has cautioned that over-reliance on AI could undermine the 'human touch' service model that differentiates the brand [3].
