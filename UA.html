Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Apparel & Accessories ·NYSE ·United States
Under Armour designs, manufactures, and distributes branded athletic performance apparel, footwear, and accessories globally through North America, EMEA, Asia-Pacific, and Latin America segments. Founder Kevin Plank returned as CEO in April 2024 after multiple leadership changes (Plank to Frisk to Browne to Plank). FY2026 (ending March 2026) delivered $5.0B revenue (-3.8% YoY) with a net loss of $495.6M (-$1.16 EPS), as Q4 gross margin declined 470bps to 42.0% on tariff headwinds [1]. FY2027 outlook guides revenue to decline slightly with gross margin expansion of 220-270bps [2]. Market cap approximately $2.4B.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 3 /10
Under Armour under returning CEO Kevin Plank is playing a finite game — the company is in turnaround mode focused on stabilizing quarterly results, cutting costs, and restructuring leadership. While Plank's founding vision was genuinely inspirational, four CEO changes in six years, declining revenue, and reactive strategic shifts indicate a company playing to survive rather than playing to keep playing. The FY2027 guidance of declining revenue with margin improvement frames the strategy in financial terms, not purpose terms [1][2].
Just Cause
4
Just Cause (1/3): Kevin Plank founded Under Armour with a genuine athletic performance purpose that differentiated the brand for its first 20 years. However, the cause has been diluted by years of inconsistent strategic direction — pivoting from performance to lifestyle to DTC to back-to-basics — and the revolving door of CEOs. Plank's return represents an attempt to reconnect with the founding purpose, but the turnaround is framed in financial terms (margin improvement, cost reduction) rather than mission terms [1][2].
Trusting Teams
2
Trusting Teams (0/2): Under Armour has experienced severe organizational instability — four CEO tenures since 2020 with corresponding executive reshuffles and layoffs [1]. This level of leadership churn destroys psychological safety and creates a culture where employees protect themselves rather than take risks. The serial restructuring signals an organization that has not found stability or trust at its core.
Worthy Rivals
3
Worthy Rivals (0/1): Under Armour's competitive language has historically been about 'beating' Nike and 'winning' market share. The 'Protect This House' framing is inherently defensive and finite. The company has shifted reactively — chasing Lululemon's athleisure success, then On Running's performance running momentum — rather than using rivals as mirrors for self-improvement [2].
Existential Flexibility

Existential Flexibility (1/2): Under Armour has attempted strategic pivots — the Connected Fitness digital platform, DTC-first strategy, international expansion push — though none were transformative successes. The willingness to divest MyFitnessPal and MapMyRun (acknowledging the digital strategy failed) and Plank's return to refocus on core athletic performance show some capacity for strategic adaptation, though driven more by necessity than vision [1].
Courage to Lead
6
Courage to Lead (1/2): Kevin Plank's decision to return as CEO after stepping down demonstrates personal conviction, and accepting near-term revenue declines to clean up wholesale distribution and restructure the brand requires tolerating short-term pain [2]. The FY2027 guidance accepting revenue decline while investing in margin improvement shows some willingness to choose long-term positioning over immediate growth. However, these are reactive survival moves rather than proactive long-term capability investments.

Investment Thesis
Under Armour at $5.67 with a $2.4B market cap offers deep-value turnaround potential under founder Kevin Plank's return. The bull case rests on: (1) significant insider buying — insiders purchased 44M+ shares net and Director Fitzpatrick added 100K shares at $4.93 in August 2026, signaling informed conviction in the recovery [6]; (2) FY2027 guidance for gross margin expansion of 220-270bps as tariff headwinds normalize, channel mix improves, and restructuring savings flow through [2]; and (3) a globally recognized $5.0B revenue brand with infrastructure that could support meaningful margin recovery if product execution improves. The Curry partnership provides relevance in basketball, and direct-to-consumer channel growth offers higher-margin revenue potential.
The bear case is formidable: FY2026 posted a -$495.6M net loss (-$1.16 EPS), a 146% deterioration from the prior year, while Q4 gross margin declined 470bps to 42.0% on tariffs and product cost inflation [1]. FY2027 revenue is guided to decline further with North America down low single-digits [2]. Forward P/E of 54x prices in a full earnings recovery that has not materialized — ROE of -30.39% and profit margin of -10.44% reflect a fundamentally unprofitable business [4]. Four CEO tenures in six years have destroyed organizational continuity and brand coherence. Under Armour faces structural market share losses to Nike, adidas, On Running, and Hoka with no evidence of reversing the trend. ESG critics have flagged the absence of credible emissions reduction targets, with 98% of emissions in the supply chain and no clear pathway to address them [5]. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Under Armour's core business — designing, manufacturing, and distributing physical athletic apparel and footwear — is structurally protected from AI displacement. Physical product manufacturing, athlete sponsorships, and retail distribution require capabilities that AI cannot substitute [1]. Consumer preference for trying on athletic apparel and the tactile nature of performance gear create natural moats. However, AI tools could accelerate competitors' design processes, supply chain optimization, and personalized marketing, potentially narrowing Under Armour's already-thin competitive differentiation against Nike and adidas, which are investing heavily in AI-driven design and personalization. The company's lack of an AI strategy is a competitive weakness in an industry where digital product development is accelerating. Analysts have not flagged AI as a direct substitution risk. AI Opportunity (0/1): Under Armour does not generate quantifiable revenue from AI-related products or services. The previously divested Connected Fitness platforms (MapMyRun, MapMyFitness) are no longer part of the business.
