Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Apparel Manufacturing ·NYSE ·United States
Under Armour develops, markets, and distributes branded performance apparel, footwear, and accessories globally through wholesale, direct-to-consumer (e-commerce and brand/factory house stores), and licensing channels. Founded in 1996 by Kevin Plank, who returned as CEO in April 2024 after two failed successor CEOs. FY2026 (ended March 2026) revenue declined 4% to $5.0B with North America down 8%, net loss widened to $496M including $247M tax valuation allowance, and the company expanded restructuring charges to $305M. FY2027 guidance projects a slight revenue decline with adjusted EPS of $0.08-$0.12. Headquartered in Baltimore, Maryland.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 1 /10
Under Armour under Kevin Plank's return is a textbook finite game player in Sinek's framework. The founder's comeback is driven by personal desire to restore past glory rather than a clear vision of the future. Plank's self-described micromanagement style, the revolving door of senior leadership (three CEOs in five years), the company's fixation on competing with Nike rather than advancing a cause, and the failure to maintain a consistent strategic direction all signal a profoundly finite mindset. The expanded $305M restructuring and FY2027 guidance for continued revenue decline reflect survival mode, not infinite game thinking.
Just Cause
4
Just Cause (1/3): Under Armour's founding purpose — making athletes better through innovation — had genuine conviction when Plank was developing moisture-wicking fabric from his grandmother's basement in 1996. But the purpose has not been validated through consistent execution over the past decade [2]. Multiple CEO changes, strategic pivots (fitness apps acquired then sold, international expansion then retraction), and revenue declining 15% over three years suggest the purpose no longer drives strategic decisions. The current strategy centers on brand survival and financial restructuring, not Just Cause advancement.

Investment Thesis
Under Armour's investment case rests solely on deep value and activist accumulation. At $4.71 per share (~$2.0B market cap), the stock trades at 42% below its 52-week high of $8.15, pricing in significant pessimism [5]. Major shareholder V. Prem Watsa (Fairfax Financial) accumulated over $121M in shares, and director Mohamed El-Erian bought $520K — signaling conviction that the brand's $5.0B revenue base has restructuring-driven upside [4]. The forward PE compressed to 24.0x (from 53.5x) as analysts project a return to profitability in FY2027 ($0.08-$0.12 adjusted EPS), and the PEG ratio of 1.19 is reasonable [5]. International revenue grew 4% in FY2026 (EMEA +9%, Latin America +9%), suggesting the brand retains global appeal, and D/E improved to 96.4% from 118% [1].
The bear case remains overwhelming. Revenue has declined 15% over three years from $5.9B to $5.0B, gross margins declined 240 basis points to 45.5% due to tariffs representing the largest margin headwind, and the company posted a $496M net loss in FY2026 including a $247M valuation allowance on U.S. deferred tax assets [1]. Kevin Plank's return as CEO after two failed successors raises governance concerns — he champions micromanagement and has overseen chronic leadership turnover [2]. North America revenue fell 8%, footwear revenue fell 11%, and the brand is losing ground to Hoka, On Running, and New Balance. FY2027 guidance projects a slight further revenue decline, meaning the contraction has not bottomed. Under Armour walked back its science-based emissions reduction commitments, drawing shareholder pushback [3]. ROE of -29.8% and -10% profit margin reflect a company that continues destroying shareholder value despite restructuring efforts.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Under Armour's core business — designing, manufacturing, and distributing physical performance apparel and footwear — is fundamentally protected from AI substitution. Consumers cannot replace physical clothing with AI-generated alternatives [2]. The brand's value proposition is tied to material science, athletic fit, and physical product performance that requires tangible manufacturing. However, AI is reshaping adjacent functions: AI-powered design tools are accelerating competitors' product development cycles, AI personalization is transforming e-commerce (where Under Armour lags), and AI supply chain optimization widens efficiency gaps with better-resourced competitors like Nike. Fast-fashion AI-enabled competitors like Shein could further erode Under Armour's casual wear business. AI Opportunity (1/1): Under Armour has opportunities in AI-driven demand forecasting, product customization, and e-commerce personalization. The company's heritage in performance data (connected fitness via MapMyRun, though largely divested) positions it to leverage AI for athlete insights, though no specific AI revenue contribution has been quantified.
