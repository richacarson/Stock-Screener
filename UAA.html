Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Apparel Manufacturing ·NYSE ·United States
Under Armour develops, markets, and distributes branded performance apparel, footwear, and accessories globally through wholesale, direct-to-consumer (e-commerce and brand/factory house stores), and licensing channels. Founded in 1996 by Kevin Plank, who returned as CEO in April 2024 after two failed successor CEOs. FY2026 (ended March 2026) revenue declined 4% to $5.0B with North America down 8%, net loss widened to $496M, and the company expanded restructuring charges to $305M. FY2027 guidance projects a mid-single-digit revenue decline. Headquartered in Baltimore, Maryland.
6,200 Employees ·www.underarmour.com

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: FINITE Overall: 1 /10
Under Armour under Kevin Plank's return is a textbook finite game player in Sinek's framework. The founder's comeback is driven by personal desire to restore past glory rather than a clear vision of the future. Plank's self-described micromanagement style, the revolving door of senior leadership (three CEOs in five years), the company's fixation on competing with Nike through cost-cutting rather than purpose-driven innovation, and the failure to maintain a consistent strategic direction all signal a profoundly finite mindset. The expanded $305M restructuring is about survival, not about advancing a cause worth sacrificing for.
Just Cause
4
Just Cause (1/3): Under Armour's founding purpose — making athletes better through innovation — had genuine conviction when Plank was developing moisture-wicking fabric from his grandmother's basement in 1996. But the purpose has not evolved or been validated through consistent execution over the past decade [2]. Multiple CEO changes (Plank to Frisk to Linnartz back to Plank), strategic pivots (fitness apps acquired then sold, international expansion then retraction), and chronic underperformance suggest the purpose no longer drives strategic decisions. The current strategy feels like brand survival and financial restructuring, not Just Cause advancement.

Investment Thesis
Under Armour's investment case rests on deep value and activist accumulation. At $5.26 per share (~$2.3B market cap), the stock trades at a fraction of its 2015 peak, pricing in significant pessimism. Major shareholder V. Prem Watsa (Fairfax Financial) accumulated over $121M in shares since December 2025, and director Mohamed El-Erian bought $520K — signaling conviction that the brand's $5.0B revenue base has restructuring-driven upside [4]. International revenue grew 4% in FY2026, suggesting the brand retains global appeal. FY2027 guidance targets a return to adjusted profitability ($0.08-$0.12 EPS), and Plank's SKU reduction of 25% and shortened 12-month product-to-market timeline could improve product relevance and margins over the medium term [1].
The bear case is overwhelming. Revenue has declined 15% over three years from $5.9B to $5.0B, gross margins collapsed 470 basis points to 42% due to tariffs and product cost inflation, and the company posted a $496M net loss in FY2026 on top of a $201M loss the prior year [1]. Kevin Plank's return as CEO after two failed successors raises governance concerns — he publicly defends micromanagement and has overseen continued leadership turnover that he admits is 'central to inability to stay agile' [2]. North America revenue fell 8%, footwear revenue fell 11%, and the brand is losing ground to Hoka, On Running, and New Balance. FY2027 guidance projects a further mid-single-digit revenue decline, meaning the contraction has not bottomed. D/E of 118% on a declining revenue base creates financial fragility, and the expanded $305M restructuring signals desperation rather than strategic clarity. ROE of -30% and -10% profit margin reflect a company destroying shareholder value.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Under Armour's core business — designing, manufacturing, and distributing physical performance apparel and footwear — is fundamentally protected from AI substitution. Consumers cannot replace physical clothing with AI-generated alternatives [2]. The brand's value proposition is tied to material science, athletic fit, and physical product performance. However, AI is reshaping adjacent functions: AI-powered design tools are accelerating competitors' product development cycles, AI personalization is transforming e-commerce (where Under Armour lags), and AI supply chain optimization widens efficiency gaps with better-resourced competitors like Nike. The fashion design and marketing functions face some AI exposure as AI-generated content reduces the cost advantage of established brands. AI Opportunity (1/1): Under Armour has opportunities in AI-driven demand forecasting, product customization, and e-commerce personalization. The company's connected fitness heritage (MapMyRun) positions it to leverage AI for athlete data insights, though the company has not quantified any AI-specific revenue contribution.
