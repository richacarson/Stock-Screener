Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NYSE ·United States
United States Antimony Corporation is the only significant antimony producer in the United States, vertically integrated across mining, milling, smelting, and sales at its Thompson Falls, Montana facility. Produces antimony trioxide (flame retardants), antimony metal (batteries, ordnance), and antimony trisulfide (ammunition primers). Also operates a zeolite segment for soil amendment and water filtration. CEO Gary C. Evans (appointed December 2024) is leading a capacity expansion backed by a $248M Defense Logistics Agency contract. Q2 2026 revenue was $7.9M (-25% YoY) but management lowered 2026 guidance to $60-75M from $125M due to antimony pricing declines. Market cap approximately $1.5B.

Investment Thesis
UAMY offers a unique critical minerals play as the only significant domestic antimony producer, backed by a $248M Defense Logistics Agency contract and $27M DoD direct investment that validate strategic importance [1][2]. The balance sheet is virtually debt-free (D/E ~0%), CEO Evans's $900K+ in personal open-market purchases signal strong insider conviction, and antimony volumes grew 26% YoY in Q2 2026 despite pricing headwinds — demonstrating operational momentum even as revenue declined [1][3]. The hydrometallurgical facility targeting 1,000 tons/month of 99.9% purity antimony by 2028 could unlock premium pricing and new defense/battery applications. With China restricting exports and the U.S. government prioritizing domestic critical mineral production, UAMY occupies a monopoly position with powerful secular tailwinds.
The bear case centers on execution risk and commodity dependence. Management's dramatic guidance cut from $125M to $60-75M for 2026, driven by a 52% drop in average antimony selling prices, reveals the company's acute sensitivity to commodity cycles despite its domestic monopoly positioning [1]. Q2 2026 revenue of $7.9M (-25% YoY) means the company must generate approximately $22-30M/quarter for the remaining half to hit even the lowered guidance — requiring ~3x the Q2 run rate. Profitability remains deeply negative at -41.4% margin, -10.2% ROE, and negative free cash flow, meaning expansion is consuming cash faster than the business generates it [3]. At ~$1.5B market cap on ~$32M TTM revenue (~47x sales), the market has priced in massive future production that remains unproven. If China loosens export restrictions, antimony prices decline further, or the Pentagon contracts underperform, the valuation has significant downside. The 100-person organization has unproven capacity to scale to a multi-hundred-million-dollar enterprise.
Key Catalysts
  1. Stibnite Hill mine production ramp and new hydrometallurgical facility commissioning driving sequential revenue acceleration toward the $60-75M revised 2026 target, validating the domestic production expansion thesis [1]
  2. Additional Pentagon or Department of Defense critical minerals contracts expanding UAMY's government revenue base beyond the existing $248M five-year DLA agreement, potentially including antimony for next-generation battery applications [2]
  3. Antimony price recovery from the 52% YoY decline, potentially driven by continued Chinese export restrictions or increased global demand from defense, electronics flame retardants, and energy storage applications
Key Risks
  1. 2026 guidance cut from $125M to $60-75M reveals extreme commodity price sensitivity — a 52% drop in antimony prices collapsed revenue despite 26% volume growth, and further price declines could render the expansion uneconomic [1]
  2. Deeply negative profitability (-41.4% margin, negative FCF) during capacity expansion means the company is burning cash on infrastructure investment and may need dilutive capital raises at the ~$1.5B market cap if commodity prices remain depressed [3]
  3. China loosening antimony export restrictions or antimony prices declining further would undermine the entire thesis — UAMY's monopoly value depends on geopolitical conditions and elevated pricing that could shift unpredictably
