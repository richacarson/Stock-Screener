Investment Thesis
Uber posted a milestone Q2 2026 — $14.2B revenue (+12% YoY), gross bookings of $58.0B (+22% YoY), non-GAAP EPS of $0.81 (+35% YoY), and TTM free cash flow surpassing $10B for the first time — validating years of margin discipline layered on top of the core marketplace [1]. The balance sheet and returns profile are excellent (37.16% ROE, 42% D/E, 17.34% profit margin), and a forward PEG of just 0.53 suggests the market has not fully priced in the durability of Uber's growth [4]. Most distinctively, CEO Dara Khosrowshahi has turned the single greatest long-term threat to Uber's business — autonomous vehicles — into a growth vector by making Uber the aggregation layer for nearly every major AV operator, with Waymo robotaxis already live in Austin and Atlanta and additional commitments from Lucid/Nuro (20,000+ vehicles) and Stellantis/Pony.ai (up to 50,000 vehicles) [2].
The bear case centers on the same autonomous-vehicle dynamic that powers the bull case: if a single AV operator (most plausibly Waymo) achieves sufficient scale and consumer-facing brand strength, it could eventually disintermediate Uber's aggregation layer entirely, particularly in dense urban markets where AV economics work best — and Khosrowshahi's own 20-year timeline for robots handling 'the majority of trips' underscores how structurally uncertain the driver-marketplace model's long-term durability really is [2]. Uber's Inspiration score remains weak (2/10) given thin community-impact and retention evidence, and the founding-era history of aggressive regulatory arbitrage (including a $200M Proposition 22 campaign) continues to color the company's Just Cause credibility. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED), driven by abortion and LGBT activism attributions, may meaningfully limit allocation for faith-based investors despite otherwise strong operational and ESG-adjacent metrics across labor, environmental, and safety dimensions.
