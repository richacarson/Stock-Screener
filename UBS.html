Company Profile
Financial Services ·Capital Markets ·NYSE ·Switzerland
UBS Group AG is the world's largest wealth manager with over $6 trillion in invested assets, operating across four divisions: Global Wealth Management, Personal & Corporate Banking, Asset Management, and the Investment Bank. Following the 2023 acquisition of Credit Suisse, UBS became the dominant Swiss universal bank with approximately 115,000 employees across 50+ countries. Q1 2026 delivered $3B net profit (+80% YoY) on CHF 13.6B revenue (+18%), while Q2 2026 produced $2.8B net profit with the Investment Bank achieving record Q2 revenues. All four divisions delivered double-digit profitability growth. CEO Sergio Ermotti is leading the CS integration to completion with 340 live AI use cases deployed across operations.

Investment Thesis
UBS at 12.3x forward earnings is the world's largest wealth manager executing a once-in-a-generation integration that is proving successful. Q1 2026 delivered $3B net profit (+80% YoY) on CHF 13.6B revenue (+18%), while Q2 produced $2.8B net profit with record Investment Bank revenues and all four divisions in double-digit profitability growth [1][3]. Net new wealth management assets of $37.4B in Q1 demonstrate the platform's pull. The Forward P/E of 12.29 provides a significant margin of safety compared to US wealth management peers trading at 15-18x. The Credit Suisse integration is nearing completion with $13B in targeted cost synergies, providing a clear path to ROE improvement above 15% by 2027-2028. CEO Ermotti's leadership through the CS rescue has been validated by results. The bank has deployed 340 AI use cases with a dedicated Chief AI Officer [4]. The stock has appreciated 15% from $47.98 to $55.38 since the prior screen but remains reasonably valued.
The bear case centers on structural banking headwinds and legacy risks. ROE of 10.16% remains below the 15% cost of equity for a globally systemically important bank, and D/E of 413% (structural banking leverage) amplifies downside to credit deterioration or AUM declines [1]. Swiss regulatory demands for higher capital buffers post-Credit Suisse could structurally constrain capital returns — the FINMA 'too big to fail' review creates uncertainty about future capital requirements. Revenue is heavily dependent on market levels and investor sentiment — a sustained equity bear market would compress wealth management fees and Investment Bank revenues simultaneously. Geographic concentration in Europe exposes UBS to slower economic growth versus US-centric peers. Unquantified litigation risks from the Credit Suisse legacy portfolio remain a tail risk [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) limits allocation for faith-based investors. The dividend yield of just 1.07% provides minimal income support despite mid-teens dividend growth commitment.

AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): UBS's core wealth management business serving ultra-high-net-worth clients is protected by deep relationship moats, regulatory complexity (banking licenses, AML/KYC compliance across 50+ countries), and the fundamental human trust required in managing $6T+ of personal wealth [1][4]. Physical branch infrastructure, cross-border tax planning, and estate structuring require nuanced human judgment that AI cannot replicate. The Investment Bank's M&A advisory, capital markets, and complex structured products also require institutional relationships and human expertise.
