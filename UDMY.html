Company Profile
Consumer Staples ·Education & Training Services ·NASDAQ (delisted May 11, 2026) ·United States
Udemy operated a global online learning marketplace spanning Enterprise (Udemy Business) and Consumer segments, offering courses across technology, business, and soft skills alongside an AI-powered learning assistant. On December 4, 2025, Coursera agreed to combine with Udemy; the deal received shareholder approval and closed on May 11, 2026, at which point UDMY was delisted from Nasdaq and Udemy became part of the combined Coursera entity serving roughly 290 million learners, 18,000 enterprise customers, and 95,000 instructors.

Investment Thesis
IMPORTANT: Udemy is no longer an independently investable security. Coursera completed its acquisition of Udemy on May 11, 2026, and UDMY was delisted from Nasdaq the same day; this report is retained for historical/reference purposes only [1]. At the time of the deal, Udemy's Enterprise segment (Udemy Business) was growing steadily — $132.9M in Q1 2026 versus $127.7M a year earlier — with a mission ('anyone can build the life they imagine') that meaningfully shaped the decision to combine with Coursera rather than fight for share independently [1]. The balance sheet was clean (D/E of 5%, $348.1M net cash, Forward P/E of 8.41x, PEG of 0.54), and management framed the combination as enabling $115M of two-year operating-cost savings plus greater scale to invest in AI-native learning products [1].
The standalone bear case that ultimately led to the sale: Consumer segment revenue fell from $72.6M to $58.5M YoY in Q1 2026 as transactional course sales declined, consolidated FY2025 revenue growth slowed to just 0.42%, and Q1 2026 net loss widened to $12.6M from $1.8M a year earlier [1][2]. Structurally, Udemy's pre-recorded video-course marketplace model faced real AI substitution risk from chatbots and AI tutors capable of personalized, on-demand instruction — a risk explicitly cited as a driver of the industry-wide 'intense disruption' Udemy itself discussed in its own research [1]. Post-IPO culture also showed strain, with Glassdoor reviews citing a shift to being 'profitable at cost of team talent' and a 'toxic work culture' in some regional leadership. IOWN should not initiate any new position in UDMY given the completed delisting; any residual analysis is informational only.

The Consumer segment's revenue decline (to $58.5M from $72.6M in Q1 2026, driven by falling transactional course sales) is consistent with this substitution pressure, though causation is not proven. Enterprise revenue (Udemy Business, 69% of Q1 2026 revenue) has higher switching costs and compliance requirements that partially insulate it, and the explicit strategic rationale for combining with Coursera was to gain scale to 'invest in and scale AI innovations' rather than face the AI transition alone [1].
