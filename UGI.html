Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities—Diversified ·NYSE ·United States
UGI Corporation is a holding company that distributes, stores, transports, and markets energy products through its subsidiaries, including AmeriGas (the largest U.S. retail propane distributor serving ~1.4 million customers), UGI Utilities (Pennsylvania natural gas and electric distribution), and UGI International (LPG and natural gas distribution in Europe). Headquartered in King of Prussia, Pennsylvania, the company has paid dividends for 138 consecutive years and raised its dividend for 35 consecutive years. UGI is the subject of a $9B takeover bid from KKR, driven by rising AI data center demand for energy infrastructure [5]. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was revised to $2.75-$2.90, down from FY2025's record $3.32.

Investment Thesis
UGI trades at an undemanding 11.64x forward earnings while yielding 4.17%, reflecting a business in the middle of a multi-year turnaround that has attracted KKR's $9B takeover bid [5][6]. The KKR offer validates the thesis that energy infrastructure is undervalued in a world where AI data centers are driving unprecedented demand for dispatchable power — if the deal closes, shareholders receive a premium; if it fails, UGI trades at a discount with a management team executing the AmeriGas restructuring, a $470M electric division sale to sharpen focus, and a Prime Data Centers partnership to serve AI-driven electricity demand [1][2][5]. The 5-7% long-term EPS CAGR target through FY2029, backed by a pipeline of 75+ large-load industrial projects, offers a credible growth path layered onto a 138-year unbroken dividend payment record and a physically-delivered energy business structurally immune to AI disruption.
The bear case centers on declining earnings, execution risk, and binary deal outcome. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.75-$2.90 represents a 13-17% decline from FY2025's record $3.32, driven by lower Midstream & Marketing contributions and slower AmeriGas improvements [1]. Revenue growth is nearly flat at 0.56% YoY for 9M FY2026, and the 5yr revenue CAGR is negative at -0.5%, reflecting volatile commodity-driven propane volumes [6]. D/E of 130% is nearly double the IOWN target, limiting balance sheet flexibility for the infrastructure buildout. Profit margin of 8.71% is thin for the utility sector. The KKR bid introduces binary risk: failure to close could send shares back toward pre-bid levels, erasing the 13% announcement pop [5]. Leadership instability through three CEO transitions in 2023-2025 adds execution uncertainty to an already complex multi-segment turnaround, and the 2.6% 5yr dividend growth CAGR barely keeps pace with inflation.
Key Catalysts
  1. KKR's $9B takeover bid at a significant premium, with AI data center energy demand providing strategic rationale — deal closure would deliver immediate shareholder value [5]
  2. Prime Data Centers partnership building natural gas infrastructure for AI data center power demand in Pennsylvania, part of a 75+ project industrial pipeline supporting 5-7% EPS CAGR through FY2029 [1][2]
  3. Completion of the ~$470M electric division sale and AmeriGas operational turnaround progress, potentially restoring dividend growth and margin expansion [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. FY2026 EPS declining 13-17% to $2.75-$2.90 from FY2025's record $3.32, driven by midstream delays, lower Appalachian volumes, and slower AmeriGas improvements — persistence of the decline could compress the multiple [1]
  2. KKR bid failure risk — if the $9B takeover does not close due to regulatory, financing, or strategic disagreements, shares could re-rate significantly lower toward pre-bid valuations [5]
  3. D/E of 130% versus a 70% target limits balance sheet flexibility to fund the large-load industrial and data center infrastructure pipeline without additional leverage or equity dilution [6]
