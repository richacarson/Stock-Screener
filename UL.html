Company Profile
Consumer Staples ·Household & Personal Products ·NYSE ·United Kingdom
Unilever PLC is a global consumer goods company operating across Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care, Home Care, and Nutrition segments following the 2025 separation of its Ice Cream business. Power Brands (Dove, Knorr, Hellmann's, Rexona, OMO, Lux) represent approximately 78% of turnover. Under new CEO Fernando Fernandez, the company is pursuing a 'Desire at Scale' strategy focused on premiumization, beauty, e-commerce, and geographic expansion in the US and India. H1 2026 delivered 4.8% underlying sales growth with 4.2% volume growth, and Q2 2026's 5.5% volume growth was the best quarterly volume performance in more than a decade.

Investment Thesis
Unilever offers defensive stability with a 3.84% dividend yield, 89-year consecutive payment history (since 1937), and reasonable forward P/E of 17.52x backed by 30.96% ROE [5]. H1 2026 demonstrated accelerating momentum under CEO Fernandez: underlying sales growth of 4.8% with 4.2% volume growth, and Q2 2026's 5.5% volume growth was the best quarterly performance in more than a decade [1]. Underlying operating margin improved to 20.3%, FCF improved by €0.5B to €1.5B, and the full-year outlook was upgraded to 4-6% USG. Power Brands (78% of turnover) are the growth engine, and the 'Desire at Scale' strategy's seven priorities — more beauty, wellbeing, personal care, premium, ecommerce, US, India — provide a clear execution roadmap. At $64.77, the stock trades at a discount to Polman-era valuations, creating potential re-rating if mid-single-digit growth sustains.
The bear case centers on anemic long-term growth and purpose erosion. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 3% (including the Ice Cream separation distortion) reflects chronic underperformance versus consumer staples peers, and the 5-year dividend growth CAGR of just 1.43% barely keeps pace with inflation, undermining the income case for the Dividend sleeve [5]. D/E of 161% with €33.2B in debt against €5.88B cash is elevated for a consumer staples company. Reported revenue declined 3.8% in 2025, and while this reflects the Ice Cream separation rather than organic decline, it compresses trailing metrics. CEO Fernandez is the fourth CEO in five years (Polman → Jope → Schumacher → Fernandez), creating leadership instability that undermines long-term strategic execution. The ESG rollbacks — dropped plastic reduction, living wage, and diversity pledges — risk alienating purpose-driven institutional investors who supported Unilever's premium valuation under Polman [4]. AI Risk Protection (7/9): Unilever's core business — manufacturing and distributing physical consumer products (personal care, home care, food, beauty) — is structurally immune to AI substitution [1]. Tangible consumer goods are required regardless of AI advancement, and the manufacturing, distribution, and retail infrastructure cannot be replicated by software. Brand loyalty built over decades (Dove, Knorr, Hellmann's) provides additional moat. However, AI could disrupt consumer discovery channels, enable private label competition through personalized marketing, and shift brand loyalty dynamics — particularly in e-commerce where AI recommendation algorithms control product visibility. The Ice Cream separation demonstrated the company is not immune to broader structural market shifts.
