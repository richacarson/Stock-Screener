Company Profile
Industrials ·Airlines ·NASDAQ ·United States
Frontier Group Holdings operates Frontier Airlines, a Denver-based ultra-low-cost carrier serving leisure travelers across the United States, Mexico, the Caribbean, and Central America with an all-leased fleet of 164 Airbus A320-family aircraft (68 A320neo, 69 A321neo, 27 ceo). Q3 2026 revenue reached a record $1.36B (+53% YoY) with RASM up 30% to 11.87 cents, producing adjusted EPS of $0.18 but a GAAP net loss of $104M driven by early lease return charges. CEO James 'Jimmy' Dempsey replaced longtime CEO Barry Biffle in December 2025 and is adding First Class seating, Wi-Fi, and loyalty enhancements. Market cap approximately $1.5B.

Investment Thesis
Frontier is in the middle of a sharp revenue inflection: Q3 2026 revenue hit a record $1.36B (+53% YoY) as RASM rose 30% to 11.87 cents on 18% more capacity, and adjusted EPS of $0.18 swung from -$0.34 a year earlier and beat both guidance and consensus despite higher-than-planned fuel [1][2]. Management guides Q4 RASM up more than 20% YoY on only 5-6% capacity growth, while adjusted CASM ex-fuel rose just 4%, showing that the unit-revenue gains are outrunning costs [1]. Liquidity of $1.18B (about 25% of TTM adjusted revenue), the completed return of 13 surplus A320neos, and new First Class, Wi-Fi, and loyalty products under CEO Jimmy Dempsey give the airline a credible path to sustained profitability [1][3].
The bear case is that Frontier remains structurally fragile. GAAP results are still losses — $104M in Q3 2026 and -$137M for FY2025 — and Yahoo Finance shows a profit margin of -8.9%, ROE of -175%, and a debt-to-equity ratio above 13,000% because years of losses have nearly erased book equity against $5.0B of lease-heavy debt [1][4][5]. Q4 guidance of -$0.15 to +$0.05 adjusted EPS at an assumed $4.75/gallon fuel shows how thin the margin for error is, and S&P consensus still expects a full-year loss [2]. Leadership turnover (Biffle's December 2025 exit amid investor skepticism of the ULCC model) and heavy insider selling in Q3 2026 temper conviction [3][6].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q4 2026 RASM guided up more than 20% YoY on 5-6% capacity growth, extending the record Q3 unit-revenue performance [1]
  2. Fleet right-sizing complete: all 13 A320neos under the second early-return agreement handed back by October 2026, replaced by 10 higher-gauge A321neo direct leases in Q4 2026-Q1 2027 [1]
  3. Premium product rollout — First Class seating, onboard Wi-Fi, and enhanced loyalty benefits — broadening the customer base beyond pure fare shoppers [1]
Key Risks
  1. Fuel price sensitivity: Q4 guidance assumes $4.75/gallon and the adjusted EPS range of -$0.15 to +$0.05 leaves almost no buffer for a fuel spike [1]
  2. Near-zero book equity (D/E above 13,000%), $5.0B of debt and lease obligations, and an all-leased fleet create high fixed-cost leverage in any demand downturn [1][4]
  3. Execution and leadership risk following the December 2025 CEO change, negative employee sentiment signals, and continued GAAP losses that could pressure liquidity if revenue momentum fades [3]
