Company Profile
Technology ·Computer Hardware ·NYSE American ·United States
Unusual Machines, Inc. is an Orlando, Florida-based maker of NDAA-compliant drone components and subsystems, including motors, flight controllers, cameras, and FPV goggles. It also sells consumer FPV gear through its Fat Shark and Rotor Riot brands. The company has shifted from retail hobby sales to enterprise and defense customers, which made up about 95% of Q2 2026 revenue of $16.7M (up 687% YoY). It has a pending acquisition of Upgrade Energy. CEO Allan Evans leads the company, and Donald Trump Jr. serves as an advisor and shareholder.

Investment Thesis
Unusual Machines is a direct beneficiary of the U.S. push to remove Chinese components from military and enterprise drones. Q2 2026 revenue of $16.7M rose 687% YoY and 106% sequentially, and management targets $25M in Q4 2026, excluding the Upgrade Energy acquisition [1][2]. With about $229.6M of cash, essentially no debt (0.8% D/E), and a market it says is supply-constrained through 2027, UMAC can fund domestic capacity while Drone Dominance program demand ramps [1][2][5].
The bear case is concentration, losses, and political risk. One customer was about 42% of Q2 revenue, the company lost $7.8M in Q2, and management expects gross margin (34.7%) to dip in Q3 [1][2]. The stock's moves have been closely tied to Donald Trump Jr.'s advisory role, and an August 2026 House inquiry into his venture firm cut market-implied odds of a U.S. government equity stake from 45% to 26% [4][6]. Ongoing ATM issuance ($60M in Q2) and insider selling dilute shareholders.
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results against the internal $12-14M revenue target, and progress toward $25M in Q4 as Drone Dominance orders begin
  2. Closing the Upgrade Energy acquisition, adding power-system content per drone
  3. Further DJI and Chinese component restrictions pushing U.S. drone OEMs toward NDAA-compliant domestic suppliers
Key Risks
  1. Customer concentration: the largest customer was about 42% of Q2 2026 revenue, so one order slip could miss the $25M Q4 target
  2. Political and reputational risk from Trump Jr. ties: congressional scrutiny and ethics concerns could affect Pentagon contracting and valuation
  3. Persistent GAAP losses (-20% profit margin) and repeated ATM equity raises diluting shareholders, with net insider selling
