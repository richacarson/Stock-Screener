Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services — Fiber Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Uniti Group is a fiber infrastructure and communications services provider headquartered in Little Rock, Arkansas. Following its August 2025 merger with Windstream, Uniti now operates an integrated fiber and copper network delivering wholesale, enterprise, and consumer (Kinetic brand) connectivity across the United States, with 564,000 fiber subscribers and an expanding wholesale fiber footprint targeting hyperscale and AI data center demand [1][4].

Investment Thesis
Uniti Group's August 2025 merger with Windstream repositioned the company as an integrated fiber operator directly levered to AI data center demand — Q1 2026 fiber revenue grew 15% consolidated (26% in the Kinetic consumer segment) with fiber subscribers up 22% to 564,000, and Uniti Wholesale has signed AI-driven contracts worth $500M+ connecting Dallas to emerging AI compute markets [1][4][5]. As physical fiber infrastructure, the business is structurally immune to AI disruption and is instead a direct beneficiary of hyperscale buildout, positioning Uniti as a picks-and-shovels play on AI infrastructure growth with a $1B planned 2026 capital program extending its network reach into underserved Tier 2/3 markets [5].
The balance sheet, however, is severely stretched: $11.11B in total debt against a market cap of just $2.68B implies extreme leverage, the dividend has been suspended since August 2024 with no REIT-driven obligation to reinstate it, and management guides to a full-year 2026 net loss of $400-450M [3][4][6]. Consolidated revenue growth of only ~1% YoY masks a business still working through legacy copper attrition even as fiber outgrows it, and the company's low P/E (2.7x) reflects market skepticism about deleveraging, not undiscovered value. Culture and leadership are freshly reset (new CEO and Chief People Officer in 2025) with no multi-year track record, and mixed Glassdoor sentiment (3.2/5) offers limited visibility into execution risk on the ambitious buildout plan [2].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued AI/hyperscale-driven wholesale fiber contract wins similar to the $500M, 1,100-mile Dallas-to-AI-market build, expanding recurring high-margin wholesale revenue
  2. Fiber subscriber growth (up 22% YoY to 564,000) and Kinetic consumer fiber revenue growth (26% YoY) continuing to outpace legacy copper attrition, improving the revenue mix
  3. Successful execution of the $1B 2026 capital program without further leverage deterioration, potentially enabling credit rating stabilization and eventual capital returns
Key Risks
  1. Extreme leverage ($11.1B debt vs. $2.68B market cap) combined with a guided FY2026 net loss of $400-450M leaves minimal margin for execution missteps or refinancing stress
  2. No dividend and no REIT structure removes the income-oriented investment case that historically supported the stock, while the newly reset leadership team has no multi-year track record
  3. Integration risk from the large, complex Windstream merger — combining fiber and legacy copper operations under new management — could disrupt planned synergies or delay the AI-driven buildout timeline
