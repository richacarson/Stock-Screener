Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services — Fiber Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Uniti Group is a vertically integrated fiber infrastructure and communications services provider headquartered in Little Rock, Arkansas, formed through the August 2025 merger of Uniti Group (formerly a fiber-leasing REIT) and Windstream Holdings. The company operates ~240,000 fiber route miles and ~11.7 million fiber strand miles across 300+ metro markets, serving ~603,000 consumer fiber subscribers through the Kinetic brand with a target of 675,000-700,000 by year-end 2026 [1][4]. Three segments: Kinetic (consumer fiber broadband, $539M Q2 revenue), Fiber Infrastructure (wholesale/hyperscaler, $234.1M Q2 revenue), and Uniti Solutions (enterprise/government, $182.5M Q2 revenue). Q2 2026 consolidated revenue reached $909.7M with $357.1M adjusted EBITDA (39% margin), though the company guides to a FY2026 net loss of $480-530M driven by $805M in annual interest expense on $11.1B of debt [1].

Investment Thesis
Uniti Group offers a picks-and-shovels play on AI-driven fiber demand at a distressed valuation. Q2 2026 fiber infrastructure bookings hit a record $2.2M MRR (30% above prior record) driven explicitly by hyperscaler and neocloud AI connectivity demand, while Kinetic set records for fiber net adds (~38,000) and premises constructed (141,000) [1]. Consolidated revenue of $909.7M with $357.1M adjusted EBITDA (39% margin) demonstrates the operational engine works, and the twin-engine strategy — consumer fiber buildout via Kinetic (targeting 675K-700K subs by year-end, 19% consumer fiber revenue growth) plus wholesale fiber via Fiber Infrastructure (10% revenue growth) — provides diversified exposure to broadband infrastructure demand [1][5]. At $10.08 per share (0.69x P/S, ~1.7x EV/EBITDA on guided $1.475B), the stock prices in severe distress despite accelerating operational momentum and a $1B nonrecurring cash revenue target by 2028.
The bear case is dominated by overwhelming leverage and structural unprofitability. Uniti carries $11.1B in total debt against just $161.5M in equity (D/E of ~6870%), with $805M in annual interest expense consuming 55% of guided adjusted EBITDA ($1.475B) [1][3]. The company guides to a FY2026 net loss of $480-530M despite growing revenue, meaning every dollar of fiber revenue growth services the debt stack rather than building equity. The $3B in securitizations at 6.18% weighted average coupon locks in high financing costs, and any interest rate increase on the floating-rate portion could further compress margins. Legacy copper/voice services continue declining, partially offsetting fiber gains — total company revenue growth of ~8% masks the drag. The $799M H1 2026 capex leaves minimal free cash flow, and a Kinetic fiber slowdown or hyperscaler demand pullback would leave the leveraged balance sheet with no margin for error [1][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued record fiber infrastructure bookings from hyperscaler/neocloud AI connectivity demand ($2.2M MRR in Q2, 30% above prior record), suggesting an accelerating revenue ramp that could meaningfully improve EBITDA trajectory through 2027 [1]
  2. Kinetic fiber buildout targeting 450,000-500,000 new premises in 2026 with consumer fiber revenue growing 19% YoY and a path to $1B in nonrecurring cash revenue by 2028, creating a durable broadband franchise [1][5]
  3. Potential strategic alternatives — CEO Gunderman has discussed exploring options that could unlock value from the fiber asset base, including asset sales, joint ventures, or recapitalization to address the leverage profile [6]
Key Risks
  1. Extreme leverage ($11.1B debt, $805M annual interest expense, D/E of ~6870%) leaves zero margin for execution missteps — the $161.5M equity cushion is negligible relative to the debt stack, and any refinancing stress could threaten solvency [1][3]
  2. Persistent GAAP net losses (guided $480-530M in FY2026) despite growing revenue mean the company is eroding book equity, and the $799M H1 2026 capex requirement makes positive free cash flow elusive until fiber buildout matures [1]
  3. Legacy copper/voice service decline continues to offset fiber growth — total company revenue growth of ~8% significantly trails the 15-19% fiber-segment growth rates, and copper attrition could accelerate as easier-to-migrate markets are exhausted [1][6]
