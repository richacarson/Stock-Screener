Company Profile

Union Pacific operates one of two major Class I railroads in the western United States, running approximately 32,000 route miles across 23 states. Q2 2026 operating revenue reached a record $6.9B (+12% YoY) with freight revenue up 12% to $6.5B, driven by intermodal volume surging 26%. Union Pacific has paid dividends for over 50 consecutive years and grown them for 16 consecutive years. Market cap is approximately $174B.

Investment Thesis
Union Pacific at 20.45x forward earnings is a high-quality infrastructure compounder delivering accelerating growth from a defensible duopoly position. Q2 2026 record revenue of $6.9B (+12% YoY) with intermodal surging 26% demonstrates that secular trends (nearshoring, data center construction, e-commerce) are driving volume growth through the railroad's irreplaceable western U.S. network [1]. Net margin of 28.85% and ROE of 40.38% reflect the capital efficiency of a natural duopoly with massive barriers to entry. The dividend profile is strong: 2.07% yield, 16 consecutive years of growth, 6.9% 5-year CAGR, and a sustainable 46% payout ratio fund continued mid-single-digit dividend growth [4]. Management raised full-year 2026 EPS guidance to high single digits, with a 3-year CAGR target of high-single to low-double digits through 2027. The AI infrastructure buildout is a multi-year tailwind as data center construction drives demand for bulk materials shipped by rail.
The bear case centers on valuation, leverage, and cyclical exposure. Forward P/E of 20.45x sits at the upper end of historical ranges for rail stocks, pricing in sustained volume growth that is vulnerable to macroeconomic slowdown — freight volumes are inherently cyclical and could decline in a recession [1]. D/E of 178% reflects aggressive financial engineering through share buybacks funded by leverage, leaving limited balance sheet flexibility if volumes contract. The 5-year revenue CAGR of only 3.1% reveals that the recent 12% growth is an acceleration from a period of flat-to-modest top-line performance, and analysts forecast only 4.9% annual revenue growth going forward. Railroad labor relations remain a structural risk — the 2022 near-strike required Congressional intervention, and future labor actions could disrupt operations and erode the operating ratio improvements that underpin margin expansion. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors despite Union Pacific's strong sustainability programs and positive ESG attributions.

Key Risks

  2. Railroad labor relations remain structurally contentious — the 2022 near-strike required Congressional intervention, and ongoing disputes over scheduling, crew sizes, and quality of life could disrupt operations or increase labor costs [3]
  3. Valuation risk: Forward P/E of 20.45x prices in sustained high-single-digit EPS growth, but the 5-year revenue CAGR of 3.1% suggests the current growth acceleration may not be sustainable, creating downside risk if volume trends normalize [2]
