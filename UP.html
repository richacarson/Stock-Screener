Company Profile
Industrials ·Airports & Air Services (Private Aviation) ·NYSE ·United States
Wheels Up Experience Inc. is a U.S. provider of on-demand private aviation, selling membership programs, prepaid Funds, charter, and aircraft management services, and flying members on an owned and operated fleet supplemented by third-party operators. Backed by Delta Air Lines, the company under CEO George Mattson is in the middle of a multi-year fleet modernization from legacy King Air, Citation X and Hawker jets to Embraer Phenom 300 and Bombardier Challenger 300-series aircraft. First-half 2026 revenue was $350.9M (down 4% YoY) and the company continues to report large net losses.
1,250 Employees ·www.wheelsup.com

Investment Thesis
The bull case is a turnaround. Wheels Up is replacing a costly, aging mixed fleet with standardized Phenom 300 and Challenger 300 jets, and revenue from that modern fleet more than doubled YoY in Q1 2026 [1]. Gross profit improved by $7M YoY to $9.6M in Q2 2026, and Delta's ownership, distribution partnership and credit support give the company a lifeline most distressed aviation operators lack [1]. If the modern fleet reaches scale and utilization improves, operating leverage could be significant on a market cap of only about $116M [2].
The bear case is that Wheels Up is still burning cash with no profit in sight. The Q2 2026 net loss was $107M ($2.97/share), 1H 2026 revenue fell 4% to $350.9M, and membership revenue fell 31% [1][3]. Stockholders' equity is in deficit, the forward P/E is negative, and average daily dollar volume of about $336K is below IOWN's $1M liquidity floor, which makes the stock untradeable for the strategy [2]. Equity holders are also exposed to further dilution or restructuring if Delta and the lenders change their stance.
Key Catalysts
  1. Fleet modernization reaching scale: Phenom/Challenger revenue more than doubled in Q1 2026; continued retirement of legacy jets could raise gross margin from the Q2 2026 level of $9.6M [1]
  2. Deeper Delta Air Lines commercial integration (corporate travel distribution, SkyMiles linkage) widening the customer funnel [5]
  3. Operating cost reductions from the Palantir-enabled operations platform and the divestiture of non-core services businesses [1][4]
Key Risks
  1. Continued heavy losses ($107M Q2 2026 net loss) against a stockholders' deficit, so further dilution or restructuring is a real possibility [1]
  2. Membership revenue down 31% in 1H 2026, which suggests weakening loyalty to the core product versus NetJets, Flexjet and charter marketplaces [3]
  3. Illiquidity: about $336K average daily dollar volume, below IOWN's $1M floor, which makes entry and exit impractical [2]
