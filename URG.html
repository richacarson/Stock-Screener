Company Profile
Energy ·Uranium ·NYSE ·United States
Ur-Energy is America's largest and fastest-growing domestic in situ recovery (ISR) uranium producer, operating the Lost Creek facility in Wyoming and commissioning the Shirley Basin mine in early 2026 to create a two-asset production platform. Under CEO Matthew Gili (appointed December 2025), the company is ramping production with combined licensed and toll-processing capacity of 4.2 million pounds of U3O8 per year. Q2 2026 sales totaled 215,000 lbs U3O8 at $14.4M revenue with a cash cost of $40.20/lb, and H1 2026 revenue reached $18.3M (+76% YoY). The company holds $123M in unrestricted cash.

Investment Thesis
Ur-Energy offers leveraged exposure to the uranium bull market at a critical inflection point — the company is transitioning from a single-mine operator to a two-asset production platform with 4.2M lb licensed capacity just as AI data center demand and nuclear energy policy support are creating a structural supply-demand imbalance in uranium markets [1][2]. H1 2026 revenue of $18.3M (+76% YoY) with cash costs of $40.20/lb versus spot uranium prices above $80/lb demonstrates expanding margins as production scales. The $123M cash position provides a runway through the Shirley Basin ramp without dilution risk. As the largest domestic ISR uranium producer, URG benefits from U.S. government procurement preferences for domestically-produced uranium under nuclear fuel supply chain initiatives. The Inspire Impact Score of 31 (ALIGNED) makes URG accessible to faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on execution risk and commodity cyclicality. URG remains unprofitable despite growing revenue — the company widened its net loss in H1 2026 as Shirley Basin ramp-up costs and G&A expenses outpaced revenue growth, and the Forward P/E of 158.73x prices in substantial future profitability that has not yet materialized [4]. Uranium prices are cyclical and a downturn from current $80+/lb levels could squeeze margins and delay the path to profitability. The D/E ratio of 104.45% has tripled over five years, reflecting construction debt that must be serviced through production cash flows. URG is a small-cap ($715M market cap) with only 3.27M shares average daily volume, creating liquidity risk for institutional investors. The company has no AI products, no dividend, and its Innovation score (3/10) reflects the inherent limitations of a commodity mining business. Production execution at Shirley Basin is unproven — commissioning delays or operational issues could significantly impact the investment thesis [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Shirley Basin mine ramp-up to full production in 2026-2027, increasing total licensed capacity by 83% to 4.2M lbs U3O8 and transforming URG from a single-asset operator to a multi-mine platform with diversified production risk [1]

  2. Shirley Basin execution risk — the mine is newly commissioned and production performance at scale is unproven, with potential for operational delays, wellfield issues, or regulatory complications that could delay the production ramp and extend the period of unprofitability [1]
