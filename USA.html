Company Profile
Financial Services ·Closed-End Fund - Equity ·NYSE ·United States
Liberty All-Star Equity Fund is a diversified, multi-managed closed-end investment company with approximately $2.0 billion in net assets. Managed by ALPS Advisors, Inc., the fund allocates assets equally among five independent investment managers: Aristotle Capital Management, Fiduciary Management, Pzena Investment Management, TCW Investment Management, and Loomis Sayles & Company (added June 2026). The fund follows a Large-Cap Core strategy combining three value-style and two growth-style managers, with a distribution policy targeting 10% of NAV annually. Listed since 1986.

Investment Thesis
Liberty All-Star Equity Fund offers an attractive ~12.3% distribution yield backed by a multi-manager Large-Cap Core strategy that has delivered solid returns (20.89% in 2024, 23.25% in 2023, 8.8% NAV return in 2025) [1]. The 11.2% discount to NAV as of May 2026 provides a margin of safety, and the recent addition of Loomis Sayles as a growth manager (June 2026) refreshes the investment approach [2]. With ~$2.0B in net assets, 40 years of continuous distributions since 1986, and zero leverage, the fund provides institutional-quality diversified equity exposure with a high-income component for yield-focused investors.
The bear case is structural rather than operational. The IOWN Return on Intention framework evaluates companies on innovation, inspiration, and purpose-driven leadership — dimensions where a closed-end fund inherently scores poorly because it is a financial vehicle, not an operating company with a mission. The 10% of NAV distribution policy erodes capital in down years (return of capital distributions), and the payout ratio exceeds 100% when investment returns fall short of the distribution target [3]. Active management faces secular headwinds from passive indexing and AI-driven quantitative strategies, threatening the fund's ability to generate alpha sufficient to justify its management fee structure. The low overall score reflects the IOWN framework's structural mismatch with investment vehicles rather than a judgment on the fund's investment quality.
Key Catalysts
  1. New portfolio manager Loomis Sayles (added June 2026) led by Aziz Hamzaogullari could reinvigorate the growth allocation and improve NAV performance
  2. 11.2% discount to NAV creates potential for capital appreciation if the discount narrows toward historical averages through market recovery or fund-level actions
  3. Strong equity market environment could drive NAV growth above the 10% distribution rate, eliminating return-of-capital distributions and improving tax efficiency
Key Risks
  1. 10% of NAV distribution policy erodes capital in years when investment returns fall below the distribution threshold, creating a structural drag on long-term NAV compounding
  2. Active multi-manager equity strategy faces secular competitive pressure from passive index funds, ETFs, and AI-driven quantitative strategies that deliver lower-cost market exposure
  3. Persistent 9-11% discount to NAV signals structural investor skepticism about closed-end fund vehicles, limiting price appreciation even when underlying holdings perform well
