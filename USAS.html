Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NYSE American ·Canada
Americas Gold and Silver Corporation is a North American-focused precious metals producer operating the Galena Complex in Idaho (silver, lead, copper) and the Cosalá Operations in Sinaloa, Mexico (silver, gold, zinc). Under CEO Paul Huet's turnaround leadership since 2024, the company achieved commercial production from the high-grade EC120 zone at Cosalá and reported Q1 FY2026 revenue of $67.8M with record production, followed by Q2 FY2026 revenue of $46M (up 71% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $12M [1][2]. The company guided for approximately 30% annual production growth in 2026 and published its inaugural corporate sustainability report in 2025 [3][4].

Investment Thesis
USAS at $3.78 with a $1.3B market cap is a small-cap silver turnaround story riding the precious metals bull market. Under CEO Paul Huet's leadership, the company achieved 72% YoY revenue growth to $182M TTM, record production at both the Galena Complex and Cosalá operations, and guided for approximately 30% production growth in 2026 driven by the high-grade EC120 zone [1][2]. The balance sheet is clean at 19.8% D/E, forward P/E of 12.0 suggests cheap valuation relative to production trajectory, and rising silver prices (driven by AI data center demand for electronic components and solar energy deployment) provide a favorable commodity tailwind. Adjusted EBITDA of $12M in Q2 2026 signals the company is approaching profitability as production scales.
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability, execution risk, and commodity dependence. Despite 72% revenue growth, USAS posted a $5M net loss in Q2 2026 with a -26.3% profit margin, and the company has never achieved sustained profitability — ROE of -27.1% reflects chronic value destruction for shareholders [1]. The company has a $1.3B market cap but generates only $182M in TTM revenue, implying significant speculative premium. Operations in Sinaloa, Mexico carry jurisdictional risk including cartel activity, regulatory uncertainty, and infrastructure challenges. Mining is capital-intensive and the 30% production growth target requires sustained investment that could strain the balance sheet if metal prices decline. Revenue of $46M in Q2 versus $67.8M in Q1 shows sequential volatility that undermines production consistency [1][2]. As a junior miner with no pricing power, USAS is entirely dependent on silver and gold spot prices that could reverse sharply in a risk-off environment.
Key Catalysts
  1. EC120 high-grade zone at Cosalá ramping to commercial production with approximately 30% annual production growth guidance, providing a step-change in output and potential path to sustained profitability as fixed costs are absorbed [1][2]
  2. Silver price tailwind from structural demand growth — AI data center expansion, solar panel deployment, and industrial electronics create increasing physical demand against constrained global mine supply, supporting realized prices above cost curves [1]
  3. Turnaround inflection to profitability — Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $12M suggests the company is approaching breakeven, with further production growth and operational efficiency potentially delivering positive net income within 2-3 quarters [1]
Key Risks
  1. Persistent unprofitability with -26.3% profit margin and -27.1% ROE despite 72% revenue growth — the company has never demonstrated sustained profitability and may require additional capital raising that dilutes shareholders [1]
  2. Commodity price dependence with zero pricing power — a 20% decline in silver prices from current levels would likely push the company deeper into losses and could force production curtailments at higher-cost operations
  3. Operational and jurisdictional risk at Cosalá in Sinaloa, Mexico — the region faces security challenges, regulatory uncertainty, and infrastructure limitations that could disrupt production; sequential revenue decline from $67.8M (Q1) to $46M (Q2) already shows production volatility [1][2]
