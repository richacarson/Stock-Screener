Investment Thesis
U.S. Energy (rebranding to Big Sky Industrial) controls unique integrated assets in Montana — 1.3 BCF of certified helium resources and 444 BCF of CO2, combined with the Cut Bank oil field for enhanced oil recovery [2]. The company reached a final investment decision on Big Sky and secured a helium offtake agreement, with three independent monetization pathways: helium sales, Section 45Q carbon credits ($85/ton), and CO2-EOR [2][3]. Helium demand is growing structurally due to semiconductor manufacturing, AI data center cooling, and MRI applications, with a global supply shortage tightening as geopolitical disruption affects Russian, Algerian, and Qatari supply. The 36% insider ownership aligns management with shareholders, and the Inspire Impact Score of 29 (ALIGNED) supports faith-based investor positioning. D/E of only 2% provides a clean balance sheet for the transformation [3][4].
The bear case centers on execution risk and financial distress. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.6M missed estimates, EPS of -$0.08 missed by double, and TTM revenue declined -23% [1][4]. The path to 'meaningful cash flow beginning in 2027' relies on successful commercial-scale helium extraction and carbon sequestration — technologies involving geological and engineering risk that may not deliver on schedule. At $0.99/share, the stock trades at borderline liquidity (~$990K daily dollar volume) making it essentially uninvestable for institutional portfolios. ROE of -65.87% and profit margin of -155% reflect a company burning cash with no clear timeline to profitability [4]. Forward estimates project continued losses of -$0.24/share in 2026 with only $7.6M in revenue. The name change to Big Sky Industrial creates investor confusion during a critical execution period [5]. The company has insufficient revenue to fund operations indefinitely and may require additional dilutive financing.
