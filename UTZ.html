Company Profile

Utz Brands manufactures and distributes branded salty snacks in the United States, including potato chips, tortilla chips, pretzels, cheese snacks, pork skins, and popcorn under brands such as Utz, Zapp's, On The Border, Boulder Canyon, Golden Flake, and Hawaiian. Founded in 1921 and headquartered in Hanover, Pennsylvania. PENDING ACQUISITION: Intersnack Group to acquire all Class A shares at $14.25/share (~$2.9B enterprise value), announced July 21, 2026.

Investment Thesis
Utz Brands has entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Intersnack Group at $14.25/share in an all-cash transaction valued at ~$2.9B enterprise value, announced July 21, 2026 [4]. The stock trades at $14.10, representing a $0.15 merger-arb spread to the deal price. The underlying business showed improving momentum in Q1 2026 with branded salty snacks up 5.2% and Boulder Canyon surging 101%, and the Power Four portfolio (Utz, On The Border, Zapp's, Boulder Canyon) is outpacing the broader salty snacks category [1]. The founding Rice and Lissette families are rolling their equity into 50% ownership of the post-merger entity, signaling long-term confidence in the business under Intersnack's global platform [4].
The bear case is defined by the deal structure and fundamental challenges. Multiple law firms are investigating whether the $14.25 price is fair to public shareholders, noting that the founding families receive preferential treatment by rolling into 50% post-merger ownership (an ~8% gain in collective ownership) while public shareholders are cashed out [4]. Pre-deal fundamentals remain weak: GAAP profit margin of -0.58%, ROE of -0.58%, Altman Z-Score of 0.8 (financial distress territory), and $1.04B in debt [5]. Revenue growth of 2.6% and flat ~$1.45B revenue for four consecutive years reflects structural challenges competing against Frito-Lay's 60%+ market share and massive scale advantages [1]. If the deal fails, the stock would likely return to pre-deal levels near $8-10, representing 30-45% downside. The dividend was cut (-5.3% growth) and yield dropped to 1.79%, eliminating the income appeal [5].
