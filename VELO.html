Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery — Metal Additive Manufacturing ·NASDAQ ·United States
Velo3D designs and manufactures metal additive manufacturing (3D printing) systems for mission-critical parts in defense, aerospace, space, semiconductor, and energy applications, headquartered in Fremont, California. Following a December 2024 financial restructuring that cancelled 81.7% of secured notes and installed new CEO Dr. Arun Jeldi (from majority owner Arrayed Additive), the company has pivoted from a pure equipment-sales SPAC-era model to a recurring-revenue Rapid Production Services (RPS) model [1][3]. Q2 2026 revenue reached $20.7M (+52.3% YoY) with gross margin swinging from -11.7% to +21.5%, backlog doubled to $31M, and FY2026 guidance was raised to $65-75M [1].

Investment Thesis
Velo3D is a speculative post-restructuring turnaround in metal additive manufacturing, directly levered to defense, aerospace, and AI-adjacent semiconductor/energy demand. Q2 2026 delivered 52.3% YoY revenue growth to $20.7M with gross margin swinging from -11.7% to +21.5%, backlog doubled to $31M, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $65-75M [1]. The December 2024 restructuring eliminated 81.7% of secured debt, creating a clean balance sheet (D/E ~21%) rare for a company of this scale. Defense contract wins (DoD multi-year RPS, $9.8M DLA JAMA IDIQ) provide recurring-revenue visibility, and the Livermore Production Campus is expected to triple manufacturing capacity to serve accelerating demand across aerospace, defense, and semiconductor markets [1][4]. As physical manufacturing infrastructure, the business is structurally immune to AI disruption and benefits from AI compute buildout demand.
The risks remain substantial despite the turnaround momentum. Velo3D is still deeply unprofitable with -89.6% profit margin and -61.4% ROE, and even the raised $65-75M revenue guidance implies a small-scale operation (~$70M annual revenue, 134 employees) with limited diversification [5]. Shares have diluted 212% in the past year through the debt-for-equity restructuring. The path to profitability depends on gross margins exceeding 30% in H2 2026 (management target) and continued volume ramp at the Livermore facility — any production delays or customer program cancellations could derail the trajectory. Cultural recovery from the pre-restructuring toxic leadership is underway but unproven at scale, with Glassdoor senior management ratings still at 2.5/5 [4]. Arrayed Additive's 95% equity control concentrates governance risk with a single creditor-turned-owner.
Key Catalysts
  1. Gross margin expansion toward management's 30%+ target in H2 2026 as production volumes rise and operational efficiencies improve at the Livermore Production Campus, validating the path to profitability [1]
  2. Continued defense and aerospace program wins building on the DoD multi-year RPS contract, DLA JAMA IDIQ award, and growing engagement from strategic customers across production-scale metal AM [4]
  3. Livermore Production Campus capacity tripling expected to unlock new revenue from semiconductor, energy, and space customers, expanding beyond the current defense-heavy backlog [1]
Key Risks
  1. Deeply negative profitability (-89.6% profit margin, -61.4% ROE) and small revenue base (~$70M annual guidance) mean the company remains far from sustainable economics despite the turnaround momentum [5]
  2. Massive share dilution (212% in one year) from the debt-for-equity restructuring concentrates 95% ownership with Arrayed Additive, limiting governance independence and creating overhang risk if the controlling shareholder sells [5]
  3. Pre-restructuring cultural damage (Glassdoor senior management 2.5/5, prior CEO described as abusive) may impair talent recruitment and retention critical to scaling manufacturing operations with only 134 employees [4]
