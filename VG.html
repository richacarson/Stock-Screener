Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYQ ·United States
Venture Global is a Virginia-based LNG developer, constructor, and exporter that pioneered a modular, factory-built construction approach enabling faster and lower-cost facility development than traditional stick-built LNG projects. The company operates Calcasieu Pass LNG in Louisiana, is ramping Plaquemines LNG toward Q4 2026 commercial operations, and has CP2 LNG permitted with FID targeted for early 2027. Q2 2026 delivered record results: $4.6B revenue (+48% YoY), $2.5B Adjusted EBITDA (+79%), and 127 cargos exported. Full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance was raised to $8.7-$9.1B.

Investment Thesis
Venture Global at $13.29 is a high-growth LNG exporter trading at an attractive 13.3x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.4 and delivering explosive financial performance — Q2 2026 revenue grew 48% YoY to $4.6B, Adjusted EBITDA surged 79% to $2.5B (54% margin), and full-year EBITDA guidance was raised to $8.7-$9.1B with 91% of cargos contracted [1]. The modular construction approach has proven commercially successful with ROE of 33.6% and profit margins expanding to 22.1%, demonstrating that VG's engineering innovation translates into real capital efficiency. The pipeline is substantial: Plaquemines Phase I commercial operations expected Q4 2026, CP2 FID targeted early 2027, and the company targets 100 MTPA capacity by 2030. AI data center power demand is a secular tailwind for natural gas demand.
The bear case is multi-dimensional. VG's D/E of 354.6% represents $42.8B in debt — an extreme leverage position even for capital-intensive LNG infrastructure — leaving minimal margin for error if spot prices soften, expansion projects face delays, or arbitration settlements require material cash outlays [1]. The pattern of breaching long-term supply contracts to capture spot-market premiums (BP arbitration loss with $1B+ in damages, pending disputes with Shell, Repsol, Edison, Galp) raises fundamental questions about counterparty trust that could impair future project financing and contract negotiations [2]. Environmental compliance is problematic: the Louisiana DEQ logged nearly 200 air emission incidents at Calcasieu Pass from 2022-2024, and advocacy groups allege 4,500+ hours of permit-violating operations [3]. Internal culture signals are weak (Glassdoor 3.2/5, 'abusive upper level leadership'), congressional scrutiny of government financing is ongoing, and insider transactions have skewed toward selling. The Infinite Game score of 2/10 reflects a company optimizing for near-term cash capture at the expense of trust and long-term relationships.
