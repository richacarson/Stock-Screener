VICI Properties is an S&P 500 experiential REIT owning one of the largest portfolios of market-leading gaming, hospitality, and entertainment destinations, including Caesars Palace Las Vegas, MGM Grand, and the Venetian Resort. The portfolio comprises 103 experiential assets across 63 gaming and 40 other experiential properties totaling approximately 130 million square feet with 66,000 hotel rooms. Q2 2026 revenue rose 5.7% YoY to $1.1B with AFFO of $679.6M ($0.62/share), and management updated full-year AFFO guidance to $2.675-$2.695/share.

Investment Thesis
VICI at $22.91 offers an 8.0% dividend yield backed by one of the most defensible real estate portfolios in the REIT universe — 103 experiential properties including Caesars Palace, MGM Grand, and the Venetian, leased to operators under long-term triple-net agreements with contractual rent escalators [1][6]. Q2 2026 revenue grew 5.7% YoY to $1.1B with AFFO of $679.6M ($0.62/share), and the company raised full-year AFFO guidance to $2.675-$2.695/share [1]. At 7.66x forward earnings with 7 consecutive years of dividend growth averaging 8.5% CAGR, investment-grade balance sheet at 4.9x net debt/EBITDA, and AI-immune physical assets, VICI offers a compelling combination of current income and modest growth. Expansion into non-gaming experiential assets (Canyon Ranch, Great Wolf, Chelsea Piers, Club Med) diversifies the earnings base beyond gaming concentration.
The bear case centers on VICI's gaming concentration risk and structural growth limitations. Despite diversification efforts, Caesars Entertainment and MGM Resorts account for the majority of rental income, creating binary counterparty risk in an economic downturn [3]. Revenue growth has decelerated to mid-single digits as the acquisition-driven growth phase matures, with organic growth limited to 1.5-2.5% annual rent escalators, capping AFFO/share growth in mid-single digits absent new deals [1]. The stock has fallen ~13% from its 52-week high, reflecting interest rate sensitivity — as a high-yield, long-duration asset, VICI's valuation compresses mechanically with higher-for-longer rates, and the 8.0% dividend yield must compete with risk-free alternatives [6]. GAAP EPS declined 41.4% YoY due to comparison effects, and the 70% payout ratio is above the 40% target, limiting FFO retention. The Inspire Impact Score of -65 (MISALIGNED) due to Gambling and Sexually Explicit attributions limits allocation for faith-based portfolios despite 13 positive ESG attributions [4].

  3. Organic growth ceiling — contractual rent escalators of 1.5-2.5% annually limit AFFO/share growth to mid-single digits without acquisitions, requiring continued capital markets access in an increasingly competitive experiential real estate market [1]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): VICI's core business — owning physical real estate leased to gaming, hospitality, and entertainment operators under long-term triple-net leases — is structurally immune to AI substitution [5]. Casinos, hotels, resorts, and entertainment venues are fundamentally physical experiences that AI cannot replicate or replace. Triple-net leases generate contractual rent escalators (1.5-2.5% annually) regardless of technology shifts. AI-driven productivity gains may actually increase disposable income and leisure time, boosting demand for experiential entertainment. The only indirect risk is AI enabling virtual gambling, but regulatory barriers and the social experience of gaming floors limit substitution [1]. AI Opportunity (0/1): VICI itself generates no revenue from AI-related products, services, or infrastructure — the company is a pure-play real estate owner. While tenants (Caesars, MGM) are investing in AI-driven personalization, VICI does not directly monetize these capabilities.
