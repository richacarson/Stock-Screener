Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Travel Services ·NYSE ·Bermuda
Viking Holdings is a premium cruise operator offering river, ocean, and expedition voyages focused on culturally enriching, destination-focused experiences for affluent, curious travelers. Founded by Torstein Hagen in 1997, the company IPO'd on the NYSE in May 2024 in the largest IPO of the year. As of Q2 2026, Viking operates a fleet of over 100 ships including 89 river vessels, 12 ocean ships, and 2 expedition ships, with 12 new ships expected to be delivered in 2026. In May 2026, Hagen transitioned to Executive Chairman and longtime financial executive Leah Talactac was named CEO. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.19B (+16.5% YoY) with net income of $587.7M and Adjusted EBITDA of $748.4M (+18.2%). The company is 96% sold for 2026 and 53% sold for 2027. Market cap approximately $39B.

Investment Thesis
Viking at approximately 27x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.65 offers premium cruise exposure at a reasonable growth-adjusted valuation -- Q2 2026 revenue of $2.19B (+16.5% YoY) and net income of $587.7M (+60% H1 YoY) demonstrate accelerating profitability as the fleet scales [1][2]. Booking visibility is exceptional with 96% of 2026 sold and 53% of 2027 sold as of August 2026, providing revenue certainty rarely seen in consumer discretionary businesses. The fleet expansion (12 new ships in 2026, options to 2032) drives capacity-led growth while per-passenger yield growth reflects pricing power with affluent demographics. Net leverage of only 1.2x EBITDA, $4B cash, and $1B undrawn revolver provide financial flexibility despite the high D/E ratio that is structural for ship-financed businesses [2]. Viking's brand differentiation -- destination-focused, culturally enriching, no casinos or children -- creates a defensible niche that mass-market competitors cannot easily replicate without cannibalizing their own brands.
The bear case centers on CEO transition risk, macroeconomic sensitivity, and leverage. Founder Torstein Hagen's transition to Executive Chairman in May 2026 removes the visionary leader who built Viking over 27 years, and new CEO Leah Talactac (a financial executive) may prioritize operational efficiency over brand-building and innovation [3]. Viking's premium cruise product targets affluent retirees, making it sensitive to wealth effects from equity market declines and to recession-driven spending pullbacks -- if consumer sentiment deteriorates, discretionary luxury travel is among the first expenses cut. The D/E of 374.76% reflects heavy ship financing that amplifies both upside (when ships are full) and downside (when demand falters), as demonstrated by the industry's near-collapse during COVID [5]. The estimated 5-year revenue CAGR of 20% is partly inflated by COVID recovery base effects (2021 revenue was near zero), and normalizing growth closer to 12-15% would make the 27x forward PE less compelling. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) reflects a neutral faith alignment profile. Environmental scrutiny of cruise ship emissions (Viking CEO Hagen has publicly challenged the industry's fuel choices) represents an emerging ESG risk [4].
