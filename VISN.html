Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Vistance Networks is the renamed remainder of CommScope after the sale of its Connectivity and Cable Solutions business to Amphenol and its RUCKUS enterprise Wi-Fi business to Belden (~$1.846B, closed July 1, 2026). The continuing company is Aurora Networks, which supplies cable broadband access equipment (nodes, amplifiers, and related network products) to cable and fiber operators. Q2 2026 continuing net sales were $319.6M (-1.4% YoY) with core adjusted EBITDA margin compressing to 14.2% on memory chip costs; the company has eliminated all $7.3B of long-term debt and returned $15/share (~$3.4B) to shareholders in 2026. CEO Chuck Treadway leads; market cap approximately $1.5B.

Investment Thesis
Vistance is now a clean, debt-free pure play on cable broadband access equipment. The CCS and RUCKUS divestitures funded repayment of all $7.26B of long-term debt, redemption of $1.28B of Series A preferred stock, and $15/share (~$3.4B) of special distributions in 2026, and management expects $700-750M of year-end cash with no debt plus a $160M tax refund in 2027 [1]. At $6.54, the equity (~$1.5B market cap) trades at about 9.4x forward earnings, so year-end cash alone represents roughly half the market value [1][3]. Aurora's Q1 2026 sales jumped 32.6% YoY to $298.4M, showing demand when operators upgrade networks, and a $100M buyback authorization gives management a lever to shrink the share count [1][2].
The bear case is that what remains is a smaller, cyclical, single-customer-segment hardware business with deteriorating margins. Q2 2026 continuing sales fell 1.4% to $319.6M, Aurora operating income collapsed 86% to $7.0M, core adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 14.2% from 24.7%, and full-year EBITDA guideposts were cut by $25M to $200-225M on memory chip pricing and availability [1]. Adjusted EPS of $0.12 missed the $0.28 consensus, and Q2 continuing operations posted an operating loss with net income supported by a $28.7M tax benefit [2][4]. Yahoo Finance's PEG of 2.94 and ROE of 4.3% show limited earnings power relative to the price, and management's openness to 'inorganic growth investments' raises capital-allocation risk for the remaining cash [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results and confirmation of the $200-225M full-year adjusted EBITDA guidepost, with any easing in memory chip costs restoring Aurora margins [1]
  2. Deployment of $700-750M expected year-end cash and the $160M 2027 tax refund through the $100M buyback authorization or further distributions [1]
  3. Cable operator network upgrade cycles that drove Aurora's 32.6% Q1 2026 sales growth to $298.4M [2]
Key Risks
  1. Memory chip pricing and availability, which already forced a $25M cut to 2026 EBITDA guideposts and compressed core margins by ~10 points YoY [1]
  2. Customer concentration in cable operators whose capex cycles are lumpy, as shown by Q2's flat sales after Q1's surge and the loss of high-margin license sales [1][2]
  3. Capital-allocation risk: management is evaluating 'inorganic growth investments' with the remaining cash, after a decade at CommScope marked by a leveraged acquisition that ended in forced divestitures [1]
