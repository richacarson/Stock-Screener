Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·Mexico
Vista Energy is a Latin American oil and gas exploration and production company focused on the Vaca Muerta shale play in Argentina's Neuquina basin. Founded in 2017 by former YPF CEO Miguel Galuccio, Vista has rapidly scaled production to 156,000 BOE/d following the July 2026 closing of the $712M Equinor Vaca Muerta asset acquisition. Vista is the largest independent Vaca Muerta operator and top independent exporter, with 2026 production guidance of 158,000 BOE/d.

Investment Thesis
Vista Energy is a high-growth Vaca Muerta pure-play delivering exceptional results — Q2 2026 revenue of $1.15B (89% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $805M (99% YoY increase, 70% margin), and production of 156,000 BOE/d (32% YoY) following the successful closing of the $712M Equinor asset acquisition [1][2]. At 5.33x forward P/E with 89% revenue growth and 332.7% EPS growth, Vista trades at a significant discount to North American shale peers despite superior growth rates and comparable margins. The company raised 2026 production guidance to 158,000 BOE/d with a long-term target of 200,000+ BOE/d by decade-end. Forward PEG of 0.14 is among the lowest of any high-growth E&P globally. The $6.88B market cap is supported by $4.6B+ annualized revenue run-rate and 30.42% ROE [5].
The primary risk is commodity price exposure — production guidance and EBITDA depend on oil prices, and every $5/bbl Brent decline compresses FCF by an estimated $100-150M [1]. D/E of 112.57%, while improved from 131.3%, reflects aggressive acquisition-funded growth that leaves limited margin for error in a downturn. The Equinor integration, while producing immediate revenue gains, increases operational complexity and financing requirements. Argentine sovereign risk — currency controls, export taxes, and political volatility — creates structural uncertainty for cash repatriation and foreign investment returns. The forward P/E of 5.33 partly reflects this country risk discount. ESG and climate transition risks are inherent to an oil-focused E&P business, though Vista's 59% renewable energy share and electric drilling innovations provide partial mitigation [4][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Equinor asset integration driving production guidance increase to 158,000 BOE/d, with Q2 2026 already delivering 156,000 BOE/d (32% YoY) and $805M EBITDA (99% YoY) [1][2]
  2. Forward P/E of 5.33 with 89% revenue growth represents extreme value relative to North American shale peers trading at 8-12x forward — potential for multiple re-rating as execution continues [5]
  3. Export volume expansion and new pipeline capacity unlocking international price realizations, with Vista already the leading independent Vaca Muerta exporter [2]
Key Risks
  1. Commodity price dependency — a sustained Brent decline below $60/bbl would compress the 70% EBITDA margin and threaten FCF generation at 112.57% D/E leverage [1][5]
  2. Argentine sovereign risk — currency devaluation, export tax changes, or political instability could disrupt operations, cash repatriation, and the 5.33x forward multiple's country risk discount [5]
  3. Execution risk on rapid production scaling — integrating Equinor's 27,000+ net acres while maintaining 80-90 annual well tie-ins demands consistent drilling performance and infrastructure buildout [1][2]

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Vista's core business — physical oil and gas extraction from Vaca Muerta shale — is structurally immune to AI substitution. Drilling, completion, reservoir engineering, and hydrocarbon production involve complex physical-world operations requiring geological expertise, heavy equipment, and on-the-ground execution that AI cannot replace [1].
