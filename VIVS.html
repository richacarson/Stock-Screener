VivoSim Labs, Inc. (formerly Organovo Holdings, Inc.) is a pharmaceutical and biotechnology services company providing testing of drugs and drug candidates in three-dimensional (3D) human tissue models of liver and intestine. The company's AI-enabled NAMkind platform delivers liver and intestinal toxicology insights for pharmaceutical and biotech companies, supporting the FDA's transition away from animal testing models under the FDA Modernization Act 2.0. Renamed from Organovo in April 2025. Founded in 2007, headquartered in San Diego, California.
15 Employees ·vivosim.ai

Investment Thesis
VivoSim Labs operates in a structurally attractive market — the FDA Modernization Act 2.0 explicitly supports non-animal testing alternatives, and the global market for new approach methodologies (NAMs) exceeds $10 billion. The company's AI-enabled NAMkind platform for liver and intestinal toxicology addresses a genuine regulatory need, and the appointment of CSO Dr. Amar Sethi brings three decades of pharma R&D experience to strengthen the scientific foundation [1][3]. Zero debt provides a clean balance sheet, and the pivot from failed Organovo 3D bioprinting to focused toxicology services represents a more commercially viable strategy.
However, VivoSim is a nano-cap ($5M market cap) pre-revenue company generating just $131K annually against a $13.84M net loss — a burn rate that consumes capital at an alarming pace with no clear path to breakeven [1][2]. Revenue declined 9% YoY and has been falling at 18.2% annually over five years, indicating the Organovo-to-VivoSim pivot has not reversed the commercial trajectory. The company has 15 employees competing against CROs with thousands of scientists and billions in revenue (Charles River, Labcorp Drug Development). Cash runway depends entirely on periodic capital raises, with the latest SEK 53.8M rights issue providing limited runway. Executive Chairman Keith Murphy splitting time as CEO of Viscient Bio raises questions about management focus. This is a speculative early-stage bet with extreme dilution risk and no demonstrated ability to commercialize its technology [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. FDA Modernization Act 2.0 implementation driving pharmaceutical companies to adopt non-animal testing alternatives, expanding the addressable market for NAMkind platform services
  2. Potential validation partnerships with major pharmaceutical companies for ADC toxicology screening, where VivoSim claims differentiated accuracy versus animal models
  3. Appointment of CSO Dr. Amar Sethi could accelerate commercial partnerships through his pharma industry network and CRO leadership experience
Key Risks
  1. Cash burn of ~$13.84M annually against $131K revenue creates existential funding risk — the company will require additional capital raises within 12-18 months, likely at significant dilution to existing shareholders
  2. Competition from both established CROs (Charles River, Labcorp Drug Development) and AI-based in silico screening platforms (Recursion, Insilico Medicine) that could capture NAM market share without physical tissue models
  3. Persistent revenue decline (-9% YoY, -18.2% 5yr CAGR) after nearly two decades of operation suggests fundamental commercial viability challenges that a rebrand alone cannot solve
