Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
Valero Energy Corporation is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, with 15 refineries and about 3.2 million barrels per day of throughput capacity across the U.S., Canada, the U.K. and Ireland. It also operates 12 ethanol plants and is the 50/50 joint venture partner in Diamond Green Diesel, North America's largest renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel producer. Valero reports three segments (Refining, Renewable Diesel, Ethanol) and is led by Chairman and CEO Lane Riggs.

Valero is a disciplined, well-run refiner that optimizes for safety, reliability, capital discipline and shareholder returns. Those are admirable finite goals, but they are not a Just Cause in Sinek's sense. Investment Thesis
Valero is earning windfall profits in a historically tight refining market. Q3 2026 net income was about $6.8B ($23.89/share vs. $3.53 a year ago) on revenue of about $46.1B (+43%), and refining margin per barrel reached $39.24 versus $13.14 a year earlier [1][2]. Operating cash flow of $6.9B in the quarter, D/E of 35%, a forward P/E of only 7.2 and a payout ratio near 11% leave large capacity for buybacks and dividend increases [6]. Diamond Green Diesel's return to profitability ($353M of operating income) adds a second engine, and as the world's largest independent refiner Valero captures the most upside from structurally constrained global capacity [1].
The main risk is that Q3 2026 is close to a cyclical peak. Margins that tripled in a year can mean-revert just as quickly, and the 7x forward P/E shows the market already discounting that. Profit margin is structurally thin (8.8% even at peak) and throughput fell about 4% YoY, so the earnings rest entirely on crack spreads [1]. The dividend yield is only about 1.25%, the growth streak is just 4 years after a three-year freeze at $3.92, and long-run energy transition, emissions regulation and environmental-justice scrutiny remain overhangs [7][8].
Key Catalysts
  1. Sustained elevated crack spreads: refining margin of $39.24/bbl in Q3 2026 vs. $13.14 a year earlier could produce record annual free cash flow and larger buybacks [2]
  2. Diamond Green Diesel recovery ($353M of Q3 2026 operating income vs. a $28M loss) and Port Arthur SAF volumes benefiting from aviation decarbonization mandates [1]
  3. Dividend increase in January 2027 from a very low payout ratio (about 11% of TTM EPS), extending the growth streak [7]
Key Risks
  1. Crack-spread mean reversion: the margin gain driving Q3 2026's roughly 7x EPS increase is cyclical, and normalization would compress earnings by more than half [1]
  2. Environmental and regulatory pressure, including Clean Air Act fuel-standard penalties, environmental-justice scrutiny at Gulf Coast refineries, and Climate Action 100+ engagement [8]
  3. Uncertain RFS and LCFS credit values, which drive the economics of the renewable diesel and ethanol segments

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Refining is a physical, heavily permitted, capital-intensive chemical process. With no new U.S. refineries being built and global capacity tight, which helped Q3 2026 margins roughly triple YoY, there is no AI substitution pathway [1][2].
