Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing ·NYSE ·United States
Valero Energy Corporation is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, operating 15 petroleum refineries with a combined throughput capacity of approximately 3.2 million barrels per day. The company also operates 13 ethanol plants and is the world's second-largest corn ethanol producer, and a joint venture partner in Diamond Green Diesel, the largest renewable diesel producer in North America. Headquartered in San Antonio, Texas, Valero operates through three segments: Refining, Renewable Diesel, and Ethanol, serving customers across the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Ireland, and Latin America.

Investment Thesis
Valero delivered record Q2 2026 results with $3.7B net income and adjusted EPS of $12.54 on $44.48B revenue (+48.8% YoY), fueled by exceptional refining margins of $23.62/barrel on 2.95 million barrels/day throughput [1][2]. The constructive refining backdrop — constrained global capacity, lower light-product inventories, and robust crack spreads — supports continued strong profitability through 2026. Diamond Green Diesel and ethanol posted sharp profit gains, giving Valero three meaningful earnings engines. The balance sheet is conservatively managed with D/E of 48%, and the $4.80 annual dividend ($1.20/quarter, increased January 2026) is covered at just 30% of earnings [5][7]. Forward P/E of 16.94 with PEG of 0.48 represents compelling value for a diversified energy company with category-leading refining scale. Capital investments of $2B planned for 2026 include the St. Charles FCC optimization ($230M) starting Q3 2026.
The primary risk is the inherent cyclicality of refining economics. Crack spreads are volatile and driven by global supply-demand dynamics that management cannot control — the record Q2 2026 margins may not be sustainable if global refining capacity additions or demand weakness compress spreads. TTM ROE of 8.28% reflects the weak FY2025 environment (revenue down 6.5% YoY) and illustrates how quickly refining profitability can deteriorate [5][6]. TTM profit margin of 1.9% is structurally thin for refiners whose revenue includes pass-through crude costs, leaving minimal cushion during margin compression. Dividend yield of 1.59% is below the 2% threshold typically associated with income stocks, and the consecutive growth streak of only 4 years (reset by the 2020 COVID crisis) limits the Dividend sleeve's appeal. Energy transition risks remain, with potential regulatory tightening on fossil fuels, though Valero's renewable diesel investments partially hedge this exposure [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Record refining margins and constrained global capacity supporting strong profitability through 2026 — Q2 refining segment operating income of $4.47B with $23.62/barrel margin on 2.95M bpd throughput [1][2]
  2. St. Charles FCC Unit optimization ($230M project) beginning operations Q3 2026, adding higher-margin product yields to the refining portfolio [2]
  3. Three-engine earnings model: Refining, Renewable Diesel, and Ethanol all posting strong Q2 results, reducing dependence on any single commodity cycle and providing earnings diversification [1]
Key Risks
  1. Refining margin cyclicality — crack spreads are volatile and driven by global supply-demand dynamics beyond management control; the record Q2 2026 margins may normalize, compressing earnings materially as seen in the weak FY2025 period [5][6]
  2. TTM profit margin of 1.9% reflects the structural thinness of refining economics where revenue includes pass-through crude oil costs, leaving minimal cushion during margin compression periods
  3. Energy transition regulatory risk — potential tightening of emissions standards, carbon taxes, or accelerated EV adoption could structurally reduce demand for refined petroleum products over the medium to long term [4]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (8/9): Petroleum refining is a physical, capital-intensive industrial process that AI structurally cannot displace — refineries require massive physical infrastructure, specialized equipment, and complex chemical processing that must be operated and maintained by skilled workers [1]. Regulatory requirements (EPA, OSHA, state environmental agencies) add additional layers of protection. Global refining capacity is constrained, with multiple refinery closures in recent years tightening supply and supporting margins. AI actually increases demand for refined fuels indirectly: AI data centers consume enormous amounts of electricity, and the buildout of AI infrastructure drives demand for construction fuels, logistics, and power generation. Renewable diesel and ethanol are also physical manufacturing processes with regulatory mandates (RFS, LCFS) that AI cannot substitute. AI Opportunity (0/1): Valero does not generate revenue from AI-related products or services. AI may be used operationally for refinery optimization, trading, and supply chain management, but no AI-related revenue streams have been disclosed.
