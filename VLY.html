Company Profile
Financial Services ·Banks - Regional ·NASDAQ ·United States
Valley National Bancorp is the holding company for Valley National Bank, a relationship-focused regional bank with approximately $60B in assets serving commercial, small business, and consumer customers across New Jersey, New York, Florida, and Alabama. The bank reported Q2 2026 revenue of $560.7M (beating consensus of $557M) with EPS of $0.30, net interest income growing 12.2% YoY, and management raised full-year guidance for loan growth and net interest income [1][2]. Founded in 1927 and headquartered in Wayne, New Jersey, Valley has invested in digital transformation with 80%+ cloud migration and 50+ digital initiatives under COO Russell Barrett, recognized on American Banker's inaugural 2026 Most Innovative People in Finance list [3].

VLY at $12.66 with a $7.0B market cap is a well-positioned regional bank showing improving fundamentals at a discount valuation. Q2 2026 revenue of $560.7M beat consensus with net interest income growing 12.2% YoY, EPS grew 31.8% YoY, and management raised full-year guidance for loan growth and net interest income, signaling confidence in the earnings trajectory [1][2]. The forward P/E of 8.2 is below the regional bank peer median of approximately 10x, and the 3.48% dividend yield with a conservative 37.3% payout ratio provides attractive income with room for future dividend increases. Valley's technology investments — 80%+ cloud migration, voice AI agent, 50+ digital initiatives, and executive AI goals — position it ahead of many similarly-sized community banks in operational efficiency [3]. EPS growth of 31.8% and a PEG of 1.16 suggest the earnings recovery has further room to run.
The bear case centers on low returns on capital, dividend stagnation, and structural challenges facing regional banks. ROE of 8.95% is below the 15% cost-of-equity threshold for most bank investors, meaning Valley is destroying economic value at current returns despite accounting profitability [1]. The dividend has been flat at $0.44 annually since at least 2020 — six consecutive years without an increase — yielding a 0.0% 5-year dividend CAGR that signals either capital constraints or management unwillingness to reward shareholders [1]. Revenue CAGR of approximately 5.0% over five years includes the Leumi acquisition, implying organic growth in the low single digits. The bank's $60B in assets places it in the regulatory middle ground — too large to be a simple community bank, too small to achieve the scale advantages of money-center banks, and potentially subject to enhanced regulatory scrutiny under evolving capital rules. Regional banks face persistent deposit competition from higher-yielding alternatives, commercial real estate concentration risk, and fintech disruption of consumer banking that larger banks can better absorb [3].
