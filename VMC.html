Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Building Materials ·NYSE ·United States
Vulcan Materials Company is the nation's largest producer of construction aggregates — primarily crushed stone, sand, and gravel — and a major producer of aggregates-based construction materials including asphalt and ready-mixed concrete. The company operates approximately 400 active aggregates facilities across 17 states and Washington, D.C., serving highway, infrastructure, commercial, and residential construction markets. Following the 2021 acquisition of U.S. Concrete for $1.6B, Vulcan expanded into ready-mixed concrete operations. Founded in 1909 and headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama, the company reported 2025 revenue of $7.94B and reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $2.4-$2.6B.

Investment Thesis
Vulcan Materials offers a pure-play bet on U.S. infrastructure spending through its dominant position as the nation's largest aggregates producer. The company's approximately 400 facilities across 17 Sun Belt states create irreplaceable logistics moats — aggregates are heavy and expensive to transport, making local quarry proximity the ultimate competitive advantage [1]. With 2025 revenue of $7.94B, reaffirmed 2026 EBITDA guidance of $2.4-$2.6B, and pricing discipline that pushed Q2 2026 mix-adjusted aggregates prices to the upper end of the 4-6% range, Vulcan is executing well on its aggregates-first strategy [1]. EPS grew 18.4% in 2025 to $8.16, and analysts project $9.27 for 2026 — a further 15.9% increase. D/E of 58% is conservative for an asset-heavy business, and IIJA infrastructure spending provides a multi-year demand tailwind [5].
The bear case centers on premium valuation and cyclical exposure. At 30.1x forward earnings and PEG of 1.97, VMC is priced for sustained growth that Q2 2026's modest 3% revenue increase may not deliver [1][5]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of 10.6% is flattering due to the COVID-depressed 2020 base — recent annual growth of 7.1% and quarterly deceleration to 3% suggest normalization. ROE of 12.97% underperforms the 15% target, reflecting capital intensity inherent to quarry operations. While IIJA provides a demand floor, housing and commercial construction remain cyclically sensitive, and the 2008-2012 downturn saw aggregates volumes fall 35%. The 0.74% dividend yield offers minimal income cushion, and environmental permitting delays or community opposition to quarry expansions could constrain reserve replacement in high-growth markets [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. IIJA infrastructure spending acceleration through 2027-2028 providing multi-year demand floor for aggregates — each $1B in highway spending requires approximately 40,000 tons of aggregates, directly benefiting VMC's dominant Sun Belt positions [1]
  2. AI-driven data center construction boom creating incremental aggregates demand — each hyperscale facility consumes hundreds of thousands of tons of crushed stone for foundations, roads, and site preparation
  3. Continued aggregates pricing discipline with Q2 2026 mix-adjusted pricing exiting at the upper end of the 4-6% range, supporting margin expansion toward the $2.6B high end of EBITDA guidance [1]
Key Risks
  1. Premium valuation at 30.1x forward earnings and PEG of 1.97 leaves minimal margin of safety — any deceleration from Q2 2026's already modest 3% revenue growth could trigger meaningful multiple compression [5]
  2. Cyclical exposure to residential and commercial construction: while IIJA provides a public infrastructure floor, housing starts are sensitive to interest rates and could decline, pressuring volumes as they did 35% during 2008-2012
  3. Environmental permitting risk as local communities increasingly oppose new quarry development and expansions, potentially constraining long-term reserve replacement in VMC's highest-growth Sun Belt markets [4]
