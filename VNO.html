Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Office ·NYSE ·United States
Vornado Realty Trust is a fully integrated REIT with a premier New York City office, retail, and multifamily portfolio, anchored by the $2.5 billion PENN District redevelopment on Manhattan's West Side. The company also owns flagship properties in Chicago (THE MART) and San Francisco (555 California Street). Steven Roth has served as Chairman and CEO since 1980, and 95% of the office portfolio is LEED certified.

Investment Thesis
Vornado's bull case rests on the PENN District inflection point and opportunistic capital deployment — $200M+ of currently unrecognized PENN District rent is expected to convert into earnings by 2027, Manhattan leasing is showing double-digit mark-to-market rent growth (+11.7% GAAP), and management just acquired a 49% stake in a 99%-occupied building at 65-70% below replacement cost with an 8% GAAP yield [1]. FFO consensus of $2.35/share in 2026 rising to $2.69 in 2027 (+14.5%) signals a credible earnings acceleration, and Director Daniel Tisch's sustained open-market buying (215,000 shares, $5.6M+ invested in Q1 2026 alone) reflects real insider conviction from someone close to the assets [4].
The bear case centers on financial engineering scars and near-term earnings softness: Q1 2026 comparable FFO fell 17.5% YoY to $0.52/share on higher interest expense and a ground-rent accrual reversal, and forward P/E of 236.72 reflects how little the GAAP earnings line captures true REIT economics [5]. D/E of 125% remains elevated versus the 70% target, and the dividend was cut twice (2020 and 2023) from levels several multiples above today's $0.74/share, with the 5-year dividend growth CAGR still deeply negative even as the payment itself has held flat for two years running. Structural office-demand uncertainty from hybrid work, even in Vornado's premium-transit-adjacent niche, remains the multi-year swing factor for whether the PENN District and new acquisitions deliver the promised 2027 earnings inflection.
Key Catalysts
  1. PENN District lease-up progressing toward the 2027 earnings inflection, with $200M+ of currently unrecognized rent expected to convert into FFO growth as occupancy climbs
  2. Newly acquired 49% stake in a 99%-occupied office building at 65-70% below replacement cost (8% GAAP yield) adding ~$0.10/share on a full run-rate basis, demonstrating continued opportunistic capital deployment
  3. Sustained insider buying by Director Daniel Tisch ($5.6M+ across Q1 2026) signaling informed conviction as FFO consensus builds from $2.35/share (2026) to $2.69/share (2027)
Key Risks
  1. Q1 2026 comparable FFO declined 17.5% YoY to $0.52/share on higher interest expense, showing near-term earnings remain choppy ahead of the promised 2027 inflection
  2. Elevated leverage at 125% D/E limits balance sheet flexibility, and forward P/E of 236.72 underscores how disconnected GAAP earnings are from the underlying real estate economics investors must instead track via FFO
  3. Legacy of two dividend cuts (2020, 2023) leaves the 5-year dividend growth CAGR deeply negative; failure to resume real per-share dividend growth beyond the current flat $0.74 could continue to weigh on the income-oriented shareholder base
