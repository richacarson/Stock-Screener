Company Profile
Communication Services ·Telecom Services ·NASDAQ ·United Kingdom
Vodafone is a pan-European and African telecommunications group providing mobile, fixed broadband, and business digital services, with leading positions in Germany, the UK (following the merger with Three UK), Turkey, and — through Vodacom and now fully consolidated Safaricom — sub-Saharan Africa, including the M-Pesa mobile money platform. FY2026 (to March) revenue was about €40.5B, and Q1 FY27 revenue rose 9.7% to €10.3B with 5.2% organic service revenue growth and a 28.5% adjusted EBITDAaL margin. CEO Margherita Della Valle, a long-time Vodafone executive and former CFO, has run the company since 2023 and has exited Spain and Italy to simplify the portfolio. Market cap approximately $36.8B.

Investment Thesis
Vodafone's restructuring is starting to show up in growth. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 9.7% to €10.3B and organic service revenue grew 5.2% with every segment contributing, adjusted EBITDAaL grew 6.2% organically with margin up 0.6 points to 28.5%, and management raised FY27 guidance to €13.0-13.3B adjusted EBITDAaL and €2.6-2.9B adjusted free cash flow, now expecting the upper end [1]. Business service revenue growth accelerated to 5.0% on high double-digit growth in SaaS, IoT, and cloud and security, and Africa grew 12.6% organically, with Safaricom fully consolidated from July 1, 2026 [1]. At 9.5x forward earnings with a 3.4% yield and a Yahoo Finance PEG of 0.59, the shares price in little of that recovery [4].
The bear case is Germany and the balance sheet. Germany, the largest market, grew only 1.2% organically amid mobile competition, and the UK just 0.6% [1]. FY2026 was another statutory loss (-€0.40B net income after a €1.8B tax charge, per FMP), Yahoo Finance shows ROE of 0.1% and D/E near 100% on €54B of debt, and the payout ratio exceeds 100% of trailing earnings [4][5]. The dividend was roughly halved in 2025 and is far below 2021 levels (a derived -12.7% 5yr CAGR), and FY2026 revenue of €40.7B remains below FY2021's €43.8B after the Spain and Italy exits [4][5]. MSCI cut its ESG rating to A from AA, with labour management contributing almost 25% of the rating [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. H1 FY27 results in November 2026 confirming the raised €13.0-13.3B EBITDAaL guidance and upper-end free cash flow [1]
  2. Safaricom full consolidation from July 1, 2026 and continued double-digit Africa growth in connectivity and M-Pesa financial services [1]
  3. Three UK merger synergies and business digital services (SaaS, IoT, cloud and security, AI) moving business customers toward the CEO's medium-term goal of ~50% of group revenue [1][2]
Key Risks
  1. German mobile competition holding Germany organic growth to 1.2% and pressuring the group's largest earnings contributor [1]
  2. Leverage and weak statutory returns: ~€54B of debt, D/E ~100%, ROE ~0.1%, and a payout ratio above 100% of trailing EPS leave limited room for dividend growth [4]
  3. Execution risk on the UK merger integration and on monetizing AI and cybersecurity services in the enterprise market against hyperscalers and IT services firms [2]
