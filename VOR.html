Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Vor Biopharma is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company that pivoted in mid-2025 from engineered hematopoietic stem cell therapies for blood cancers to autoimmune diseases after winding down its original cell therapy operations and laying off 95% of its workforce. The company licensed global rights to telitacicept, a dual BAFF/APRIL inhibitor, from China-based Remegen and is now pursuing Phase 3 trials in generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) and Sjogren's disease. As of Q2 2026, VOR holds $466.1M in cash providing runway through early 2029.

Investment Thesis
Vor Bio at $18.06 with a $1.07B market cap holds $466M in cash providing runway through early 2029, fully funding Phase 3 readouts without near-term dilution risk [1][3]. The UPSTREAM MG Phase 3 trial completed enrollment in September 2026 with preliminary results expected H1 2027, while the Sjogren's disease Phase 3 trial initiated dosing in March 2026 [2]. Telitacicept's dual BAFF/APRIL mechanism has been clinically validated across 8+ autoimmune indications in China with 10,000+ patients treated commercially under five approved indications, providing meaningful de-risking. The stock's $1.07B market cap against $466M in cash implies the market values the telitacicept pipeline at approximately $600M — potentially a discount to intrinsic value if Phase 3 trials succeed in a combined >$2B addressable market.
The bear case is severe for a pre-revenue biotech executing a forced pivot. Vor Bio has zero revenue, negative stockholders' equity of -$164M, and is entirely dependent on the clinical and regulatory success of a single in-licensed asset [1][3]. Net loss of $62.8M in Q2 2026 reflects the burn rate required to fund global Phase 3 programs. Telitacicept faces formidable competition from established players including Novartis and Amgen in autoimmune indications, and China clinical data may not translate to FDA/EMA approval standards [3]. The near-total organizational reset — from 155 to 8 employees, rebuilt to approximately 60 under a new CEO — creates execution risk in running complex global Phase 3 programs. The stock has declined from $21.98 (50-day SMA) to $18.06, reflecting market uncertainty about the pivot's ultimate success. If Phase 3 trials miss endpoints, the remaining cash provides optionality but the company would face another existential crisis.
