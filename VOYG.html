Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
Voyager Technologies, Inc. is a defense technology and space solutions company operating through three segments: Defense & National Security (mission-critical solutions including next-generation interceptors and Golden Dome defense work), Space Solutions (in-space propulsion systems, the Bishop Airlock module on the ISS, and space science services), and Starlab Space Stations (commercial space station in partnership with Airbus, targeted for 2029 launch to replace ISS capabilities). The company also acquired Astrobotic for $300M to enhance lunar capabilities. Founded in 2019, headquartered in Denver, Colorado.

Investment Thesis
Voyager Technologies is positioned at the intersection of two secular growth trends — expanding U.S. defense spending (Golden Dome, next-gen interceptors) and the commercial space infrastructure buildout as the ISS approaches retirement by 2030 [1][4]. Q1 2026 delivered record backlog of $275.3M (up 54% YoY) with a 1.3 book-to-bill ratio, and management raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $230-255M, representing 38-53% growth. The Starlab program, partnered with Airbus and leveraging Palantir AI, is one of three NASA-selected commercial space station destinations. Defense & National Security revenue is accelerating on Golden Dome-related awards and Raytheon preproduction contracts, providing near-term revenue diversity beyond Starlab [1][2].
The bear case centers on capital intensity and path to profitability. Voyager posted a $44M Q1 net loss and $33.3M adjusted EBITDA loss, with consensus 2026 EPS of -$2.27 projecting continued losses as Starlab development costs mount [1][3]. Total debt of $491M against estimated equity of ~$236M (D/E ~208%) creates meaningful financial leverage risk. The Starlab program carries execution risk — a 2029 launch target with billions in remaining investment depends on continued NASA funding, which faces political uncertainty. The $300M Astrobotic acquisition increased cash burn and near-term capital needs. Morgan Stanley recently downgraded VOYG to Underweight, citing stretched valuation at $1.8B market cap for a company with $166M in 2025 revenue and sustained losses [3][5].
Key Catalysts
  1. Golden Dome defense program expansion driving accelerating bookings in Defense & National Security segment, with Q1 2026 bookings of $45M pushing backlog to record $275.3M
  2. Starlab commercial space station achieving additional development milestones ($24M in NASA cash receipts in Q1 2026) ahead of planned 2029 launch as ISS replacement
  3. Revenue guidance increase to $230-255M for 2026 (38-53% growth) with improving gross margins expected to reach mid-20s by Q4 2026, creating a path toward eventual profitability
Key Risks
  1. Sustained operating losses ($44M net loss in Q1 2026 alone) with D/E of ~208% and $491M total debt create meaningful balance sheet risk if defense or NASA funding is delayed or reduced
  2. Starlab program carries multi-billion dollar execution risk with a 2029 launch target that depends on continued NASA appropriations and technical milestones across a complex Airbus partnership
  3. Morgan Stanley downgrade to Underweight with $39 price target flags valuation concern at $1.8B market cap for a company generating only $166M in 2025 revenue with no clear profitability timeline
