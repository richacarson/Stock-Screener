Company Profile
Industrials ·Consulting Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Verisk Analytics, Inc. provides data analytics and technology solutions to the insurance industry in the United States and internationally. The company offers underwriting solutions, catastrophe modeling, claims analytics, anti-fraud detection, life insurance solutions, and specialty business management services. Founded in 1971 and headquartered in Jersey City, New Jersey, Verisk serves as the insurance industry's essential data backbone with offices in 30+ countries. Market cap ~$25.5B.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 7 /10
Verisk demonstrates strong infinite game characteristics in several dimensions. The 2022 Wood Mackenzie divestiture for $3.1B to focus purely on insurance analytics was a courageous strategic decision prioritizing long-term clarity over diversified revenue. The 50+ year history as the insurance industry's data backbone, Great Place to Work certification across five countries, and orderly CEO succession reflect organizational durability. However, the $1.5B accelerated share repurchase program and aggressive buyback-driven capital return strategy signal optimization over long-term investment, and mid-single-digit organic growth suggests a steady compounder rather than a transformative mission-driven enterprise.

Investment Thesis
Verisk Analytics is a high-quality, near-monopoly data analytics franchise embedded in the US P&C insurance industry's core workflows, trading at 24.2x forward P/E on FY2026 guidance of $3.19-$3.24B revenue [1]. Q1 2026 delivered $782.6M revenue with subscription revenues growing 7% and EBITDA margin of 55.7%, demonstrating the mission-critical nature of Verisk's data solutions even during a 'trough' period of low weather activity [1]. The bull case centers on Verisk's irreplaceable proprietary data moat — 50+ years of forms, rules, loss costs, and claims databases that insurers cannot replicate — creating exceptional pricing power reflected in 29.6% profit margins and $1.0B+ annual free cash flow [1][4]. The $1.5B accelerated share repurchase program signals management confidence and drives robust EPS growth. AI-powered underwriting and claims products, including AI-driven insurance fraud detection, position Verisk to capture incremental value as insurers automate workflows [2]. Inspire Impact Score of 58 (ALIGNED) with 17 positive ESG attributions and SBTi-validated emissions targets make it attractive for faith-based portfolios [3].
The bear case centers on valuation expansion and decelerating growth. At 28.5x trailing P/E and 24.2x forward (up from 20.7x in early 2026), Verisk is priced as a premium compounder, but Q1 2026 revenue growth of 3.9% and full-year guidance implying ~4.7% growth suggest limited upside acceleration from mid-single-digit organic rates [1][4]. Management attributed Q1 softness to a 'trough' from low weather activity, tough comparisons, and a federal government contract work stoppage, but these secular headwinds could persist. The D/E of 1,582% — driven by the $1.5B ASR and aggressive buybacks that have nearly eliminated equity — creates balance sheet fragility if growth slows or credit conditions tighten, despite strong cash flow [4]. While AI enhances Verisk's analytics capabilities, it also enables insurtechs and alternative data providers to build competing solutions that could erode data exclusivity over time, and FTC scrutiny of the terminated AccuLynx acquisition signals regulatory limits to growth-by-M&A strategies [1]. The insurance industry's inherent conservatism limits Verisk's ability to accelerate growth through new product adoption.
Key Catalysts
  1. FY2026 guidance of $3.19-$3.24B revenue with subscription revenue growing 7%, reflecting pricing power and expanded analytics adoption that should reaccelerate as weather-related headwinds normalize [1]
  2. AI-powered underwriting automation, fraud detection (including AI-generated image manipulation), and claims analytics embedded in customer workflows through Life Solutions FAST platform, driving incremental revenue per customer [2]
  3. $1.5B accelerated share repurchase plus ongoing open-market buybacks drive robust EPS growth beyond the organic revenue trajectory, with diluted EPS up 5% YoY in Q1 2026 despite modest revenue growth [1]
Key Risks
  1. Extremely leveraged balance sheet with D/E of 1,582% from aggressive buybacks — the $1.5B ASR program further reduces equity, creating fragility if organic growth decelerates below mid-single digits or credit conditions tighten [4]
  2. Q1 2026 revenue growth of 3.9% and full-year guidance implying ~4.7% growth suggest deceleration from prior 6.6% trajectory; management's 'trough' attribution may mask structural growth challenges rather than temporary headwinds [1]
  3. AI-enabled insurtechs and alternative data providers could build competing analytics layers on non-Verisk data, while regulatory trends toward open data sharing and FTC scrutiny of acquisitions limit both organic and inorganic growth options [1]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Verisk's core moat — 50+ years of proprietary insurance industry data including forms, rules, loss costs, and claims databases — is structurally protected from AI disruption because AI tools require the very data Verisk aggregates [1]. Insurers cannot replicate this data independently, and switching costs are extremely high as underwriting and claims systems are built on Verisk's infrastructure. Analysts have debated AI disruption concerns but express confidence in Verisk's proprietary data strength [2]. However, AI creates a double-edged risk: large language models could eventually reduce dependence on traditional forms and rules by automating policy language generation, and AI enables insurtechs to build competing analytics layers on alternative data sources. CMS and regulatory trends toward open data sharing could erode data exclusivity. AI Opportunity (1/1): Verisk generates meaningful AI-related revenue through AI-powered underwriting automation, fraud detection algorithms tackling AI-generated image manipulation, and ML-enhanced catastrophe models embedded in subscription fees [2].
