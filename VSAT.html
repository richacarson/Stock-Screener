Company Profile
Technology ·Communication Equipment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Viasat, Inc. is a global communications company providing satellite-based broadband connectivity, defense communications, and aviation internet services. Following its $7.3B acquisition of Inmarsat in 2023, Viasat operates a multi-orbit satellite fleet serving commercial airlines, maritime, government/defense, and consumer broadband markets. The ViaSat-3 constellation is entering service to provide next-generation high-capacity satellite bandwidth.

Investment Thesis
Viasat is at an inflection point: FY2026 revenue reached $4.64B with record $1.55B adjusted EBITDA, the net loss narrowed dramatically to $34.1M (from hundreds of millions in prior years), and the company generated $60M positive free cash flow in Q1 with guidance for $180M in FY2027 [1]. The ViaSat-3 constellation entering service in August/September 2026 provides a step-function increase in bandwidth capacity that should drive revenue acceleration and margin expansion. Aviation revenue grew 14% YoY on 9% more aircraft in service, and Defense & Advanced Technology revenue jumped 15% to $344M [1]. Backlog of $4.07B provides visibility, and the stock has rallied 280% in the past 52 weeks as the market prices in the improving trajectory. Oppenheimer sees $140 upside, suggesting significant analyst conviction in the turnaround thesis [3].
The bear case is substantial: Viasat carries $7.08B in debt against $1.23B cash, creating -$5.85B net debt that translates to D/E of 155% -- more than double the IOWN target of 70% [3]. ROE is deeply negative at -10.17%, and profit margin of -11.4% (TTM) means the company has not yet proven it can generate sustainable profits from its massive satellite infrastructure investment. SpaceX's Starlink is rapidly expanding into aviation and maritime markets -- Viasat's most attractive growth segments -- with lower latency and increasingly competitive pricing backed by SpaceX's dramatically lower launch costs. Organic revenue growth of just 2% YoY (Q4 FY2026) is tepid for a company valued at $4.7B with massive debt loads. ViaSat-3 has experienced delays and the first satellite suffered partial power system issues, creating execution risk for the August/September 2026 service entry that is critical to the investment thesis. Insider selling ($11M net in 12 months) suggests management is not backing the turnaround with personal capital.
Key Catalysts
  1. ViaSat-3 service entry in August/September 2026 providing a step-function increase in bandwidth capacity, driving revenue acceleration in high-margin commercial aviation and consumer broadband segments [1]
  2. Defense & Advanced Technology segment growing 15% YoY with $4.07B backlog conversion providing steady government contract revenue with high switching costs and security clearance requirements
  3. Path to sustained positive free cash flow ($180M guided FY2027) and eventual profitability as Inmarsat integration synergies fully materialize and ViaSat-3 capacity ramps
Key Risks
  1. SpaceX Starlink's aggressive expansion into aviation, maritime, and consumer broadband markets with lower latency LEO technology and SpaceX's dramatically lower launch costs threatens Viasat's core growth segments
  2. ViaSat-3 execution risk -- the first satellite suffered partial power system issues and service entry has been delayed, creating uncertainty about whether August/September 2026 timeline will hold and whether capacity will meet expectations
  3. Debt burden of $7.08B (D/E 155%) creates significant financial fragility -- any revenue disappointment or ViaSat-3 delay could strain the balance sheet and force dilutive capital raises or asset sales

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Viasat's core business -- operating physical satellites in orbit, ground station infrastructure, and defense communications systems -- is structurally immune to AI disruption. Satellite manufacturing, launch, and orbital operations require massive capital investment, specialized engineering, regulatory licensing (spectrum rights), and physical infrastructure that AI cannot substitute. The defense communications segment ($344M Q1 revenue, +15% YoY) operates under classified government contracts with security clearances and decades-long procurement cycles [1]. Growing global demand for broadband connectivity -- driven in part by AI applications requiring ubiquitous internet access -- creates a secular tailwind for satellite operators. Viasat's multi-orbit constellation serves markets (aviation, maritime, remote areas) where terrestrial alternatives are physically impossible. The primary competitive threat is from SpaceX's Starlink, not AI substitution. AI Opportunity (0/1): No evidence of meaningful AI-driven revenue or AI products. Viasat may use AI for network optimization internally but has not reported quantifiable AI revenue contributions.
