Company Profile
Consumer Discretionary ·Apparel Retail ·NYSE ·United States
Victoria's Secret & Co. is a specialty retailer of women's intimate apparel, PINK, and Adore Me brands, operating approximately 850 stores in the US, Canada, and China plus ~580 international franchise/license locations. The company changed its NYSE ticker from VSCO to VSXY on June 2, 2026 to signal its brand turnaround under CEO Hillary Super's 'Path to Potential' strategy [1][5]. Q2 2026 net sales rose 10% YoY to $1.61 billion and the company raised full-year 2026 net sales guidance to $7.03-7.13 billion (from $6.85-6.95 billion), up from $6.553 billion in FY2025, alongside a sharp upward revision to adjusted operating income guidance ($550-580M vs. prior $430-460M) [1].

Investment Thesis
Victoria's Secret's turnaround under CEO Hillary Super is delivering measurable results — Q1 2026 net sales grew 15% and Q2 2026 grew 10% YoY, prompting management to raise full-year 2026 net sales guidance to $7.03-7.13B and adjusted operating income guidance to $550-580M, up sharply from prior guidance of $430-460M [1]. Forward P/E of 14.65 and a Forward PEG of just 0.47 suggest the market has not fully priced in the earnings reacceleration, and ROE of 46.91% reflects strong capital efficiency off a lean equity base. The brand repositioning — ending the 'Angels'/Fashion Show model, broadening inclusivity, and deploying AI-powered shopping tools via Google Cloud — is a credible, evidence-backed strategic pivot rather than a cosmetic rebrand, reinforced by the symbolic June 2026 ticker change to VSXY.
The bear case centers on balance sheet leverage and thin margins: Debt-to-Equity of 276% is close to the scoring floor and reflects debt inherited from the 2021 L Brands spin-off, while Profit Margin of just 5.47% leaves little room for error against ~$160M of incremental 2026 tariff costs [1]. Q3 2026 revenue growth decelerated to 9.3% YoY with a continued net loss per share (-$0.46, albeit improved from -$0.71), showing the turnaround is not yet linear. The stock's +187% 52-week run has already priced in substantial optimism, so any stumble in holiday-quarter execution or renewed tariff escalation could compress the multiple quickly.
