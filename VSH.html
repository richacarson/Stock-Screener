Company Profile
Technology ·Electronic Components ·NYSE ·United States
Vishay Intertechnology, headquartered in Malvern, Pennsylvania, manufactures one of the world's largest portfolios of discrete semiconductors and passive electronic components (resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, MOSFETs) serving automotive, industrial, computing, consumer, telecom, military/aerospace, and medical markets [1][6]. Q2 2026 adjusted revenue reached a record $919 million, up 20.5% YoY, with a record book-to-bill ratio of 1.32 and backlog growing 18% to $1.9 billion, driven by broad-based demand including industrial (+30.1% YoY), aerospace/defense, and rapidly accelerating AI data center power applications [1][6].

Investment Thesis
Vishay is a direct beneficiary of the AI infrastructure buildout without carrying AI valuation risk — Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $919 million (+20.5% YoY), the book-to-bill ratio hit a record 1.32, and backlog grew 18% to $1.9 billion (6.1 months of coverage), with management quantifying AI data center exposure moving from under $100 million to 'well above that' and growing north of 20% [1][6]. As a passive/discrete component manufacturer, Vishay's products (MOSFETs, capacitors, inductors, resistors) are structurally immune to AI substitution while directly benefiting from rising AI power-management content per data center rack. Gross margin expansion to 24% arrived a full quarter ahead of schedule, and a Forward P/E of 22.49 is reasonable against 20%+ forward revenue growth expectations from sell-side analysts.
The bear case centers on weak current profitability and organizational quality: Return on Equity of just 1.13% and Profit Margin of 0.86% reflect a business only recently turning consistently profitable, compounded by elevated effective tax rates (33.7% in Q2, guided 35-40% in Q3) that are compressing bottom-line conversion of the strong top-line growth [1]. Full-year 2026 free cash flow is guided negative due to heavy capacity-expansion capex, and the dividend's 192.78% payout ratio is unsustainable against current earnings, creating a risk of a dividend cut if the AI demand cycle slows before capacity investments pay off. A 'Bottom 5%' Comparably culture rating and a finite-game strategic posture (weighted Infinite Game score of just 3/10) suggest limited organizational differentiation beyond the current cyclical AI tailwind, and a downturn in the broader semiconductor cycle — as seen in the 0.93 book-to-bill in Q3 2025 — could quickly reverse the current backlog momentum.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued acceleration of AI data center power semiconductor demand, with management guiding the AI-exposed segment to grow north of 20% as total exposure moves well above the prior ~$100 million base [6]
  2. Further gross margin expansion beyond the 24% Q3 2026 target as the higher-margin AI power product mix (MOSFETs, inductors, optocouplers) grows as a share of revenue [1][6]
  3. Capacity expansion investments ($300-350M capex) coming online to convert the record $1.9 billion backlog into recognized revenue and improved free cash flow in 2027
Key Risks
  1. A cyclical semiconductor demand downturn — as seen in the 0.93 book-to-bill ratio in Q3 2025 — could quickly reverse the current record backlog and book-to-bill trend, given the industrial and AI end markets remain order-driven and capacity-sensitive [6]
  2. Payout ratio of 192.78% on the current dividend is unsustainable against Return on Equity of 1.13% and Profit Margin of 0.86%, creating dividend-cut risk if profitability does not improve alongside revenue growth
  3. Elevated and volatile effective tax rates (33.7% in Q2, guided 35-40% in Q3) tied to US taxation of foreign earnings are compressing net income conversion of strong revenue growth, and negative full-year 2026 free cash flow reduces balance sheet flexibility during the capacity build-out [1]
