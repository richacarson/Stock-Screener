Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Versant Media Group is an independent media company spun off from Comcast's NBCUniversal and founded January 2, 2026, owning cable networks including USA Network and CNBC alongside digital platforms such as Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes, GolfNow, and GolfPass. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.644B (-3.8% YoY) as linear distribution fell 6.3% on subscriber losses, while adjusted EBITDA of $624M (~38% margin) and $350M of quarterly free cash flow fund a $0.375 quarterly dividend and accelerated buybacks. CEO Mark Lazarus, former chairman of NBCUniversal Media Group, is steering the company away from roughly 80% cable dependence toward digital and sports businesses. Market cap approximately $4.4B.

Investment Thesis
Versant is a cash machine priced for liquidation. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $624M on $1.644B of revenue (~38% margin), six-month free cash flow reached $908M, and management raised full-year guidance to $6.2-6.45B revenue and $1.9-2.05B adjusted EBITDA while holding $1.0-1.2B of free cash flow [1]. At $31.42 the stock trades at 4.85x forward earnings with a 4.8% dividend yield and a Yahoo Finance PEG of 0.64, and the company is shrinking its share count through back-to-back $100M accelerated repurchases with ~$800M of authorization remaining [1][4]. Platforms revenue grew 9.3% excluding the divested SportsEngine, led by Fandango and GolfNow, and directors bought roughly $6M of stock in March 2026, led by David Novak's $5.27M purchase [1][5].
The bear case is secular decline. Linear distribution — $954M, or 58% of Q2 revenue — fell 6.3% on subscriber losses, total revenue fell 3.8%, adjusted EBITDA fell 8.9%, and diluted EPS dropped to $1.49 from $2.09 [1]. Yahoo Finance shows earnings down 28.7% YoY, and the business was built around cable TV, 'an industry in obvious decline,' leaving management racing to build new businesses with cash flow from a shrinking core [2][4]. Net debt is about $1.5B ($2.95B debt vs. $1.48B cash), Lazarus is eligible for a $35M first-year pay package, and the shares have fallen from ~$41 in August to $31, near the bottom of a $27-59 52-week range [1][3][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results and delivery of raised 2026 guidance ($6.2-6.45B revenue, $1.9-2.05B adjusted EBITDA, $1.0-1.2B free cash flow) [1]
  2. Continued capital returns: a second $100M ASR in Q3 2026, ~$800M remaining authorization, and a $1.50 annualized dividend at a 4.8% yield [1][4]
  3. Digital and sports expansion — Fandango's ad-supported streaming launch, the Full Swing golf acquisition, GolfPass growth, and a five-year Bundesliga rights deal with 300+ matches per year [1]
Key Risks
  1. Accelerating cord-cutting: linear distribution revenue fell 6.3% in Q2 2026 and is 58% of revenue, so any acceleration in subscriber losses overwhelms platform growth [1]
  2. Advertising and affiliate renewal risk as a standalone cable network group without Comcast's scale in negotiations with distributors [2]
  3. Capital-allocation and governance risk: acquisitions to diversify away from cable, plus a $35M first-year CEO pay package, could erode the cash returns that underpin the valuation [1][3]
