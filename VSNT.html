Company Profile
Communication Services ·Broadcasting & Cable TV ·NASDAQ ·United States
Versant Media Group is an independent media company spun off from Comcast on January 2, 2026, operating a portfolio of cable television networks including USA Network, Bravo, CNBC, MS NOW (formerly MSNBC), E!, Syfy, Oxygen, and Golf Channel, along with digital platforms Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes, GolfNow, GolfPass, and SportsEngine. In April 2026, Versant acquired StockStory, an AI-powered financial insights platform, to support CNBC's digital subscription growth strategy. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.644B with EPS of $1.49 (beat estimates); full-year 2026 guidance raised to $6.2-6.45B revenue and $1.9-2.05B adjusted EBITDA.

Investment Thesis
Versant Media trades at a deeply discounted 5.71x forward P/E with a 3.65% dividend yield while demonstrating improving fundamentals — Q2 2026 EPS of $1.49 beat the $1.38 estimate, adjusted EBITDA of $624M maintained 30%+ margins, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $6.2-6.45B revenue and $1.9-2.05B EBITDA [3]. The StockStory acquisition and CNBC subscription service launch signal a credible digital transformation strategy beyond managing linear TV decline, with Platforms revenue growing 9.5% to $192M and advertising decline narrowing from -5% in Q1 to -0.6% in Q2 [1][3]. At $41.06 with approximately $1.86B annual FCF, the stock offers a ~32% free cash flow yield. Significant insider buying ($5.27M by Director Novak, $530K by Director Potter) signals management conviction in the post-spinoff transformation [4].
The structural bear case remains powerful: linear TV distribution revenue (approximately 60% of total) is in secular decline as cord-cutting accelerates — Q2 advertising was still down 0.6% and total revenue declined 3.7% YoY despite improving trends [3]. Versant was deliberately created as a spinoff of Comcast's declining assets while Comcast retained the growing Peacock streaming platform, signaling that even the parent company viewed these properties as ex-growth. The CNBC subscription service and StockStory integration are early-stage initiatives that must prove they can generate meaningful revenue in competition with established fintech platforms (Bloomberg, Robinhood, Yahoo Finance). CEO Lazarus's background is in sports media management, not digital platform building, raising execution risk on the technology transformation. The stock's 52-week range of $27-$59 with current price at $41 reflects ongoing uncertainty about whether the low multiple is a value opportunity or a value trap. The 0% Inspire Impact Score (MIXED) reflects the company's limited ESG track record as a newly independent entity.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Versant's cable TV networks face moderate AI exposure — live sports (Golf Channel) and breaking news programming (CNBC, MS NOW) are structurally protected from AI substitution, as audiences demand real-time, human-anchored coverage that AI cannot replicate [3]. However, scripted and unscripted entertainment content (Bravo, USA, Syfy, E!) faces potential disruption as AI tools reduce production costs for competitors. CNBC's financial news faces particular exposure since financial data analysis is a core AI competency — AI can generate earnings summaries, market commentary, and stock analysis at scale [1]. Advertising revenue could face compression as AI-powered programmatic platforms reduce the premium cable TV commands. AI Opportunity (1/1): The StockStory acquisition is an AI-powered product delivering financial analysis, stock recommendations, and market insights to customers, reflecting measurable AI revenue generation — Versant is monetizing AI directly through CNBC's digital platform expansion [1].
