Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Independent Power Producers ·NYSE ·United States
Vistra, based in Irving, Texas, is an integrated retail electricity and power generation company. It serves about 5 million retail customers (TXU Energy and other brands) and runs roughly 44,000 MW of natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar and battery capacity across ERCOT, PJM and other markets. The 2024 Energy Harbor acquisition made it the second-largest US nuclear fleet operator. It has since signed a 20-year, up-to-1,200 MW Comanche Peak power deal with AWS and, in January 2026, 20-year PPAs with Meta for more than 2,600 MW from its PJM nuclear plants. 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance is $6.8-7.6B, and the Cogentrix acquisition is pending. CEO Jim Burke has led the company since 2022.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 5 /10
Vistra has made long-horizon bets that look infinite on the surface. It extended Comanche Peak to 2053, signed 20-year nuclear contracts and restarted idle units to keep the grid reliable. Its identity, though, rests on capital allocation, with heavy buybacks and serial acquisitions, rather than a Just Cause that would survive a change of CEO. Sinek would admire the nuclear commitment but would question how much of the strategy is aimed at per-share metrics.

Existential Flexibility
10
Existential Flexibility (2/2): Vistra has fundamentally reinvented its asset base. A coal-heavy, Texas-centric generator that emerged from the Energy Future Holdings bankruptcy is now the second-largest US nuclear operator, selling 20-year carbon-free power to hyperscalers. Getting there meant retiring coal plants in Illinois and Ohio and entering an entirely new contracting model [3][7]. That is a transformation, not an incremental adjustment.
Courage to Lead
6
Courage to Lead (1/2): Committing Comanche Peak to operate through 2053 and pursuing nuclear uprates backed by a DOE loan of up to $4.2B are decades-long bets [3][5]. Restarting Martin Lake Unit 1 also chose reliability over short-term cost [1]. Against that, about 30% of the share count has been retired since 2021, partly with debt that pushed D/E to 373%, a pattern Sinek would read as optimizing per-share results [2].

Investment Thesis
Vistra is one of the most direct ways to own firm power for the AI build-out. Q2 2026 ongoing-operations adjusted EBITDA was $1.767B, up more than 30%, driven by Generation EBITDA of about $994M versus $593M a year earlier. Management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $6.8-7.6B and adjusted FCF before growth of $3.925-4.725B, and expects results at or above the midpoint [1]. The 2027 opportunity range of $7.4-7.8B excludes upside from Cogentrix and the Meta PPAs [1]. About 3.8 GW of nuclear output is contracted for 20 years with AWS and Meta, with up to 3.2 GW more under discussion [3]. At $138.37 the stock trades at 14.5x forward earnings with a 0.39 PEG and 43% ROE, near its $132.66 52-week low [2]. CEO Burke bought about $1.17M of stock in August-September 2026 [8].
Leverage and market exposure are the main risks. D/E is 373% with $20.5B of debt against $435M of cash, which leaves less room if power prices or capacity auctions weaken [2]. GAAP results are volatile: Q2 net income of $305M included a $472M unrealized hedge loss, and 2025 net income fell to $944M from $2.66B in 2024 [1][6]. The stock fell from about $165 in early October to $138, and several executives and directors sold at $150-170 [8]. Coal units at Martin Lake and Oak Grove face environmental rules, and ESG raters score Vistra's risk exposure as high [7]. Regulatory pushback on co-locating data centers at nuclear plants could slow contracting.
Key Catalysts
  1. Closing of the Cogentrix acquisition, followed by updated guidance that adds Cogentrix and Meta PPA contributions on top of the 2027 adjusted EBITDA opportunity range of $7.4-7.8B [1]
  2. Contracting the remaining up to 3.2 GW of nuclear potential at Beaver Valley and Comanche Peak with hyperscalers under long-term PPAs [3]
  3. Execution of the Perry, Davis-Besse and Beaver Valley uprates supported by a DOE conditional loan commitment of up to $4.2B, plus Helix data-center platform milestones [4][5]
Key Risks
  1. Balance sheet leverage of 373% D/E ($20.5B debt) magnifies exposure to lower ERCOT and PJM power prices or capacity auction results [2]
  2. Regulatory and political risk around behind-the-meter data center co-location at nuclear plants and around PJM capacity market reforms, either of which could reduce contract economics [3]
  3. Environmental and operational risk from the remaining coal fleet (Martin Lake, Oak Grove) and battery storage facilities, under tightening federal and state rules [7]
