Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities - Independent Power Producers ·NYSE ·United States
Vistra Corp. is an integrated retail electricity and power generation company operating approximately 44,000 MW of generation capacity across natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar, and battery storage facilities. The company serves approximately 5 million retail customers and owns the second-largest competitive nuclear fleet in the United States, including the Comanche Peak Nuclear Plant in Texas and PJM nuclear facilities acquired through Energy Harbor. Vistra achieved investment grade credit ratings at two major agencies in 2026.

Investment Thesis
Vistra delivered a breakout Q1 2026 with operating revenues of $5.64B (up 43.5% YoY) and net income of $1.029B versus a loss of $268M a year earlier, driven by higher realized capacity prices and full-period Lotus acquisition contributions [1]. The company reaffirmed 2026 EBITDA guidance of $6.8-7.6B and adjusted FCFbG of $3.925-4.725B, was upgraded to investment grade by a second major credit agency, and trades at a Forward P/E of 17.45 with a PEG of 0.38 — making it one of the most attractively valued AI-adjacent growth stories in the market [1][6]. The 20-year nuclear PPAs with Meta (2,600+ MW) and Amazon provide exceptional revenue visibility, and Vistra's 6.4 GW nuclear fleet with license extensions into the 2050s positions it as a durable beneficiary of the structural increase in U.S. electricity demand driven by AI data center buildout. ROE of 42.9%, profit margin of 11.52% (up from 5.3%), and ~30% share reduction through buybacks demonstrate improving capital efficiency and shareholder alignment.
The bear case centers on leverage, environmental liabilities, and insider selling. D/E of 355% is elevated even after improvement, reflecting $19.93B in total debt against only $658M in cash, which leaves limited financial flexibility if power prices decline or interest rates remain elevated [6]. CEO Burke has been a consistent net seller of VST shares, with approximately $60M+ in stock sales during 2025-2026, suggesting the CEO may view current valuations as extended despite the compelling growth narrative [7]. The Moss Landing battery storage facility fires in 2024 and 2025 caused community evacuations, environmental contamination with heavy metals and asbestos, and triggered an EPA federal response action — creating ongoing regulatory and litigation exposure [4]. Martin Lake coal plant remains one of the nation's top sulfur dioxide and mercury polluters, carrying forced-retirement risk from tightening EPA regulations [5]. Nuclear operational risk persists — an unplanned outage at Comanche Peak or PJM facilities during contracted PPA periods would create material financial penalties and reputational damage.
