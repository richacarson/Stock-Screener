Company Profile
Industrials ·Rental & Leasing Services ·NYSE ·United States
Vestis Corporation provides uniform rentals and workplace supplies in the United States and Canada, spun off from Aramark in October 2023. The company offers uniform rental programs, managed restroom supplies, first-aid supplies, floor mats, towels, and linens to manufacturing, hospitality, retail, food service, automotive, and healthcare customers. Vestis is executing a business transformation under CEO Jim Barber, appointed June 2025, following a period of operational dysfunction and a securities fraud lawsuit against prior leadership.

Investment Thesis
Vestis has shown its first credible signs of a genuine turnaround: Q2 FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA grew year-over-year for the first time in more than two years ($74.5M vs. $47.6M), management raised full-year guidance to $295-325M Adjusted EBITDA and $120-150M free cash flow (up 145% at the midpoint), and the stock jumped 22% on the news [1]. Activist director Keith Meister's Corvex-affiliated funds backed the turnaround with real capital, buying over 1 million additional shares around $6.80 in December 2025 — a price roughly 60% below today's $16.50 — reflecting informed conviction well ahead of the market's re-rating [3]. The dividend suspension freed up cash for deleveraging and the transformation plan, and CEO Jim Barber's disciplined, metrics-driven approach (plant productivity, complaint reduction, on-time delivery) is now producing measurable results.
The bear case remains significant despite the improving trend. Revenue is still roughly flat to slightly down (TTM -0.8%), meaning the EBITDA improvement is entirely margin-driven rather than growth-driven, and profit margin (TTM -0.63%) and ROE (-1.97%) remain negative [4]. D/E of 153% keeps the balance sheet fragile, and forward P/E of 31.69 with a PEG of 1.69 already price in a substantial continuation of the recovery, leaving limited room for execution missteps. An unresolved federal securities class action alleging former leadership concealed systemic customer service failures 'costing millions weekly' remains in early procedural stages with no lead plaintiff yet appointed, creating ongoing legal and reputational overhang [3]. Employee culture, as reflected in Glassdoor reviews, has not yet recovered from the 2025 mass layoff and leadership crisis, and Vestis is still competing from a position of weakness against category leader Cintas.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued Adjusted EBITDA margin recovery toward the raised FY2026 guidance of $295-325M, building on Q2's first year-over-year EBITDA growth in over two years
  2. Free cash flow guidance raised to $120-150M (+145% at the midpoint), providing capital for deleveraging the elevated 153% D/E balance sheet now that the dividend is suspended
  3. Sustained large-scale insider buying by activist director Keith Meister/Corvex, whose December 2025 purchases at ~$6.80 signal continued board-level conviction in the turnaround
Key Risks
  1. Revenue remains roughly flat to down (TTM -0.8%), meaning the entire earnings recovery narrative depends on margin execution rather than underlying demand growth — a structurally shrinking or stagnant top line limits how far cost discipline alone can carry the stock
  2. Unresolved federal securities class action alleging former CEO and CFO concealed systemic service failures from investors remains in early stages with no settlement, creating ongoing legal liability and potential distraction for current management
  3. Elevated valuation after the recent rally (forward P/E 31.69, PEG 1.69) combined with 153% D/E and still-negative ROE and profit margin leaves little margin for error if the transformation plan's momentum stalls
