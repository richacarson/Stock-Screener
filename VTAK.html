Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Devices ·NYSE American ·United States
Catheter Precision designs, manufactures, and sells medical technologies for cardiac electrophysiology, including the VIVO (View Into Ventricular Onset) non-invasive 3D cardiac mapping system and the LockeT suture retention device, both FDA-cleared and CE-marked and sold in 15 countries. In late 2025 the company acquired Flyte, a regional air-mobility operator flying Cirrus Vision Jets, and in early 2026 announced plans to sell its core cardiac electrophysiology business to focus on aviation, while disclosing substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern.

Investment Thesis
The bull case rests on VIVO and LockeT's genuine clinical validation — FDA clearance, CE Mark, deployment across 15 countries, and demonstrated diagnostic accuracy in ventricular tachyarrhythmia cases — combined with Q1 2026 revenue growth of 200% YoY to $432K, driven by both the legacy medical device segment and the newly launched aviation platform [1]. Management believes the cardiac EP portfolio may be worth more to a larger, better-capitalized acquirer than reflected in VTAK's own market capitalization, and a sale of that unit could provide cash runway for the Flyte aviation pivot [1][2].
The bear case is overwhelming. The company explicitly disclosed substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern, with just $0.4M in cash against an $18.5M working-capital deficit at Q1 2026, and a net loss of $17.2M in FY2025 following a $16.6M loss in FY2024 [1][3]. Two prior reverse stock splits (1-for-10 in 2024, 1-for-19 in August 2025) and shareholder authorization for a further split of up to 1-for-100 signal a company structurally unable to maintain its share price organically, while preferred stock structures convertible into hundreds of millions of shares create severe dilution overhang [3]. The pivot from cardiac electrophysiology — the company's core competency for nearly two decades — into regional aviation via the Flyte acquisition has no strategic coherence and reads as a search for any viable revenue source rather than a considered long-term strategy [1][2].
Key Catalysts
  1. A sale of the cardiac electrophysiology business (VIVO, LockeT) to a larger, better-capitalized medtech acquirer could generate cash to fund the Flyte aviation platform and reduce going-concern risk [1][2]
  2. Continued international expansion of VIVO across its 15-country footprint could support medical device revenue ahead of any divestiture [1]
  3. Successful scaling of the Flyte regional air mobility platform, if it can access sufficient capital, would represent a genuine (if unrelated) new revenue base [2]
Key Risks
  1. Going-concern doubt is explicit in company filings, with only $0.4M cash against an $18.5M working-capital deficit — any disruption to financing access could force insolvency or a distressed sale [1][3]
  2. Severe, structural dilution risk from convertible preferred stock structures (Series B, C, D, J) potentially issuable into hundreds of millions of shares, compounding the effect of repeated reverse stock splits [3]
  3. Strategic incoherence from the simultaneous divestiture of the core cardiac EP business and pivot into an unrelated regional aviation business raises fundamental doubts about management's capital allocation discipline [1][2]
