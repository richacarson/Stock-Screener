Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - Specialty & Generic ·NASDAQ ·United States
Viatris Inc. is a global pharmaceutical company formed in November 2020 from the combination of Mylan N.V. and Pfizer's Upjohn division. The company develops, manufactures, and distributes a broad portfolio of generic, branded generic, complex generic, and biosimilar medicines across more than 165 countries. Q2 2026 delivered $3.8B revenue (+5% YoY) with raised FY2026 guidance to $14.75B revenue, $4.40B adjusted EBITDA, and $2.52 adjusted EPS. CEO Scott Smith leads ongoing Phase 2 strategic plan targeting 5-6% revenue CAGR through 2030.

Investment Thesis
Viatris at 6.94x forward earnings with a 3.0% dividend yield and 30% payout ratio offers deep value in a company generating $14.75B in guided FY2026 revenue across 165+ countries. Q2 2026 delivered $3.8B revenue (+5% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $1.2B (+8% operational), and management raised all guidance midpoints — revenue, EBITDA, EPS, and free cash flow [1]. The balance sheet is improving with gross leverage declining to 2.9x, and the company returned ~$550M to shareholders through buybacks and dividends in H1 2026. The PEG ratio of 0.14 suggests the market significantly underestimates earnings growth potential as the portfolio stabilizes and new product revenues ($450-550M FY2026 target) offset legacy product erosion. Greater China growth of 3.5%+ operational provides geographic diversification beyond mature U.S. generics [1].
The bear case centers on structural decline, GAAP losses, and culture erosion. Despite positive adjusted metrics, GAAP net loss of $119M in Q2 2026 reflects ongoing impairment charges and Tyrvaya write-downs that mask persistent profitability challenges [1]. ROE of -2.75% and net margin of -2.04% indicate the underlying business generates inadequate returns on capital. Revenue has declined from $17.3B (2021) to ~$14.75B (2026E), with 5yr CAGR of approximately -3% reflecting structural shrinkage through divestitures and pricing pressure. The top-down corporate culture with 3.4/5 Glassdoor ratings, low morale, and fear-based management creates talent retention risk in a competitive pharma labor market [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of -62 (MISALIGNED) due to abortifacient product exposure limits allocation for faith-based investors. The PEG of 0.14 looks attractive but reflects consensus expectations of only 2% operational revenue growth — this remains a value trap risk if adjusted EPS targets prove aspirational [4].
