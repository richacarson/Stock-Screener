Company Profile
Healthcare ·Drug Manufacturers - Specialty & Generic ·NASDAQ ·United States
Viatris Inc. is a global pharmaceutical company formed in November 2020 from the combination of Mylan N.V. and Pfizer's Upjohn division. The company develops, manufactures, and distributes a broad portfolio of generic, branded generic, complex generic, and biosimilar medicines across more than 165 countries under brands including Lipitor, Celebrex, Viagra, EpiPen, and Creon. Q2 2026 delivered $3.8B revenue (+5% YoY) with raised FY2026 guidance to ~$14.75B revenue and $2.52 adjusted EPS. CEO Scott Smith leads the Phase 2 strategic plan targeting 5-6% revenue CAGR through 2030.

Investment Thesis
Viatris at $17.25 and 6.45x forward earnings offers deep value in a global pharmaceutical company generating ~$14.75B in guided FY2026 revenue across 165+ countries with a 2.78% dividend yield [1][4]. Q2 2026 delivered $3.8B revenue (+5% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $1.2B (+8% operational), and management raised all guidance midpoints for revenue, EBITDA, EPS, and free cash flow. H1 2026 revenue of $7.27B (+6%) demonstrates sustained recovery [1]. The balance sheet is improving with gross leverage declining to 2.9x, and the adjusted EPS guidance of $2.52 implies a payout ratio of only 19%, providing substantial dividend coverage and free cash flow for continued deleveraging and buybacks. Greater China growth and new product revenues targeting $450-550M provide geographic and pipeline diversification beyond mature U.S. generics.
The bear case centers on structural decline, GAAP losses, and cultural challenges. Despite positive adjusted metrics, GAAP net loss of $119M in Q2 2026 reflects ongoing impairment charges and Tyrvaya write-downs that mask persistent profitability issues — ROE of -2.8% and profit margin of -2.8% indicate the underlying business generates inadequate GAAP returns [1][4]. Revenue has declined from $17.3B (2021) to ~$14.75B (2026E), with 5yr CAGR of approximately -4% reflecting structural shrinkage through divestitures and generic pricing pressure. The PEG of 3.19 suggests the market prices in only ~2% operational growth, making the forward PE of 6.45x less compelling than it appears. Glassdoor reviews describing a top-down culture with low morale, fear-based management, and 3.4/5 culture rating create talent retention risk critical for complex generics development [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of -62 (MISALIGNED) due to abortifacient product exposure limits allocation for faith-based investors. U.S. generics face 5-10% annual pricing deflation, creating persistent headwinds in the Developed Markets segment [1].
