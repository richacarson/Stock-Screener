Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Vitesse Energy, Inc. acquires, develops, and produces non-operated oil and natural gas properties primarily in the Williston Basin of North Dakota and Montana, with additional interests in the Central Rockies (Denver-Julesburg and Powder River Basins). The company owns non-operated working interest and royalty interest positions covering 53,301 net acres across 7,800+ wells with 30 operators. Spun off from Jefferies Financial Group in January 2023. Q2 2026 production averaged 17,354 BOE/day (60% oil), with revenue of $91.0M and net income of $33.1M.

Investment Thesis
Vitesse Energy offers a differentiated, capital-light approach to Williston Basin oil exposure through its non-operated model — 37 employees manage 53,301 net acres producing 17,354 BOE/day with 60% oil mix [1]. Q2 2026 was exceptional: revenue of $91.0M (+11.3% YoY), net income of $33.1M, and diluted EPS of $0.77 significantly beat consensus estimates [1]. The 10.2% dividend yield ($1.75 annualized, 15 consecutive quarterly payments) provides substantial income while the stock trades 37% below its 52-week high of $27.15 at $17.16 [5]. D/E of only 25.5% provides balance sheet conservatism, and proved reserves of 47.8 million BOE (71% proved developed) offer multi-year production visibility. Analyst consensus is Buy with a $21.25 price target (24% upside) [5]. New CEO Jamie Benard has demonstrated strong operational execution in Q2 2026.
The bear case centers on commodity risk, dividend sustainability, and structural limitations. The 10.2% dividend yield looks attractive but the payout ratio relative to GAAP earnings is unsustainable at 273% — the company funds distributions from cash flow rather than earnings, and a sustained decline in oil prices below $55/bbl could force further dividend cuts beyond the 22% reduction already implemented in Q1 2026 [1][5]. TTM net income is -$11.25M despite the strong Q2, indicating that Q3/Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 included significant impairments or losses tied to commodity price swings [5]. The non-operated model means Vitesse has zero control over drilling decisions, well spacing, or technology adoption — it is entirely dependent on third-party operators' capital allocation. The March 2026 leadership crisis (three CEOs in three months) raises governance concerns, though operations appear stabilized under Benard. The 5-year revenue trajectory is volatile ($181M → $258M → $212M → $221M → $251M → $259M TTM), reflecting commodity sensitivity rather than secular growth.
