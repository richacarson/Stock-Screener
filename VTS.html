Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·United States
Vitesse Energy, Inc. acquires, develops, and produces non-operated oil and natural gas properties primarily in the Williston Basin of North Dakota and Montana, with additional interests in the Central Rockies (Denver-Julesburg and Powder River Basins). The company owns non-operated working interest and royalty interest positions covering 53,301 net acres across 7,800+ wells with 30 operators. Spun off from Jefferies Financial Group in January 2023. CEO Jamie Benard appointed May 2026 following founder Bob Gerrity's resignation. Q2 2026 production averaged 17,354 BOE/day (60% oil), with revenue of $91.0M (+11% YoY) and net income of $33.1M.

Investment Thesis
Vitesse Energy at $17.73 offers a differentiated, capital-light approach to Williston Basin oil exposure through its non-operated model — 37 employees manage 53,301 net acres producing 17,354 BOE/day with 60% oil mix [1]. Q2 2026 was strong: revenue of $91.0M (+11% YoY), net income of $33.1M, and production up 9% sequentially, demonstrating operational stability under new CEO Jamie Benard [1]. The 9.87% dividend yield ($1.75 annualized) provides substantial income while D/E of only 25.5% offers balance sheet conservatism. The Powder River Basin acquisition (closed April 2026) adds Central Rockies diversification, and proved reserves of 47.8 million BOE (71% proved developed) offer multi-year production visibility. Revenue growth of 9.2% YoY suggests the production base is expanding.
The bear case centers on commodity risk, dividend sustainability, and governance concerns. The 9.87% dividend yield looks attractive but trailing GAAP earnings are negative (-$0.28 EPS), meaning the company funds distributions from operating cash flow rather than net income — a sustained decline in oil prices below $55/bbl could force further dividend cuts beyond the 22% reduction already implemented in Q1 2026 ($0.563 to $0.438/quarter) [1][4]. Forward P/E of 73.9x on only $0.24 forward EPS reflects analyst skepticism about near-term earnings recovery, and trailing ROE of -1.7% and profit margin of -4.3% indicate GAAP profitability remains challenged by non-cash charges [4]. The March 2026 leadership crisis (three CEOs in three months — Gerrity resigned, Cree interim, Benard appointed) raises governance questions despite operational stabilization [2]. The non-operated model means Vitesse has zero control over drilling decisions, well spacing, or technology adoption — it is entirely dependent on third-party operators' capital allocation.
