Company Profile
Financial Services ·Closed-End Fund - Senior Secured Loans ·NYSE ·United States
Invesco Senior Income Trust is a closed-end fixed income fund formed in 1998 and managed by Invesco Advisers, Inc. The fund invests primarily in floating- or variable-rate senior secured loans to corporations, partnerships, and other entities across a variety of industries and geographic regions, seeking a high level of current income consistent with preservation of capital. Invesco terminated the fund's Managed Distribution Plan in March 2025, after which the monthly distribution was cut roughly 12% and the fund's price fell to a persistent discount to net asset value.

Investment Thesis
VVR offers a 15.41% headline distribution yield and 27 consecutive years of monthly distributions since 1998, backed by a diversified portfolio of floating-rate senior secured corporate loans that carry legal claim priority over unsecured creditors [1][3]. Invesco's March 2025 decision to terminate the Managed Distribution Plan — though it caused a near-term price decline — was a fiscally disciplined move that stopped the fund from systematically overdistributing relative to earned income, and the fund's 27-year track record demonstrates durability through multiple credit cycles [1].
The bear case dominates. TTM revenue fell 18.16% YoY to $80.52M and net income fell 65.96% to $13.65M as declining benchmark rates compressed floating-rate loan income, while the payout ratio of 473.46% shows the fund is distributing many multiples of what it actually earns, guaranteeing continued reliance on return of capital and NAV erosion [1][3]. The fund's 1-year dividend growth of -11.76% following the Managed Distribution Plan termination confirms the income base cannot support even the reduced payout, and further Fed rate cuts would compress floating-rate loan yields further, likely forcing additional distribution cuts. With no capacity for strategic adaptation, no meaningful differentiation from dozens of competing senior-loan CEFs, and a fund-level ROE of roughly 2.7%, VVR is a structurally challenged income vehicle whose yield is not well supported by its earnings power [1][4].
Key Catalysts
  1. A pause or reversal in the Federal Reserve's rate-cutting cycle would boost floating-rate loan income and could stabilize or reverse the revenue decline [1]
  2. Narrowing of the fund's discount to NAV, if credit conditions stabilize and investor sentiment toward leveraged loan CEFs improves, would benefit total returns independent of income performance [3]
  3. Improved credit quality across the underlying senior loan portfolio would reduce default risk and support NAV preservation [1]
Key Risks
  1. Continued Fed rate cuts would further compress floating-rate loan income, which fell 18.16% YoY already, likely forcing additional distribution cuts beyond the ~12% cut already taken in 2025 [1][3]
  2. A payout ratio of 473.46% means the fund is distributing far more than it earns, guaranteeing ongoing NAV erosion through return-of-capital distributions regardless of near-term credit performance [3]
  3. A credit downturn or recession would increase defaults among the fund's below-investment-grade senior loan holdings, directly impairing NAV with no offsetting strategic flexibility available to the fund [1]
