Investment Thesis
Verizon offers a 6.16% dividend yield backed by $17.5B annual free cash flow, with 20 consecutive years of dividend increases and a forward P/E of just 8.70x — deep value pricing for a business generating $139B in annual revenue with irreplaceable spectrum and infrastructure assets [1]. CEO Schulman's transformation is delivering results: Q2 2026 set records with $13.7B adjusted EBITDA (+7.2% YoY), 40.1% EBITDA margin (highest in company history), and adjusted EPS of $1.30 beating estimates [1]. Full-year guidance raised for the second consecutive quarter to 6-7% EPS growth and 9-10% FCF growth. The first positive Q1 postpaid phone net adds since 2013 and 348K broadband net additions in Q2 signal competitive turnaround. AI-driven operational transformation targeting 13K-15K job reductions could generate billions in structural cost savings flowing to margins and FCF. Forward EPS of $5.28 implies significant earnings recovery.
The bear case centers on structural low growth, massive debt, and workforce disruption. Trailing revenue growth of -0.7% and 5-year CAGR of ~0.7% confirm telecom is a near-zero-growth industry at the top line — Verizon is optimizing a mature business, not building a growth platform. D/E of 184% reflects $193.6B in total debt from network buildout and the Frontier acquisition, creating significant interest expense burden and limiting strategic flexibility — total cash of only $1.8B against that debt load leaves minimal buffer. Dividend growth of only ~2% annually barely keeps pace with inflation, and the 72.8% payout ratio leaves limited margin for error. The 13K-15K planned layoffs create organizational disruption, potential service quality degradation, and union conflicts that could offset AI efficiency gains [2]. Trailing EPS growth of -22% reflects the earnings headwinds from integration costs. AI Resilience MODERATE

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Telecommunications infrastructure — wireless towers, fiber optic networks, spectrum licenses, and physical last-mile connections — is essential physical-world infrastructure that AI structurally cannot displace [1]. AI data centers require massive bandwidth, making Verizon a net beneficiary of AI adoption as compute demand drives network traffic growth. Regulatory barriers (FCC licensing), massive capital requirements ($16B+ annual capex), and spectrum scarcity create additional moats. However, AI is actively disrupting portions of Verizon's business — CEO Schulman expects AI to replace 'a large percentage' of customer service roles, with 13,000-15,000 job cuts planned [2]. The deployed Google Gemini across 100,000+ employees demonstrates AI is reshaping internal operations fundamentally. While this is Verizon disrupting itself rather than being disrupted externally, it confirms AI impacts meaningful portions of the telecom operating model. AI Opportunity (0/1): Verizon uses AI extensively for internal efficiency (Project 624, Gemini deployment, network optimization) but does not generate quantifiable revenue from AI-related products or services sold to customers. The AI applications are cost-reduction tools, not revenue-generating products.
