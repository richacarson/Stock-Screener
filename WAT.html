Company Profile
Healthcare ·Diagnostics & Research ·NYSE ·United States
Waters Corporation designs, manufactures, and sells analytical instruments including liquid chromatography (HPLC/UPLC), mass spectrometry, and thermal analysis systems used in pharmaceutical R&D, quality control, food safety, and environmental monitoring. The company recently transformed its scale through the acquisition of BD's Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions businesses, roughly doubling revenue to an approximately $6.4B run-rate. CEO Udit Batra (since 2020) has driven a transformation program centered on rediscovering the company's 'indomitable spirit.' Headquartered in Milford, Massachusetts with 16,000 employees.

Investment Thesis
Waters Corporation delivered strong Q2 2026 results with $1.645B total revenue and organic growth accelerating to 9% constant-currency, driven by broad end-market strengthening across pharmaceutical, industrial, and academic customers [1]. The transformative BD acquisition is integrating well, with the acquired businesses accelerating to mid-single-digit growth and contributing $817M in Q2 revenue. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $6.415-6.476B revenue with adjusted EPS of $14.45-$14.65 (10-12% growth). At $443/share and ~27x forward adjusted earnings, the stock reflects confidence in the combined platform's growth trajectory. Waters' dominant 35-40% HPLC/UPLC market share, expansion into flow cytometry and diagnostics via BD, and strong free cash flow generation provide a solid foundation for durable value creation [1][2].
The bear case centers on valuation, integration risk, and temporarily depressed GAAP financials. Forward P/E of 26.79x and PEG of 1.65 leave moderate margin of safety — any organic growth deceleration below the 7-9% guidance range could trigger multiple compression [2]. GAAP metrics are deeply distorted by the BD acquisition: trailing profit margin of 3.59% and ROE of 1.92% reflect massive amortization of acquired intangibles and $5B+ in acquisition-related debt [1][2]. The trailing P/E of 112x underscores the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings ($3.05 adj EPS vs. substantially lower GAAP). Mixed employee sentiment on Glassdoor, with some citing constant restructuring, changing leadership, and low morale, suggests the transformation is creating organizational strain that could impact execution during the critical BD integration period [5].
