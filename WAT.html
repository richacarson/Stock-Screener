Company Profile
Healthcare ·Diagnostics & Research ·NYSE ·United States
Waters Corporation designs, manufactures, and sells analytical instruments including liquid chromatography (HPLC/UPLC), mass spectrometry, and thermal analysis systems used in pharmaceutical R&D, quality control, food safety, and environmental monitoring. The company recently transformed its scale through the acquisition of BD's Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions businesses, roughly doubling revenue to an approximately $6.4B run-rate. Headquartered in Milford, Massachusetts, Waters has pioneered separations science for over 50 years and operates globally.

Investment Thesis
Waters Corporation delivered strong Q2 2026 results with $1.645B total revenue and organic growth accelerating to 9% constant-currency, driven by broad end-market strengthening across pharmaceutical, industrial, and academic customers [1]. The transformative BD acquisition is integrating well, with the acquired businesses accelerating to mid-single-digit growth and contributing $817M in Q2 revenue. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $6.415-6.476B revenue with adjusted EPS of $14.45-$14.65 (10-12% growth). At $406.86/share and 26x forward adjusted earnings, the stock reflects confidence in the combined platform's growth trajectory. Waters' dominant 35-40% market share in HPLC/UPLC, expansion into flow cytometry and diagnostics, and $398M TTM free cash flow provide a solid foundation for long-term value creation.
The bear case centers on valuation, integration risk, and temporarily depressed GAAP financials. The forward P/E of 26x and PEG of 2.08 leave limited margin of safety — any organic growth deceleration below the 7-9% guidance range could trigger multiple compression [2]. GAAP metrics are deeply distorted by the BD acquisition: trailing profit margin of 3.59% and ROE of 1.92% reflect massive amortization of acquired intangibles, while total debt of $5.09B creates financial leverage that constrains strategic flexibility [1][2]. The GAAP net loss of $136M in Q2 2026 underscores that the company's reported earnings will remain depressed until acquisition charges amortize. Mixed employee sentiment on Glassdoor, with some citing low morale and concerns about CEO priorities, suggests the transformation is creating organizational strain that could impact execution during the critical integration period [5]. If the BD businesses underperform mid-single-digit growth expectations, the premium paid may prove difficult to justify.
Key Catalysts
  1. BD Biosciences and Diagnostics integration accelerating to mid-single-digit growth validates the acquisition thesis and creates potential for revenue synergies as Waters cross-sells analytical instruments to BD's clinical and research customer base

  2. GAAP financials will remain heavily distorted for years as $5B+ in acquired intangible assets amortize, with trailing profit margin at 3.59% and GAAP Q2 net loss of $136M masking the underlying business quality and complicating investor analysis
  3. Mixed employee sentiment amid the transformation, with Glassdoor reviews citing low morale and CEO leadership concerns, creates integration risk as the company merges Waters and BD cultures during a critical post-acquisition period
