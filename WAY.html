Company Profile
Healthcare ·Health Information Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Waystar provides cloud-based healthcare payment technology and revenue cycle management (RCM) solutions, serving 30,000 clients representing over 1 million distinct healthcare providers. The platform processes more than 7.5 billion healthcare payment transactions annually. CEO Matt Hawkins leads the company's 'autonomous revenue cycle' vision, deploying AI and automation to address the $100B healthcare RCM labor pool. Waystar went public in June 2024 and reported Q2 2026 revenue of $319.7M (+18% YoY) with raised FY2026 guidance of $1.28-1.29B in revenue and $535-545M in adjusted EBITDA. The company has a market cap of $4.81B.

Investment Thesis
Waystar offers a compelling growth-at-a-reasonable-price thesis in healthcare payments technology — 19.2% TTM revenue growth with Q1 2026 accelerating to 22%, a Forward P/E of just 14.42x, and a PEG ratio of 0.81 suggesting the market undervalues the growth trajectory [1][2]. The company serves 30,000 clients processing 7.5B annual transactions, creating significant recurring revenue visibility. Raised FY2026 guidance to $1.28-1.29B revenue and $535-545M adjusted EBITDA demonstrates management confidence and execution consistency [1]. The AI-powered 'autonomous revenue cycle' vision targeting the $100B RCM labor pool provides a multi-year growth runway — AI is an enabler, not a threat. The 24-analyst Strong Buy consensus with a $33.17 average price target implies 32% upside. EPS growth of 45.5% demonstrates operating leverage as the platform scales, and the Inspire ALIGNED rating (37) adds appeal for faith-based investors [2].
The bear case centers on execution risk in a competitive market and valuation sensitivity to growth deceleration. Revenue growth decelerated from 22% in Q1 to 18% in Q2 2026, and sustained growth above 15% requires continued client acquisition in a competitive RCM landscape where Optum/Change Healthcare, R1 RCM, and athenahealth have larger installed bases [1]. ROE of just 3.77% reflects a PE-backed acquisition structure with significant goodwill — the $4B equity base inflated by M&A limits returns on capital deployed. Total debt of $1.48B creates leverage risk if growth slows. The company has only been public since June 2024 and lacks a multi-year public track record through economic cycles. Profit margin of 13% is below the 20% target and below best-in-class SaaS peers, suggesting room for margin expansion but also potential competitive pressure on pricing. The 26% YTD share price decline from $41.47 to $25.07 indicates market skepticism about the growth premium, and any miss on the raised FY2026 guidance could trigger further multiple compression [2].

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Waystar's healthcare RCM platform is protected by deeply regulated healthcare payment processes, complex payer-provider relationships, HIPAA compliance requirements, and integration with thousands of EHR systems that create high switching costs [3]. The company processes 7.5B annual transactions across 30,000 clients — this scale of healthcare data and workflow integration creates a structural moat that AI-native startups cannot easily replicate. Healthcare payments involve unique payer rules, denial management processes, and regulatory requirements that require domain-specific expertise beyond general AI capabilities. Rather than being disrupted by AI, Waystar is deploying AI to enhance its platform — the $100B RCM labor pool represents AI-enabled growth opportunity, not threat [1]. However, large technology companies (Microsoft/Nuance, Google Health) with superior AI capabilities could potentially enter RCM at scale. AI Opportunity (1/1): Waystar generates meaningful revenue from AI-powered products deployed across its 30,000-client base, including AI-driven denial management, automated claims processing, and the recently launched AI solution for recovering lost provider revenue from payer adjustments [1][3]. AI is central to the company's growth strategy and revenue model.
