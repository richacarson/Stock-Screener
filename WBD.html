Company Profile
Communication Services ·Entertainment ·NASDAQ ·United States
Warner Bros. Discovery is a global media and entertainment company formed from the April 2022 merger of WarnerMedia and Discovery, Inc. Operates premium content franchises across streaming (Max with 100M+ global subscribers), film studios (Warner Bros. Pictures), television networks (HBO, CNN, Discovery Channel, HGTV, TLC), and gaming. Q2 2026 revenue of $8.7B declined 11.2% YoY as linear TV advertising fell 22% and theatrical revenue dropped 46% from box office misses including Supergirl and The Bride. Streaming revenue topped $3B for the first time with adjusted streaming EBITDA of $512M (+60% YoY). The company is subject to a pending acquisition by Paramount Skydance, with CEO David Zaslav set to depart. Market cap approximately $66B.

Investment Thesis
WBD's streaming business is reaching an inflection point — Max topped $3B in quarterly revenue for the first time with adjusted streaming EBITDA of $512M, up 60% YoY, demonstrating that the direct-to-consumer pivot is generating real profitability [1]. The pending Paramount Skydance acquisition provides a floor on the stock at approximately $27.75 per share and could unlock significant content synergies by combining HBO, Discovery's unscripted library, and Paramount's film/TV franchises. The company's premium IP portfolio (HBO, DC, Harry Potter, CNN) represents irreplaceable branded content, and management stated '2027 could be its strongest HBO year yet' based on the content slate in development [1].
The bear case is overwhelming and structural. Revenue declined 11.2% YoY to $8.7B in Q2 2026, with linear TV advertising down 22% and theatrical revenue down 46% on box office failures [1]. CEO Zaslav has sold $173M+ in personal stock while negotiating a departure package potentially approaching $800M from the Paramount Skydance merger — a clear signal that leadership's incentives are misaligned with long-term value creation [3]. The $32.5B debt load against a backdrop of declining revenue creates significant financial fragility. ROE is negative at -4.96%, profit margin is barely positive at 1.64%, and forward P/E of 1383x reflects near-zero expected GAAP earnings [5]. Post-merger culture has not cohered after four years, with Glassdoor reviews describing fear-based management and layoff-driven morale destruction [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of -49 (MISALIGNED) further limits the investment universe for faith-based allocators. Without a strategic vision beyond the pending merger, WBD is a financial restructuring story rather than a business built to endure.
