Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NYSE ·Spain
Wallbox N.V. designs, manufactures, and sells electric vehicle charging and energy management solutions for residential, semi-public, and public use worldwide. Products include the Pulsar Plus and Pulsar Pro AC home chargers, Quasar bidirectional DC charger (first residential V2G charger), Commander 2 commercial charger, Supernova DC fast charger, and myWallbox energy management platform. Founded in 2015, headquartered in Barcelona, Spain. Listed on NYSE following SPAC merger in October 2021. The company is in severe financial distress: Q2 2026 revenue of E23.9M missed guidance of E33-36M by 30%, and the company completed a court-approved restructuring of ~E169.6M in financial debt including E15.8M in new equity and E5.4M bank financing [1][3].

Investment Thesis
Wallbox operates in the structurally attractive EV charging infrastructure market, benefiting from accelerating global EV adoption, government charging mandates (EU's Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation, US NEVI program), and the long-term electrification megatrend [5]. The company has a differentiated product portfolio anchored by the Quasar bidirectional DC charger and improving gross margins (38% in Q2 2026, up from 34.2% in FY2024) [1]. CEO Enric Asuncion's $886K personal stock purchase at $2.72/share in June 2026 demonstrates insider conviction [4]. Q2 2026 order intake grew 11% sequentially, building a backlog of nearly E12M, suggesting demand is not the problem — operational execution is [1]. The completed E15.8M equity raise and NYSE compliance plan approval in July 2026 provide near-term stabilization [2][3].
However, the investment case is overwhelmed by existential financial risk and execution failure. Q2 2026 revenue of E23.9M catastrophically missed guidance of E33-36M by approximately 30%, with management attributing the shortfall to 'operational constraints rather than demand weakness' — but a company that cannot ship products it has orders for has a fundamental execution problem [1]. Wallbox has negative equity of -E31M, E168.2M in debt, and generated a -E103.2M net loss in FY2025 on only E145.1M of revenue (-71.1% net margin) [3]. The adjusted EBITDA loss of -E7.8M in Q2 was nearly flat YoY and wider than Q1, showing deterioration rather than improvement. With the stock at $3.11 (down from $4.46 in July), average daily dollar volume has fallen to approximately $467K — far below the $1M IOWN liquidity floor, making the stock effectively untradable for institutional portfolios [6]. The EV charging market is increasingly commoditized with well-capitalized competitors (Tesla, ChargePoint, ABB) who can absorb losses that would be fatal to Wallbox. The Inspire Impact Score of 71 (ALIGNED) reflects strong ESG positioning but cannot overcome the financial distress.
