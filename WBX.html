Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NYSE ·Spain
Wallbox is a Barcelona-based maker of electric vehicle charging hardware and software, including the Pulsar Plus home AC charger, the Quasar bidirectional DC charger, the Supernova and Hypernova public DC fast chargers, the myWallbox management platform and the Electromaps e-mobility app. In 2026 the company went through a court-supervised debt restructuring after entering Spanish pre-insolvency proceedings over about EUR 170M of bank liabilities, extending maturities to 2030 and raising new equity. Q2 2026 revenue was about EUR 23.9M, down 19% sequentially, with Q3 guided to EUR 29-31M. The NYSE has granted an 18-month cure period to regain compliance with listing standards. Market cap approximately $63M.

Investment Thesis
The bull case is that the 2026 restructuring resets Wallbox's capital structure: maturities were extended to December 2030, creditors holding about 83% of financial debt signed on, and new equity was raised. Order intake rose 11% sequentially in Q2 2026, building a backlog of nearly EUR 12M, and management guided Q3 revenue to EUR 29-31M with 38-40% gross margins as vendor constraints ease [3][4]. Wallbox's product range across home, bidirectional and public DC charging gives it exposure to any recovery in European EV adoption [1].
The bear case is severe. Revenue fell from EUR 164M in 2024 to EUR 139M in 2025 and is down further in 2026, the TTM net margin is about -94%, cash was only about EUR 20M at June 30, 2026, and the company needed pre-insolvency protection [2][3][7][11]. Average daily trading of well under $1M makes the stock untradeable for IOWN, the NYSE listing depends on an 18-month cure period, and further dilution is likely if the Q3 recovery does not materialize [3][9].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 revenue landing within the EUR 29-31M guidance as vendor terms normalize and the ~EUR 12M order backlog converts
  2. Completion and court approval of the debt restructuring with maturities extended to 2030
  3. A recovery in European EV sales and uptake of bidirectional (Quasar) and public DC (Supernova) charging
Key Risks
  1. Liquidity and solvency risk: ~EUR 20M of cash against ~EUR 140M of non-current debt after the restructuring
  2. NYSE delisting risk if listing standards are not regained within the 18-month cure period
  3. Further dilution and continued revenue declines as slow European EV adoption and charger competition pressure volumes and pricing
