Company Profile
Industrials ·Waste Management ·NYSE ·Canada
Waste Connections is the third-largest waste management company in North America, providing non-hazardous waste collection, transfer, disposal, and recycling services across the United States and Canada. Founded in 1997 and headquartered in Woodbridge, Ontario, the company operates under a Servant Leadership philosophy targeting underserved and exclusive markets through a disciplined tuck-in acquisition strategy. CEO Ronald Mittelstaedt returned as CEO in April 2023, bringing 35+ years of industry experience. Q2 2026 revenue rose 6.4% to $2.562B with adjusted EBITDA of $840.1M (32.8% margin), and the company raised FY2026 guidance to $10.02-10.05B in revenue and $3.33-3.34B in adjusted EBITDA. The company has committed $100M to deploy seven enterprise-level AI initiatives with BCG through 2027. Market cap approximately $39B.

Investment Thesis
Waste Connections is a best-in-class defensive compounder operating in a structurally AI-immune industry with exceptional culture and management quality. Q2 2026 revenue rose 6.4% to $2.562B with adjusted EBITDA of $840.1M (32.8% margin, up 10bps YoY), and the company raised FY2026 guidance to $10.02-10.05B revenue and $3.33-3.34B adjusted EBITDA [1]. The Servant Leadership culture produces record employee engagement (95% response rate, 8.6 score) that translates directly into operational excellence — lower turnover reduces hiring costs, improves route efficiency, and drives customer retention [2]. CEO Mittelstaedt's $7.6M personal stock purchase signals extraordinary insider conviction [4]. Core pricing of 5-5.5% combined with tuck-in acquisitions provides a durable mid-single-digit organic growth engine, while the $100M AI investment program targeting seven enterprise-level initiatives with BCG should drive margin expansion through 2027 [2]. The waste management business is structurally recession-resistant and entirely immune to AI disruption.
The bear case centers on premium valuation, slowing growth, and leverage. At 25x forward earnings and 37.8x trailing P/E, Waste Connections trades at a significant premium to the S&P 500 and to direct peers, reflecting the market's recognition of WCN's defensive qualities but leaving limited room for multiple expansion [4]. Organic revenue growth is decelerating — volumes declined 1.9% in Q1 2026, with core pricing (5-5.5%) carrying the growth burden as the economy slows [1]. D/E of 121.7% represents meaningful leverage for a company with 10.9% profit margins, and the acquisition-driven growth strategy requires continued capital deployment. The 0.9% dividend yield, while growing at 8.8% 5-year CAGR, falls well below the 2% threshold for income-focused investors. ROE of 13.0% is solid but below the 15% target, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of waste management infrastructure. The PEG ratio of 1.51 suggests the market is paying a fair-to-full price for mid-single-digit growth. The stock at $155.23 is below its 50-day SMA of $166.30, reflecting recent market rotation away from defensive names despite raised guidance.

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
9
AI Risk Protection (9/9): Waste collection and disposal is structurally immune to AI disruption — the core business involves physical trucks collecting physical waste from physical locations and transporting it to physical landfills, transfer stations, and recycling facilities [1]. AI cannot substitute for garbage collection, landfill operations, or environmental remediation. The business is additionally protected by regulatory moats (environmental permits, EPA compliance, municipal franchise agreements), physical infrastructure barriers (landfill permitting takes 5-10 years), and local network effects (route density economics). AI may actually increase demand — data center expansion requires waste management for construction debris and electronics recycling, and broader economic growth drives waste volumes. The waste management industry has faced zero substitution risk from AI, and no analysts have flagged AI disruption as a concern for the sector. AI Opportunity (0/1): Waste Connections uses AI extensively for internal operations — $100M investment in seven AI initiatives covering route optimization, dynamic pricing, safety monitoring, and predictive maintenance [2]. However, the company does not generate revenue from AI products or services sold to external customers. AI improves operational efficiency but is not separately monetized.
