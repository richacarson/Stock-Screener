Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NMS ·United States
Workday provides enterprise cloud applications for financial management, human capital management, planning, and analytics worldwide. The company serves professional services, financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, education, government, technology, and retail industries with subscription revenue comprising over 93% of total revenue. TTM revenue is approximately $10.2B with 12.8% growth, anchored by a 97% gross revenue retention rate and a 12-month subscription backlog of $8.21B. Founded in 2005 and headquartered in Pleasanton, California, Workday returned co-founder Aneel Bhusri to the CEO role in February 2026 to lead its AI transformation around the Illuminate platform.

The return of Aneel Bhusri as CEO in February 2026 — after Carl Eschenbach's departure — signals the board's conviction that Workday's next chapter requires the founder's product vision and cultural authority rather than operational scaling. Bhusri's framing of AI as 'a bigger transformation than SaaS' reflects long-term thinking, and Workday's 97% retention rate suggests customers experience genuine value alignment. Investment Thesis
Workday at $204 with a $49B market cap is a high-quality enterprise SaaS platform trading at an unusually attractive valuation — forward PE of 15.4x and PEG of 0.57 represent significant compression from historical multiples as the market digests the CEO transition and revenue growth deceleration [1][4]. The investment case centers on AI monetization: the Illuminate platform's 80+ GA features, 1 billion AI actions, and Flex Credits consumption model position Workday to capture incremental revenue from AI agents across HCM and finance workflows, with a reported $1B Sana deal validating enterprise demand [3]. Subscription revenue grew 14.6% to $2.24B in Q3 FY2026 with 97% gross retention and $8.21B in 12-month subscription backlog, providing exceptional revenue visibility [1]. Operating cash flow of $588M (up 45% YoY) and $3B+ in annual free cash flow fund both reinvestment and shareholder returns, while the balance sheet carries $3.4B in cash against $3.8B in debt [4].
The bear case centers on execution risk and competitive threats. Co-founder Bhusri's return as CEO, while culturally significant, follows Carl Eschenbach's departure after leading the company through its global scaling phase — the transition signals urgency around AI execution rather than stability, and triggered a 5% share price decline [2][3]. Revenue growth is decelerating from 16% to an expected 13% for FY2027, and the company's adjusted operating margin guidance of approximately 30% came in below the 31.2% consensus, suggesting the AI investment cycle will compress near-term profitability [3]. AI-native competitors like Rippling and Deel are building modern HCM platforms with AI at the core, while SAP and Oracle are investing billions in their own AI capabilities, creating a multi-front competitive battle where Workday's incumbent advantage may erode. GAAP profit margin of just 12.3% significantly trails SaaS leaders, and the gap between GAAP and non-GAAP metrics reflects heavy stock-based compensation that dilutes shareholder value over time [4].
