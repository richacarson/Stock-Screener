Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NASDAQ ·United States
Workday provides enterprise cloud applications for human capital management (HCM), financial management, planning, and analytics, serving over 60% of Fortune 500 companies. Co-founded by Aneel Bhusri and Dave Duffield in 2005, the company went public in 2012. In February 2026, Bhusri returned as CEO after Carl Eschenbach stepped down, driven by Bhusri's belief that Workday had lost its startup culture and needed clearer AI strategy. The company is repositioning as 'the enterprise AI platform for managing people, money, and agents.' In August 2026, shares surged 25% on reports of Silver Lake exploring a takeover. FY2026 total revenue was $9.55B (+13.1% YoY) with subscription revenue of $8.83B (+14.5%).

Investment Thesis
Workday at 17.2x forward earnings with a 0.80 PEG represents compelling value for a cloud franchise serving 60%+ of the Fortune 500 with 30%+ FCF margins. FY2026 delivered $9.55B in total revenue (+13.1%) with subscription revenue growing 14.5% to $8.83B, while non-GAAP operating margin expanded to ~30% [1]. The $1.1B Sana Labs acquisition and pivot to 'the enterprise AI platform for managing people, money, and agents' positions Workday at the center of enterprise AI transformation, with co-founder Bhusri's return as CEO signaling conviction in the company's long-term trajectory [2]. FCF of $2.97B provides substantial capital for buybacks, acquisitions, and AI investment. The August 2026 report of Silver Lake exploring a takeover at a premium validates the strategic asset value and establishes a potential floor for the stock [5].
The bear case centers on decelerating growth, cultural deterioration, and AI disruption risk. Revenue growth is slowing from 17.3% five-year CAGR to 12-13% FY2027 guidance, suggesting Workday may be transitioning from a growth to a mature enterprise software company [1]. GAAP profit margin of only 8.60% and ROE of 10.86% indicate heavy stock-based compensation continues to dilute real economic returns [6]. The CEO transition from Eschenbach back to Bhusri signals strategic disagreement at the board level, and Glassdoor reviews describe cultural instability with layoffs and 'AI at all costs' pressure [4]. Insider selling is unanimous — 106 sales and zero purchases in six months, with co-founder Duffield selling 3.1M shares [5]. AI-native enterprise software competitors could emerge to challenge Workday's monolithic platform, and the $1.1B Sana Labs acquisition may prove insufficient defense if AI fundamentally changes how enterprises manage HR and finance.
