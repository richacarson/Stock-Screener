Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas E&P ·NYSE ·Australia
Woodside Energy Group is an Australian-headquartered global energy company engaged in the exploration, development, production, and marketing of hydrocarbons, primarily LNG, pipeline gas, crude oil, and condensate. Key assets include Pluto LNG, North West Shelf, Wheatstone, and the Scarborough project under development. Founded in 1954, Woodside merged with BHP Petroleum in 2022, transforming into a top-tier global LNG producer. Full year 2025 revenue was $12.98B with record production of 198.8 MMboe (545 Mboe/day). The company targets 50% sales volume growth by 2032 to over 300 MMboe.
4,693 Employees ·www.woodside.com

Investment Thesis
Woodside Energy at 11.6x forward earnings with a 5.26% dividend yield offers deep value exposure to a top-tier global LNG producer with record production of 198.8 MMboe in 2025 and a clear growth trajectory targeting 50% volume growth to 300+ MMboe by 2032 [1]. The Scarborough LNG project coming online in 2026-2027 provides the next leg of production growth, while the low D/E of 32.76% and $4.5B cash position provide balance sheet flexibility. Management guides approximately $9B in net operating cash flow by 2032, representing 6%+ CAGR. The structural tailwind from AI-driven data center power demand supports long-term natural gas prices, and Woodside's Asia-Pacific LNG positioning serves the fastest-growing demand centers.
The bear case centers on commodity price exposure, ESG risks, and leadership instability. Woodside's revenue declined from $16.8B in 2022 to $13.0B in 2025 as energy prices normalized from post-Ukraine crisis peaks, and NPAT fell 24% YoY in 2025 to $2.72B [1]. The payout ratio of 69.78% is unsustainably high relative to targets, and the dividend has declined from 2022 levels — there is no consecutive growth streak, making the yield precarious if commodity prices weaken further. Woodside faces mounting ESG pressure: Glass Lewis issued an ESG alert, shareholders voted down the climate plan, activists have accused the company of greenwashing, and the abandoned $5B new energy target intensifies reputational risk with ESG-focused investors [3]. The CEO departure (Meg O'Neill to BP) and appointment of Acting CEO Liz Westcott creates leadership uncertainty at a critical execution moment as Scarborough approaches first production. ROE of only 7.68% suggests capital is not being deployed efficiently relative to the asset base inherited from the BHP merger.
Key Catalysts
  1. Scarborough LNG project first production in 2026-2027, adding significant production volumes and driving toward the 300+ MMboe by 2032 target — the single largest growth driver in Woodside's portfolio [1]
  2. Structural demand growth for natural gas from AI data centers requiring reliable baseload power, supporting long-term LNG pricing and validating Woodside's hydrocarbon-focused strategy [1]
  3. Permanent CEO appointment to replace Acting CEO Liz Westcott could provide strategic clarity and restore investor confidence in the management team [2]
Key Risks
  1. Commodity price exposure — Woodside's revenue and dividend are directly tied to global LNG, oil, and gas prices, which declined ~23% from 2022 peaks and remain vulnerable to demand slowdowns, particularly from a Chinese economic downturn [1]
  2. Escalating ESG and legal risks — greenwashing allegations, shareholder climate plan rejection, Glass Lewis ESG alert, and the Australian Conservation Foundation lawsuit create regulatory, reputational, and litigation overhang [3]
  3. Leadership vacuum with Acting CEO since December 2025 and no permanent successor announced, coinciding with Scarborough project execution that requires decisive capital allocation decisions [2]
