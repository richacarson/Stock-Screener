Company Profile
Utilities ·Utilities — Regulated Electric & Gas ·NYSE ·United States
WEC Energy Group is a regulated utility holding company providing electric and natural gas services to 4.7 million customers across Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, and Minnesota through subsidiaries including Wisconsin Electric Power Company, Wisconsin Gas, and We Energies. The company operates 72,800 miles of electric distribution lines and 48,500 miles of natural gas distribution and transmission lines. Under CEO and Chairman Scott Lauber (Chairman since May 2026), WEC has a $37.5B five-year capital plan through 2030 with approximately 15% of the total asset base expected to serve very large data center customers including Microsoft and Oracle by the end of the period. Q2 2026 EPS of $0.91 beat consensus of $0.83, and the company reaffirmed 2026 EPS guidance of $5.51-$5.61.

Investment Thesis
WEC Energy Group is a best-in-class regulated utility offering a compelling combination of income, growth, and execution reliability. The company has delivered or exceeded the top end of earnings guidance for 22 consecutive years — an extraordinary track record that justifies the premium valuation [1]. Q2 2026 EPS of $0.91 beat consensus of $0.83, and full-year 2026 guidance of $5.51-$5.61 was reaffirmed with long-term growth of 7-8% annually through 2030 [1]. At 3.57% current yield with 22 consecutive years of dividend increases at approximately 7% CAGR, the dividend provides a growing income stream with strong coverage from regulated earnings [3]. The $37.5B capital plan includes significant data center infrastructure, with Microsoft and Oracle-related projects driving a 3.9 GW load increase over five years — approximately 15% of the total asset base will serve data center customers by 2030, providing a differentiated growth catalyst versus utility peers [2]. ROE of 23.15% is exceptional for a regulated utility, and the Inspire Impact Score of 41 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes WEC a faith-friendly income investment.
The primary risk is the leveraged balance sheet and valuation premium. D/E of 158% and $21B+ in total debt create sensitivity to interest rate movements — rising rates increase borrowing costs on the $37.5B capital plan while compressing the utility sector's valuation premium versus fixed income alternatives [4]. The forward P/E of 20.74x and payout ratio of 73.1% (above WEC's 65-70% target range) leave limited margin of safety if earnings growth slows below the 7-8% guidance range. Q2 2026 revenue of $2.06B missed the $2.11B consensus estimate despite the EPS beat, suggesting cost discipline is compensating for softer-than-expected top-line growth in some quarters [1]. Data center demand concentration — relying on Microsoft and Oracle for a significant share of incremental load growth — creates customer concentration risk if projects are delayed or scaled back. If regulators deny or delay rate case approvals, or if construction costs on the renewables capital plan escalate, the carefully calibrated earnings-growth-to-dividend-payout equation could be disrupted. The MSCI ESG rating of AA reflects strong practices but also means WEC's environmental commitments create compliance costs that could pressure margins if regulatory recovery lags spending [3].
