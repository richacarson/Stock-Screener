Company Profile
Industrials ·Trucking ·NASDAQ ·United States
Werner Enterprises, Inc. transports truckload shipments of general commodities across the U.S., Mexico, and Canada through medium-to-long-haul, expedited, regional, and temperature-controlled fleets, alongside non-asset-based logistics, intermodal, and Werner Final Mile services. Founded in 1956 and headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska, Werner operated 7,100 trucks and 28,780 trailers as of year-end 2025. The company posted a net loss for full-year 2025 but has shown a sharp recovery in 2026, aided by the FirstFleet dedicated-fleet acquisition [1]. Derek Leathers has served as CEO since May 2016 and Chairman since May 2021.

Investment Thesis
Werner is showing a genuine operational recovery in 2026 after a difficult 2025: Q1 2026 revenue rose 14% to $808.6M and Q2 2026 trucking revenue (net of fuel surcharge) grew 27% year-over-year with adjusted diluted EPS up 178%, driven by the FirstFleet dedicated-fleet acquisition and the 'strongest revenue per truck growth in a decade' from the One-Way Truckload restructuring [1]. A forward P/E of just 14.9x and D/E of 68.4% (below the 70% target) suggest the market is not yet fully crediting the turnaround, and the stock's 52 Inspire Impact Score reflects genuinely strong ESG credentials across employee wellbeing, human rights, and emissions management [4].
The bear case dominates the mechanical score: Werner's full-year 2025 results were weak (operating income fell to $11.7M from $66.1M, a $0.24 diluted loss per share, ROE of -3.9%, profit margin of -1.4%), and the 5-year revenue CAGR is negative (-3.3%), leaving Infrastructure at just 3/10 despite the 2026 recovery signals [1][4]. The 136.6% payout ratio against a loss-making trailing year raises real dividend-sustainability questions even at a modest 1.7% yield. Longer-term, commercial-scale autonomous hub-to-hub trucking (Gatik's fully driverless deployments in 2026) introduces genuine structural risk to Werner's one-way/OTR truckload segments over the coming decade, even if Dedicated and Final Mile services remain more insulated [2]. A $36M+ EEOC discrimination verdict and a functional rather than distinctive mission keep the Infinite Game score at 3/10 (FINITE), and the combination of weak trailing fundamentals, moderate AI exposure, and a finite competitive posture drives the overall score to 37 and a SELL recommendation, notwithstanding the encouraging 2026 quarterly trend that bears monitoring for a potential future re-rating.
