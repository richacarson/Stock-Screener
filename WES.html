Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Western Midstream Partners, LP is a master limited partnership formed to acquire, own, develop, and operate midstream assets. The partnership provides natural gas gathering, processing, and transportation services as well as crude oil gathering and produced water disposal services, primarily in the DJ Basin (Colorado) and Delaware Basin (West Texas/New Mexico). Formerly an Anadarko Petroleum subsidiary, WES became independently managed following Occidental Petroleum's acquisition of Anadarko in 2019.

Investment Thesis
Western Midstream delivered record FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $2.48B and TTM revenue of $3.84B, with the partnership raising 2026 distribution guidance to at least $3.70/unit ($3.72 annualized for Q2 2026) — an 8.52% yield at the current price of $43.67 [1][2]. The company trades at a compelling 13.51x forward earnings with 30% profit margin and 32.2% ROE, reflecting strong fee-based cash flow conversion. 2026 EBITDA guidance of $2.5-2.7B with reduced capex of $850M-$1B positions WES to generate substantial free cash flow. Dominant positions in the prolific DJ and Delaware Basins provide production-driven organic growth, and AI data center construction is creating incremental natural gas demand as a structural tailwind.
The primary risk is that WES's distribution exceeds GAAP net income (payout ratio ~128% of earnings), creating reliance on depreciation-shielded cash flow to fund unitholdings returns — while standard for MLPs, this becomes vulnerable if capital expenditure requirements increase or production volumes decline [1]. D/E of 212% represents meaningful leverage risk in a rising rate environment, and the partnership's concentration in the DJ and Delaware Basins creates geographic risk if either basin faces regulatory restrictions or production declines. The recent CEO transition introduces execution uncertainty as Oscar Brown implements his strategic vision. WES's long-term revenue growth narrative ($4.5B revenue by 2028) requires 7.1% annual growth, which depends on continued producer drilling activity and commodity prices remaining supportive [2]. Sustainalytics has flagged ESG controversy exposure in recent years, suggesting potential environmental or operational incidents that could create reputational and regulatory headwinds [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Distribution growth to $3.72+/unit annualized (8.52% yield) supported by record EBITDA and reduced capital intensity, with management targeting continued distribution increases through 2027
  2. AI data center construction driving incremental natural gas demand in the DJ and Delaware Basins, where WES has dominant gathering and processing infrastructure
  3. Reduced 2026 capex of $850M-$1B versus prior years enables higher free cash flow conversion, supporting unit buybacks and distribution growth simultaneously
Key Risks
  1. Distribution payout exceeds GAAP net income at ~128% payout ratio, creating reliance on depreciation-shielded cash flow — any increase in sustaining capex requirements could pressure distribution sustainability
  2. Geographic concentration in the DJ and Delaware Basins creates risk from Colorado regulatory actions, produced water disposal restrictions, or production declines in either basin
  3. Recent CEO transition (Oscar Brown replacing Michael Ure in October 2024) introduces strategic execution uncertainty; Sustainalytics has flagged elevated ESG controversy exposure
