Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Midstream ·NYSE ·United States
Western Midstream Partners, LP is a master limited partnership that acquires, owns, develops, and operates midstream assets. The partnership provides natural gas gathering, processing, and transportation services as well as crude oil gathering and produced water disposal services, primarily in the DJ Basin (Colorado) and Delaware Basin (West Texas/New Mexico). Became independently managed following Occidental Petroleum's 2019 acquisition of Anadarko. In 2026, closed the $1.67B Brazos Delaware acquisition adding 900 miles of pipeline and 460 MMcf/d of processing capacity. Q2 2026 record quarterly adjusted EBITDA of $736.5M.

Investment Thesis
Western Midstream at 12.23x forward earnings with a 7.87% distribution yield and record EBITDA delivers compelling income-plus-growth for midstream investors [5]. Q2 2026 produced record quarterly adjusted EBITDA of $736.5M (+19% YoY), and the 2026 revenue forecast was raised to $4.29B, driven by the $1.67B Brazos Delaware acquisition adding 900 miles of pipeline and 460 MMcf/d of processing capacity [1][2]. ROE of 34.1% and profit margin of 29.0% reflect strong fee-based cash flow conversion, and the AI data center construction boom provides a structural tailwind for natural gas demand. The partnership has returned $4.6B to unitholders through distributions and repurchases, and FY2025 record adjusted EBITDA of $2.48B with 2026 guidance of $2.5-2.7B provides earnings visibility.
The primary risk is WES's distribution exceeding GAAP net income (payout ratio ~128% of earnings), creating reliance on depreciation-shielded cash flow to fund unitholder returns — while standard for MLPs, this becomes vulnerable if capital expenditure requirements increase or production volumes decline [1][5]. D/E of 207% represents meaningful leverage risk, amplified by the $1.67B Brazos Delaware acquisition financed partly through debt. Geographic concentration in the DJ and Delaware Basins creates risk from Colorado regulatory actions, produced water disposal restrictions, or production declines in either basin. The CEO transition to Oscar Brown, while orderly, introduces execution uncertainty as he implements his strategic vision and integrates newly acquired assets [3]. Revenue growth of 30% is acquisition-driven and will normalize — the organic business grows at mid-single-digit rates, and any downturn in producer drilling activity would directly impact throughput volumes. Sustainalytics has flagged ESG controversy exposure, suggesting potential environmental or operational incidents [4].
