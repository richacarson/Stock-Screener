Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Cactus, Inc. designs, manufactures, sells, and rents wellhead and pressure control equipment and provides field services to oil and gas operators. Operates two segments: Pressure Control (wellheads, frac trees, production trees) and Spoolable Technologies (FlexSteel brand pipes). In January 2026, Cactus acquired 65% of Baker Hughes' Surface Pressure Control business for $344.5M, creating Cactus International and dramatically expanding its global footprint with $600M in acquired backlog. Q2 2026 revenue reached $449.5M. Founded in 2011 by the Bender family, headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Investment Thesis
Cactus is a well-capitalized oilfield equipment company that dramatically expanded its scale through the transformative $344.5M Baker Hughes Surface Pressure Control acquisition, which added $600M in international backlog and roughly doubled the revenue base [1]. Q2 2026 revenue reached $449.5M with adjusted earnings of $0.93 per share beating the $0.63 consensus by 48%, and adjusted EBITDA margins expanded to 29.5% [1]. The balance sheet is exceptional: D/E of just 3.3% with $365.8M in cash provides financial safety and flexibility for further acquisitions or to weather commodity cycles. Forward P/E of 17.17 reflects reasonable valuation for a company with 64% YoY revenue growth and an estimated 5-year revenue CAGR of 15%. The Inspire ALIGNED score of 39 and family-led culture since 2011 add qualitative appeal [4].
The WATCH recommendation reflects cyclical exposure and valuation normalization risk. The trailing P/E of 52.3 is distorted by Q1 2026 GAAP losses from JV accretion charges, but the normalized forward P/E of 17.17 may not offer sufficient margin of safety for a cyclical oilfield equipment company [4]. Profit margin of 12.0% is below the 20% target, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of equipment manufacturing and field services. The oilfield services industry is inherently cyclical — tied to oil prices, rig counts, and E&P capital spending — and a sustained oil price decline below $60/bbl would compress both revenue and margins simultaneously. The SPC integration creates execution risk as Cactus absorbs a business with 85% Middle East revenue concentration, exposing the company to geopolitical risk in a region where it has limited operating history. Insider ownership of only 0.97% is low for a founder-led company, and no meaningful insider buying was identified to confirm management conviction at current prices [4].
Key Catalysts
  1. Cactus International integration delivering revenue synergies and margin improvement as the combined Baker Hughes SPC operation achieves scale economies — Q2 adjusted EBITDA margins already expanded to 29.5% from 25.8% in Q1 [1]
  2. Rising energy demand from AI data center construction and global electrification driving increased drilling activity and wellhead equipment demand across U.S. unconventional and Middle East markets [2]
  3. FlexSteel spoolable pipe technology gaining market share in production and gathering pipeline applications as operators seek faster, lower-cost pipe installation solutions [1]
Key Risks
  1. Oilfield services cyclicality creates binary risk — a sustained oil price decline below $60/bbl or a meaningful drop in U.S. rig counts would compress revenue, margins, and valuation multiples across both Pressure Control and Spoolable Technologies segments [1]
  2. Baker Hughes SPC integration execution risk with 85% Middle East revenue concentration exposing Cactus to geopolitical disruption, currency risk, and customer concentration in a region where the company has limited operating history [1]
  3. Trailing P/E of 52.3x reflects Q1 GAAP losses and integration charges — if normalized earnings disappoint relative to the $3.62 forward EPS estimate, the stock faces de-rating risk from its current $62 level toward tangible book value of $17.80 [4]

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (8/9): Cactus's core business — manufacturing wellhead and pressure control equipment, providing on-site field services for equipment installation and maintenance, and producing spoolable pipe systems (FlexSteel) — is structurally immune to AI disruption [1][2]. These are physical-world operations requiring specialized metallurgy, high-pressure engineering, field crews, and safety-critical equipment that cannot be substituted by software. AI cannot replace a wellhead or install pressure control equipment on a drilling rig. The oil and gas extraction process is inherently physical, and Cactus's products are critical safety infrastructure at well sites globally. A minor exposure exists if AI-optimized drilling reduces the number of wells needed, but this is offset by AI-driven data center construction increasing energy demand.
