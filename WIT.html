Investment Thesis
Wipro offers a 3.98% dividend yield at a reasonable 13.32x forward P/E with minimal debt (D/E 27%) and a net cash balance sheet, making it one of the most accessible income plays in global IT services [4]. FY2026 marked a return to growth with 7.7% YoY IT services revenue expansion and operating margin of 17.2%. CEO Pallia's AI-first strategy — the $1B ai360 ecosystem, Anthropic partnership, Lab45's 50+ Agentic AI solutions, and the new AI-Native Business Unit — positions Wipro to capture enterprise AI transformation spending [2]. Large deal bookings of $1,626M in Q1 FY27 (+12.9% QoQ) signal improving demand. The 150B INR share buyback (~5.7% of equity) and consistent dividend demonstrate shareholder-friendly capital allocation. Wipro achieved 92% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions and 94% renewable energy, earning an ESG rating of 74 [3].
The bear case centers on existential AI disruption risk to the labor-based IT services model. AI-powered code generation, automated testing, and agentic platforms could reduce demand for 30-40% of traditional IT services tasks within 3-5 years, structurally threatening the 230,000-person workforce model that generates Wipro's revenue [1]. IT services segment revenue grew only 1.0% YoY in USD in Q1 FY27, and Q2 guidance of -1.5% to +0.5% sequential CC growth signals near-term softness despite the AI investment. The PEG ratio of 2.76 indicates the market sees limited growth ahead. Wipro has persistently underperformed TCS and Infosys in growth, margins, and deal wins — the stock's discount to peers may be structurally warranted rather than a buying opportunity. CEO Pallia sold shares while receiving stock awards, indicating net insider selling [1]. The $1.945 ADR price has declined 28.94% in the last 52 weeks, reflecting market skepticism about the AI pivot's ability to offset structural headwinds [4].

  2. Near-term growth weakness with IT services at only +1.0% YoY in USD and Q2 FY27 guidance of -1.5% to +0.5% sequential CC growth, contradicting the narrative of an AI-driven services boom [1]
  3. Persistent competitive gap versus TCS and Infosys in growth, margins, and large deal wins could widen as AI transformation spending concentrates with market leaders who invested in AI capabilities earlier [1]
