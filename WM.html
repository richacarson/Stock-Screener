Investment Thesis
WM is compounding through a disciplined diversification strategy — Q2 2026 free cash flow jumped 35% YoY to $1.10 billion, adjusted operating EBITDA margin expanded to 30.9% (with 2026 guidance raised to 31.0-31.2%), and the Stericycle-derived Healthcare Solutions segment is running ahead of integration schedule with EBITDA margin up 200bps to 19% and $32M of $50M in targeted synergies already captured [1]. Return on Equity of 29.84% and a 23-year streak of consecutive dividend increases (2 years from Dividend Aristocrat status) reflect a durable, well-run capital allocator. The core waste collection and disposal business is essential infrastructure immune to AI disruption, while WM is simultaneously using AI and robotics in its own automated recycling facilities to improve margins and safety — a rare combination of defensive resilience and genuine operational innovation.
The bear case centers on valuation and leverage: Forward P/E of 25.17 and Forward PEG of 2.52 price in more growth than the 4.0% Q2 2026 revenue increase (and a trimmed full-year 2026 revenue guide of $26.275-26.475B, down ~0.5% on softer solid waste volumes and delayed RNG pipeline connections) can clearly support [1]. Debt-to-Equity of 235%, elevated by the Stericycle acquisition, leaves less balance sheet flexibility, and heavy recent insider selling — including a $10M sale by CEO Jim Fish near all-time highs and no reported insider purchases in the trailing 90 days — is a soft sentiment signal worth monitoring even if not fundamentally decisive [4]. The planned 5,000-role reduction via automation, while handled through attrition rather than layoffs, introduces execution and labor-relations risk during a multi-year integration of Stericycle's medical waste operations.
Key Catalysts
  1. Completion of the remaining $18M in Stericycle cross-sell synergies (of the $50M target) by Q1 2027, which would further lift Healthcare Solutions margins beyond the current 19% [1]
  2. Continued automated MRF and robotics rollout improving recycling sorting accuracy and reducing contamination-driven cost overruns, supporting the 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin target of 31.0-31.2% [1][2]
  3. Renewable natural gas (RNG) plant connections to pipelines resolving current delays, which management flagged as a drag on 2026 revenue guidance and represents pent-up upside once resolved [1]
Key Risks
  1. Elevated Debt-to-Equity of 235% from the $7.2B Stericycle acquisition limits balance sheet flexibility if integration costs run higher than planned or if a broader economic slowdown pressures solid waste volumes further [1][5]
  2. Valuation (Forward P/E 25.17, Forward PEG 2.52) is not cheap relative to the current mid-single-digit revenue growth rate, leaving limited room for multiple compression if growth or margin execution disappoints
  3. Sustained net insider selling, including a $10M CEO sale near all-time highs and no reported insider buying in the trailing 90 days, is a sentiment flag that should be monitored even though it is not independently a fundamental red flag [4]
