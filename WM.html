Company Profile
Industrials ·Waste Management ·NYSE ·United States
WM is North America's largest provider of comprehensive environmental solutions, operating collection, transfer, landfill disposal, recycling, and renewable natural gas (RNG) assets, plus the WM Healthcare Solutions medical-waste business acquired with Stericycle in 2024. 2025 revenue reached $25.2B, and Q2 2026 revenue was $6.68B (+4.0% YoY) with a 30.9% adjusted operating EBITDA margin. CEO Jim Fish will retire and hand the role to President John Morris effective January 4, 2027. Headquartered in Houston, Texas.

Mindset: MIXED Overall: 6 /10
Through Sinek's lens WM is a mixed but leaning-infinite player: it has a genuine stewardship cause and has repeatedly spent heavily for the long term (Stericycle, RNG, recycling automation), and it is executing an orderly, insider-led CEO succession. What holds it back is a cause still more commonly expressed in operating metrics than as a vision people sacrifice for, an organizational trust story asserted by leadership more than evidenced by data, and a competitive posture built on landfill scarcity rather than learning from rivals.

Investment Thesis
WM is a toll road on an essential, AI-proof service: Q2 2026 revenue rose 4.0% to $6.68B, adjusted operating EBITDA grew 5.5% to $2.07B with margin up 40bps to 30.9%, and free cash flow jumped 34.5% to $1.10B, with full-year FCF guided to $3.75-3.85B and the EBITDA-margin outlook raised to 31.0-31.2% [1]. The Stericycle healthcare vertical, RNG plants, and automated recycling add growth and margin levers on top of price-led core growth, and a 23-year dividend record with ~8.6% five-year dividend CAGR rewards patient owners [1][6][7].
The bear case is valuation and leverage: at ~22x forward earnings and a 1.85 PEG for a business growing revenue ~4% (2026 revenue guide of $26.3-26.5B implies roughly flat-to-low growth after acquisitions anniversary), there is little margin for error, and D/E of 235% limits flexibility until deleveraging completes [1][7]. Collection volumes are soft, RNG economics depend on volatile environmental-credit (RIN) prices, and the January 2027 CEO transition introduces execution risk on Stericycle integration [2]. Sustainalytics rates WM's ESG risk at 18 with a moderate controversy level, so environmental-compliance headlines around landfills remain a recurring, if manageable, overhang [8].
Key Catalysts
  1. Full capture of the $50M Stericycle cross-sell synergy target and healthcare-segment margin expansion toward the 19%+ range, supporting the raised 31.0-31.2% 2026 EBITDA margin guide
  2. New RNG plants and automated MRFs from the $1.6B sustainability program reaching full run-rate in 2026-2027, adding high-margin sustainability EBITDA
  3. Deleveraging back to target range on $3.75-3.85B annual FCF, reopening capacity for buybacks and dividend increases
Key Risks
  1. Leverage of ~235% D/E following the $7.2B Stericycle deal leaves less room if volumes or RIN prices weaken further
  2. Premium valuation (~22x forward P/E, PEG 1.85) on mid-single-digit revenue growth exposes the stock to multiple compression
  3. CEO transition from Jim Fish to John Morris in January 2027 coincides with ongoing Stericycle integration and automation-driven workforce reductions

AI Resilience LOW RISK

AI Risk Protection (9/9): WM's business — physically collecting, hauling, and landfilling waste under local permits and franchise agreements — cannot be substituted by software; analysts frame AI exclusively as a cost lever (route optimization, robotic sorting, automated trucks), and the irreplaceable, permit-constrained landfill network is the moat [1][3]. AI actually widens WM's advantage versus smaller haulers, since only a scaled operator can fund a $1.6B automation program. AI Opportunity (0/1): WM generates no quantifiable revenue from AI products or services; its AI use is internal efficiency, so the tiebreaker point is not earned.
