Petco Health and Wellness Company, Inc. operates as a health and wellness company for pets, offering pet food, supplies, companion animals, grooming, training, and veterinary services through approximately 1,400 pet care centers across the United States and online at petco.com. The company also operates full-service veterinary hospitals under the Vetco brand. Founded in 1965, headquartered in San Diego, California.

Investment Thesis
Petco's FY2025 marked a return to GAAP profitability ($9.1M net income vs. $101.8M loss prior year) with adjusted EBITDA improving 21.3% to $408.2M and free cash flow reaching $186.9M, enabling meaningful deleveraging from 4.2x to 3.0x net debt/EBITDA [1]. The forward P/E of 12.78 on adjusted earnings suggests the market prices in a meaningful earnings recovery, and CEO Anderson's purchase of 2 million shares demonstrates insider conviction in the turnaround trajectory. The pet industry's secular tailwinds — U.S. pet ownership at 66% of households and pet spending exceeding $150B annually — provide a supportive backdrop.
The structural challenges remain severe. Petco carries $2.78B in debt with a 241% debt-to-equity ratio, leaving minimal financial flexibility for the investments needed to modernize a technology infrastructure employees describe as 'held together by 1980s tape' [3][5]. Revenue declined 2.5% in FY2025, and FY2026 guidance of flat to 1.5% growth signals continued share loss to Chewy, Amazon, and mass-market retailers. GAAP profitability is razor-thin at 0.09% margin, meaning any operational setback pushes the company back into losses. The 2025 class action lawsuit alleging hollow ESG claims and unsustainable pandemic-era growth assumptions adds legal risk [4]. At $2.59/share with a $724M market cap, WOOF is priced as a distressed turnaround — appropriate given the balance sheet and competitive headwinds.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued margin expansion through the 'Reach for the Sky' Phase 3 strategy, with adjusted EBITDA guidance of $415-430M representing further improvement if achieved
  2. Acceleration of veterinary services expansion (Vetco hospitals), which carry higher margins than product retail and create customer stickiness through recurring pet healthcare visits
  3. Further debt reduction below 3.0x net leverage, potentially unlocking balance sheet flexibility for technology modernization and competitive investment
Key Risks
  1. Revenue decline of 2.5% YoY and flat-to-1.5% FY2026 guidance signal ongoing market share loss to Chewy (online) and mass retailers (Walmart, Amazon) with superior technology and logistics
  2. Debt load of $2.78B with D/E of 241% creates existential refinancing risk if interest rates remain elevated and the turnaround stalls, with GAAP profitability too thin to absorb any execution miss
  3. Outdated technology infrastructure limits AI and digital commerce capabilities, widening the competitive gap against better-capitalized omnichannel competitors while simultaneously driving employee dissatisfaction and turnover

AI Risk Protection (7/9): Petco's core business is fundamentally protected from AI disruption — pet grooming, veterinary care, and physical retail require in-person service delivery that AI cannot substitute [1]. Pet food and supplies purchasing involves tactile product evaluation, and companion animal sales require physical interaction. The in-store services model (grooming, training, vet hospitals) creates a defensible moat against pure e-commerce displacement. However, Petco faces indirect AI-enabled competitive pressure: AI-driven personalization at Chewy and Amazon improves their ability to capture pet spending online, and AI tools could further automate supply chain optimization for larger competitors with better technology infrastructure [3]. The company's acknowledged technology debt limits its ability to deploy AI defensively. AI Opportunity (0/1): No quantifiable AI-driven revenue.
