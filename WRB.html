Company Profile
Financial Services ·Insurance - Property & Casualty ·NYSE ·United States
W. R. Berkley Corporation is a specialty property and casualty insurance holding company operating through approximately 50 decentralized operating units across two segments: Insurance and Reinsurance & Monoline Excess. Founded by William R. Berkley in 1967 and now led by his son W. Robert Berkley Jr. as CEO, the company underwrites a diversified portfolio including commercial auto, general liability, professional liability, workers compensation, and specialty lines. In March 2026, Mitsui Sumitomo Insurance (MS&AD Group) completed a strategic 15% equity stake acquisition, validating WRB's franchise value. Q2 2026 delivered gross premiums written of $4.1B (+4.2% YoY), operating EPS of $1.27 (+21% YoY), and annualized operating ROE of 20.5%. Market cap approximately $27B.

WRB at 16.47x forward earnings is a best-in-class specialty P&C insurer delivering 20.5% operating ROE and 23% EPS growth -- Q2 2026 gross premiums written hit a record $4.1B (+4.2% YoY) with operating EPS of $1.27 (+21% YoY) [1][2]. The bear case centers on revenue growth deceleration, catastrophe risk, and P&C market cycle dynamics. Revenue growth of approximately 4% YoY is modest, reflecting WRB's underwriting discipline and a softening commercial insurance market where rate adequacy is becoming more competitive [1]. The P&C insurance cycle is inherently mean-reverting -- the current favorable combined ratios reflect a hard market that began in 2019, and a cyclical softening could compress margins and slow premium growth. Catastrophe losses, while below average in H1 2026, remain an ever-present tail risk that can significantly impact quarterly results. The PEG ratio of 2.68 suggests the stock's growth rate (relative to its premium) is less compelling than the absolute PE indicates. Profit margin of 13.01% is below the 20% target, structurally limited by the loss ratio inherent in insurance. The Mitsui 15% stake reduces Berkley family control, which could introduce governance complexity if strategic priorities diverge. Revenue growth of 7% CAGR over 5 years, while solid for insurance, limits the stock's appeal to growth-oriented investors.
