Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Specialty Retail — Eyewear ·NYSE ·United States
Warby Parker Inc. is a vertically integrated lifestyle eyewear company that designs, manufactures, and retails prescription glasses, sunglasses, contact lenses, and eye exams through 287+ retail stores and e-commerce. The company is developing AI-powered smart glasses in partnership with Google and Samsung. Founded in 2010, headquartered in New York City, and a certified B Corporation with its Buy a Pair, Give a Pair program having distributed over 25 million glasses globally.

Investment Thesis
Warby Parker is executing a compelling multi-act growth story: from DTC disruptor to hybrid retailer with 287+ stores to AI-powered smart eyewear platform. Q1 2026 revenue of $242.5M grew 8.3% YoY with improving operational leverage (adjusted SG&A improving from 49.3% to 48.3% of revenue), and full-year 2026 guidance of $959-$976M implies 10-12% growth with $117-$119M in adjusted EBITDA [1]. The $150M Google/Samsung partnership to develop prescription AI smart glasses positions Warby Parker uniquely at the intersection of fashion, healthcare, and technology — no competitor combines prescription lens expertise, 287+ fitting locations, and AI platform partnerships [2]. Active customers of 2.69M with $331 average revenue per customer (up 6.9% YoY) demonstrate brand strength and pricing power [1]. Five-year revenue CAGR of 17.2% from ~$394M to $872M validates consistent execution.
The primary concern is near-zero net profitability at a premium valuation. Forward P/E of 46x with net margin of just 0.15% prices in a profitability inflection that has not yet materialized [4]. Revenue growth has decelerated from 37% (2021) to 13% (2025) to 8.3% (Q1 2026), raising questions about the growth runway. The smart glasses category has a mixed track record — Google Glass (2013) and Snap Spectacles (2016) failed to achieve mass adoption — and Warby Parker is betting a significant portion of its growth narrative on an unproven consumer category against Meta's established Ray-Ban smart glasses ecosystem with far greater resources. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 12.2% trails the 15-20% margins of mature specialty retail peers, and the ROE of 0.46% reflects no meaningful return on shareholder equity [4]. EssilorLuxottica's ~80% premium eyewear market share and Meta partnership create formidable competitive barriers.
Key Catalysts
  1. AI-powered smart glasses launch with Google/Samsung in late 2026, entering a multi-billion dollar wearable technology market with differentiated prescription lens capability no competitor can match
  2. Profitability inflection as operating leverage improves — 50 new stores in 2026 scale fixed costs, with path to 15%+ adjusted EBITDA margins as store count matures toward long-term 900+ store target
  3. Average revenue per customer expansion beyond $331 through cross-selling contact lenses, eye exams, progressive lenses, and eventually smart glasses to the 2.69M active customer base
Key Risks
  1. Forward P/E of 46x with 0.15% net margin leaves no margin of safety — any miss on 10-12% revenue guidance or smart glasses launch delays could trigger significant multiple compression
  2. Smart glasses category risk: Google Glass, Snap Spectacles, and other AI eyewear have failed to achieve mass adoption; consumer appetite for head-worn technology remains unproven at scale
  3. Revenue growth deceleration from 37% (2021) to 8.3% (Q1 2026) raises questions about store-driven expansion runway in an increasingly competitive optical retail market dominated by EssilorLuxottica

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience LOW RISK
8
AI Risk Protection (7/9): Warby Parker's core business — manufacturing and selling physical eyeglasses through 287+ retail stores with in-store eye exams — is structurally AI-resistant [1]. Physical glasses cannot be digitized, eye exams require licensed optometrists and equipment, and the try-on-and-fit experience creates switching costs. The prescription eyewear market is healthcare-adjacent with regulatory requirements. Some AI pressure on the DTC/online channel from personalized shopping tools and virtual try-on competitors, but the hybrid physical model insulates the core. AI Opportunity (1/1): The $150M Google/Samsung partnership to develop AI-powered smart glasses with Android XR and Gemini AI represents direct AI revenue potential in a new category [2]. AI Virtual Try-On technology already ships to millions of customers. Warby Parker's prescription lens capability gives it a differentiated entry point no purely tech-focused competitor can match.
