Company Profile
Technology ·Autonomous Driving Software ·NASDAQ ·China
WeRide Inc. is a Guangzhou-based autonomous driving technology company founded in 2017 by Tony Han, dual-listed on NASDAQ (WRD) and HKEX. It develops L2++ to L4 autonomous driving solutions across Robotaxi, Robobus, Robovan, Robosweeper and ADAS (WRD 3.0, adopted by nearly 30 vehicle models). H1 2026 revenue rose 73.3% to RMB345.9M with gross margin of 36.6%, international revenue grew 154.4%, and the company targets a global robotaxi fleet above 2,600 by end of 2026. Management targets first single-quarter positive cash flow in 2028 and full-year breakeven in 2029. Market cap approximately $1.36B at $4.14 per share.

Investment Thesis
WeRide is one of the few autonomous driving companies with real revenue growth across several product lines and countries. H1 2026 revenue rose 73.3% to RMB345.9M, gross margin expanded to 36.6% from 30.6%, and international revenue grew 154.4% [1][3]. The fleet is expected to roughly double from about 1,300 robotaxis in April to more than 2,600 by year-end, helped by a 2,000-vehicle plan with Geely's Farizon, while WRD 3.0 ADAS is now on nearly 30 vehicle models [3][4]. With about RMB5.38B of cash against RMB616M of debt and a consensus price target of $12.32 against a $4.14 share price, the market is pricing in substantial execution risk [7].
The bear case is ongoing cash burn and China regulation. The FY2025 net loss was RMB1.65B on RMB684.6M revenue, Q2 2026 net loss stayed near RMB401M, and management does not expect full-year breakeven until 2029 [1][6]. China's reported pause on new self-driving approvals affects its home market, and the stock is near its 52-week low of $4.03 after falling from $11.15 [3][7]. As a China-based ADR it also faces US-China technology and delisting risk, and Waymo, Baidu and Tesla all have deeper resources.
Key Catalysts
  1. Fleet expansion to more than 2,600 robotaxis by end of 2026, including the 2,000-unit Geely Farizon program, which would roughly double operating scale [3][4]
  2. Continued international growth (H1 2026 international revenue +154.4%) through fully driverless commercial service in Dubai and Switzerland [1][5]
  3. Progress toward the 2028 single-quarter positive cash flow target, with gross margin already up to 36.6% [1]
Key Risks
  1. Cash burn: FY2025 net loss of RMB1.65B on RMB684.6M revenue, with full-year breakeven not targeted until 2029 [1][6]
  2. China's pause on new autonomous driving approvals could slow domestic fleet growth if it is not, as management claims, short-term [3]
  3. US-China geopolitical risk for a China-based ADR, plus competition from better-funded Waymo, Baidu Apollo and Tesla [3][7]
