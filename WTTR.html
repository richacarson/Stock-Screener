Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Select Water Solutions provides water management and chemical technology solutions to the U.S. energy industry. Rebranded from Select Energy Services in May 2023, the company operates through three segments: Water Infrastructure (recycling, gathering, disposal via permanent pipeline networks), Water Services (sourcing, transfer, monitoring, automation), and Chemical Technologies (fracturing polymers, production chemicals, water treatment). Under founder-CEO John Schmitz, WTTR has pivoted toward recurring water infrastructure revenue, posting record Q2 2026 consolidated revenue of $395.8M (+8.7% YoY) with record Water Infrastructure ($102M) and Chemical Technologies ($96M) segment revenues. Market cap ~$2.5B.

Investment Thesis
Select Water Solutions at $2.5B market cap offers a differentiated water infrastructure story within the energy sector — the pivot from transactional services to permanent infrastructure (pipelines, recycling facilities, treatment plants) is creating recurring revenue with improving unit economics. Q2 2026 record revenue of $395.8M grew 8.7% YoY, with Water Infrastructure segment revenue up 26% YoY to a record $102M and Chemical Technologies reaching a record $96M [2]. The balance sheet is pristine at 25.7% D/E, providing ample capacity for the $250-290M annual capex buildout. CEO Schmitz's 18-year founder-led tenure provides strategic continuity, and the Inspire Impact Score of 65 (ALIGNED) with zero negative attributions makes WTTR attractive for faith-based investors. Management guided for continued double-digit growth into 2027 supported by a robust project pipeline [2].
The bear case centers on rich valuation, thin profitability, and cyclical exposure. At 28.7x forward earnings and 68.9x trailing P/E, the stock prices in substantial future growth from the infrastructure pivot — any execution misstep or slowdown in E&P activity would trigger meaningful multiple compression [4]. The 2.2% profit margin and 3.1% ROE are critically weak for a company trading at nearly 70x earnings, indicating the infrastructure buildout is consuming nearly all operating margin. Revenue peaked at $1.59B in 2023 and declined to $1.41B in 2025, raising questions about whether the 2026 recovery is sustainable or merely reflects a cyclical uptick. EPS of $0.28 supports a $19.29 stock price only on growth expectations, not current earnings power. The company's 100% payout ratio (dividend rate $0.28 equals trailing EPS $0.28) leaves no margin of safety for the dividend if earnings dip. Oil and gas production activity remains the ultimate demand driver, creating cyclical vulnerability that the infrastructure pivot has not fully eliminated [1].
