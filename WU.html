Company Profile
Financial Services ·Credit Services ·NYSE ·United States
The Western Union Company provides cross-border and domestic money transfer services, digital payments, and consumer financial services worldwide. Operating through Consumer Money Transfer and Consumer Services segments, the company facilitates remittances via a network of ~500,000 retail agent locations across 200+ countries. Q2 2026 adjusted revenue was $1B (-1% YoY), with branded digital transactions surging 25%. Management slashed FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $1.25-$1.35 (from $1.75-$1.85), citing margin pressures and retail headwinds. The Intermex acquisition is expected to close September 1, 2026. Headquartered in Denver, Colorado.
7,500 Employees ·www.westernunion.com

Investment Thesis
Western Union at 4.96x forward earnings with a 12.86% dividend yield offers deep value characteristics if the digital transformation can stabilize the business. Q2 2026 showed branded digital transactions surging 25% with Consumer Services revenue up 12%, and adjusted revenue decline moderating to just -1% YoY [1]. The Intermex acquisition (closing Sept. 1) should deliver the first year of revenue growth since 2021 through Latin American corridor expansion, with FY2026 adjusted revenue guidance of 4-6% growth including the deal. ROE of 43.97% and Forward P/E of 4.96x reflect efficient capital deployment at a deeply discounted valuation [2]. The 200+ country regulatory moat remains a real barrier to fintech entrants, and WU's Solana stablecoin pilot positions the company at the intersection of traditional remittances and next-gen payment rails.
The bear case is structural and severe. The mid-year EPS guidance slash from $1.75-$1.85 to $1.25-$1.35 — a 27% reduction — signals accelerating earnings deterioration that the Intermex acquisition cannot mask [1]. Five consecutive years of organic revenue decline reflect digital-native competitors (Wise, Remitly, Revolut) systematically undercutting WU's take rates by 50-80%, and the G20 target to cut average remittance costs to 3% by 2030 will compress pricing further [3]. D/E of 295% — just 5 percentage points from the scoring floor — leaves almost no balance sheet flexibility for transformation investment, and the 12.86% yield is a classic distress signal with the payout ratio climbing to 72% against declining earnings. Glassdoor reviews describe a demoralized workforce with political internal culture and avoidant leadership, undermining the human capital needed for digital reinvention [4]. AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
5
AI Risk Protection (4/9): Western Union's core cross-border money transfer business is EXPOSED to AI-enabled disruption. AI-powered fintech platforms (Wise, Remitly, Revolut) use machine learning to optimize FX rates, reduce compliance costs, and deliver superior digital experiences at significantly lower fees, directly undercutting WU's agent-based model [3]. AI-driven fraud detection and KYC/AML compliance — historically WU competitive advantages — are being commoditized and made available to any fintech entrant. However, WU retains meaningful defensive moats: money transmission licenses across 200+ countries represent years of regulatory investment that AI cannot shortcut, and the physical agent network remains essential for cash-based populations in developing markets where digital infrastructure is limited. AI does not eliminate the need for cross-border payments — it enables more efficient delivery, creating substitution risk in channels rather than demand destruction. AI Opportunity (1/1): WU's HCLTech AI partnership generates measurable operational savings through compliance automation and fraud detection, and the Solana stablecoin pilot positions WU at the intersection of traditional remittances and crypto rails [3]. The 25% digital transaction growth in Q2 2026 is partly enabled by AI-driven customer acquisition and retention.
