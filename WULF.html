Company Profile

TeraWulf is a digital infrastructure company that has pivoted from Bitcoin mining to high-performance computing (HPC) hosting for AI workloads. Operates the Lake Mariner facility in New York State. Q2 2026 revenue of $44.8M with HPC lease revenue of $31.9M representing 71% of total revenue. Approximately $3.0B in cash and restricted cash. Market cap ~$5.5B.

Investment Thesis
TeraWulf has executed one of the most compelling strategic pivots in digital infrastructure — from Bitcoin mining to AI data center hosting — winning a landmark ~$19B 20-year Anthropic lease that provides decades of contracted revenue visibility [2]. HPC lease revenue reached $31.9M in Q2 2026 (71% of total), up 52% sequentially, with 102 MW operational and 336 MW under construction [1]. The company expanded aggressively into Kentucky (Justified site for Anthropic) and acquired the gigawatt-scale Muskie Data Campus, targeting 250-500 MW of new contracted capacity annually. With $2.6B in cash and restricted cash, TeraWulf has liquidity to fund expansion. Analysts anticipate 98% revenue growth for full-year 2026 as HPC capacity ramps. The Inspire Impact Score of 53 (ALIGNED) makes this one of the few pure-play AI infrastructure companies accessible to faith-based investors.
The bear case centers on extreme financial profile and execution risk. D/E of 3549.6% reflects massive leverage relative to a thin equity base, and the stock has declined 41% from $26.26 to $15.49, destroying significant shareholder value. Trailing EPS of -$4.46 and forward EPS of -$0.24 confirm the path to GAAP profitability is distant — operating margins of -3070% reflect massive infrastructure investment relative to the $165M TTM revenue base. Total revenue declined 6% YoY as bitcoin mining contracted faster than HPC scaled [1]. The $19B Anthropic contract, while transformative, concentrates customer risk in a single counterparty — any renegotiation or financial difficulty at Anthropic would materially impact TeraWulf's growth trajectory. Greenwashing allegations are a credibility concern — the company walked back its 'zero-carbon' branding after critics showed it doesn't purchase RECs and the Nautilus nuclear facility was sold [3]. Building 250-500 MW annually with only 141 employees creates operational scalability risk on permitting, grid interconnection, and construction. Free cash flow of -$234M confirms the company is in heavy cash burn mode.
