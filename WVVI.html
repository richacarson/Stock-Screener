Company Profile
Consumer Defensive ·Beverages - Wineries & Distilleries ·NCM ·United States
Willamette Valley Vineyards produces and sells premium Oregon wines across multiple brands including Willamette Valley Vineyards (Pinot Noir, Chardonnay), Domaine Willamette (sparkling), Griffin Creek, Pambrun, and Maison Bleue. Founded in 1983 by Jim Bernau as a community-owned winery with over 24,000 shareholders, the company operates through Direct Sales (tasting rooms, wine club, online) and Distributor Sales channels. Q1 2026 revenue rose 9.5% YoY to $8.26M driven by a 22.2% increase in case sales, though the TTM revenue of $37.75M is down 1.6% YoY and the company reported a TTM net loss. Wine.com founder Michael Osborn was appointed CEO in May 2025 to lead the company's next growth chapter.

Investment Thesis
Willamette Valley Vineyards at $1.87 with a $9.3M market cap is a deeply undervalued premium Oregon winery trading below book value, with Q1 2026 revenue up 9.5% to $8.26M and case sales surging 22.2% YoY as the new leadership team under Wine.com founder Michael Osborn begins to take hold [1][2]. The company's community ownership model with 24,000+ shareholders creates a unique competitive moat through brand loyalty and direct-to-consumer distribution, while LIVE and Salmon-Safe sustainability certifications position the brand for the growing conscious consumer segment. Gross margins of 59-63% demonstrate pricing power for premium Oregon Pinot Noir, and broad insider buying — CEO, President, and three directors all purchasing shares on the open market in 2025-2026 — signals management conviction that the stock is mispriced [5].
The bear case centers on persistent unprofitability and severe illiquidity. WVVI has reported net losses in three of the last four fiscal years, with FY2025 showing a $918K loss on $37.2M revenue (-5.9% margin), and TTM revenue is declining 1.6% in a U.S. wine market facing secular headwinds from younger consumers shifting to spirits, cannabis, and moderation trends [1][5]. Daily dollar volume of only $28K makes the stock essentially untradeable for any institutional investor, and the $9.3M market cap places WVVI in penny stock territory with virtually no analyst coverage. The balance sheet carries $27.7M in debt against only $590K cash, creating meaningful financial risk if wine sales decline further, and the CEO transition from a founder-led culture to a professional manager introduces execution risk during a challenging period for the U.S. wine industry [2][5].
