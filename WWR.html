Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NYSE American ·United States
Westwater Resources is a pre-revenue energy technology company developing domestic battery-grade natural graphite supply for the U.S. electric vehicle and energy storage industries, having pivoted from its historical uranium mining business. Its Kellyton Graphite Processing Plant in Alabama has approximately $129.6M invested against a $245M Phase I budget, sourced from the Coosa Graphite Deposit — the largest natural flake graphite deposit in the contiguous United States [1][2]. EXIM Bank approved a $25M loan for Kellyton in 2026, partially closing the financing gap. However, SK On terminated its Products Procurement Agreement, removing a committed offtake customer, and the company raised going-concern doubt in recent filings due to widening losses and funding shortfalls [1][2]. H1 2026 net loss was $9.0M under CEO Frank Bakker. Market cap approximately $73M.

Investment Thesis
Westwater Resources is a strategically positioned but deeply speculative bet on U.S. domestic battery-grade graphite supply, anchored by Coosa — the largest natural flake graphite deposit in the contiguous United States — and $129.6M already invested in the Kellyton Graphite Processing Plant [1][2]. EXIM Bank's approval of a $25M loan for Kellyton is a meaningful validation of the project and partially closes the financing gap. Management targets initial production within approximately 12 months of securing remaining financing, which would make Westwater one of the first vertically integrated U.S. battery-anode graphite producers at a moment of intensifying policy focus on de-risking critical mineral supply chains from China [2]. The Inspire Impact Score of +48 (ALIGNED) with seven positive attributions including Low Ecological Impact and Ethical Supply Chain Management makes this one of the more faith-aligned names in the screen.
The bear case is severe. Westwater fails IOWN's liquidity floor outright — daily dollar volume of roughly $290,000 is well below the $1M threshold, making the stock difficult to trade in institutional size and driving the Infrastructure score to 0 [4]. The company has raised going-concern doubt in recent filings due to widening losses ($9M net loss in H1 2026), rapidly declining cash (from $41.5M at year-end 2025 to approximately $13M), and a substantial remaining financing gap for Phase I [1]. SK On's termination of its Products Procurement Agreement removed a committed offtake customer, undermining the demand case for Kellyton output [1]. Even with the $25M EXIM loan, approximately $90M+ in additional financing is required — likely at severely dilutive terms given the micro-cap market cap of $73M. With only 21 employees, the organization has limited bench strength to simultaneously manage financing, permitting, construction, and eventual plant commissioning. The stock's 52-week range of $0.26 to $4.26 reflects extreme volatility typical of micro-cap development stories.
