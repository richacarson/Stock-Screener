Company Profile
Basic Materials ·Other Industrial Metals & Mining ·NYSE American ·United States
Westwater Resources is a pre-revenue energy technology company developing domestic battery-grade natural graphite supply for the U.S. electric vehicle and energy storage industries, having pivoted from its historical uranium mining business. Its Kellyton Graphite Processing Plant in Alabama has approximately $129.6M invested against a $245M Phase I budget, sourced from the Coosa Graphite Deposit — the largest natural flake graphite deposit in the contiguous United States — with initial production expected within roughly 12 months of securing the remaining ~$115M in project financing [1][2]. The company filed a Clean Water Act NPDES permit for Coosa in March 2026, but lost a key offtake customer when SK On terminated its Products Procurement Agreement, and reported a Q1 2026 net loss of $4.7M [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Westwater Resources is a strategically positioned but deeply speculative bet on U.S. domestic battery-grade graphite supply, anchored by the largest natural flake graphite deposit in the contiguous United States and $129.6M already invested in the Kellyton Graphite Processing Plant [1][2]. The company filed a key Clean Water Act NPDES permit for the Coosa Graphite Deposit in March 2026, advancing the regulatory pathway, and holds $41.5M in cash providing over a year of non-discretionary runway [2]. If the remaining ~$115M in Phase I financing is secured, management guides to initial production within approximately 12 months, which would make Westwater one of the first vertically integrated U.S. battery-anode graphite producers at a moment of intensifying policy focus on de-risking critical mineral supply chains from China [1][2].
The bear case is severe. Westwater fails IOWN's liquidity floor outright — daily dollar volume of roughly $289,000 is well below the $1M threshold, making the stock difficult to trade in institutional size and driving the Infrastructure score to 0 [3]. The company is pre-revenue with ROE of -18.70%, and SK On's termination of its Products Procurement Agreement removed a committed offtake customer for a portion of planned Phase I capacity, a material setback to the demand case [1][2]. Q1 2026 net loss widened to $4.7M from $2.7M a year earlier, and the ~$115M funding gap for Phase I remains unresolved, meaning further dilutive capital raises at depressed share prices are likely. With only 21 employees, the organization has limited bench strength to simultaneously manage financing, permitting, and eventual plant commissioning.
