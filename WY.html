Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Specialty ·NYQ ·United States
Weyerhaeuser is one of the world's largest private owners of timberlands, owning or controlling over 10 million acres in the U.S. and managing additional public timberlands under long-term licenses in Canada. Founded in 1900 and structured as a timber REIT, the company operates through Timberlands, Real Estate/Energy/Natural Resources, and Wood Products segments, generating $6.9B in FY2025 revenue. Q2 2026 revenue was approximately $1.87B with adjusted EBITDA of $310M, including a $71M gain from timberlands divestitures. The company manages 100% of its timberlands on a sustainably certified basis [1][2].

Investment Thesis
Weyerhaeuser at $19.61 with a $14.1B market cap is the premier U.S. timber REIT offering exposure to housing recovery and sustainable natural resource ownership. The company controls 10M+ acres of timberlands — an irreplaceable, self-renewing asset base that has generated value for 126 years. The dividend yields 4.28% with 49 years of continuous payment, and D/E of 57.6% reflects conservative leverage for a REIT [1]. Q2 2026 lumber realizations rose 15% QoQ on seasonal demand and supply constraints, and earnings of $0.23/share included a $71M timberlands divestiture gain [1]. The AI Resilience score of 9 reflects complete structural immunity to AI disruption, and the Infinite Game score of 6 captures the inherently long-term nature of managing timber on multi-decade rotation cycles [2].
The bear case centers on the protracted housing downturn. Revenue declined 0.9% YoY and is down 32% from the 2022 peak of $10.2B, reflecting weak single-family housing starts from elevated mortgage rates — approximately 65% of demand comes from residential construction [1]. Profit margin of 6.9% and ROE of 4.97% are deeply compressed from cycle peaks, with forward P/E of 31.7 pricing in significant earnings recovery that depends on housing recovery timing [1]. The payout ratio of 129.2% exceeds 100%, meaning the company is distributing more than GAAP earnings — sustainable for a REIT with timber depletion adjustments but signaling limited dividend growth capacity until earnings recover. OSB remains pressured by oversupply and weak demand. Q3 guidance calls for wood products earnings to decline from Q2 due to higher fuel costs and mix shift [1]. The 5yr dividend growth CAGR of 10.5% is distorted by the abnormally low 2020 COVID base ($0.51/share), masking the actual declining dividend trend since 2022 ($2.17 to $0.84 in 2025). The stock trades at 10% below its 50-day SMA of $21.81.
Key Catalysts
  1. Housing recovery — any decline in mortgage rates would accelerate single-family housing starts, directly driving lumber, OSB, and EWP demand that constitutes ~65% of Weyerhaeuser's revenue base [1]
  2. Irreplaceable timberland asset base of 10M+ acres appreciates over time as sustainably managed timber grows, providing biological growth optionality regardless of commodity prices [2]
  3. Climate solutions and carbon credit monetization represent emerging revenue streams from the timberland portfolio, with Weyerhaeuser's science-based GHG targets positioning it as a sustainability leader [3]
Key Risks
  1. Protracted housing downturn — elevated mortgage rates have suppressed single-family starts below long-term averages, compressing revenue 32% from 2022 peak; Q3 2026 guidance calls for declining wood products earnings [1]
  2. Payout ratio exceeds 100% (129.2%) — while sustainable for a timber REIT with non-cash depletion charges, further dividend cuts risk if earnings remain depressed; total dividends have already declined from $2.17 (2022) to $0.84 (2025) [1]
  3. Commodity price exposure — lumber and OSB prices are volatile and subject to supply-demand dynamics beyond management control; OSB remains pressured by oversupply even as lumber benefits from seasonal strength [1]
