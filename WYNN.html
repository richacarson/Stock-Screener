Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Resorts & Casinos ·NASDAQ ·United States
Wynn Resorts, Limited develops, owns, and operates ultra-luxury destination casino resorts, holding more Forbes Five-Star awards than any independent hotel company in the world across its Wynn Las Vegas, Encore Boston Harbor, Wynn Macau, and Wynn Palace (Cotai) properties. Under CEO Craig Billings, who took over in February 2022 after serving as CFO, the company reported FY2025 operating revenue of $7.14 billion and is investing $1.1 billion in a new Encore Tower in Las Vegas while building Wynn Al Marjan Island in the UAE -- the first legal casino resort in the Middle East, expected to open in 2027.

Investment Thesis
Wynn Resorts trades at a reasonable 16.3x forward earnings and 0.78 PEG for an ultra-premium gaming operator with accelerating Macau momentum -- Wynn Palace revenue rose $113.8M year-over-year in Q2 2026 -- and a brand moat that is among the most AI-resistant in global hospitality [1][2][4]. The $1.1B Encore Tower redevelopment and the first-mover Wynn Al Marjan Island project in the UAE give the company genuine long-duration growth optionality beyond the mature Macau/Las Vegas duopoly, while CEO Craig Billings has demonstrated financial discipline since stepping up from CFO in 2022 [1][2].
The bear case centers on gaming-sector cyclicality and geopolitical/regulatory concentration: roughly half of Wynn's revenue still depends on Macau, where Chinese consumer spending and regulatory posture can shift abruptly, and the UAE project carries meaningful execution risk as the first legal casino resort in an unproven regulatory jurisdiction [1][2]. The Inspire Impact Score of -1 (MIXED) reflects the fundamental tension in the gaming business model -- a single Gambling attribution offsets an otherwise long list of positive ESG attributions spanning labor practices, human rights, and sustainability, meaning faith-based investors will weigh the core product itself, not just Wynn's operating practices [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Wynn Al Marjan Island opening in 2027 as the first legal casino resort in the Middle East, opening an entirely new, high-growth regional market years ahead of any competitor [2]
  2. Continued Macau recovery momentum, with Wynn Palace revenue up $113.8M and Wynn Macau up $7.3M year-over-year in Q2 2026, suggesting further share gains in the ultra-premium segment [1]
  3. Completion of the $1.1B Encore Tower in Las Vegas with AI-integrated smart-room and concierge technology, which could support premium room rate growth upon opening [2]
Key Risks
  1. Heavy revenue concentration in Macau exposes Wynn to Chinese regulatory policy shifts, currency controls, and consumer spending cycles largely outside management's control [1]
  2. Wynn Al Marjan Island carries substantial execution and regulatory risk as a first-of-its-kind project in a jurisdiction with no prior legal gaming precedent, and delays or cost overruns could pressure the balance sheet [2]
  3. A stressed or negative stockholders' equity position (reflected in the exclusion of standard ROE/D/E metrics from this analysis) signals a highly leveraged capital structure that leaves less room for error during a gaming-demand downturn [4]
