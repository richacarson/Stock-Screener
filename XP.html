Investment Thesis
XP Inc. has established itself as Brazil's leading independent financial services platform, with combined client assets reaching R$2.2T (+17% YoY) and R$28B in net new money in Q2 2026, disrupting the traditional banking oligopoly through a technology-driven, low-fee open platform model [1]. The company delivered consistent growth from R$11.9B revenue in 2021 to R$17.8B in FY2025 and R$18.6B TTM, with wholesale revenue up 32% and corporate revenue up 117% as XP diversifies beyond retail brokerage into institutional and banking verticals [1]. At a forward P/E of 7.39, trailing P/E of 8.59, and a PEG ratio of just 0.46, XP trades at a significant discount to global fintech peers on both an earnings and growth-adjusted basis, reflecting both Brazil country risk and emerging market valuation compression. Management continues to target double-digit 2026 revenue growth, combined with 22.56% ROE and 28.54% profit margin, presenting an attractive risk-reward profile for investors willing to accept Brazilian macro exposure.
The primary concerns center on country risk and governance. Brazil's macroeconomic volatility — including currency fluctuations, elevated interest rates, and political uncertainty — creates persistent headwinds; CEO Thiago Maffra noted Q2 2026 results would have shown low-teens revenue growth without wider credit spreads and softer debt-capital-markets issuance [1][5]. The D/E ratio of 642% is standard for financial services but amplifies sensitivity to credit cycles and interest rate movements. The structured notes (COE) controversy, involving complaints about mis-selling high-risk products to moderate-risk clients and unilateral position liquidations, raises governance questions about whether growth targets are consistently prioritized over client welfare [4]. A May 2026 board reshuffle — a director resignation and a new interim independent director installed as ESG committee chair — shows governance is actively evolving but also underscores it is not yet fully settled [4]. Competition from BTG Pactual's digital platform and traditional banks' fintech initiatives could pressure margins in XP's core investment distribution business.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued double-digit revenue growth target for 2026, with wholesale/corporate revenue (+32%/+117% in Q2) diversifying XP beyond retail brokerage commissions toward more resilient institutional revenue
  2. 26%+ of client assets now under fee-based advisory frameworks, shifting the revenue mix toward stable, recurring income and reducing sensitivity to transaction volumes
  3. Continued disruption of Brazil's banking oligopoly as financial literacy and digital adoption increase among Brazil's 200M+ population, expanding the addressable market toward the R$2.2T+ combined client asset base
Key Risks
  1. Brazilian macroeconomic volatility — currency depreciation, elevated interest rates, and political uncertainty — continues to dampen results, as management itself attributed the Q2 2026 growth shortfall to wider credit spreads and weaker debt issuance
  2. Structured notes (COE) mis-selling complaints and unilateral liquidation practices represent a reputational and regulatory risk that could erode client trust and invite regulatory scrutiny
  3. Intensifying competition from BTG Pactual's digital platform and traditional banks' fintech initiatives could pressure take rates and client acquisition costs in XP's core investment distribution business
