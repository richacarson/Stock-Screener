Company Profile
Industrials ·Electrical Equipment & Parts ·NASDAQ ·United States
Expion Energy (formerly Expion360) designs, assembles, manufactures, and sells lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) batteries and accessories for recreational vehicles, marine, golf, home energy storage, industrial, and off-the-grid applications. Founded in 2016 and headquartered in Redmond, Oregon, the company rebranded in August 2026 after acquiring oil and gas exploration assets in Eastern Louisiana. Full year 2025 revenue was $9.65M (+71.5% YoY) on a base of $5.6M, with a net loss of $6.2M. The company has 22 full-time employees and entered a strategic partnership for the DASGen Hybrid Energy Storage System for construction and industrial applications.

Investment Thesis
Expion Energy's investment case rests on the long-term secular growth in energy storage demand driven by AI data centers, grid resilience needs, and electrification trends. The company's LiFePO4 batteries offer safety and longevity advantages, and FY2025 revenue of $9.65M (+71.5% YoY) demonstrated the ability to scale from a small base [1]. The DASGen Hybrid Energy Storage System partnership opens the industrial/construction market, and the e360 Home Energy Storage solutions target residential applications. Gross margins improved to 25.3% in Q1 2026 as the company shifted to higher-margin products, and the $3.4M oil and gas acquisition could provide near-term cash flow to fund battery operations [2].
The bear case is overwhelming for a company of this profile. Revenue declined 24% YoY in Q1 2026 to $1.6M, reversing the FY2025 growth momentum and suggesting the 71% annual growth was driven by a temporary OEM inventory build rather than sustainable demand [1]. The net loss of $6.2M against $9.65M revenue represents a -64% net margin, and with only $1.5M cash and 22 employees, the company faces severe liquidity risk and potential dilutive capital raises. Two CEO changes in under one year destroy organizational continuity and strategic coherence. The oil and gas acquisition contradicts the clean energy storage narrative and may confuse potential battery customers and partners. The company competes against Battle Born, RELiON, and Chinese manufacturers with vastly greater scale, manufacturing efficiency, and distribution. The elevated average daily volume of 2.5M shares appears driven by speculative retail trading rather than institutional conviction, creating volatility risk.
