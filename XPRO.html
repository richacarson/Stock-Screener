Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Expro Ltd (formerly Expro Group Holdings N.V.) provides energy services and solutions across the full well lifecycle, including well construction (tubular running, cementing, drilling), well flow management, subsea well access, and well intervention and integrity. Formed through the 2021 merger of legacy Expro and Frank's International, the company serves oil, gas, and emerging energy markets in approximately 60 countries. CEO Mike Jardon (since 2021) leads 7,000 employees from Houston, Texas. In May 2026, Expro acquired Enhanced Well Technologies AS from Shell Ventures for ~NOK 2B. FY2026 guidance: revenue $1.65-1.7B, adj EBITDA $355-365M.

Investment Thesis
Expro offers a conservatively financed energy services platform with D/E of just 11.4% and FY2026 guidance of $1.65-1.7B revenue with adj EBITDA of $355-365M [1][3]. Q2 2026 showed sequential improvement with $393M revenue (up 7% from Q1) and $76M adjusted EBITDA (19.3% margin), with management guiding H2 margins above 24% and Q4 above 26% [1]. Forward PEG of 0.74 is the standout metric, indicating the market expects significant earnings growth as margins expand from trough levels. The May 2026 acquisition of Enhanced Well Technologies from Shell Ventures for ~NOK 2B broadens the technology portfolio [2]. At 13.5x forward earnings, the stock offers value if the margin expansion thesis materializes.
The bear case centers on persistently weak current profitability and cyclical risk. Net margin of 1.33% and ROE of 1.37% are barely above breakeven — any operational disruption could push into losses [3]. Trailing P/E of 88.7x confirms trough earnings. Revenue declined 7% YoY impacted by Middle East conflict, and the forward PEG of 0.74 implicitly requires substantial earnings growth that depends on the macro recovery in offshore drilling activity [1]. The oilfield services sector faces structural headwinds from energy transition investment shifts, and Expro's geothermal/CCUS initiatives remain too small to offset any decline in traditional oil and gas capex. Earnings growth of -88.5% YoY demonstrates the company's sensitivity to commodity cycles and geopolitical disruption [3]. Mid-tier positioning between SLB/Halliburton and pure-play specialists limits pricing power.

Key Risks

  2. Revenue decline of 7% YoY driven by Middle East conflict disruptions demonstrates the company's vulnerability to geopolitical risk and commodity cycle sensitivity [1]
  3. Structural energy transition away from fossil fuels threatens long-term demand for core oilfield services, and geothermal/CCUS diversification remains unproven at commercial scale [4]
