Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ·NYSE ·United States
Expro Group Holdings N.V. provides energy services and solutions across the full well lifecycle, including well construction, well flow management, subsea well access, and production optimization. Formed through the 2021 merger of legacy Expro and Frank's International, the company serves oil, gas, and emerging energy markets globally with operations in approximately 60 countries.

Investment Thesis
Expro offers a conservatively financed energy services platform with debt-to-equity of just 11% and a $2.5B backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility [1][3]. The Q2 2026 results showed sequential improvement with $393M revenue (up 7% from Q1) and $76M adjusted EBITDA, and the company is targeting mid-20s EBITDA margins as offshore activity strengthens [1]. The merger integration with Frank's International is largely complete, providing a full-lifecycle well services offering that differentiates from smaller competitors. At 17.94x forward earnings, the valuation is reasonable if margin expansion materializes.
The bear case centers on persistently weak profitability — net margin of 2.32% and ROE of 3.42% are woefully inadequate for a company trading at 35.8x trailing earnings [3]. Revenue declined 6.2% in 2025 and 2026 guidance implies flat growth at best, meaning the P/E compression thesis depends entirely on margin improvement rather than top-line growth [2]. The oilfield services sector faces structural headwinds from energy transition investment shifts, and Expro's geothermal/CCUS initiatives remain too small to offset any decline in traditional oil and gas capex. The PEG ratio of 4.17 signals the market is paying a growth premium for a company that is not growing [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Offshore drilling activity recovery driving backlog conversion of the $2.5B order book, particularly deepwater projects in West Africa, Brazil, and the Middle East
  2. EBITDA margin expansion toward mid-20s percentage target through integration synergies, operational leverage, and higher-margin subsea well access contracts
  3. Geothermal and CCUS revenue ramping from pilot projects to meaningful commercial scale, validating the energy transition diversification strategy
Key Risks
  1. Net profit margin of 2.32% and ROE of 3.42% leave virtually no margin of safety — any operational disruption or pricing pressure could push the company into losses
  2. Flat 2026 revenue guidance of $1.60-1.65B against a PEG of 4.17 means the stock is priced for growth that is not materializing, creating significant downside risk on any earnings miss
  3. Structural energy transition away from fossil fuels threatens long-term demand for core oilfield services, and the geothermal/CCUS pivot remains unproven at commercial scale
