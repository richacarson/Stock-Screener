Company Profile
Healthcare ·Medical Instruments & Supplies ·NASDAQ ·United States
Dentsply Sirona is the world's largest manufacturer of professional dental products and technologies. The company designs, manufactures, and markets dental consumables, dental equipment (including CAD/CAM CEREC systems, imaging equipment, and treatment centers), and dental technology solutions for dentists, dental laboratories, and specialists worldwide. Formed through the 2016 merger of DENTSPLY International and Sirona Dental Systems. New CEO Daniel Scavilla joined in August 2025 from Globus Medical. The SEC concluded its financial reporting investigation in October 2025 with no enforcement action. Q2 2026 revenue was $898M (-4.1% YoY) with net income of $37M, returning to GAAP profitability after a $628M net loss in 2025.

Investment Thesis
Dentsply Sirona at 7.4x forward earnings represents deep value if CEO Scavilla can execute the turnaround from Globus Medical's playbook. Q2 2026 showed early signs of stabilization — revenue of $898M beat consensus by ~1%, the company returned to GAAP profitability ($37M net income vs. $628M loss in 2025), and free cash flow surged to $55M from $16M aided by $44M in tariff refunds [1]. The $300M cost reduction program is restoring margins, and maintained FY2026 guidance of $3.5-3.6B revenue with $1.40-$1.50 adjusted EPS provides a baseline. The dental market benefits from aging demographics, emerging market growth, and increasing digital adoption. Two board directors bought shares in the open market in June 2026, signaling some insider conviction at current prices [5]. The 2.88% dividend yield and 32-year payment history provide income while waiting for the turnaround.
The bear case is multidimensional and severe. Revenue has declined five consecutive years from $4.23B (2021) to a projected $3.5-3.6B (2026), with Q2 Orthodontic and Implant Solutions falling 14.9% and management warning Q3 would be weaker [1]. The 50% dividend cut (from $0.64 to $0.32 annual) broke a 13-year growth streak and signals financial stress. D/E of 172% with $2.35B debt against $190M cash and an Altman Z-Score of 0.96 raise bankruptcy risk concerns [6]. GAAP ROE of -33% and profit margin of -15% demonstrate severe capital destruction. Organizational instability — three CEOs in eight years, 39% employee trust in leadership, and mass turnover — makes executing any turnaround strategy extremely difficult [4]. The $44M tariff refund that inflated Q2 cash flow is non-recurring. Competitors like Straumann, Align Technology, and Envista are gaining share while Dentsply retreats, and the stock's 24% decline over 52 weeks reflects the market pricing in continued deterioration.
Key Catalysts
  1. CEO Scavilla's turnaround execution — his track record at Globus Medical (successful $3B NuVasive acquisition) could bring operational discipline and strategic clarity if he can stabilize the organization and restore employee trust [2]
  2. $300M cost reduction program reaching full run-rate should restore adjusted EBITDA margins toward historical 20% levels, and Q2 2026's return to GAAP profitability ($37M) suggests the trough may be passing [1]
  3. Forward P/E of 7.4x creates substantial re-rating potential — if the company demonstrates two consecutive quarters of stable or positive organic revenue growth, multiple expansion toward peer averages (15-20x) would double the stock price [6]
Key Risks
  1. Revenue declining for five consecutive years with Q2 2026 Orthodontic and Implant Solutions down 14.9% signals ongoing market share losses to Straumann, Align Technology, and Envista — no pipeline of differentiated products exists to reverse the trajectory [1]
  2. D/E of 172%, $2.35B debt against $190M cash, Altman Z-Score of 0.96, and a 50% dividend cut create existential financial risk — any sustained revenue shortfall could trigger covenant pressure or further dividend elimination [6]
  3. Three CEOs in eight years with only 39% employee trust in leadership represents structural governance failure — Scavilla is the fourth attempt at turnaround leadership and employee morale may be too damaged to support execution [4]
