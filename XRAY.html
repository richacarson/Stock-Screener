Investment Thesis
DENTSPLY SIRONA at $8.87 with a $1.8B market cap trades at a deep discount — 5.9x forward earnings and 0.55x PEG — reflecting severe skepticism about the turnaround under CEO Scavilla [1]. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.52 beat estimates, though $0.17 came from one-time tariff refunds. Full-year guidance of $3.5-$3.6B revenue and $1.40-$1.50 adjusted EPS was maintained [1]. The company generates $319M in operating cash flow and $142M in free cash flow, providing financial stability despite the negative GAAP margins. The 149-year franchise owns the world's largest dental products portfolio (CEREC, SureSmile, endodontic tools, implants), and Scavilla's pivot toward integrated digital workflows via DS Core could unlock value if execution improves.
The bear case is substantial and structural. Revenue has declined for three consecutive years (from $3.96B in 2023 to an annualized ~$3.6B in 2026), and the Orthodontic and Implant segment dropped 13.2% YoY in Q2 2026, signaling market share losses to Align Technology and Straumann in the fastest-growing dental categories [1]. GAAP profitability is deeply negative at -15% margin due to accumulated goodwill impairments and restructuring charges, and ROE of -33% reflects destroyed shareholder equity. The $2.3B debt load against $239M cash is precarious with negative earnings, and the dividend was suspended in 2026 after 32 years of payments. Three CEO changes in four years, an SEC investigation, and shareholder lawsuits over accounting practices under former CEO Casey create governance risk that will take years to resolve [2][3]. The turnaround thesis requires faith in a new CEO who has been in the role barely a year, at a company where previous management failed to execute.
