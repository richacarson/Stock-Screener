Company Profile
Real Estate ·REIT - Healthcare Facilities ·NYSE ·United States
Chiron Real Estate Inc. (formerly Global Medical REIT Inc.) is a healthcare-focused real estate investment trust that owns and manages properties leased to healthcare providers. The company rebranded in February 2026 under CEO Mark Decker Jr., pivoting from a pure medical office REIT toward a diversified healthcare real estate platform with significant exposure to seniors housing operating properties (SHOP). The portfolio includes 4.6M leasable square feet of outpatient medical properties and growing seniors housing communities. Headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland.

Investment Thesis
Chiron Real Estate is executing an ambitious strategic pivot under CEO Mark Decker Jr. from a stagnant medical office REIT to a diversified healthcare real estate platform targeting the aging demographic megatrend. Q2 2026 revenue grew to $39.7M with 95% outpatient medical occupancy, and the company invested $249M in luxury seniors housing communities while recycling $217M from inpatient rehabilitation facility dispositions [2]. At 8.4x forward core FFO ($4.30-$4.45 guidance) with a 5.25% dividend yield, the stock offers attractive value for income investors willing to bet on the transformation [1]. The 39.9% leverage ratio, 78% fixed-rate debt, and no maturities through 2027 provide financial stability during the transition. Same-property cash NOI growth of 0.8% YoY in Q2 demonstrates stable underlying operations while the portfolio is being repositioned [2].
The bear case centers on execution risk and scale. Chiron is a $500M market cap REIT making outsized bets — the $249M seniors housing acquisition alone equals half the company's equity market value — creating concentrated risk if occupancy ramp-ups disappoint [2]. The Riviera seniors housing community was only 23% occupied at quarter-end (26% by July), and stabilization to the targeted 7.0-7.5% cash yield is uncertain. White Rock Medical Center, the company's Dallas acute-care hospital tenant, remains in Chapter 11 bankruptcy with lease affirmation status uncertain, representing potential income loss [2]. The GAAP net income of $63.3M in Q2 was almost entirely driven by a $71.9M gain on sale, masking that core FFO per share actually declined from $1.14 to $1.04 YoY. Average daily volume of just 100K shares limits institutional interest and creates liquidity risk for investors seeking to build or exit meaningful positions. The company has undergone a complete leadership transformation, rebrand, and strategic pivot simultaneously, and any misstep could compound across all three dimensions.
Key Catalysts
  1. The Pinnacle seniors housing acquisition (~$176M, expected Q4 2026) plus continued SHOP portfolio expansion targeting the $5.6T senior living market as 10,000 Baby Boomers turn 65 daily through 2030
  2. Stabilization of newly acquired seniors housing communities (The Landing at 93% occupancy, The Riviera ramping from 23%) toward targeted 7.0-7.5% stabilized cash yields, demonstrating the SHOP strategy's viability
  3. Leverage reduction through asset recycling — the Beaumont Surgical Hospital disposition (~$49M at 5.9% cap rate) and future dispositions could reduce the leverage ratio below 35%, enabling additional accretive acquisitions
Key Risks
  1. Concentrated execution risk from simultaneous strategic pivot, leadership overhaul, and corporate rebrand — any failure in senior housing integration while core medical office FFO declines could erode investor confidence and access to capital
  2. White Rock Medical Center Chapter 11 bankruptcy creates potential rent loss and recovery uncertainty for a key acute-care hospital asset, with lease affirmation status still unresolved
  3. Core FFO per share declined from $1.14 to $1.04 YoY in Q2 despite revenue growth, suggesting dilution from preferred stock issuance and new acquisitions not yet contributing at full run-rate — continued FFO compression would pressure the 5.25% dividend yield sustainability
