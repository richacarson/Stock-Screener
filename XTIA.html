Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NASDAQ ·United States
XTI Aerospace is a micro-cap company ($45M market cap) that acquired Drone Nerds, a commercial drone distribution and services business, while developing the TriFan 600 vertical lift crossover airplane (VLCA). The company has undergone turbulent transformation: originally Inpixon (indoor mapping), it rebranded to XTI Aerospace in 2024 and executed a 1-for-250 reverse stock split in January 2025 to maintain Nasdaq listing compliance. In August 2026, CEO Scott Pomeroy resigned amid an internal governance review, triggering securities fraud investigations from multiple law firms including Pomerantz, Rosen, and Glancy. The TriFan 600 program was shelved to focus resources on Drone Nerds. Q1 2026 revenue was $27.7M with a loss of $(0.46)/share. Cash position was $15.2M.

Investment Thesis
XTI Aerospace's Drone Nerds acquisition provides a revenue-generating platform in the fast-growing commercial drone market — Q1 2026 revenue of $27.7M with a target of $160M+ for the full year, 19-21% gross margins, and a breakeven cash flow target in Q3 2026 suggest rapid scaling if executed [1]. At just $45M market cap, operational success is not priced into the stock, and the $5.00 analyst target implies ~310% upside [4]. The commercial drone market is growing rapidly with FAA regulatory expansion and increasing enterprise adoption across agriculture, infrastructure inspection, and public safety applications.
The investment case is overwhelmed by governance risk — CEO Scott Pomeroy resigned in August 2026 amid an internal review, triggering securities fraud investigations from at least four major law firms [2]. The company delayed its quarterly filing, received a Nasdaq deficiency notification, and trades at $1.22 on a $45M market cap — well below the $13.75/share at which a $20M offering was priced in early 2025 [2][3]. The 1-for-250 reverse stock split in January 2025 demonstrates the severity of prior dilution, and the departing CEO's $200K separation and 2M options package raises questions about governance alignment [2]. Cash of $15.2M against quarterly losses of ~$18M provides less than one quarter of runway without additional dilutive capital raises. The TriFan 600 — the company's only truly innovative product — has been shelved [3]. The Drone Nerds business, while generating revenue, is a low-margin distribution operation in a market that could be disrupted by manufacturer direct-sales.
