Company Profile
Industrials ·Specialty Industrial Machinery ·NYSE ·United States
Xylem Inc. designs, manufactures, and services water and wastewater technology solutions globally. The company operates through four segments: Water Infrastructure, Applied Water, Measurement & Control Solutions, and Integrated Solutions & Services. Products include pumps, valves, treatment systems, analytics instruments, smart meters, and digital water management platforms. Acquired Evoqua Water Technologies in 2023 for $7.5B, creating the largest pure-play water technology company globally. Headquartered in Washington, D.C.
23,000 Employees ·www.xylem.com

Investment Thesis
Xylem is the world's largest pure-play water technology company following the 2023 Evoqua acquisition, with $9B+ annual revenue across water infrastructure, treatment, smart metering, and digital solutions [1][2]. Q1 2026 delivered $2.125B revenue with 14% EPS growth and improving margins — adjusted EBITDA margin of 20.6%, guiding to 22.9-23.3% for FY2026 — demonstrating Evoqua synergy realization [1]. The company benefits from powerful secular tailwinds: aging water infrastructure requiring $1T+ in global investment, growing water scarcity from climate change, and AI-driven data center expansion increasing industrial water demand. With D/E of just 18%, Xylem has exceptional balance sheet flexibility, and the 14-year dividend growth streak at 9.2% annual increases provides growing income.
The bear case centers on decelerating organic growth and premium valuation. Organic revenue growth was flat in Q1 2026 despite strong secular trends, and FY2026 guidance of 2-4% organic growth suggests the Evoqua integration boost is fading while underlying demand remains tepid [1]. At 21.7x forward earnings with only low-single-digit organic growth, any execution miss could trigger multiple compression. ROE of 8.72% is well below the 15% threshold, depressed by $5B+ in Evoqua-related goodwill inflating the equity base — if synergy targets fall short, the return profile may remain suboptimal for years [5]. Profit margin of 10.79% is structurally constrained by manufacturing and service delivery costs. Glassdoor feedback (3.6/5 culture) suggests tension between innovation ambitions and short-term financial targets [3]. Municipal budget constraints and infrastructure spending delays in a slower economic environment could further pressure near-term revenue.
Key Catalysts
  1. Evoqua integration synergies driving adjusted EBITDA margin expansion from 20.6% toward 23%+ by FY2026, with potential for additional cost savings as integration matures [1]
  2. AI-driven data center buildout increasing industrial water demand — each hyperscale facility requires 1-5M gallons daily for cooling, directly benefiting Xylem's treatment and infrastructure segments [3]
  3. US Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act deploying $55B for water/wastewater over 5 years, creating sustained demand for Xylem's municipal water solutions [2]
Key Risks
  1. Organic revenue growth of flat to low-single-digits in 2026 suggests underlying demand momentum is weaker than the secular water scarcity narrative implies, with municipal budget constraints limiting near-term spending [1]
  2. ROE of 8.72% reflects post-Evoqua goodwill dilution — if synergy targets are not fully realized, the company may have overpaid for the $7.5B deal, permanently impairing capital efficiency [5]
  3. Forward P/E of 21.7x and PEG of 1.67 leave limited valuation upside, creating downside risk if organic growth remains below mid-single-digits or margin expansion stalls versus the 22.9-23.3% EBITDA target [5]
