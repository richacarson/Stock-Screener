Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NYSE ·United States
Yelp Inc. operates a platform connecting consumers with local businesses across categories including restaurants, home services, health, beauty, and auto. The company provides advertising products (cost-per-click, multi-location ads), Yelp Guest Manager (restaurant SaaS), Yelp Guaranteed, data licensing through Yelp Fusion and an OpenAI partnership, and AI-powered lead management via the Hatch acquisition. Founded in 2004 by CEO Jeremy Stoppelman, Yelp operates as a remote-first company with over 280 million cumulative reviews. Record FY2025 net revenue of $1.46B; 2026 guidance of $1.455-1.475B revenue with $310-330M EBITDA.

Investment Thesis
Yelp is a profitable founder-led platform trading at a deep value of 5.17x forward earnings with minimal leverage (D/E 19%) and ROE of 18.2%, reflecting severe market skepticism about AI disruption risk [5]. At $19.63 per share and a $1.07B market cap (just ~0.7x revenue), the valuation prices in significant business deterioration that has not yet materialized — FY2025 delivered record $1.46B revenue and $146M net income [1]. Yelp's 280M+ review database is an irreplaceable data asset evidenced by the OpenAI data licensing partnership, and the Hatch acquisition adds AI-powered lead management to transform Yelp from a review site into an AI-enabled local commerce platform [3]. Other revenue (data licensing, SaaS, transactions) grew 17% YoY, diversifying away from the advertising model most vulnerable to AI disruption. The deliberate $40-60M EBITDA compression in 2026 funds AI transformation under a founder-CEO with 22 years of commitment.
The bear case is that AI represents an existential threat to Yelp's core business model. Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity synthesize local business recommendations without directing consumers to Yelp, potentially collapsing the traffic and advertising model [3]. Revenue growth has decelerated sharply to 1.4% YoY, and 2026 guidance of $1.455-1.475B implies flat revenue while EBITDA declines 11-16% ($310-330M vs $369M) — margin compression without revenue acceleration [1][2]. CFO Schwarzbach acknowledged 'macroeconomic strain' at the Technology Leadership Forum 2026, compounding AI disruption concerns [3]. The stock's 18% decline below its SMA50 ($24.03 vs $19.63) reflects market consensus that Yelp may be on the wrong side of the AI disruption curve. Earnings growth of -14.9% confirms the deteriorating earnings trajectory. Yelp's 22-year history of failing to escape Google's shadow in local search raises fundamental questions about management's ability to execute an AI pivot now, and the Hatch acquisition adds execution risk during a period of declining financial performance [5].
