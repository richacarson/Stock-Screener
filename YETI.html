Company Profile
Consumer Cyclical ·Leisure ·NYSE ·United States
YETI Holdings designs, retails, and distributes premium outdoor products including hard and soft coolers, drinkware (Rambler, Yonder collections), bags, outdoor living products, and apparel under the YETI brand name. Founded in 2006 in Austin, Texas by brothers Roy and Ryan Seiders, YETI has expanded from premium coolers to a broad outdoor lifestyle platform sold in 11 international markets. Q2 2026 sales grew 9% YoY with international revenue up 19%, and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $2.94-$3.00, representing 19-21% earnings growth.

Investment Thesis
YETI is delivering broad-based growth with Q2 2026 sales up 9% YoY, international revenue growing 19%, and raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.94-$3.00 representing 19-21% earnings growth [1]. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E 41.5%) with strong cash generation ($304M operating cash flow, $127M FCF), and ROE of 25.3% reflects efficient capital deployment [4]. At 12.6x forward earnings and a 1.27 PEG ratio, the stock offers attractive growth-adjusted valuation for a premium consumer brand with expanding international footprint and category diversification. Adjusted operating margin guidance of 14.9% signals improving profitability as the brand scales globally [1].
The bear case centers on consumer cyclicality and competitive threats. YETI sells premium discretionary products — $300 coolers and $40 tumblers are vulnerable to consumer spending pullbacks during economic downturns, and the stock carries a 1.70 beta reflecting this sensitivity [4]. Profit margin of 9.2% is relatively thin for a premium brand, constrained by materials costs and the DTC/wholesale channel mix. The viral success of Stanley tumblers in 2024-2025 demonstrated that YETI's drinkware moat is more permeable than its cooler franchise, and new entrants can rapidly capture mindshare in the insulated drinkware category through social media marketing [2]. Glassdoor reviews highlighting VP-level turnover in R&D, political culture, and low morale suggest internal execution risk that could impair product innovation velocity at a critical growth stage. Revenue growth of 8.5% is solid but below the double-digit pace the market typically rewards with premium multiples for growth stocks.
Key Catalysts
  1. International expansion to 11 markets with 19% revenue growth creating a long runway for geographic diversification and reducing U.S. consumer spending dependence [1]
  2. Adjusted EPS growth of 19-21% driven by operating leverage, DTC channel mix improvement, and category expansion into cookware and outdoor living products [1]
  3. Conservative balance sheet (D/E 41.5%, $304M operating cash flow) provides firepower for share buybacks, acquisitions, or accelerated international market entry without dilution [4]
Key Risks
  1. Consumer discretionary spending sensitivity — premium outdoor products are early casualties in economic downturns, and YETI's 1.70 beta amplifies portfolio volatility during risk-off periods [4]
  2. Drinkware category commoditization as Stanley, Hydro Flask, and social media-driven entrants demonstrate that insulated drinkware brand loyalty is more fragile than cooler brand loyalty, compressing YETI's largest category margins
  3. Internal culture challenges — Glassdoor reports of VP-level R&D turnover, political dynamics, and low employee morale could impair product innovation and talent retention during a critical international scaling phase [2]
