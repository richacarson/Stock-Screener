Company Profile
Technology ·Internet Content & Information ·NYSE ·China
Full Truck Alliance (FTA) operates China's dominant digital freight platform, connecting shippers with truckers through AI-powered matching technology. The platform encompasses transaction services, freight brokerage, and value-added services including fuel cards (Sinopec partnership) and financial products. Q2 2026 revenue was RMB3.38B (+4.4% YoY) with operating income up 30.4% to RMB1.49B. Fulfilled orders reached 68.5M (+12.7%) with 3.57M average shipper MAUs (+12.8%). The company holds RMB33.4B in cash and generated RMB2.04B in free cash flow in Q2. Market cap is approximately $9.1B.

Investment Thesis
FTA at 10.89x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.66 is one of the most attractively valued platform businesses in the global market, trading at a significant discount to US tech peers despite dominant market position and exceptional profitability [4]. The company generates 33% profit margins with zero debt and $3.46B in net cash — a fortress balance sheet that eliminates balance sheet risk. Transaction services revenue growing 33.1% YoY in Q2 2026 demonstrates the platform's ability to shift toward higher-value, higher-margin revenue streams [1]. Fulfilled orders (+12.7%) and shipper MAUs (+12.8%) show healthy platform engagement growth. Free cash flow of RMB2.04B in Q2 alone funds the 3.83% dividend yield at a comfortable 39.83% payout ratio. The AI-powered matching platform benefits from a structural tailwind as China's freight industry digitizes — FTA has no comparable competitor at scale, giving it a platform monopoly advantage that could compound for years.
The bear case centers squarely on China risk — regulatory, geopolitical, and macroeconomic. FTA's stock has declined 20% over the past year despite strong fundamentals, reflecting the persistent China ADR discount driven by US-China tensions, potential delisting risk, and VIE structure uncertainty [4]. Revenue growth has decelerated sharply to 4.4% YoY in Q2 2026, with Q3 guidance of flat performance around RMB3.32-3.42B — the Chinese economic slowdown is compressing freight volumes and pricing power [1]. The company's history with Chinese regulatory scrutiny (2021 cybersecurity review) is a reminder that government intervention can materially impact operations with little warning. While profit margins are exceptional, the company is increasingly dependent on transaction services to offset declining freight brokerage and value-added services — a mix shift that may be difficult to sustain if shipper volumes plateau. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) and limited ESG transparency typical of Chinese companies may limit allocation for faith-based investors.
