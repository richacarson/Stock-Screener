Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Application ·NYSE ·United States
Clear Secure (CLEAR) operates a biometric secure-identity platform best known for CLEAR+ expedited airport security lanes, and increasingly sells identity verification to enterprises (hiring, e-signature, healthcare check-in) and venues. Q2 2026 revenue grew 26.6% to $277.8M, total bookings rose 32.8% to $295.9M, and quarterly free cash flow hit a record $189M. Founded in its current form in 2010 when Caryn Seidman Becker and Ken Cornick bought the assets of bankrupt predecessor Verified Identity Pass; headquartered in New York City.

Investment Thesis
CLEAR is a high-margin cash compounder priced like a value stock: Q2 2026 revenue rose 26.6% to $277.8M, bookings grew 32.8%, operating margin reached 29.9%, adjusted EBITDA margin hit 36.4% (beating the 35% IPO target), and quarterly free cash flow set a record $189M, with full-year FCF guided to at least $480M (+40%) [1]. At ~16x forward earnings with net cash, the market is pricing only the airport subscription, not the option value of enterprise identity verification as deepfake fraud rises [5][8].
The bear case is regulatory and platform risk: CLEAR's airport economics depend on TSA and airport authority relationships, TSA is rolling out its own facial-recognition and digital-ID lanes, and the Traveler Privacy Protection Act and civil-liberties scrutiny of airport biometrics could constrain the model [7]. Enterprise identity is still small and faces well-funded verification competitors, the stock already trades below its 50-day average (~$45), and frontline labor turnover could pressure service quality [4][8].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 delivery on guidance of $284-287M revenue and $311-316M bookings, sustaining 20%+ bookings growth
  2. Enterprise identity wins (Docusign, Greenhouse-style integrations) proving a second growth engine against deepfake-driven hiring and account fraud
  3. Deployment of at least $480M of 2026 free cash flow into buybacks and dividends, supporting per-share growth
Key Risks
  1. TSA's own facial-recognition and mobile digital-ID programs could reduce the value of a paid CLEAR+ membership at checkpoints
  2. Biometric-privacy legislation (e.g., Traveler Privacy Protection Act) or a data-security incident could restrict operations or damage member trust
  3. Deepfake and presentation attacks against liveness detection, plus frontline staffing turnover, could erode the service quality that justifies the subscription
