Company Profile
Energy ·Oil & Gas Integrated ·NYSE ·Argentina
YPF is Argentina's largest integrated energy company, operating across upstream oil and gas exploration/production anchored by the Vaca Muerta shale formation, midstream/downstream refining and marketing, LNG development, petrochemicals, and new energies. The Argentine government holds majority ownership (~51%). CEO Horacio Marin has repositioned YPF as a world-class shale operator, with shale oil production at 205,000 bpd in Q1 2026. H1 2026 delivered revenues of $11.52B (+24.6% YoY), net profit of $1.614B, and operating cash flow of $4.237B. Headquartered in Buenos Aires.

Investment Thesis
YPF offers compelling exposure to the Vaca Muerta shale revolution at a deep value entry point. H1 2026 delivered transformative results: $11.52B revenue (up 24.6% YoY in USD), $1.614B net profit (versus just $48M in H1 2025), and $4.237B operating cash flow (more than double YoY) [1]. Shale oil production reached 205,000 bpd in Q1 2026 (up 39% YoY), now comprising 76% of total output, while lifting costs dropped 42% to $8.8/BOE — approaching Permian Basin efficiency levels. At 10.50x forward P/E, YPF trades at a massive discount to global integrated oil peers despite superior production growth trajectory. The VMOS export pipeline (62% complete, Phase I January 2027) is the key catalyst unlocking Argentina's ability to export crude at international prices, with capacity expandable to 550,000 bpd [1]. CEO Marin's operational discipline has driven record adjusted EBITDA margins of 32% in Q1 2026, and Stanley Druckenmiller's 433% stake increase to $150M validates the institutional case [4].
The bear case is dominated by Argentine sovereign and currency risk. Past peso devaluations have converted record EBITDA into net losses, and future currency instability could repeat this pattern regardless of operational performance — the trailing PE of 29.7x versus forward PE of 10.5x reflects this accounting distortion [4]. Government majority ownership (~51%) creates structural risks: political appointments drive leadership changes, capital controls can trap cash flows in-country, and export restrictions may limit realization of international pricing even after VMOS completion. D/E of 84.75% is manageable but elevated for an emerging market operator with commodity price exposure and currency volatility. Without new discoveries, current reserves last only ~8 years for oil at current production rates, creating a reserve replacement treadmill [3]. Environmental lawsuits related to fracking could delay Vaca Muerta expansion, and the YPF ADR's 52-week range reflects extreme volatility driven by Argentine political and macro factors beyond management's control. ROE of 7.26% (trailing, impacted by peso conversion) and profit margin of 4.0% (trailing) understate current profitability but highlight the financial reporting challenges that limit institutional investor confidence.
Key Catalysts
  1. VMOS oil export pipeline Phase I operational January 2027 (180,000 bpd capacity, expandable to 550,000 bpd), enabling access to international crude pricing and potentially transforming YPF's revenue and margin profile on exported barrels [1]
  2. Shale oil production ramp to 250,000 bpd exit rate by December 2026 with rig fleet expanding from 12 to 18 company-owned rigs, driving volume-led EBITDA growth on declining per-barrel lifting costs [1]
  3. Continued lifting cost reduction toward Permian Basin levels ($5-6/BOE) from current $8.8/BOE, expanding margins even at lower oil prices and demonstrating world-class operational execution [1]
Key Risks
  1. Argentine sovereign and currency risk — peso devaluation has historically destroyed reported earnings despite record operational performance, and future instability could prevent international ADR investors from realizing returns [1][4]
  2. Government majority ownership and political interference — leadership changes tied to election cycles, capital controls, and export restrictions can redirect strategy and constrain shareholder value realization independent of operational execution [2]
  3. Reserve life of approximately 8 years for oil at current production rates requires continuous high-capex Vaca Muerta development; any permitting delays, environmental litigation, or capital constraints could stall the production growth trajectory [3]
