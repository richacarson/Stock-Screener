Company Profile
Industrials ·Aerospace & Defense ·NYSE ·United States
York Space Systems is a space and defense prime providing mission-critical spacecraft design, production, integration, and operation for national security, government, and commercial customers. The company offers S-CLASS, LX-CLASS, and M-CLASS satellite platforms with proprietary software for payload integration and constellation management. IPO'd in January 2026 at $34/share raising $629M, the stock has since fallen ~75% amid a Wolfpack Research short report alleging Pentagon 'severe disappointment,' a securities fraud class action against the CEO and CFO, and a dramatic FY2026 guidance cut from $570M midpoint to $375-405M. Q2 FY2026 revenue was $92.5M (+10% YoY) with gross margin expanding to 24%.

Investment Thesis
York Space Systems retains $592M in backlog covering the majority of its reduced FY2026 revenue guidance of $375-405M, and gross margin expansion to 24% (+13pp YoY) in Q2 2026 demonstrates underlying operational improvement [1]. The balance sheet remains clean with D/E of ~10% and $684M in liquidity from IPO proceeds, providing runway to execute the pending $355M All.Space acquisition that would diversify the platform into satellite communications [1][5]. The company operates three spacecraft platforms in a growing national-security space market with structural tailwinds from U.S. government investment in space-based infrastructure.
The bear case has intensified dramatically since the IPO. Full-year guidance was slashed from $570M midpoint to $390M midpoint — a $180M reduction — citing IDIQ contract shifts and supply chain delays, destroying the 48% growth narrative that anchored the $34 IPO price [1][3]. Wolfpack Research alleges the Pentagon expressed 'severe disappointment' in York and that the company 'deceived the SDA with false advertising to win contracts,' leading to multiple securities fraud class actions against CEO Dirk Wallinger and CFO Kevin Messerle [3]. The stock has fallen ~75% from IPO price with no insider buying to signal management conviction. TTM profit margin of -22.75% means the company remains deeply unprofitable. Glassdoor reviews (2.2/5, CEO approval 13%) describe a low-trust culture that poses talent retention risk during this crisis period [4]. The combination of governance risk, execution failure, and cultural dysfunction creates a uniquely challenging risk profile for a company less than 9 months past its IPO.
Key Catalysts
  1. Closing and integration of the $355M All.Space acquisition, which would diversify York into satellite communications and potentially demonstrate strategic execution capability [1]
  2. Resolution of IDIQ contract timing delays pushing H2 2026 revenue recognition higher toward the $375-405M guidance range [1]
  3. Successful defense against the securities fraud class action, which if dismissed would remove a significant governance overhang and could restore investor confidence [3]
Key Risks
  1. Securities fraud class action against CEO and CFO alleging misleading IPO disclosures, combined with Wolfpack Research allegations of Pentagon 'severe disappointment' and deceptive SDA contract practices, create existential governance risk [3]
  2. FY2026 guidance cut from $570M to $375-405M midpoint — a 32% reduction — suggests fundamental demand and execution problems, not just timing, with IDIQ procurement shifts potentially indicating structural loss of Pentagon contract pipeline [1][3]
  3. Severely negative culture metrics (Glassdoor 2.2/5, CEO approval 13%, 'Stay FAR FAR AWAY' reviews) during a period of crisis management, acquisition integration, and securities litigation pose acute talent retention risk in a tight aerospace labor market [4]
