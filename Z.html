Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NASDAQ ·United States
Zillow Group, Inc. operates the most-visited US residential real estate marketplace, monetizing through Premier Agent and For Sale services, Rentals (including multifamily), and Zillow Home Loans. Under CEO Jeremy Wacksman (CEO since 2024, at Zillow since 2009) the company is pursuing a 'housing super app' strategy and calls itself an AI-native company, launching 'AI Mode' in 2026. Q2 2026 revenue rose 18% to $772M, with Rentals up 31% and Mortgages up 75%; Q3 2026 guidance is $745-760M with results due November 4, 2026. Market cap approximately $7.0B at $30.96.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 5 /10
Zillow has a durable consumer purpose and a CEO who has been inside the company for 17 years, which gives it continuity few internet companies have. Its biggest strategic swing, iBuying, ended in a costly retreat in 2021, and its current competitive posture is litigation: defending its listing rules against Compass and suing Compass and MRED in return. Seen through Sinek's lens, the cause is real but the competitive mindset is finite.

Investment Thesis
Zillow is growing well above the housing market. Q2 2026 revenue rose 18% to $772M, above the top of guidance, with For Sale up 14%, Rentals up 31% and Mortgages up 75% on a 95% rise in purchase originations [1][2]. The business has turned GAAP-profitable (FY2025 net income $23M versus a $112M loss in 2024), carries more cash than debt, and generates $244M of free cash flow [3][8]. At a forward P/E of 11.2 and a PEG of 0.93, with a consensus target of $41.17, the shares price in little of the super app strategy, and Q3 results on November 4 against $745-760M guidance are a near-term catalyst [1][8].
The bear case is that AI assistants and listing-access fights threaten the traffic monopoly that funds everything else. The stock has fallen about 60% from its 52-week high of $77.07, ROE is only 1.2%, and the trailing P/E is 135 [8]. Litigation with Compass and MRED is ongoing and could restrict listing data, and insiders have sold steadily with no open-market buying over the past year [6][7]. Management guides Q4 residential growth only in line with the purchase mortgage industry, so a stalled housing market would cap growth [1].
Key Catalysts
  1. Q3 2026 results on November 4 against $745-760M revenue guidance, compared with $676M in Q3 2025 [1]
  2. Rentals (+31%, multifamily +42%) and Zillow Home Loans (+75%) scaling into a larger share of revenue in the housing super app [2]
  3. AI Mode adoption raising engagement and agent conversion, in line with management's 'AI-native company' claims [4][5]
Key Risks
  1. AI search and chat assistants pulling home-search traffic away from portals, weakening the agent lead-generation model [4][5]
  2. Antitrust litigation with Compass and MRED over listing standards could limit Zillow's access to listings data [6]
  3. Thin profitability (1.2% ROE, 2.0% margin) leaves earnings exposed to a prolonged housing slowdown [8]
