Company Profile
Real Estate ·Real Estate Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Zillow Group operates the leading real estate marketplace in the United States, connecting buyers, sellers, renters, and agents through its digital platforms. The company operates across Residential (agent advertising and connections), Rentals (multifamily marketplace), Mortgages (purchase loan origination), and Other segments. Key properties include Zillow.com, Trulia, HotPads, and StreetEasy, collectively reaching approximately 220 million monthly unique users. CEO Jeremy Wacksman is executing a 'housing super app' strategy integrating search, touring, financing, and transaction management. Revenue of $2.69B TTM.

Investment Thesis
Zillow is the undisputed leader in online real estate with 220M monthly users and no close second, now executing a 'housing super app' strategy that is driving accelerating revenue diversification — Q1 2026 revenue grew 18.4% to $708M, with mortgage origination volume up 96% and rentals revenue up 42% to $183M (multifamily up 57%) [1]. The stock's 52.9% YTD decline to $31.15 has created a compelling valuation: forward P/E of approximately 14x and forward PEG of 0.33 for a company guiding mid-teens revenue growth with meaningful back-half margin expansion [1][4]. Net income margin improved 520bps YoY to 6% in Q1, and adjusted EBITDA of $182M (26% margin) demonstrates the operating leverage emerging as the marketplace model scales. Zillow's AI-powered platform strategy under CEO Wacksman is differentiated and gaining traction.
The bear case centers on Zillow's dependence on the US housing market, which remains structurally challenged by elevated mortgage rates and affordability constraints — existing home sales remain well below the 5-6M annual pace that prevailed pre-pandemic [1]. The 52.9% YTD stock decline reflects investor concerns that macro headwinds could persist through 2026-2027, limiting transaction volume growth regardless of Zillow's platform improvements. Trailing profit margin of just 2.27% and ROE of 0.47% demonstrate that Zillow has not yet proven it can convert revenue growth into meaningful shareholder returns [4]. The 5-year revenue CAGR of -5.0% — distorted by the iBuyer cycle — raises questions about the stability of Zillow's revenue model. The company's dual-class share structure (Z/ZG) concentrates control with co-founders Rich Barton and Lloyd Frink, limiting shareholder influence. Competitive threats from CoStar/Homes.com (investing $1B+ in agent advertising) could pressure Zillow's core Residential revenue segment.
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage origination volume up 96% YoY in Q1 2026 — as Zillow captures a larger share of purchase mortgage flow from its 220M-user marketplace, this high-margin revenue stream could drive significant margin expansion [1]
  2. Rentals revenue growing 42% YoY ($183M in Q1, multifamily up 57%) with management guiding approximately 30% full-year growth — the rentals marketplace is becoming a second major growth engine beyond residential [1]
  3. Forward PEG of 0.33 at $31.15 represents extreme growth-adjusted undervaluation — any housing market recovery or continued margin expansion could trigger substantial multiple re-rating from current depressed levels [4][5]
Key Risks
  1. US housing market remains structurally challenged by elevated mortgage rates and affordability constraints — existing home sales volume well below pre-pandemic norms limits Zillow's transaction-based revenue potential [1]
  2. 52.9% YTD stock decline reflects deep investor skepticism about near-term earnings power — trailing profit margin of 2.27% and ROE of 0.47% demonstrate Zillow has not yet proven sustainable profitability [4]
  3. CoStar Group/Homes.com investing $1B+ to compete directly with Zillow's agent advertising business, creating a well-funded competitive threat to the core Residential revenue segment that represents 64% of total revenue [1]

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
7
AI Risk Protection (6/9): Zillow's core business is protected by powerful network effects — 220M monthly users and relationships with hundreds of thousands of real estate agents create a marketplace flywheel that AI cannot easily replicate [2]. Real estate transactions inherently involve physical-world activities (home tours, inspections, closings) and heavily regulated processes (mortgage origination, title insurance) that AI cannot substitute. However, AI assistants and agents could theoretically disintermediate Zillow's search-to-agent connection model by directly matching buyers with agents or properties, bypassing the platform entirely. AI-powered property valuation tools could erode the Zestimate's competitive advantage. The company faces moderate but manageable AI exposure — the physical and regulatory nature of real estate provides meaningful protection. AI Opportunity (1/1): AI is embedded throughout Zillow's platform — search personalization, predictive intelligence, agent productivity tools, and the new CPO appointment focused on AI-driven product development demonstrate that AI is actively enhancing the user experience and driving engagement [2].
