ZenaTech, Inc. is a technology company specializing in AI-powered drones, Drone-as-a-Service (DaaS), enterprise SaaS, and quantum computing solutions. The company completed 20 acquisitions in 2025, pivoting from enterprise SaaS to become primarily a drone company with 93% of revenue from the DaaS segment. ZenaTech established Zena AI, Inc. in Baton Rouge, Louisiana for AI and autonomy development targeting defense and homeland security applications. CEO Shaun Passley, Ph.D., holds 48.7% insider ownership. Incorporated in British Columbia, Canada; listed on NASDAQ.

Investment Thesis
ZenaTech is a micro-cap ($131M) technology company that completed a remarkable transformation in 2025, pivoting from enterprise SaaS to become an AI drone and Drone-as-a-Service platform through 20 acquisitions, driving FY2025 revenue to $12.9M (up 558% YoY) with total assets growing 188% to $99.8M [1]. Q1 2026 continued the acceleration with $8.4M in revenue (up 640% YoY), and 93% of revenue now comes from the DaaS segment. The company established Zena AI, Inc. for defense and homeland security AI applications and is progressing toward US defense customer field demonstrations and pilot programs in 2026 [2]. CEO Passley's 48.7% insider ownership provides extreme alignment, and at $1.49 per share ($131M market cap), any defense contract win could drive outsized returns.
The bear case centers on execution risk and financial sustainability. ZenaTech generated approximately -$48M in net losses on $12.9M in FY2025 revenue — a staggering -372% profit margin — meaning the company destroys roughly $4 in value for every $1 in revenue [3]. ROE of -80.7% confirms ongoing equity destruction. The stock has declined 76% from its 52-week high of $7.11 to $1.49, suggesting the market is deeply skeptical despite explosive revenue growth [3]. Twenty acquisitions in 12 months with a lean corporate team creates severe integration risk, and the company's simultaneous pursuit of drones, AI, quantum computing, SaaS, and defense applications — each competing against vastly better-funded rivals (Skydio, Shield AI, AeroVironment, IBM, Google) — spreads resources dangerously thin. With $4.36M in cash against $15.65M in debt, additional dilutive capital raises are likely. The growth narrative may be promotional rather than substantive — revenue growth is acquisition-driven, not organic [1][3].
Key Catalysts
  1. US defense contract award or successful pilot program for ZenaDrone products would provide revenue visibility, credibility, and a potential re-rating from speculative micro-cap to legitimate defense technology provider
  2. Demonstration of organic revenue growth and a credible path to profitability across the integrated DaaS portfolio, validating that the 20-acquisition rollup creates real operating leverage beyond revenue stacking
  3. Completion of the 5-qubit quantum computing prototype in late 2026 could attract partnership interest from defense primes seeking quantum sensing and optimization capabilities
Key Risks
  1. Massive operating losses (-$48M net loss on $12.9M revenue, -372% profit margin) with only $4.36M cash on hand creates material going-concern risk and near-certainty of dilutive equity raises at the depressed $1.49 share price
  2. Acquisition integration failure — 20 deals in 12 months with a lean corporate team creates severe execution risk, potential for value destruction, and operational chaos across disparate architecture/engineering firms
  3. Narrative over substance risk — simultaneous pursuit of AI drones, DaaS, quantum computing, SaaS, and defense without profitability or major contracts in any vertical suggests promotional strategy; stock down 76% from 52-week high reflects market skepticism
