Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·Canada
ZenaTech is an enterprise software technology company that develops cloud-based software applications and manufactures autonomous AI-powered drones. The company operates two segments: Drone as a Service (DaaS), which drives ~78% of revenue through acquired land surveying and inspection companies, and Enterprise SaaS for healthcare, public safety, and facility management. Founded in 2017 and headquartered in Vancouver, ZenaTech completed 20 acquisitions in 2025 and reported FY2025 revenue of $12.9M (+558% YoY) with Q1 2026 revenue of $8.4M (+640% YoY). The company remains deeply unprofitable with ROE of -132%, zero profit margin, and $56.5M in negative free cash flow.
260 Employees ·www.zenatech.com

Investment Thesis
ZenaTech is a micro-cap ($129M) technology company executing an aggressive acquisition-led roll-up in the drone-as-a-service market, delivering explosive revenue growth (558% FY2025, 640% Q1 2026 YoY) through 20 completed acquisitions of land surveying and inspection companies [1][2]. The DaaS market is projected to grow at 36%+ annually, and ZenaTech's strategy of digitizing legacy service industries with AI-powered drone technology addresses a real market need. The company has established a global DaaS headquarters in Orlando and is pursuing dual-use defense and commercial drone capabilities with favorable regulatory tailwinds. With analyst price targets averaging $4.48 (3x upside from $1.45), the stock offers significant upside if the roll-up strategy achieves integration milestones and a path to profitability.
The bear case is substantial and dominates at current execution levels. ZenaTech is deeply unprofitable with -132% ROE, zero profit margin, and $56.5M in negative free cash flow against just $20.2M in trailing revenue [3][4]. Shareholders have been diluted 204% in the past year to fund acquisitions, and GuruFocus assigns a GF Score of 36/100 reflecting severe financial health concerns. Seeking Alpha analysts note 'lofty promises, unproven execution' and flag that most patents and revenue are acquired rather than organically developed [3]. The stock has declined 80% from its 52-week high of $7.11 to $1.45, suggesting deep market skepticism. Twenty acquisitions in 12 months with a lean corporate team creates severe integration risk, and simultaneous pursuit of drones, AI, quantum computing, SaaS, and defense applications spreads resources dangerously thin. The Inspire Impact Score of 0 (MIXED) provides no faith-alignment signal. For IOWN, the combination of micro-cap size, negative earnings, extreme dilution, and governance concerns makes ZENA unsuitable at current execution levels.
