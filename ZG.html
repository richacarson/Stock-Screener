Company Profile
Real Estate ·Real Estate Services ·NASDAQ ·United States
Zillow Group, Inc. operates the largest real estate marketplace in the United States through Zillow, Trulia, StreetEasy, and ShowingTime. The company's 'Housing Super App' strategy integrates home search, financing (Zillow Home Loans), and rentals into a unified platform. Revenue segments include Residential (Premier Agent), Rentals, and Mortgages. Founded in 2006 by Rich Barton and Lloyd Frink, headquartered in Seattle, Washington. ZG represents Class A voting shares.

Investment Thesis
Zillow's Q2 2026 revenue of $772M (up 18% YoY) demonstrates the Housing Super App strategy is working, with Mortgages origination volume surging 95% to $2.2B and Rentals revenue growing 31% (multifamily +42%) [1]. At a forward PEG of 0.49 and forward P/E of 13.0 — after a 44% stock decline in 2026 — the market has dramatically discounted Zillow's dominant position in US real estate technology. With $795M in cash, D/E of only 10%, and the most comprehensive real estate data platform in the US, Zillow has the financial flexibility to invest through the current housing slowdown. Full-year guidance of mid-teens revenue growth with meaningful H2 margin expansion suggests the profitability inflection that investors have been waiting for may be approaching [1].
The bear case centers on Zillow's persistently thin profitability and exposure to a challenging housing market. TTM profit margin of 2.27% and ROE of 1.33% on $2.69B revenue indicate a company that has not yet proven it can convert market dominance into consistent earnings [2]. The iBuying disaster demonstrated that Zillow's strategic ambitions can result in massive capital destruction — while the pivot was courageous, it destroyed billions in shareholder value. The 44% stock decline in 2026 reflects investor skepticism that the Housing Super App will generate returns commensurate with the investment required. AI disruption risk is real — AI agents could disrupt the Premier Agent referral model that generates the majority of revenue, and competitors like CoStar are spending aggressively on Homes.com to challenge Zillow's traffic dominance [3]. The 5yr revenue CAGR is negative (distorted by iBuying) but even adjusting for that, Zillow's underlying platform revenue growth has been modest relative to the opportunity.
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgages segment scaling rapidly with 95% purchase origination volume growth to $2.2B — as Zillow integrates home search with financing, the revenue per transaction expands dramatically beyond agent referral fees alone
  2. Rentals multifamily revenue growing 42% YoY as Zillow becomes the go-to platform for apartment advertising, with long-term TAM expansion into a $30B+ rental advertising market
  3. H2 2026 margin expansion guidance materializing as operating leverage on 18% revenue growth drives the profitability inflection that would validate the Housing Super App thesis and support stock re-rating
Key Risks
  1. Persistently thin profitability — 2.27% profit margin and 1.33% ROE after 20 years of operation raises fundamental questions about whether Zillow's market dominance can convert to durable earnings power
  2. AI disruption of the Premier Agent referral model — if AI-powered real estate agents reduce the need for human agents, Zillow's core monetization mechanism faces existential compression
  3. Housing market cyclicality — rising mortgage rates and low housing inventory directly reduce transaction volumes, and Zillow's 44% stock decline in 2026 reflects investor recognition that revenue growth depends on macro factors beyond management's control

AI Resilience (25%)
AI Resilience MODERATE
6
AI Risk Protection (5/9): Zillow's core Premier Agent business — connecting homebuyers with real estate agents — faces meaningful AI disruption risk as AI-powered agents could potentially replace human real estate agents, reducing the value of Zillow's referral model [3]. AI shopping assistants from Google, OpenAI, or Apple could bypass Zillow's curated search experience, and AI-generated home valuations could challenge the Zestimate's differentiation. However, Zillow's massive proprietary dataset (decades of listing data, home values, user behavior), brand dominance in home search, and deep integrations with MLSs and agent workflows create significant switching costs and data moats that pure AI competitors would struggle to replicate [1]. AI Opportunity (1/1): Zillow's Zestimate is a foundational AI/ML product processing billions of data points, and the company is actively deploying AI across hiring, productivity, agent tools, and customer matching. CEO Wacksman is 'bullish about AI's potential' and positioning Zillow to be the AI-powered platform for real estate [3].
