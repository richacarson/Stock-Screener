Company Profile
Communication Services ·Internet Content & Information ·NASDAQ ·United States
Zillow Group operates the largest real estate platform in the United States, connecting consumers with technology, agents, loan officers, and digital solutions. The company operates through Residential (agent advertising, SaaS CRM, transaction management), Mortgages (Zillow Home Loans, lender advertising), Rentals (property manager tools, rental applications), and Other segments. Brands include Zillow, Trulia, StreetEasy, HotPads, Follow Up Boss, ShowingTime, and dotloop. Founded in 2004 by Rich Barton (co-founder of Expedia), Zillow turned profitable in 2025 with $2.6B revenue (+16% YoY) and $23M net income. CEO Jeremy Wacksman took the helm in 2024. Zillow is building an 'AI-native housing platform' with AI Mode for guided home search.

Finite vs Infinite Game — Sinek (25%)
Mindset: MIXED Overall: 5 /10
Zillow demonstrates meaningful infinite game qualities through its 20-year commitment to making real estate more accessible and its willingness to abandon the costly iBuying experiment when it conflicted with sustainable execution. The smooth CEO transition from founder Rich Barton to Jeremy Wacksman shows organizational maturity. However, the company has not faced a true existential test of its Just Cause, and the competitive framing remains oriented toward market dominance rather than learning from rivals. Investment Thesis
Zillow at 10.0x forward earnings with a PEG of 0.92 offers compelling growth-at-a-reasonable-price exposure to the dominant U.S. real estate platform, with revenue accelerating to 16-18% growth and the company turning profitable for the first time in 2025 ($23M net income vs -$112M loss in 2024) [1]. Q1 2026 revenue of $708M (+18% YoY) demonstrates broad-based strength: Residential +12%, Mortgages +56%, and Rentals +42%, with mid-teens full-year 2026 revenue growth guided [1]. The nearly debt-free balance sheet (D/E 12.95%, $698M cash) provides operating flexibility, and the forward P/E of 10.03 implies significant earnings acceleration as operating leverage compounds on 17-18% revenue growth. Zillow's AI Mode and 20 years of proprietary real estate data create an AI-native platform advantage that would be extremely difficult for competitors to replicate [2].
The bear case centers on profitability fragility, housing cycle dependency, and AI disruption risk. Trailing profit margin of only 1.96% and ROE of 1.22% mean that Zillow has barely crossed the profitability threshold — any revenue growth deceleration or margin compression could return the company to losses, particularly given the 7,232-employee cost base [4]. The real estate market remains structurally challenged by elevated mortgage rates above 6%, depressing transaction volumes that drive Zillow's agent advertising revenue. While Zillow guides mid-teens revenue growth, the trailing P/E of 127x reflects how fragile the earnings base truly is — the forward P/E compression to 10x assumes a 12x earnings improvement that may not materialize if housing market conditions deteriorate. AI-powered real estate assistants from Google, OpenAI, or Apple could potentially disintermediate Zillow's search-and-browse model by enabling conversational home discovery that bypasses traditional listing platforms. The Inspire Impact Score of -67 (MISALIGNED) may limit allocation for faith-based investors [3].
Key Catalysts
  1. Mortgage rate normalization — each 100bps decline in mortgage rates unlocks significant pent-up housing demand, directly driving agent advertising revenue (Zillow's largest segment) and mortgage origination volume (Zillow Home Loans growing 56% YoY) [1]
  2. AI Mode and AI-native platform driving engagement and conversion — Zillow's 20 years of proprietary data create a compounding AI advantage that could widen the moat versus competitors with less training data [2]
  3. Earnings inflection as operating leverage compounds — fixed-cost base against 17-18% revenue growth should drive rapid margin expansion, potentially compressing the 127x trailing P/E toward the 10x forward multiple [1]
Key Risks
  1. Housing market sensitivity — persistently elevated mortgage rates above 6% continue to suppress home transaction volumes, and a recession-driven housing downturn could simultaneously compress revenue and margins given Zillow's high fixed-cost structure [1]
  2. AI disruption of search-and-browse model — conversational AI assistants from Google, Apple, or dedicated real estate AI startups could reduce consumer reliance on Zillow's traditional listing search, threatening the core agent advertising revenue model [2]
  3. Profitability fragility — trailing profit margin of 1.96% and net income of only $23M on $2.6B revenue means the company has minimal margin of safety against cost inflation, competitive pricing pressure, or revenue growth deceleration [4]

AI Risk Protection (6/9): Zillow's core business has meaningful defensive moats against AI disruption — 20 years of proprietary real estate data (Zestimate valuations, listing history, agent transaction records), established agent relationships with high switching costs, regulatory requirements in mortgage origination (Zillow Home Loans), and dominant brand recognition in real estate search [2].
