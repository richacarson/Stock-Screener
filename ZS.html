Company Profile
Technology ·Software - Infrastructure ·NASDAQ ·United States
Zscaler, Inc. operates the Zero Trust Exchange, a cloud-native cybersecurity platform that connects and secures users, devices, and applications without exposing them to the public internet. The platform processes over 500 billion daily transactions and extends to AI Security, Data Security, and Managed Detection and Response. Founded in 2007 by CEO Jay Chaudhry, headquartered in San Jose, California. Market cap approximately $22.5 billion.

Just Cause
7
Just Cause (2/3): Zscaler's founding vision — that security must move to the cloud and follow the user, not the network — was articulated by Jay Chaudhry in 2007, years before Zero Trust became an industry standard [3]. This purpose has been validated by 17 years of consistent execution and $3.5B in ARR, proving the architectural vision was correct. Strategic decisions (remaining cloud-native, refusing to build hardware appliances, expanding the platform organically and through acquisitions) clearly trace back to this founding purpose. However, the cause hasn't been tested through a major crisis or leadership transition — Chaudhry has led since founding.

Investment Thesis
Zscaler delivered Q3 FY2026 revenue of $850.5M (+25% YoY) with record 23% non-GAAP operating margin and $3.5B ARR, demonstrating accelerating profitability alongside sustained growth [1]. The company raised full-year FY2026 guidance to $3.33B revenue and $4.10-$4.11 non-GAAP EPS (+25%), with $963M in TTM free cash flow (30% margin) providing substantial financial flexibility [1]. Secular tailwinds in Zero Trust security adoption, enterprise cloud migration, and AI-driven threat landscape expansion create a durable growth runway, with AI agent proliferation creating an entirely new category of identities requiring Zero Trust enforcement.
The primary concern is growth deceleration — FY2027 preliminary guidance of 16-17% revenue growth represents a significant step-down from 25% in FY2026, and the stock's 52% decline over the past year reflects market repricing of the growth trajectory [1][2]. At 31.4x forward earnings, the premium valuation leaves limited margin of safety if growth decelerates further. GAAP profitability remains elusive (-2.4% net margin, -3.7% ROE) due to heavy stock-based compensation, and the $1.7B in convertible senior notes creates dilution risk at maturity [2]. Competition from Palo Alto Networks' platformization strategy, CrowdStrike's expansion into Zero Trust, and Microsoft's bundled security offerings create credible competitive threats that could pressure win rates, deal sizes, and long-term pricing power.
Key Catalysts
  1. Enterprise Zero Trust adoption accelerating as AI agent proliferation expands the corporate attack surface, driving demand for Zscaler's cloud-native security platform and its new Zero Trust for AI Agents capability
  2. Non-GAAP operating margin expansion from 23% toward 25%+ as the platform scales, with $963M TTM FCF demonstrating improving unit economics and a credible path toward GAAP profitability
  3. Platform expansion into data protection, MDR (Red Canary), and AI security creating cross-sell opportunities that could reaccelerate net new ARR growth above the 16-17% FY2027 guidance
Key Risks
  1. FY2027 revenue growth guidance of 16-17% represents meaningful deceleration from 25% in FY2026, and the 31.4x forward P/E leaves minimal margin of safety against further multiple compression if growth disappoints
  2. Palo Alto Networks' aggressive platformization, CrowdStrike's Zero Trust expansion, and Microsoft's bundled security offerings intensify competitive pressure on win rates, deal sizes, and pricing power
  3. GAAP losses persist due to heavy stock-based compensation, and $1.7B in convertible notes creates dilution risk at conversion — true economic profitability remains obscured for equity holders
