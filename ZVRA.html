Company Profile
Healthcare ·Biotechnology ·NASDAQ ·United States
Zevra Therapeutics is a commercial-stage rare disease biopharmaceutical company based in Celebration, Florida. Its lead product MIPLYFFA (arimoclomol) is the first and only FDA-approved treatment for Niemann-Pick disease Type C, and its second product OLPRUVA treats urea cycle disorders. The company also collects AZSTARYS royalties and was rebranded from KemPharm around a rare-disease commercial strategy under CEO Neil McFarlane.

Investment Thesis
Zevra delivered its second consecutive quarter of strong growth, with Q2 2026 revenue of $39.7M (+53.3% YoY) beating estimates, driven by MIPLYFFA's position as the first and only FDA-approved treatment for Niemann-Pick disease Type C [1]. The company reached debt-free status in 2026 by retiring all outstanding debt with divestiture proceeds, producing a pristine balance sheet (D/E near zero, ROE of 34.8%, profit margin of 42.8%) that mechanically scores a perfect 10 on Infrastructure [1][5]. As a physical specialty-pharma commercialization business serving an uncontested ultra-rare disease niche, Zevra carries low AI disruption risk (8/10), and its existential-flexibility pivot from ADHD prodrugs to rare-disease leadership demonstrates real strategic reinvention.
The bear case centers on concentration risk and unproven organizational depth. MIPLYFFA revenue includes a meaningful expanded-access reimbursement component ($10.2M of Q1's $36.2M) that is not guaranteed to persist as commercial uptake matures, and the July 2026 EMA CHMP negative opinion on arimoclomol's EU marketing application is a concrete pipeline setback that raises questions about international expansion execution [1][4]. Levi & Korsinsky's July 2026 investor investigation notice, while not a confirmed finding of wrongdoing, adds legal overhang [5]. Inspiration and Innovation scores are held down by an absence of public data on R&D intensity, community investment, insider buying, and employee retention — gaps that reflect Zevra's small size and short operating history as a rebranded company rather than confirmed weaknesses, but which limit confidence in the durability of its current growth trajectory.
Key Catalysts
  1. Continued MIPLYFFA commercial ramp in the US NPC market, where Zevra holds an uncontested first-and-only approved therapy position with no direct domestic competitor
  2. Potential resubmission or appeal of the arimoclomol EU marketing authorization following the July 2026 negative CHMP opinion, which would open a second major geographic market
  3. Conversion of expanded-access program patients into standard commercial reimbursement, which would improve revenue quality and reduce dependence on the expanded-access contribution
Key Risks
  1. Single-product revenue concentration in MIPLYFFA leaves Zevra exposed to any safety signal, reimbursement setback, or competitive entrant in the ultra-rare NPC indication
  2. The EMA's negative CHMP opinion on arimoclomol signals regulatory execution risk that could delay or block EU revenue Zevra likely built into long-term growth assumptions
  3. Pending Levi & Korsinsky investor investigation introduces legal and reputational uncertainty independent of underlying commercial performance
